### P1 — "spurious `--ab` starvation alarms on Kokomi runs go to exactly zero"

**FAIL, as written.** The alarm still fires.

What actually happened is worth more than the prediction was. The *spurious*
alarms did go to zero: `garment / ward / conscript` are gone, and they were
unfalsifiable — tags no card carries can never reach 10%, so the alarm was
structurally guaranteed on every Kokomi run. What replaced them is a REAL
signal at the same threshold: `commander` at 2.8% and `assist` at 8.5% are
genuinely below the 10% starvation floor.

So the instrument went from "always fires, means nothing" to "fires, and
means something". The prediction was written as though the alarm's only
content was the artifact. It had real content underneath, and nobody could
see it until the artifact was removed.

Two consequences, both **new findings, not part of this landing**:

- The adaptive drafter converges on **priest in 85.8% of runs**, which also
  trips the dominance alarm (0.55 threshold) at a comfortable margin. Her
  free-draft pool has one attractor.
- `commander` and `assist` are starved as *emergent* shapes. That is a claim
  about her card pool, not about this fix, and it belongs to whoever picks up
  the pool-sweep pass.

### P2 — "adaptive free-draft Kokomi winrate moves up; magnitude unbounded, direction committed"

**FAIL.** 15.8% → 10.8%, i.e. **−5.0pt**. The direction was committed in
advance and it went the other way.

Recorded as a null-discipline result. The reasoning behind the prediction was
that a scorer which can finally see synergy should draft better than one
scoring pure printed power — and measured, it drafts *worse*.

The honest reading is narrow. Both arms score through `_static_power`, which
prices 21.2% of Kokomi's draftable pool at exactly 0.0 (audit §2.5). So this
is not a clean read on "does synergy help": it is a read on a synergy term
layered over a pricer that is blind to a fifth of her cards. Steering harder
toward priest on partly-blind prices can easily be worse than not steering.
That confound is **EPOCH 2** (`_static_power` repricing, DRAFTER 11), which
[USER] deferred to a design session and which this landing deliberately did
not wait on.

What is NOT available as an escape: the fix is still correct. A registry
naming tags no card carries is broken regardless of which way the winrate
moves, and the pre-R66 15.8% was never a number about drafting Kokomi.

### P3 — "`dominant_archetype` on R56-style priest decks returns priest, not goodstuff, in ≥80% of runs"

**PASS.** 85.8% priest, against a pre-fix 0.0% (100% goodstuff).

This is the prediction that was really about the mechanism, and the mechanism
works exactly as ruled: `dominant_archetype` self-corrected the moment the
tuple matched real tags, with no code change of its own.

### Scoreboard

**1 of 3 passed.** P3 passed cleanly; P1 failed on wording while its
underlying intent held and uncovered a real signal; P2 failed outright, with
the committed direction wrong by 5.0 points.

Both arms are working trees — the BEFORE arm is the repo at the commit

whose only reader is a grading script, one ruling after R67 deleted nine of
exactly that.

### Why a 0.0pt result needed a second measurement

The graded cell drafts this card into **6.3% of decks**, and the ratified
`demolition_weighted` battery package does not contain it **at all**. A cell
that nearly never sees a card will report ~0.0pt whether the change is
negligible or enormous, and "small effect" versus "blind instrument" is the
distinction R67 spent a whole ruling separating. Reporting the PASS without
checking would have been the same error in a new costume.

A forced-carrier arm settles it — two copies in every deck, 400 gauntlet
fights, **498 casts**:

| | BEFORE | AFTER |
|---|---|---|
| turns / fight | 4.185 | 4.160 |
| HP lost / fight | 17.622 | 17.410 |

Real, in the predicted direction, and small. Not a balance number and not
comparable to anything else — a deck holding two copies of an uncommon is
not a deck anyone drafts — but it establishes that the changed branch is
live and that its magnitude is genuinely what the prediction claimed.

One instrument was discarded mid-measurement and it is worth recording why:
**total damage dealt is useless here.** A won fight deals exactly the
enemies' HP, so it read 37000 in both arms regardless of what the card did.
Extra damage on a multi-hit attack does not buy damage; it buys tempo and
HP, which is what the table above measures.
