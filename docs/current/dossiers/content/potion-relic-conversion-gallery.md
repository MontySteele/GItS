# Register & Naming Rules Enforced (lintable)

1. **NAME-SOURCE**: every `teyvat_name` must be (a) a canon item's exact name, (b) a canon-anchored narrowing (a component or serving of a named canon object, e.g. "Glaze Lily Bulb", "Sesshou Sakura Sprig"), or (c) an invention whose `canon_source` explicitly says "in-style invention" and anchors to a named canon entity or location. FAIL: unlabeled inventions presented as canon (e.g. "Jueyun Guoba" claimed as a "real Liyue dish").
2. **CANON-FACT**: every checkable clause in `canon_source` (existence, nation, item category, effect *category*, acquisition source) must match the wiki record. An effect-category error that carries the mapping's rationale (heal vs ATK vs CRIT vs stamina vs revive; wrong drop source; wrong lore identity) = cut. Incidental magnitude misquotes on an otherwise category-correct claim = keep, but surface via item flag (e.g. Adeptus' Temptation's figures).
3. **SET-GATE (hard)**: no artifact set name, set bonus, or piece-count language anywhere in name, canon_source, flavor, or rationale. Reason code on cut: `set-shaped`. (Zero hits this pass; mentions of sets only to explain routing *around* them are permitted.)
4. **ONE-REFERENT-ONE-HOME**: a canon item (or its direct narrowing — "Noctilucous Jade Beads" ≡ Noctilucous Jade, "Sakura Bloom Cord" ≡ Sakura Bloom) may be the #1 pick of at most one gallery entry; duplicate claims elsewhere are cut as redundant unless the losing item would fall to zero survivors. Same-line different-tier items (Vajrada Sliver vs Fragment; Damaged vs Ominous Mask) may coexist but never both at #1.
5. **RESERVED-NAMES**: nothing from docs/reserved-card-names.txt, "Dodoco Tales", "Explosive Frags", character-owned vocabulary (Klee's bomb lexicon), or names the design doc reserves for future features (Wish/gacha item names such as "Acquaint Fate" — reserved for the deferred v2 shop-Wish) may be minted for an unrelated item.
6. **VOICE**: flavor is diegetic Genshin item-register — concrete object first, quiet lyric second; humor only in-world (an NPC's habit, a filed complaint), never fourth-wall; no engine vocabulary in flavor (Block, Energy, draw, turn, stacks), no quoted numbers, percentages, or durations.
7. **NO-OVERPROMISE**: flavor may not assert an element application ("Pyro"), a duration, a heal, or any rider the frozen hook lacks; names for unpowered/elementless effects stay in cooking or craft language; SKIP-relic flavor must locate the power outside the carried token (planted, set into the ground, spent).
8. **NATION-FIT**: mapped items must sit in nations the run's roster plausibly touches (Mondstadt / Liyue / Inazuma / Fontaine preferred; Sumeru acceptable for neutral materials); character-linked materials may sit in un-gated pools only when the character link is a bonus, not the load-bearing argument; owner-gated or family-shaped boons must not imply an owner the gate excludes.
9. **PAIRED-ITEMS**: sibling relics on the same hook (War Paint/Whetstone/Sand Castle; Golden Pearl/Hand of Greed) must read as a coordinated family — technique-objects vs weapon-materials vs bulk material; smaller vs larger windfall — never two unrelated claims on the same shelf.

# The Gallery

## - [ ] block_potion (potion)
*Base mechanics (frozen): Common-tier potion, id `block_potion` ("Block Potion"). Effect lives in tier0/engine/potions.py::apply_potion: `p.block += C.POTION_BLOCK`, where POTION_BLOCK = 12 (tier0/constants.py:505). So: on drink, gain 12 Block immediately. Untargeted (not in _TARGETED), instant, no power applied, no HP or energy change, no card draw; the Block is ordinary engine Block and decays under the normal end-of-turn rules. It is in DRINKABLE, and the AI use-policy `_try_defensive` treats it as the top-priority defensive drink — ahead of blood_potion — when projected incoming damage minus current Block would leave the player at or below POTION_DEFENSIVE_MARGIN. tier05/content/potions.yaml carries no effect data at all: it only lists the id under `common:` with a display name (the trailing comment "# gain 12 Block" is documentation). FROZEN: the conversion renames and re-flavors only.*

### 1. Chili-Mince Cornbread Buns
*Canon source: Chili-Mince Cornbread Buns — real Liyue dish, 4-star, recipe from the Moonchase Tales quest line of the Moonlight Merriment (Moonchase Festival) event. Canon effect: increases all party members' Shield Strength by 25/30/35% and DEF by 165/200/235 for 300s.*

> Steamed buns turned out round and rested, then hollowed and packed with chili-fried mince the moment before eating. Solid, honest festival food — the kind that sits in the stomach like a short stretch of city wall.

This is the one canon consumable line in Teyvat whose stated effect touches shields directly rather than raw defense: food that makes a shield hold. Block is exactly that object — a temporary absorb pool that stands in front of HP, is spent by the next blow, and is gone after. The drink-it-when-the-hit-is-coming beat the engine's _try_defensive policy encodes is the same beat as wolfing down Moonchase buns before a fight in Liyue; and a flat 12, not a scaling stat, reads correctly as one serving of stubborn peasant grit rather than a lasting improvement to your armor.

### 2. Fontainian Foie Gras
*Canon source: Fontainian Foie Gras — real Fontaine dish (added in 4.0). Canon effect: increases all party members' DEF by 200 for 300s.*

> A traditional dish of Fontaine: the foie gras needs only to be pan-fried, and the whole art of it is the heat. Held a heartbeat too long and the golden crust that was meant to shield the tenderness inside becomes the thing that ruins it.

Its canon buff is a flat additive number on a timer, not a percentage — the same grammar as a flat 12 Block that exists and then expires, which keeps the conversion honest about the frozen amount. Fontaine also puts the potion in Furina's home nation, where the mod already has a playable character, so the pool reads as travel rations picked up along the run. The mechanical metaphor holds at the plate level too: a seared crust guarding something soft is a layer that takes the heat first, which is what Block does before HP is touched.

### 3. Lotus Flower Crisp
*Canon source: Lotus Flower Crisp — real Liyue traditional snack, recipe from Ms. Yu at Liyue Reputation Level 4. Canon effect: increases all party members' DEF by 165/200/235 for 300s.*

> One of Liyue's old snacks: sweet lotus filling sealed inside a crisp, deep-fried shell. It shatters loudly at the first bite, which everyone agrees is the entire point of it.

A brittle shell that breaks so the soft thing inside does not is the most literal possible image of Block: the layer is consumed by the hit, makes a noise, and is not there next turn. Canon backs the reading — it is a genuine DEF-boosting dish, not an invented one. Being a cheap, widely-cooked Liyue reputation snack also suits a common-tier pool entry that a Traveler would realistically be carrying several of.

*Drafter's resistance note: Mild, and worth naming so the flavor text does not overpromise. Teyvat's consumables buff a stat (DEF, flat or %) for the whole party over a 300s window; nothing in canon food grants an instant absorb pool that eats one hit and vanishes at end of turn. The closest lore object to Block proper is a Crystallize or Geo shield, but those come from reactions and talents, not from anything you eat, so they cannot be the source item. The alchemy-bench potions are a worse fit than food despite being literal potions: Frostshield / Windbarrier and their kin are elemental-RES and environmental-survival items (Dragonspine's Sheer Cold, Anemo hazards), not general damage absorption, so mapping a generic 12-Block drink onto one would misstate what those potions do in canon. Hence the preference order above: Chili-Mince Cornbread Buns first, because Shield Strength is the only canon consumable effect that names shields at all. Golden Crab was considered and passed over — its shell-as-armor image is excellent, but its canon effect bundles a healing-bonus rider that the frozen mechanic has no room for, and importing it would invite a mechanical change that is out of scope. Whichever mapping is taken, the item text should describe a shell or a wall and avoid stating a duration, since the potion's Block follows the engine's ordinary Block rules and no timing claim may be added.*

## - [ ] fire_potion (potion)
*Base mechanics (frozen): Common-tier, single-target. On use, deals C.POTION_FIRE_DAMAGE = 20 damage to ONE enemy through refpowers.unpowered_damage (tier0/engine/refpowers.py:97) — the UNPOWERED pipeline, deliberately not effects.deal_damage_to_enemy. Consequences that are load-bearing to the flavor: it does NOT scale with the player's Strength, is NOT amplified by the target's Vulnerable or the reaction amp, and as a non-Attack source it does not detonate Bombs or fire Shatter. Enemy Block still absorbs it (Unpowered is not Unblockable); overkill is not credited. It carries NO element tag and applies no aura. Targeting: listed in _TARGETED; if no target is supplied, apply_potion picks the lowest-HP living enemy, and with no living enemy emits potion_fizzle and does nothing. Use policy (tier0/engine/potions.py:_try_offensive, elites/bosses only): fire_potion is priority 1 and is drunk only to close a kill outright — any living enemy with 0 < hp <= 20. Pool placement: common tier in tier05/content/potions.yaml (common bucket weight 0.65). All of the above is FROZEN; nothing below proposes a mechanical change.*

> **FLAG — [USER] decision needed:** Teyvat has no consumable that damages an enemy, and after canon cuts the sole survivor is Fish-Flavored Toast — a Klee-linked DEF dish in a shared colorless pool with the draft's own bomb-implication caveat — so [USER] must decide whether to accept it or commission fresh candidates.

### 1. Fish-Flavored Toast
*Canon source: Fish-Flavored Toast — canon Mondstadt dish, Klee's specialty variant of Fisherman's Toast. Canon effect: increases all party members' DEF by 151 for 300s — a DEF food, not a damage one, which is the category mismatch the FLAG above puts to [USER].*

> Klee insists that the fish practically leap onto the bread. She is, in her way, telling the truth. Best enjoyed without asking after the fishing rod.

The dish's canon origin story IS the mechanic: Klee 'fishes' by dropping a bomb, and what comes back is a fixed-size blast landing on one thing in the water. That is a flat 20 to a single target — not an attack that grows with the angler's strength, not something a stagger makes worse, just a charge with a set yield. It is also the mod's most legible common: Klee is a shipped playable character, so the joke is already in the player's hands, and a homely piece of toast sits correctly at common tier next to Block Potion and Swift Potion rather than reading as a rare. Two caveats to weigh against the first two mappings: it is a Klee-linked name in a colorless/shared potion pool (the design doc's naming sources feed character material into that character's own pool, and Klee's bomb vocabulary — Jumpy Dumpty, Dodoco — is already spoken for in docs/klee-cards.yaml), and 'toast' undersells a 20-damage finisher visually more than a red-hot bolognese or a fire-breathing bear does.

- **Cut:** Jueyun Guoba — canon error: no dish named 'Jueyun Guoba' exists — Guoba is Xiangling's summon, and the real dish (Jueyun Chili Chicken) is a CRIT-Rate food, not the claimed ATK dish
- **Cut:** Flaming Red Bolognese — canon error: verified as an HP-restoring dish (18/20/22% Max HP + regen), not the claimed attack-boosting food — the entire rationale rests on the false category

*Drafter's resistance note: Two real frictions, neither fatal, both worth naming. (1) Direction of fire: no canon Genshin consumable damages an enemy — every dish, potion and oil in the game is self- or party-targeted. StS throws Fire Potion AT something; Teyvat food is always eaten. So every mapping has to re-read 'throw it' as either 'eat it and one flat extra hit lands' (the food-buff frame the brief endorses) or delegate delivery to a summon that the food is named for — which is why Jueyun Guoba was drafted first: Guoba is the one canon object that both is a dish and points a fixed jet of flame at exactly one enemy. It was cut on canon grounds above, so nothing surviving carries that property. (2) Element promise: the engine gives this potion no element tag and no aura — apply_potion routes straight to refpowers.unpowered_damage. In a mod whose core system is auras and reactions (design principles §2), a heavily Pyro-branded potion name would advertise a Pyro application the item never makes, and a player would reasonably drink it expecting Vaporize/Melt fuel. The item text should therefore stay in cooking language — hot, seared, chili, forge-colored — and never use the word 'Pyro'. All three candidate flavors above are written to that constraint. Related minor note: because the damage is unpowered, it also won't detonate Klee's Bombs; the flavor should not imply a bomb going off in the enemy's midst — a caveat written when Fish-Flavored Toast was ranked last of three, and one that survives its promotion to sole survivor.*

## - [ ] blood_potion (potion)
*Base mechanics (frozen): FROZEN — rename/re-flavor only. Pool entry: tier05/content/potions.yaml, common tier (`blood_potion: {name: "Blood Potion"}`), common band weight 0.65. Effect lives in tier0/engine/potions.py `apply_potion` and is keyed by id: heal = int(C.POTION_BLOOD_HEAL_FRACTION * player.max_hp) with POTION_BLOOD_HEAL_FRACTION = 0.20 (tier0/constants.py:507), i.e. heal 20% of MAX HP (not current, not missing), clamped to the HP actually missing (`min(heal, max_hp - hp)`); if the clamp is 0 nothing happens and no event fires, otherwise `state.emit("heal", amount=healed, source="potion")` followed by `potion_used`. Self-targeted (not in `_TARGETED`), instant, one-shot, consumed from `player.potions` by `_drink`; grants no Block, no power, no draw, no energy, and leaves nothing behind after the heal. In DRINKABLE, so the AI use-policy may drink it: only via `_try_defensive` at the player's turn start, when projected incoming damage minus Block would drop the player to <= POTION_DEFENSIVE_MARGIN (0); it is second in priority behind block_potion, and is deliberately held rather than spent when hp == max_hp (it would heal zero). Never used offensively; not a revive (that is fairy_in_a_bottle). Potions only exist on grant_potions=True runs; dead code on the frozen battery.*

### 1. Sweet Madame
*Canon source: Sweet Madame (Genshin Impact 1-star Mondstadt dish; recipe known from the start of the game, cooked from 2x Fowl + 2x Sweet Flower). Canon effect: restores 20%/22%/24% of Max HP to the target character plus an additional 900/1,200/1,500 HP by quality.*

> Honey-roasted fowl, sweet flowers folded into the glaze. The first dish any adventurer learns and the last one they stop carrying — nothing left but cleanly picked bones, and legs that will hold you a little longer.

The numbers line up without being nudged: base-quality Sweet Madame restores exactly 20% of Max HP, the same fraction as POTION_BLOOD_HEAL_FRACTION, on the same terms the engine uses — instant, self-targeted, single-use, percentage of MAXIMUM HP, with no lingering effect afterward. Almost every other canon healing dish bundles a regen-over-time tail the engine's one-shot heal does not model; Sweet Madame is the clean instant heal, so it is the only candidate whose mechanical shape needs no apology. Rarity matches too: a 1-star dish whose recipe you own from minute one, cooked from birds and flowers gathered outside Mondstadt's gate, is precisely a COMMON-tier drop. And the use-policy reads as food rather than pharmacology — you eat when you are hurt and hold onto the plate when you are not, which is exactly the 'don't drink at full HP' guard in _try_defensive. (Honest note: canon adds a flat HP bonus on top of the percentage; the conversion borrows the percentage clause, which is the load-bearing one.)

### 2. Apple Cider
*Canon source: Apple Cider (Genshin Impact Mondstadt consumable; not cookable — bought from Charles at the Angel's Share tavern for 1,500 Mora, max 2 per day). Canon effect: restores 26% of Max HP to the selected character and regenerates HP over 30s.*

> A freshly squeezed, fruity, and entirely non-alcoholic drink, said to have a powerful sobering effect. Charles pours it for the patrons who still have to walk home; adventurers found it works just as well on those who still have to keep walking.

This is the closest canon FORM-FACTOR to a Spire potion slot: a corked bottle you carry and drain in one motion, not a plate you have to sit down to. Its effect is stated the way the engine states it — a percentage of Max HP to one selected character, instant, consumed on use, no target needed but yourself. The acquisition shape maps onto the potion economy too: Apple Cider has no recipe, it is purchased and hard-capped at two a day, so it behaves like a bought-or-found consumable rather than something you can cook to order — a scarce bottle in a belt slot. The 'sobering' flavor is the mechanical metaphor stated in Genshin's own voice: this is the drink that puts someone back on their feet mid-collapse, which is precisely when _try_defensive reaches for it. Caveat named rather than hidden: canon Apple Cider also regenerates HP over time and heals 26%, not 20% — the conversion borrows only its instant clause, and the numbers stay frozen at the engine's.

### 3. Dango Milk
*Canon source: Dango Milk (Genshin Impact Inazuma consumable; not cookable — bought from Tomoki in Inazuma City for 1,500 Mora, max 2 per day, unlocked after the Raiden Shogun story quest Imperatrix Umbrosa Chapter Act I). Canon effect: restores 26% of Max HP to the selected character and regenerates HP over 30s.*

> Three-color dango steeped in milk until the sweetness gives up and dissolves. Sold two bottles at a time to a city that would happily buy twenty — a mouthful of home for whoever has wandered too far from it.

The roster's Inazuma tie (Kokomi), and mechanically the same argument as Apple Cider on the other side of the sea: a bottled, portable drink restoring a percentage of Max HP to one selected character, instantly, on a two-per-day purchase cap that reads as potion-slot scarcity rather than an at-will resource. Chugging it costs a beat mid-fight the way drinking a potion does — you stop, you drink, you are standing. Two honest snags: canon carries a regen-over-time tail the engine does not implement, and Dango Milk's strongest lore association is with the Raiden Shogun, which sits a little oddly next to Kokomi's Watatsumi resistance — it is a neutral Inazuma City street consumable, so it survives that, but Sweet Madame carries no such friction.

*Drafter's resistance note: The mechanic maps cleanly; the NAME is what resists. Teyvat has no vampiric or sanguinary consumable — restorative power in Genshin is culinary and domestic, never visceral, so 'Blood Potion' has no Teyvat cousin and the conversion must abandon the blood conceit entirely rather than translate it. (The nearest blood-motif items — Arlecchino's specialty Hearthfire's Trail, the Sanguine-named furnishings — are dishes and decor, not bottled restoratives; there is no Genshin item that heals you by being blood.) Second, the alchemy branch the brief permits is genuinely dry here: Genshin's craftable potions (Frostshield, Windbarrier, Streaming, Dendrocide, Unmoving Essential Oil) are without exception environmental/elemental utility — none of them heal — so food has to carry this conversion, which is why all three candidates are dishes or drinks. Third, a systemic mismatch worth flagging for the whole potion gallery: most canon healing dishes pair an instant percentage heal with a heal-over-time tail, a shape the engine has no vocabulary for (the payload is a single clamped hp bump). Sweet Madame is recommended first precisely because it is pure instant heal with no tail to explain away. Finally, a nation-coverage note, not an objection: the two strongest fits are Mondstadt and the third is Inazuma, leaving Furina's Fontaine unrepresented — if the gallery wants nation spread, Poisson Seafood Soup (Fontaine, 2x Fowl + 1x Jam + 1x Marcotte) is the Fontaine %-Max-HP dish, but it carries the regen tail and is a soup rather than a carried bottle, so it fits worse on both axes.*

### 1. Adeptus' Temptation
*Canon source: Adeptus' Temptation — canon Liyue 5-star delicacy (ham, crab, matsutake, shrimp meat): "Increases all party members' ATK by 260/316/372 and CRIT Rate by 8/10/12% for 300s."*

> A dish said to lure adepti down from their peaks by smell alone. Mortals who can afford it eat it before something they do not expect to survive.

This is Teyvat's canonical "drink it for the boss" food, and the use-policy backs the reference exactly: the engine only spends this potion against an elite/boss or a big telegraph (tier0/engine/potions.py:228-231). The flat-ATK half of the buff is the Strength metaphor. The reservation against it is a rarity mismatch, not a lore one — Adeptus' Temptation is a 5-star luxury dish and the potion is common-tier, so the flavor promises more than a +2 delivers. Its CRIT Rate line is also pure flavor here; the mod does not model crit (design principles §1, "Not adopting"), so nothing about it should be read as a mechanical claim.

- **Cut:** Jueyun Chili Chicken — canon error: verified effect is party CRIT Rate 6/9/12%, not the claimed flat ATK 66/81/95 — the flat-ATK argument is the whole mapping
- **Cut:** Grilled Tiger Fish — canon error: verified as an HP-restoring dish (8/9/10% Max HP + flat HP), not the claimed party-ATK food

### 1. Dragonheir's False Fin
*Canon source: Dragonheir's False Fin — canon boss material dropped by the Bathysmal Vishap Herd in Enkanomiya.*

> A ridge of false scale grown by a vishap that never earned a dragon's name. It bore the sea's whole weight without splitting, and it split whatever came down on it instead.

A vishap's dorsal ridge is defensive armament whose entire function is passive counter-injury: the beast does not swing it, it simply refuses to be struck softly. That is the shape of the hook — a persistent, unspendable combat-long property that converts an incoming attack into outgoing damage without the holder acting. The 'false' in the canon name earns the relic's modest fixed 3: this is imitation dragon-plate, not the real article, so it answers every blow with the same small serrated toll rather than scaling. Enkanomiya's deep waters sit in Kokomi's half of the map, and 'scales' translates to a scaled sea-beast's plate more honestly than any land material.

### 2. Sharp Arrowhead
*Canon source: Sharp Arrowhead — canon common material, the blue-tier drop from Hilichurl Shooters (Firm Arrowhead / Sharp Arrowhead / Weathered Arrowhead).*

> Barbs the hilichurls knapped for their own bows, sewn point-outward into a jerkin's lining. Whoever closes to strike you finishes the arrow's journey on their behalf.

This is the plainest reading of a bronze scale coat: overlapping hard plates a body wears, here made of reclaimed enemy ammunition. The retaliation is entirely reactive and entirely the attacker's own fault — the barbs travel nowhere until someone drives themselves onto them, which is why the damage is unpowered and Strength-blind in the frozen implementation. A humble, common-tier drop suits a Common relic granting only 3, and the arrowheads' indifference to whether the hit hurt you matches the block-piercing clause: the point goes in on contact, not on injury.

### 1. Sesshou Sakura Sprig
*Canon source: Sesshou Sakura — the sacred sakura saplings grown from the Sacred Sakura's lineage and planted to repel evil in Inazuma (Chinju Forest and Narukami Island; Yae Miko's craft, cf. her technique "Yakan Evocation: Sesshou Sakura")*

> A cutting taken from a sapling of the Sacred Sakura's line, its petals still holding a faint hum of thunder. Planted at a crossroads it turns back the small malevolence that gathers there at dusk; carried in the hand, it is only a branch that has forgotten where it was meant to stand.

The Sesshou Sakura is canonically a WARD placed at a boundary — it does not fight, it makes lesser things decline to approach, and it has no say over anything greater that has already set itself against you. That is precisely the bracelet's intended shape: a bias applied at the threshold of an uncertain place against ordinary encounters only, with elites, bosses and every other node untouched. It also earns the relic's actual state honestly — the ward works when PLANTED (i.e. wired into the place-resolution), and a sprig merely held is inert, which is exactly what a SKIP relic with no channel into resolve_unknown is. Inazuma is the correct nation: the source relic is a Buddhist juzu, and Inazuma is Teyvat's Japan-analogue, so the sacred-sakura ward is the native-language version of prayer beads rather than a transplant.

### 2. Shard of a Shore Seal
*Canon source: The warding seals along Yashiori Island's coast, from the World Quest "Orobashi's Legacy" — set by the serpent-god Orobashi to hold back the wrath that seeped from his own remains. In-style as a CARRIED item: the seals exist in canon as world objects to be repaired, not as an inventory piece, so the carriable shard is the invented part; the ward itself is canon.*

> A chipped fragment of one of the seals the serpent-god left along the shore, its inked ward worn down to a whisper. It was cut to turn common things back from the water's edge, and it did so faithfully for a long while; it never once slowed the thunder.

Orobashi's seals are the purest boundary-ward in Teyvat's canon: a line laid down at a place of passage that keeps ORDINARY things from crossing while being flatly powerless against anything of real stature — the Musou no Hitotachi came through regardless. That two-tier reading is the bracelet's mechanic stated in lore terms: common encounters excluded from the uncertain node, elites and bosses entirely unaffected. The "chipped, worn to a whisper" framing is also the truthful register for a relic that is on the skip list — the ward's authority lives in the intact seal set into the ground, not in the piece you can pocket, which mirrors the missing channel between a held relic and the room that resolves at entry.

*Drafter's resistance note: Low-to-moderate, and the resistance is in two specific places rather than in the theme. (1) The theme itself converts easily — juzu are Buddhist prayer beads, Inazuma is Teyvat's Japan-analogue, and Inazuma is unusually rich in genuine ward objects (Sesshou Sakura, Electrograna/Sakura Blooms, Orobashi's shore seals, shrine torii), so there is no need to reach for a foreign nation or for an artifact piece. Nothing here is set-shaped: no artifact set, no set bonus, no piece-count language is used or implied.*
