## Andrius, Lupus Boreas — Dominator of Wolves (Wolf of the North Challenge)
*Element: Cryo primary, with Anemo added in Phase 2. Immune to BOTH Cryo and Anemo (the only enemy in the game immune to two elements); 10% RES to Physical and all other elements. No weakspot, no energy drops from the body itself (Cryo orbs come from specific attacks). Practical counter-element identity in play: Pyro (also the element that breaks his ice pillars fastest).* · *Version 1.0 — one of the two launch weekly bosses (28 September 2020), alongside Confront Stormterror (Dvalin). Both have been in the weekly rotation continuously since day one, making Andrius the oldest tier of weekly-boss pool alongside Dvalin. Access is quest-gated behind 'The Meaning of Lupical' (Razor's Story Quest), and the challenge is temporarily unavailable during the 'Dishonorable Trial' quest. Drops Tail of Boreas, Ring of Boreas, and Spirit Locket of Boreas, plus Shivada Jade / Agnidus Agate / Prithiva Topaz gemstones — notably NOT Vayuda Turquoise, despite the boss using Anemo, because the 1.0 weekly pair split gemstone coverage between them. No mechanical rework has been recorded since 1.0; the only documented changes are bug fixes around Version 1.2 (Zhongli petrification, the out-of-arena exploit).*

### Phases
- **Phase 1 — Cryo Beast (baseline rotation)** — trigger: Fight start. Open-arena encounter in Wolvendom, not a Trounce Domain; boss scales to World Level and despawns if the player leaves the arena.
  Pure Cryo melee-brawler loop with no state layers. Opener is typically Wolf Leap — telegraphed by an ice circle painted on the ground that he jumps to (83.3% ATK Cryo, generates a Cryo energy orb, i-frame-proof). Rotation cycles: Wolf Charge (windup, then a dash at the player leaving a lingering ice trail, 100% ATK); Wolf Slash (front-arc claw, 58.3% ATK Cryo); Tail Sweep, which is range-conditional — at range it fires small icicles (25% ATK) that persist on the floor as delayed mines and detonate later for 58.3% ATK plus a Cryo orb; Spinning Slash (creates circular ice surfaces around himself, then spins, 83.3% ATK, wider hitbox in front than behind, unblockable and i-frame-proof, drops a Cryo orb). After hind-leg attacks he spends a beat on Repositioning to face the player — the fight's clearest free-hit window. The turn-shape a designer would recognize: a short randomized menu of front-arc and gap-close attacks, two of them unavoidable-by-dodge (must be spaced, not blinked), interleaved with a self-inflicted recovery beat.
- **Interlude — The Circling Hunt (transition script)** — trigger: HP gate at roughly half health, OR a soft timer if the player is too slow. Sources disagree on the exact number: the Genshin Impact Wiki states below 55%; KQM's library states below 51%. Skippable entirely — if a single strike drops him past the gate hard enough (Wiki: to 0; KQM: under 41%) before the Interlude begins, the script is bypassed.
  A scripted, mostly non-interactive movement phase — the fight's only true 'cutscene with hitboxes.' The arena perimeter ices over, dealing 16.6% ATK as Cryo damage-over-time to anything standing in it, which is the strongest version of the floor hazard in the whole fight. Andrius runs a lap around the arena rim, stops, and fires a Wolf Charge at the player; he does this twice, laps once more, then Wolf Leaps to arena center. This is a forced downtime/repositioning phase: the boss is chasing rather than trading, the player's damage window is poor, and the environment is doing the pressuring instead of the boss. Exit branches: if the HP/time gate is met he enters Phase 2; if not, he simply resumes the Phase 1 rotation and can re-enter the Interlude later.
- **Phase 2 — Wolf King (Cryo + Anemo, arena-armed)** — trigger: Exiting the Interlude with the gate satisfied. Skipped outright if he is killed before the Interlude ends.
  Opens with two simultaneous setup effects, not an attack: a Pushing Vacuum Field that shoves nearby players off him, and the generation of 6 Frozen Pillars plus an icy floor ring around the arena edge (4.16% ATK Cryo DoT and -12% Movement Speed — note this floor is WEAKER than the Interlude floor but adds a slow). He keeps every Phase 1 move and layers on a second, Anemo-flavored kit: Wolf King Roar (long charge, then a huge-AoE Anemo DoT whose damage scales UP with distance — an inverted safe-zone that punishes kiting rather than melee); Wolf Slash reskinned to Anemo (58.3% ATK); Wind Blades (3 vertical blades in a 60-degree forward cone, 41.6% ATK Anemo); Icicle Storm (charge, then sky-fall icicles, 25% ATK, i-frame-proof); Ice Waves (paints an ice path in front of him that applies Cryo and heavily slows anyone on it, then sends waves down that path for 50% ATK); Tail Sweep becomes a pure melee 58.3% ATK hit; and Small Phantom Wolf adds (25% ATK Cryo each). The signature interaction: his own Wolf King Roar and Wind Blades detonate his Frozen Pillars, each exploding for 66.6% ATK Cryo and scattering 0–5 Cryo energy orbs — the boss weaponizes his own furniture, and the player farms energy off it.
- **Low-HP Escalation (enrage)** — trigger: HP falls below a low threshold — Genshin Impact Wiki states 15%, KQM states 20% — OR the player takes too long. Not a new moveset, a rate change.
  Phantom wolf summons escalate in frequency and attack speed, layering continuous chip-damage adds on top of his existing rotation. Functionally an add-spam enrage timer: the punish for a slow kill is a rising floor of unavoidable incidental damage rather than a bigger single hit.

### Signature mechanics
- Two-element immunity as an identity, not a resistance number: Andrius is immune to both Cryo AND Anemo — the exact two elements he attacks with. The fight structurally forbids mirroring the boss; you cannot answer his element with his element.
- Elemental identity shift across the phase gate: Phase 1 is monochrome Cryo. Phase 2 does not replace it — it stacks Anemo on top, including an Anemo recolor of an existing Phase 1 move (Wolf Slash) so the same animation carries a different element depending on phase.
- Boss-detonated environment: the Frozen Pillars are not player-triggered hazards. Andrius's own Wolf King Roar and Wind Blades set them off, so his AoE attacks convert into a secondary ring of 66.6% ATK Cryo explosions plus 0–5 Cryo energy orbs each. Player and boss both profit from the same detonation.
- Destructible elemental furniture: the pillars have no visible health bar but carry a Cryo elemental shield (shield strength 12) and are broken with Elemental Reactions, weakest to Pyro. Killing a pillar removes the damaging icy floor around it — a persistent, incremental arena-cleanup mechanic.
- Inverted range punish: Wolf King Roar deals MORE Anemo DoT the farther you are from Andrius (0.83–2.5% ATK scaling with distance). Nearly every other threat in the fight pushes you out; this one pulls you in.
- Dodge-proof attacks: Wolf Leap, Spinning Slash, and the falling icicles of Icicle Storm explicitly cannot be avoided with dash i-frames. Positional spacing is the only answer — the fight has moves that ignore the standard defensive verb.
- Displacement as a phase opener: the Phase 2 entry Pushing Vacuum Field is pure forced repositioning, deliberately separating the player from the boss at the exact moment the arena arms itself.
- Delayed-detonation mines: ranged Tail Sweep leaves icicles on the floor that persist and later explode for 58.3% ATK and yield a Cryo orb — a lingering hazard the player must track across later turns.
- Escalating add pressure instead of a damage enrage: Small Phantom Wolves ramp in frequency at low HP or on a soft timer.
- Burst-gate phase skipping: enough damage in a single strike at the right moment skips the Interlude and/or Phase 2 entirely. The fight's own structure is bypassable by damage.

### Arena gimmicks
- Open-world arena, not a Trounce Domain — the only weekly boss encounter of its kind. Consequences: he scales to the player's current World Level rather than a fixed domain level, he despawns if the player runs far enough or teleports away, there is no teleport directly to the boss, and the party can be swapped after the kill before claiming rewards.
- Two distinct icy-floor hazards with different numbers: the Interlude ring deals 16.6% ATK Cryo DoT; the Phase 2 ring deals 4.16% ATK Cryo DoT and applies -12% Movement Speed. Both cover the arena EDGE, compressing the safe area toward center exactly when the boss is centered.
- 6 Frozen Pillars around the perimeter in Phase 2: Cryo-shielded (shield strength 12), no visible HP bar, broken via Elemental Reactions and weakest to Pyro. Each destroyed pillar deletes the icy floor patch around it, so the arena is progressively reclaimable.
- Pillar explosions as a two-way resource: 66.6% ATK Cryo damage to the player, and 0–5 Cryo energy orbs dropped at the pillar's location — the hazard doubles as the fight's main energy economy, alongside orbs from Wolf Leap, Spinning Slash, and the icicle mines.
- Ice Waves paints a temporary terrain strip in front of Andrius that applies Cryo and drastically slows anyone standing on it BEFORE the waves travel down it — a telegraphed lane you must vacate.
- Lingering ice trail left behind by Wolf Charge, so his gap-closer permanently edits the floor it crossed.
- Cryo application plus Frozen risk from the floor and slows is the real lethality driver, which is why Pyro Resonance (reduced Cryo affliction duration) is the standard structural answer rather than a raw-damage choice.

### Translates naturally (observations, not proposals)
- The phase structure is already gate-shaped: a clean HP threshold at roughly half health, a scripted transition, then an expanded moveset. That maps one-to-one onto an HP-gate phase change with a fixed transition turn.
- Attacks are already a discrete menu with distinct telegraphs and fixed percentages, which reads as an intent list — a gap-closer, a front-arc swipe, a range-conditional tail attack, an unavoidable spin, an AoE roar.
- The range-conditional Tail Sweep (icicles at range, pure melee up close) is a positional branch on a single intent — the kind of conditional an intent system expresses easily as 'if the player did X, this attack becomes Y.'
- Both icy floors are literal damage-over-time on a per-tick basis, already the shape of an end-of-turn DoT.
- The delayed-detonation icicle mines are a countdown effect: placed on one turn, resolved several turns later.
- Frozen Pillars are discrete destructible objects with a shield value and an explicit elemental weakness (Pyro) — that is already the shape of a side-target with a counter-condition, and destroying one permanently removes part of the environmental damage.
- The pillar detonation coupling — the boss's own big attack sets off his furniture, hurting the player but handing over resources — is a clean risk/reward trade that survives turn-based abstraction intact.
- Small Phantom Wolves are add spawns with escalating frequency: a straightforward add-spawn enrage on an HP threshold plus a turn counter.
- The Repositioning beat after hind-leg attacks is a designed recovery window — recognizable as a scripted punish turn where the boss does nothing threatening.
- The double immunity is a strict damage-type restriction, trivially expressed as 'this boss takes no damage from these two damage types.'
- The burst-skip (enough damage in one strike bypasses the Interlude and Phase 2) is essentially an overkill-threshold check on the gate — expressible, though it changes meaning when damage arrives as discrete turn packets.

### Resists translation
- Dash i-frame immunity (Wolf Leap, Spinning Slash, falling icicles) exists only because a dodge button exists. In a turn-based frame there is no i-frame to deny, so the entire category of 'unblockable / undodgeable' collapses into ordinary unmitigated damage and loses its meaning.
- Wolf King Roar's inverted distance scaling (0.83–2.5% ATK, more damage the farther away you are) depends on continuous positional distance. Without a spatial axis there is nothing for the scalar to read from.
- The Pushing Vacuum Field is pure physical displacement — its entire payload is moving the player's body relative to the boss, which has no analogue when there is no position.
- The Interlude Phase is a movement script, not a turn pattern: he laps the arena, stops twice to charge, laps again, then leaps to center. Its function is forced downtime and geometric pressure from the floor, and stripped of movement it degenerates into 'the boss skips a few turns while a DoT ticks.'
- Movement Speed reduction (-12% from the Phase 2 floor, the heavy slow on the Ice Waves path) is a continuous-time debuff with no turn-based referent.
- Arena-edge geometry — hazards ringing the perimeter to squeeze players toward a centered boss — is the core spatial tension of Phase 2 and does not survive without a map.
- Ice Waves as a directional lane, and Wind Blades as a 60-degree forward cone, encode facing and arc. Facing does not exist when the boss is a single card-side entity.
- The open-arena consequences are engine-level, not fight-level: World Level scaling, boss despawn on running away, no direct teleport. A weekly-boss slot in a run-based game has no place to put 'you can walk away and he vanishes.'
- The energy-orb economy (Cryo orbs from Wolf Leap, Spinning Slash, icicle mines, pillar explosions) is tied to Genshin's element-specific burst-charging system, which is a foreign resource model rather than a mechanic that can be ported.
- Elemental Reactions as the pillar-breaking verb assume a two-element aura system underneath; the pillar is only interesting because reactions exist.

## Stormterror Dvalin ("Erstwhile King of the Skies" / 裂空的魔龍, "Demon Dragon of the Cracked Sky") — Trounce Domain: Confront Stormterror, Stormterror's Lair, Mondstadt
*Element: Anemo, with a heavy Physical component. The wiki enemy infobox lists Physical as the primary damage type and Anemo as secondary: his body-contact moves (Fly-By, Lacerating Slash, Rending Vortex) are Physical, while everything wind/bomb-flavored (Dvalin's Cleansing breath, Tempestuous Barrage, Caelestinum Finale Termini DoT, Energy Lightning) is Anemo. He is NOT elementally resistant in a meaningful way — KQM lists a flat 10% RES to Physical and to all seven elements, no immunities. The one elemental oddity is on his body, not his attacks: the corrupted crystal on his back carries a permanent self-applied Electro aura that cannot be overridden by player application.* · *Available since Version 1.0 (global launch, 28 September 2020) — Dvalin is one of the two original weekly bosses, alongside Andrius / Wolf of the North, and the Confront Stormterror Trounce Domain's change history is stamped 1.0. He is therefore a launch-pool boss with no rotation-entry event to model. Unlock is quest-gated rather than purely AR-gated: complete Prologue Act III ('Song of the Dragon and Freedom', through 'Calm Before the Storm' / the 'A Long Shot' pursuit), with Adventure Rank 25 minimum. Six difficulty tiers at AR 25/30/35/40/45/50, recommended levels 40/50/60/70/80/90. Rewards reset weekly at Monday 04:00 server time; Trounce Blossom cost is 30 Original Resin for the first three weekly-boss claims each week and 60 thereafter. Drops Dvalin's Plume, Dvalin's Claw, and Dvalin's Sigh. Notably, this is the ONLY Trounce Domain that cannot be run in Co-Op Mode (attributed to the unique fixed camera), and it is the only one that plays a short cutscene before difficulty selection — both relevant if you are modeling per-boss availability or session structure. Separately, an event-exclusive variant 'Abyssal Stormterror - Dvalin' was added in Luna V for the quest 'Variations on the Song of the Dragon and Freedom'; it reuses the moveset but is all-Physical, gains an additional Deepdark Shield worth 15% Max HP behind the normal 20% ward (halved to 7.5% on subsequent regenerations) that can only be damaged by Elemental DMG, and uses Caelestinum Finale Termini as a marked-ground pulse attack rather than as floor corruption. That variant is NOT part of the weekly rotation.*

### Phases
- **Phase 1 — Opening Sortie (aerial default, first ward)** — trigger: Fight start (after the pre-battle cutscene and difficulty select, which is unique to this Trounce Domain).
  Dvalin opens airborne and immediately establishes the fight's core state machine: he carries a non-elemental ward equal to 20% of his Max HP, and real HP is functionally untouchable until that ward is broken. Rotation is a shuffled pool rather than a fixed script, drawn from: Fly-By (vanishes offscreen, swoops the platform centerline, 100% ATK Physical — a lane-clear on the middle of the arena); Lacerating Slash (head-swipe aimed at your current position, 100% ATK Physical, and critically it leaves his head AND talons exposed AFTER the swing — this is the primary self-generated punish window); Dvalin's Cleansing (perches on the player's side of the platform and channels a wind breath that sweeps slowly toward center, 50% ATK Anemo every 0.5s — a moving sustained-damage wall whose entire channel is a punish window, since talons are exposed for its duration); Tempestuous Barrage (fires two pairs of Pulse Bombs, 50% ATK Anemo, aimed but explicitly NOT homing, blockable by Geo Constructs). Barrage is a designed tell, not a threat: it is ALWAYS followed by either Caelestinum Finale Termini or Rending Vortex. Rending Vortex is the phase's big-swing punish: he dives out of view, reappears at the far end of the arena, charges for an extended, deliberately over-long wind-up, then fires one enormous pulse bomb for 200% ATK Physical to anything on or near the ground — i.e. a full-arena ground-plane execute that must be answered by getting airborne. Phase exit is DUAL-GATED and is not HP-based: it ends when the ward has been broken 1 time total, OR when 2 minutes elapse, whichever comes first. Whichever gate fires, the transition attack is Caelestinum Finale Termini.
- **Phase 2 — First Corruption (partial floor denial)** — trigger: Resolution of the first Caelestinum Finale Termini. The transition move itself is the phase break: Dvalin rains Pulse Bombs that permanently crack the platform the player occupies plus adjacent platforms, and the camera locks onto him for the duration, forcing a no-input window.
  Identical attack pool to Phase 1, but the arena is now partly hostile floor. Cracked platforms apply an escalating Anemo damage-over-time every 2 seconds to anyone standing on them, and the tick scales with continuous exposure — 3n% ATK as Anemo DoT where n begins at 1 and increments by 1 with every instance dealt, capped at n=100. Standing still on a corrupted platform is a compounding punish; gliding over cracked ground still damages you. The design intent is that the player's safe real estate shrinks while the same rotation continues, converting a pure execution fight into a positioning-plus-execution fight. This is the long phase and the main damage phase: the gate is 3 cumulative ward breaks (not 3 more — total count since fight start) OR 8 minutes, and the transition attack is a second Caelestinum Finale Termini.
- **Phase 3 — Second Corruption (compression phase)** — trigger: Resolution of the second Caelestinum Finale Termini; more platforms are now permanently cracked.
  A deliberately short, high-pressure phase. Same rotation, but the safe-platform count is now materially reduced and the timer gate is tightened back down from 8 minutes to 2 minutes. This is the phase whose exit hands the player the fight's enrage: the gate is 5 cumulative ward breaks OR 2 minutes, and the transition is Energy Lightning, not another Caelestinum. The wiki notes Energy Lightning specifically fires 2 minutes after the second Caelestinum Finale Termini was activated, i.e. the timer gate is the expected exit here for most parties — Phase 3 is designed to be survived rather than skipped.
- **Phase 4 — Energy Lightning / Enrage (permanent arena hazard)** — trigger: Energy Lightning fires. Dvalin flies around the arena and applies a PERMANENT condition to every platform — including uncracked ones. This state persists for the remainder of the fight and never expires.
  The full existing rotation continues, now layered over a persistent background hazard: telegraphed ground markers appear periodically anywhere on the arena and energy pulses strike them for 50% ATK Anemo. This is a classic soft enrage — it does not raise his damage per attack, it removes the notion of an idle-safe tile and taxes any greedy DPS window. Punish windows (post-Lacerating-Slash exposure, the Cleansing channel, post-ward-break paralysis) become genuinely risky rather than free. Gate: 6 cumulative ward breaks OR 8 minutes, with a third Caelestinum Finale Termini as the transition.
- **Phase 5 — Terminal / No Safe Ground** — trigger: Resolution of the third Caelestinum Finale Termini. After the third cast, ALL platforms are cracked — there is no clean tile left in the arena.
  Terminal attrition state. Time Limit and Shield Limit are both listed as indefinite: there are no further phase gates, no further transition attacks, and no further escalation. The player now fights the same rotation while standing permanently in escalating Anemo DoT with Energy Lightning still active overhead. The ward/paralysis loop continues unchanged and remains the only route to real HP damage. This is a pure sustain-versus-DPS race, and it is where undergeared parties lose — not to a burst check but to the compounding 3n% floor tick outpacing their healing.

### Signature mechanics
- Ward-gate / paralysis loop (the fight's core engine). Dvalin permanently carries a non-elemental ward worth 20% of his Max HP; his actual HP bar is functionally locked behind it. Break the ward and he crashes onto the platform and is PARALYZED for a long fixed duration — a large, unambiguous burst window on his real HP. When paralysis ends he regenerates a fresh 20% ward and the loop restarts. The whole fight is a repeated 'break the gate, cash the window' cycle, and the phase counter literally counts how many times you have completed it (1 / 3 / 5 / 6 cumulative breaks).
- Dual-gated phase advance with NO HP thresholds. Every phase advances on whichever fires first: a cumulative ward-break count or a wall-clock timer (2min / 8min / 2min / 8min / indefinite). This means a strong party phases via break count and a weak party phases via timeout, and both reach the same terminal state — but the weak party arrives with far more of the arena corrupted. Widely-circulated guides claiming a '25% HP' meteor-shower threshold are inaccurate; the transitions are break-count and timer driven.
- Positional weak-point hierarchy with an explicit damage tax. There is no standard aimed-shot weak point. Instead, different body parts become hittable at different times: talons are exposed DURING Dvalin's Cleansing, head AND talons are exposed AFTER Lacerating Slash, and the corrupted crystal on his back is reachable only during paralysis (climb up over his lowered head/neck). Damage dealt to the talons is reduced by 25% — the game deliberately prices the safe, always-available target below the risky one.
- Electro-locked back crystal. The Abyssal corruption on his back is self-infused with Electro that cannot be overridden by player application. Hitting the burst-window target inherently means hitting a permanently Electro-auraed surface, so reaction choice at the highest-value moment of the fight is partly dictated by the boss, not the player.
- Caelestinum Finale Termini — permanent, escalating floor corruption used as the phase-transition move. It cracks the player's current platform plus adjacent ones, and cracked ground ticks 3n% ATK as Anemo DoT every 2s, with n starting at 1 and incrementing by 1 per instance dealt (cap n=100). The corruption never heals. It is cast exactly three times across the fight, and after the third cast every platform is cracked.
- Camera lock as an enforced no-input window. While Dvalin performs Caelestinum Finale Termini the camera is locked onto him and the player cannot move. The transition is not just a scripted attack, it is a scripted removal of agency.
- Tempestuous Barrage as a pure tell. Two pairs of aimed-but-non-homing Pulse Bombs (50% ATK Anemo) that are individually trivial, but the move is ALWAYS followed by either Caelestinum Finale Termini or Rending Vortex. It is the fight's only reliable read on an incoming heavy.
- Rending Vortex — offscreen long-charge ground-plane execute. He dives out of view, resurfaces at the opposite end of the arena, charges for an extended period, then launches one giant pulse bomb for 200% ATK Physical to characters on or near the ground. The counter is verticality, not dodging sideways.
- Energy Lightning — irreversible mid-fight enrage. Once triggered it applies a permanent condition to every platform and periodically strikes marked ground for 50% ATK Anemo for the rest of the fight. There is no dispel, no timer, no phase that removes it.
- Self-punishing paralysis. If the player falls out of bounds while Dvalin is paralyzed, the paralyzed state ends EARLY. The fight's biggest reward window can be destroyed by the player's own movement error.
- No add spawns whatsoever. This is a strict 1v1 across all five phases — unusual among Genshin weekly bosses and worth noting for pool-modeling.

### Arena gimmicks
- Broken-spire platform ring. The arena is a set of separated floating stone platforms atop the ruined Stormterror's Lair spire, not a continuous floor. The gaps between platforms are real out-of-bounds — falling is a live failure state, and it additionally cancels any active paralysis window.
- Anemograna updrafts as the traversal resource. Anemograna sit at the edges of each platform; collecting them spawns an upward wind current. That updraft is the only way to cross to another platform and is also the intended answer to Rending Vortex's ground-plane execute and to Fly-By's centerline sweep. Movement resource and defensive resource are the same resource.
- Permanent floor corruption, never cleansed. Caelestinum Finale Termini cracks platforms irreversibly. Cracked ground applies the escalating 3n%-per-2s Anemo DoT, and the escalation is per-instance rather than per-second, so the punishment for lingering compounds rather than being flat. Gliding over cracked platforms still deals damage — you cannot fully evade a corrupted tile by being airborne over it.
- Progressive arena denial as the difficulty curve. Cast 1 corrupts your platform plus adjacent ones, cast 2 corrupts more, cast 3 corrupts everything. The fight's late game has no safe tile at all — the arena itself becomes the enrage rather than a stat boost on the boss.
- Energy Lightning telegraph markers. Once active, marked strike zones appear on the ground across all platforms for the rest of the fight, layering a dodge-the-circles minigame under whatever rotation Dvalin is running.
- Fixed / partly non-tracking camera. The whole domain uses a unique fixed camera angle. Critically, the camera does not follow Dvalin while he is charging Rending Vortex, so the player must track an offscreen threat by memory. This camera is the stated reason Confront Stormterror is the only Trounce Domain that cannot be played in Co-Op Mode.
- Forced camera lock during transitions (see signature mechanics) — a scripted, unavoidable inaction window at each phase boundary.
- Geo Constructs physically block Tempestuous Barrage's Pulse Bombs — an explicit environment-object counter to a specific attack.
- Boss-as-terrain during paralysis. The paralyzed Dvalin becomes climbable geometry: the player runs up his lowered head/neck onto his back to reach the crystal. The reward window requires traversing the boss model itself.

### Translates naturally (observations, not proposals)
- The ward/paralysis loop is already a two-state turn structure: a defensive state with a fixed-size gate (20% Max HP, non-elemental, immune-until-broken) alternating with a fixed-duration Vulnerable state. This is the single most turn-legible thing in the fight — a recurring block/armor gate that regenerates, plus a stunned window, is standard turn-based grammar.
- Phase advance is already counter-driven rather than HP-driven. Advancing on 'cumulative number of times the gate has been broken' (1 / 3 / 5 / 6) is a discrete counter, not a continuous HP percentage — it survives translation to turn-based intact, unlike an HP-percentage gate that a burst turn can skip past.
- Tempestuous Barrage functions as a telegraphed multi-turn intent chain: a low-damage move that is always followed by one of exactly two heavies. That is literally an intent-preview mechanic — the low attack is an announcement, and the branching (Caelestinum OR Rending Vortex) makes it a read rather than a guarantee.
- Caelestinum Finale Termini's DoT already escalates in discrete instances rather than continuously. n increments by 1 per instance dealt, which is a stacking-debuff counter — a per-turn ramping damage-over-time that grows the longer the condition holds. The cast count (exactly three, permanent, never cleansed) is also discrete and finite.
- Energy Lightning is a permanent, one-way global effect added at a phase boundary and never removed. Irreversible mid-fight state additions with no dispel are common in turn-based encounter design.
- The 25% damage reduction on talons is a target-selection tax expressed in flat percentage terms — a discounted-damage target versus a full-damage target, already numeric and already independent of position in principle.
- The permanently Electro-auraed back crystal is a persistent status on the boss that constrains which interactions are available at the highest-value moment. Persistent boss-side status flags translate directly.
- Rending Vortex is a long-wind-up, single-instance, arena-wide heavy with a stated conditional (200% ATK to characters on or near the ground). A big telegraphed hit with a binary avoidance condition is turn-legible; the condition itself is what fights back (see resistant).
- The fight has zero add spawns across all five phases, so nothing needs to be invented to handle summon waves — the encounter is already a clean single-entity fight.
- The terminal phase has no further gates, no further escalation, and no further transitions. A final state that is explicitly 'indefinite / indefinite' is already an endgame attrition race rather than a scripted sequence.

### Resists translation
- Nearly every hazard in this fight is spatial rather than temporal. Which platform you occupy, whether it is cracked, whether you are airborne, and how far you are from the centerline are all continuous positional facts. A turn-based system with no board has nowhere to put 'the platform you are standing on is now corrupted and the adjacent ones are too.'
- The dual gate mixes a discrete counter with wall-clock seconds (2 min / 8 min / 2 min / 8 min). Real-time limits are a DPS-rate check; there is no clean turn equivalent, because turn count and elapsed time are not the same axis. The design intent — 'a slow party arrives at the terminal phase with more of the arena destroyed' — depends on real-time throughput.
- Damage cadences are sub-turn: Dvalin's Cleansing ticks every 0.5s, the cracked-floor DoT every 2s, and n can climb to 100. That granularity collapses entirely once the atomic unit becomes a turn — you lose the distinction between 'stepped off quickly' and 'lingered', which is the entire mechanic.
- Verticality is the answer to multiple attacks. Rending Vortex only hits characters on or near the ground; Anemograna updrafts are how you avoid Fly-By, cross platforms, and dodge bombs. A turn-based system has no altitude axis, so 'get airborne' has no native representation — it would have to be re-expressed as an abstract evade resource, which loses the fact that traversal and defense compete for the same Anemograna.
- The weak-point hierarchy is hitbox geometry in motion. Talons exposed during a channel, head-and-talons exposed after a specific swing, back crystal reachable only by physically climbing the paralyzed boss model — these are all 'where is your character standing relative to which part of the model right now.' The 25% talon tax translates; the reason it exists does not.
- The camera is load-bearing. The fixed angle, the camera lock during Caelestinum Finale Termini, and the fact that the camera does NOT follow Dvalin while he charges Rending Vortex are all difficulty. A turn-based interface shows you the intent; hiding the boss is not a thing it can do without simply being unfair.
- Falling out of bounds ending paralysis early is a movement-error consequence. It punishes a mistake that turn-based play cannot make, so there is nothing to attach the penalty to.
- Aimed-but-not-homing projectiles (Tempestuous Barrage) exist specifically to reward manual dodging. Once removed, the attack is just 4 small hits and its identity is gone — only its role as a tell survives.
- Geo Constructs blocking Pulse Bombs is a physics collision between a player-placed object and a projectile. It is a positional interception, not a damage-reduction effect.
- 'Gliding over a cracked platform still damages you' is a rule that only makes sense when there is a difference between being over a tile and on it.
- The fight has no meaningful elemental puzzle to preserve — a flat 10% RES to everything means the boss does not gate on element choice at all, so any elemental-identity structure in translation would be invention, not conversion.

## Childe / Tartaglia — "Enter the Golden House" (Trounce Domain, Liyue; Eleventh of the Fatui Harbingers)
*Element: Hydro → Electro → Hydro+Electro (element identity is per-phase, not fixed: P1 Hydro, P2 Electro, P3 both interleaved). Player-facing status is delivered almost exclusively through the Riptide mark rather than through the attacks themselves.* · *Entered the weekly Trounce Domain rotation in Version 1.1 (released 2020-11-11), alongside Childe's own banner debut — the third Trounce Domain in the game, after Confront Stormterror (Dvalin) and Wolf of the North Challenge (Andrius) from 1.0. Unlocked by completing the Archon Quest "A New Star Approaches"; from AR 40 it is also reachable via Adventurer Handbook Quick Challenge in single-player even if unopened. Standard Trounce economics: rewards claimable once per domain per week, resetting Monday 04:00 server time, at 30 Original Resin for the week's first three Trounce claims and 60 thereafter. Drops Tusk of Monoceros Caeli / Shard of a Foul Legacy / Shadow of the Warrior (Tartaglia and Xiao talent materials), with Varunada Lazurite, Vajrada Amethyst and Shivada Jade in the gem pool. Rebalanced once, in Version 3.1: ATK increased 100% while DMG multipliers were decreased 50% (roughly damage-neutral, but it changed how his damage scales relative to defense). Difficulty tiers at AR 35/40/45/50 (recommended levels 60/70/80/90) — for pool modeling, this is an early, permanently-available boss with no rotation-out, so it has been continuously in the pool for the game's entire post-1.0 history.*

### Phases
- **Phase 1 — Hydro Vision (bow / ranged zoner)** — trigger: Fight start. Childe stands inert at arena center and is non-hostile until the player approaches/engages — a free pre-combat setup window (heal, buff, even swap gear).
  Ranged kiting shape. Opener/rotation is bow-based chip: Rapid Fire (3-6 arrows, 35% ATK each; a 'continuous' variant at steady cadence and a slower 'full combo' variant fixed at 6), Shark Waves (2-6 shark-shaped wave projectiles, 50% ATK, applies Riptide), and Slide (reposition anywhere in the arena, or a closing slash for 62.5% ATK that consumes Riptide when the player is far). Conditional escalation: only while the player carries a Riptide mark does he open the big-hit tier — Airstrike (multi-shot arcing volley, 75% ATK, consumes Riptide) and Ultima: Celestial Voyager, a long telegraph that summons an All-Devouring Narwhal shape hitting twice (75% ATK on appearance, 100% ATK on landing) over roughly 3/5 of the arena each, undodgeable by dash i-frames. Defensive layer: on being struck by melee he raises a Hydro Shield that blocks all incoming damage, with a 13% chance per hit to drop it into a spinning-slash counter (50% ATK); if the melee hit that lands on the shield is itself Hydro, he fires Hydro Counter, a 150% ATK frontal blast (ranged attacks and deployables cannot provoke it). Punish window: accumulated poise damage (which stacks even through the shield) forces a Staggered state — heavily reduced RES and interruption resistance — that persists until he spends it on Blast, a non-damaging knockback that resets him to neutral. Wiki-listed resistances: 50% Hydro RES normally; while Staggered, global RES -30% and Hydro RES down to 20%. Also flagged as having invincibility frames. Relative stat ratios listed as HP 6 / ATK 10.
- **Phase 2 — Electro Delusion (melee rushdown)** — trigger: Phase 1 HP bar depleted ('critical health'). He slides to arena center, seals himself in a water dome and is invulnerable through the transition, then emerges Delusion-active; the transition itself pushes a large non-damaging Hydro knockback out from center.
  Complete profile inversion: bow discarded, everything is fast gap-closing melee, and he actively chases instead of zoning. Core rotation is Raging Tide, a variable-length combo of 2, 3, or 5 strikes (25% / 27.5% / 30% / 35% / 40% ATK in sequence) where the 4th strike has long reach and snaps to the player's position — the string is outrunnable up front but the 4th hit demands a real dodge. Supporting moves: Flicker (dash-in cross slash, 62.5% ATK, consumes Riptide but does not require it), Chained Dashes (2-5 rapidly chained dash attacks, 37.5% ATK each), and Turning Sweep, a 180-degree punish that fires specifically when the player is behind him. Riptide-gated finisher: Ultima: Dark Leap — paints a tracking lightning circle on the player, charges, then leaps and strikes instantly for 100% ATK; dash i-frames do not beat it, and it is a near-guaranteed kill on the on-field character. The same Hydro Shield / counter architecture recurs as Electro Shield plus Electro Counter (150% ATK frontal blast, provoked only by Electro melee hits on the shield), with the same 13% shield-drop spinning slash (50% ATK). Staggered → Blast punish loop is unchanged. Wiki-listed resistances: 50% Electro RES normally; while Staggered, global RES -50% and Electro RES to 0% — the largest damage window in the fight. Relative stat ratios listed as HP 6 / ATK 10.
- **Phase 3 — Foul Legacy Transformation (hybrid, slow and heavy)** — trigger: Phase 2 HP bar depleted. Cutscene transformation; the arena floor collapses and the fight relocates to a second, lower arena. As in Phase 1, the player is placed at the edge and Childe stands non-hostile at center until engaged — a second free setup window.
  Range-conditional element split: Electro tools against players in melee range, Hydro tools against players at distance. Slower attack cadence than Phases 1-2 with correspondingly larger payloads, and it recycles earlier-phase moves alongside new ones. Hydro side: Charged Shots (up to 4 bow shots, 40% ATK each), Abyssal Mayhem: Hydrospout (line of hydrospouts, 35% ATK each), Crescent Tides (two tracking crescent slashes, 37.5% ATK each), and Piercing Vortex — a hurled Hydro glaive, 50% ATK, up to 3 consecutive casts, which requires Riptide to trigger but does not consume it. Electro side: Twin Strikes (2 x 25% ATK), Turning Sweep (50% ATK, 180-degree arc), a 3-Part Combo (25% then 35% ATK, ending in a thrown spinning polearm ticking 15% ATK every 0.2s on contact), Blade Rain (summoned lightning cloud firing 4 Electro blades, 50% ATK each — notable as the one attack that neither applies nor consumes Riptide), an enhanced Chained Dashes at up to 8 charged dashes (37.5% ATK), and Bladewheel, a Riptide-gated wheel that circles the arena and returns without consuming the mark (40% ATK). Three Riptide-gated Ultimas rotate as the enrage tier, all immune to dash i-frames: Ultima: God Arrow (4 arrows fall into 4 blue-telegraphed strike zones, 87.5% ATK each, covering ~3/5 of the arena, with overlap regions dealing two instances — the arena center and the four cardinal edges are the safe pockets), Ultima: Celestial Voyager (the Narwhal, now aimed in front of him: 75% on appearance / 100% on landing), and Ultima: Dark Leap (Enhanced) with a much wider landing AoE. Notably, the shield/counter and Staggered states are gone in this phase — no shield to break, no RES-collapse punish window; instead he is statically resistant at 70% Hydro RES and 70% Electro RES, and gains Teleport as a listed ability. Relative stat ratios listed as HP 10 / ATK 14 — the largest HP pool of the three.

### Signature mechanics
- Riptide (the fight's spine). Most of Childe's attacks that connect leave a persistent Riptide mark on the character. The mark is the ONLY channel through which he applies elemental status — his attacks otherwise apply no aura and cannot trigger elemental reactions unless the hit also applies Riptide. It persists through character swaps.
- Riptide as an unlock gate on his own moveset. Several attacks — Airstrike, Piercing Vortex, Bladewheel, and every Ultima — cannot be used at all unless the player is currently marked. Unmarked, Childe is limited to comparatively weak, easily evaded basics; marked, he opens his lethal tier. The player's damage intake is therefore self-inflicted state, not a timer.
- Riptide consumption vs. retention. Some Riptide-gated attacks consume the mark on hit for an extra 100% ATK burst of the mark's element and cannot re-apply it; others (Piercing Vortex, Bladewheel) require the mark but leave it standing, so they can be chained.
- Riptide Override (Phase 3 only). An attack applying the opposite element to an existing mark consumes and replaces it rather than reacting — override deals only 50% ATK and explicitly does not trigger Electro-Charged, so the two-element phase is a swap, not a reaction engine.
- Reactive Shield + element-matched Counter (Phases 1-2 only). Melee contact makes Childe raise an element-matched shield that blocks all incoming damage; hitting a raised shield has a 13% chance to make him drop it into a spinning-slash counter, and hitting it with matching-element melee (Hydro on the Hydro shield, Electro on the Electro shield) provokes a 150% ATK frontal energy blast. Ranged attacks and deployables do not provoke the counter — the counter is keyed to how you hit him, not merely that you hit him.
- Poise/Stagger punish window (Phases 1-2 only). Poise damage accumulates even while the shield is up; enough of it drops him into a Staggered state with sharply reduced RES and interruption resistance, which only ends when he spends it on Blast — a knockback that deals no damage and resets to neutral. This is the fight's designed burst window, and it is under player control rather than scheduled.
- Element-inverted vulnerability per phase. Each phase resists its own element (50% in P1/P2, 70% both in P3), so the punishing-element and the profitable-element are always opposed; Staggered collapses that resistance in P1/P2 (Hydro 50%→20%, Electro 50%→0%) but Phase 3 has no such window.
- Three sequential HP bars, not one. Each phase is a discrete health pool with its own stat ratios (6/6/10 HP, 10/10/14 ATK), separated by invulnerable scripted transitions.
- Undodgeable-by-i-frames Ultimas. Every Ultima explicitly cannot be avoided with dash i-frames — they must be answered by positioning, by cleansing the Riptide that enabled them, or by burst i-frames.
- Full moveset replacement across phases, not moveset addition. P1 is a bow zoner, P2 discards the bow entirely for melee rushdown, P3 re-merges both at reduced speed and increased weight.

### Arena gimmicks
- Pyro-charged arena barrier as a cleanse station. The ring bounding the arena applies a Pyro effect; touching it reacts with the Riptide mark — Vaporize against Hydro Riptide, Overloaded against Electro Riptide — removing the mark. The reaction deals no damage to the character and (for the Electro case, and in Phase 3 generally) causes no knockback. It is a positional, always-available cleanse with a travel-time cost.
- The barrier is a double-edged zone in Phase 2. Because the wall applies Pyro, hugging it while Childe throws rapid Electro attacks means repeated Overloaded knockback on the player; the wiki flags standing near the barrier in Phase 2 as extremely dangerous. The same hazard applies to standing inside Bennett's Fantastic Voyage Pyro field.
- Cleansing mid-cast can misdirect a telegraph. If the Riptide mark is stripped while Ultima: Dark Leap is charging, the tracking circle detaches and freezes in place — Childe still strikes the circle's last position, so the cleanse converts a homing kill into a fixed, walkable-out-of AoE.
- Arena collapse between Phase 2 and Phase 3. The floor gives way and the fight moves to a second, lower arena; the player is repositioned to that arena's edge.
- Safe-pocket geometry on Ultima: God Arrow (P3). Four telegraphed circular strike zones cover roughly 3/5 of the floor; the arena's center and its four cardinal edges are untouched, and overlap regions deal two damage instances — the arena's shape is the answer to the attack.
- Vertical space as an out. Attacks including Ultima: Dark Leap lack vertical AoE, so elevation (Geo constructs, plunge/flight states) sidesteps them — the arena's third axis is live.
- Non-hostile idle at arena center opening Phases 1 and 3, allowing unpressured setup before engagement.
- No enrage timer and no fail zones. There is no instant-wipe attack for taking too long — the arena imposes no clock, and a party wipe simply resets the whole domain run from Phase 1.
- No add spawns anywhere in the fight. Childe is solo across all three phases; the only summoned entities are his own attack constructs (Blade Rain's lightning cloud, the thrown polearm, the Bladewheel), which are attacks rather than enemies.

### Translates naturally (observations, not proposals)
- Riptide is already a discrete, persistent, binary mark on the target that survives character swaps — it behaves like a status token sitting on the player rather than like a continuous aura.
- Riptide gates which attacks Childe is allowed to use at all, which is an intent-selection rule stated in exactly the form a turn-based intent table uses: condition on player state, then choose from a restricted or unrestricted move pool.
- Riptide consumption for a fixed extra 100% ATK, versus the retain-and-reuse moves (Piercing Vortex, Bladewheel), is already a token-spend vs. token-keep distinction.
- The barrier cleanse is a discrete cost-for-cleanse action: give up positioning (a turn's worth of movement) to remove the mark, with no damage taken. It has no timing component at all.
- The Stagger state is a player-driven, threshold-triggered vulnerability window with an explicit numeric RES delta and an explicit exit condition (he must spend Blast to leave it). Accumulate-to-threshold, then a bounded window, is native turn-game vocabulary — and the fact that poise accrues through the shield means the threshold is tracked separately from the damage-blocking state.
- The shield is a full damage-block state with a stated probabilistic break-into-counter (13%), which maps directly onto a block/counter state with a chance-based exit.
- The element-matched Counter is a conditional punish on how the player attacks (matching-element melee provokes it; ranged and deployables do not) — a keyword-conditional retaliation trigger.
- Three sequential HP pools with scripted invulnerable transitions are already phase gates; the per-phase resistance table (50% own-element, 70%/70% in P3) is a flat damage-modifier per phase.
- Riptide Override in Phase 3 — opposite element consumes and replaces the mark for a reduced 50% ATK and explicitly refuses to react — is already a deterministic token-replacement rule rather than a physics interaction.
- The move-count variability (Rapid Fire 3-6 arrows, Raging Tide 2/3/5 strikes, Chained Dashes 2-5 or up to 8) reads as multi-hit intents with a variable hit count, a shape turn-based games already display.
- Absence of an enrage timer and absence of adds means the fight's whole pressure curve comes from state, not from escalating spawn or clock pressure.

### Resists translation
- The entire Ultima tier is defined by spatial telegraphs — Celestial Voyager's two differently-placed ~3/5-arena zones, God Arrow's four circles with a safe center and safe cardinal edges plus double-damage overlaps, Dark Leap's tracking circle. Without a positional grid there is no 'where you stand' for these to punish, and their defining property (partial arena denial with geometric safe pockets) has no turn-based equivalent.
- 'Cannot be dodged with dash i-frames, but CAN be dodged with burst i-frames' is a distinction that only exists in an engine with per-animation invulnerability frames; a turn structure has no sub-turn timing layer to distinguish which invulnerability source applies.
- Range-conditional element selection in Phase 3 (Electro up close, Hydro at distance) depends on a continuously varying distance value that turn-based combat does not naturally expose.
- Phase 2's identity is speed — the 4th strike of Raging Tide snapping to the player's location, chained dashes that must be walked around rather than dashed through, 'outrun the first three, dash the fourth.' The difficulty is reaction-time and stamina management, both of which vanish when time is quantized into turns.
- The Slide/Flicker gap-close and the Turning Sweep's behind-him activation condition are facing- and position-dependent, with no turn-based analogue for player facing.
- Stamina as a resource is central to the intended play (conserve dashes in Phase 1 to have them for Ultimas) and has no natural turn-based counterpart distinct from ordinary resource costs.
- The barrier cleanse costs travel time — its real price is the seconds spent crossing the arena while under fire, which is exactly the part that disappears when movement is abstracted.
- Poise/interruption resistance is a continuous stagger meter fed by hit-by-hit poise values, distinct from HP damage; turn games typically have no second continuous meter fed at that granularity.
- Vertical evasion (Geo constructs and plunge/flight states beating attacks that lack vertical AoE) is a third-axis exploit with no turn-based representation.
- The shield being raised reactively in response to melee contact, mid-animation, makes it an interrupt rather than a scheduled defensive intent — a turn structure must decide the shield in advance, which changes who holds the information.
- Non-damaging mechanics that are purely kinetic — Blast's knockback, Overloaded knockback off the Pyro barrier, the Phase 2 transition shockwave — do nothing except move bodies through space, and have no effect at all once space is abstracted away.
- The mid-cast telegraph misdirect (cleansing Riptide during Dark Leap's charge freezes the targeting circle in place) requires the attack to have a resolvable spatial target that can be desynced from the player during a cast — it needs both a cast window and a location.

## Azhdaha, Sealed Lord of Vishaps — weekly boss of the Trounce Domain "Beneath the Dragon-Queller" (Nantianmen, Minlin, Liyue)
*Element: Geo is the base and permanent identity (70% Geo RES, 40% Physical RES, 10% RES to everything else). Over the fight he layers on two additional elements, in a fixed order: one of Hydro/Pyro at the first HP gate, then one of Electro/Cryo at the second. The pair is predetermined per week on a 4-variant rotation (Hydro+Electro, Pyro+Cryo, Hydro+Cryo, Pyro+Electro) and is displayed on the domain entrance seals before you ever enter. He never infuses Anemo or Dendro.* · *Entered the game in Version 1.5 "Beneath the Light of Jadeite," released April 28, 2021 — the fifth weekly boss and the first Geo one. He is unlocked by completing Zhongli's Story Quest Act II ("No Mere Stone") and is fought in the permanent Trounce Domain "Beneath the Dragon-Queller" under the Dragon-Queller tree in Nantianmen, Minlin, Liyue. Difficulty tiers unlock at Adventure Rank 35/40/45/50 (recommended levels 60/70/80/90). He has been in continuous weekly rotation since 1.5 with no removal or rework; the only thing that rotates is which of the four element pairs he uses that week (fixed 4-week cycle, resetting on the standard weekly reset). Boss-unique drops: Dragon Lord's Crown, Bloodjade Branch, Gilded Scale. Gem drops depend on the week's infused elements, always including Prithiva Topaz (Geo).*

### Phases
- **Phase 1 — Unsealed Geo Form (100% to 75% HP)** — trigger: Fight start; Azhdaha shatters the containment forcefield in the sealed arena. Ends when he is driven to 75% HP. A damage floor exists: he cannot be pushed below 65% HP before the transition resolves, so overkill in this window is clamped.
  Pure Geo, single-target, no adds. The rotation is a melee-brawler pattern gated by player positioning rather than a fixed sequence: standing at his flank baits Body Slam (66.6% ATK, wide, explicitly undodgeable by i-frames), standing behind baits Tail Slam (66.6% ATK, long wind-up, the safest intent to eat), standing at range baits Primeval Spring (a line of stones that walks toward the target from the side opposite his facing; 16.6% impact + 33.3% explosion). Core close-range rotation: Earthen Skull (head slam, 33.3% impact then a line of 66.6% ATK ground explosions), Seismo-Waves (foreleg slam into a 3-shockwave ripple at 33.3 / 16.6 / 16.6% ATK), Sweeping Branch (fast sweep, 66.6% ATK, undodgeable by i-frames), Sundering Charge (gore-and-toss rush, 33.3% then 66.6% ATK, undodgeable by i-frames), Decimating Rockfall (leap then two expanding ground detonations, 50% ATK x2). The one field-wide intent in this phase is Burrow: he leaves the playfield entirely, digs around the arena while ceiling rockfall (33.3% ATK) rains on telegraphed markers, and hits anyone in his path for 66.6% ATK each time he resurfaces, ending with an 8.3% Physical shockwave on emergence. Punish window: the long recovery on Tail Slam and Decimating Rockfall. No enrage timer of any kind.
- **Phase 2 — First Infusion, Hydro or Pyro (75% to 45% HP)** — trigger: HP gate at 75%. He performs Tremors, then enters a brief scripted invulnerability while absorbing the first element (his forepaws recolor). Damage during the absorption animation is wasted. Damage floor for the phase is 35% HP.
  Every Phase 1 intent is retained but re-skinned: base multipliers are cut (66.6% tends to drop to 46.6%, 33.3% to 23.3%) and a rider payload is bolted on, so raw burst goes down while chip and zoning go up. Pyro variant widens footprints and adds airborne fireballs that scatter on the ground for 16.6% ATK and leave burning marks ticking 11.6% ATK as DoT. Hydro variant converts to projectiles and delayed detonations: Earthen Skull loses its explosion line and instead plants a delayed pool that erupts for 66.6% ATK and launches the target upward; Seismo-Waves, Tremors and Decimating Rockfall spray radial water streams for 16.6% ATK each. Tremors becomes a recurring intent, not just a transition beat: five successive shockwaves at 50% ATK plus a final 66.6% ATK wave, undodgeable by i-frames, with hard knockback. New signature intent: The King's Rebuke, a roar-telegraphed arena-wide AoE — Torrential Rebuke (Hydro) spawns large orbs that orbit the arena as moving DoT zones at 50% ATK, with the arena center safe; Blazing Rebuke (Pyro) drops meteors for 100% ATK each, each leaving a burning-mark DoT. Defensively he gains +60% RES to the infused element, stacking to 70% total in that damage type. Every unshielded hit of the infused element applies the persistent mark DoT (see signature mechanics).
- **Phase 3 — Dual Infusion, Electro or Cryo (45% HP to death)** — trigger: HP gate at 45%. Same script: Tremors, brief invulnerability, second element absorbed (his tail recolors). From this point on 100% of his damage output is elemental — nothing he does is Geo or Physical anymore, so every connecting hit is mark-eligible.
  Both infusions run at once and are assigned per attack, not globally: the head/ground-shock family (Earthen Skull, Seismo-Waves, Decimating Rockfall, Tremors) stays on the first element, while the body/tail family (Body Slam, Tail Slam, Sweeping Branch, Sundering Charge, Primeval Spring) converts to the second — matching the paws-first-element / tail-second-element visual tell. Electro riders add lingering erratic orbs off Sweeping Branch (11.6% ATK on contact), an Electro burst around Tail Slam impact (23.3% ATK), and expanding thunderstrokes off Primeval Spring (33.3% ATK). Cryo riders add three delayed-detonation orbs off Sweeping Branch, ice spikes around Tail Slam impact (23.3% ATK each), and a lingering ice-shard field off Primeval Spring ticking 11.6% ATK as DoT. Two new ranged intents appear and are the phase's real threat: Thunder Sphere (a volley of homing Electro orbs, 46.6% ATK each) and Fractal Lance (a floating Cryo sphere that fires 4 homing crystal lances, five times over, 46.6% ATK per lance) — both can be delayed by baiting a Tail Slam. The phase's set piece is Moonlit Wave: he submerges and leaves only his tail on the surface. In this state he can still be damaged but cannot be killed; if his HP is driven to zero while submerged, one extra hit is required after he resurfaces. Thunderstorm Wave (Electro) has the tail zap rotating cone AoEs for 100% ATK; Frostspike Wave (Cryo) drops a hailstorm whose impacts spawn circular ice spikes pulsing three times for 33.3% ATK per pulse. Defensively he gains +50% RES to the second element, 60% total in that type. No berserk/enrage state and no soft-enrage timer — Phase 3 is the terminal difficulty tier.

### Signature mechanics
- Two-stage elemental infusion at hard HP gates (75% and 45%), with the element pair fixed for the week and readable before the fight from the two seals at the domain entrance. Four rotation variants cycle weekly: Hydro+Electro, Pyro+Cryo, Hydro+Cryo, Pyro+Electro.
- Compounding resistance wall as a loadout check: 70% Geo and 40% Physical from the start, 10% to all other elements, then +60% RES to the first infused element (70% total) and +50% to the second (60% total). Anemo and Dendro are never infused and are never resisted beyond the 10% baseline, so the correct answer to the wall is known before the fight begins.
- Elemental mark (the domain's Ley Line Disorder): any Hydro/Pyro/Electro/Cryo hit that lands on an unshielded character applies a persistent DoT ticking 13.3% ATK, which lasts as long as that elemental aura is on the character. A shield prevents application entirely — it is a binary gate, not a mitigation curve. Cleansing is possible by self-applying a reacting element (Anemo clears all marks; Pyro clears Hydro/Cryo/Electro; Hydro clears Pyro/Cryo; Electro clears Cryo).
- Attack-conversion table rather than a new moveset: infusion keeps every base intent's skeleton and animation, lowers its direct multiplier, and appends an element-specific rider — burning marks and scattering fireballs for Pyro, delayed pools and launching for Hydro, lingering erratic orbs and expanding strikes for Electro, delayed orbs and persistent shard fields for Cryo.
- Positional intent selection: which attack he chooses is driven by where the target stands. Flank triggers Body Slam, rear triggers Tail Slam, distance triggers Primeval Spring, and Tail Slam can be deliberately baited in Phase 3 to push back Thunder Sphere and Fractal Lance.
- A declared two-tier counterplay taxonomy: some intents are cleanly avoidable with dash invincibility frames (Earthen Skull, Seismo-Waves, Decimating Rockfall) and some are explicitly stated to be unavoidable that way (Body Slam, Sweeping Branch, Sundering Charge, Tremors) — those must be walked out of or eaten behind a shield.
- Phase-transition invulnerability plus damage floors: he cannot be driven below 65% HP before Phase 2 resolves, nor below 35% before Phase 3 resolves, and he carries unseen invulnerability frames during the absorption animation.
- A kill-denial state, not just a damage-immunity one: during Moonlit Wave he takes damage normally but cannot die, and an HP-zero result banks one extra required hit for after he resurfaces.
- Boss-leaves-the-field mechanic: Burrow removes him from the playfield while an arena-wide rockfall hazard runs, with resurface hits along his path and a physical shockwave on emergence. In Phases 2 and 3 he can burrow specifically to reposition to the arena center, skipping the rockfall segment.
- No add spawns at any point. This is a pure single-target duel for its entire length, with all pressure coming from field hazards and DoT rather than from summons.

### Arena gimmicks
- A single enclosed circular cavern beneath the Dragon-Queller tree, ringed by eroded Stone Steles (the same Geo steles Zhongli creates) held up by chains; Azhdaha begins the encounter by shattering a containment forcefield. The steles are set dressing — they are not interactable, breakable, or usable as counters.
- The domain entrance carries two elemental sigils in Liyue script naming the week's infusion pair. This is a pre-combat information gimmick: the fight is a scouted matchup, not a blind one.
- The Ley Line Disorder is a property of the arena rather than of the boss: unshielded characters hit by any of his four possible elements are marked and bleed continuous elemental damage. Elemental Burst invincibility does not stop the disorder from applying on hit — only a shield does.
- Ceiling rockfall during Burrow: telegraphed ground markers precede large falling rocks across the whole arena while the boss is underground.
- Persistent ground hazards seeded by infused attacks — Pyro burning marks, Cryo ice-shard fields, Cryo delayed-detonation orbs, Electro orbs drifting erratically — progressively convert usable floor into denial zones as the fight goes on.
- Positional safe zones tied to specific set-piece attacks: the arena center is safe from Torrential Rebuke's orbiting Hydro orbs, while the arena edge is targeted less often by Frostspike Wave. Blazing Rebuke's meteors and Thunderstorm Wave's rotating cones instead force constant relocation.
- No platform destruction, no shrinking arena, no insta-fail or corruption zones, and no shield phase that demands a specific element or break bar. The only 'shield requiring a specific counter' in the fight is the resistance wall, answered by bringing off-element damage rather than by an in-fight action.

### Translates naturally (observations, not proposals)
- The phase structure is already expressed as HP-percentage gates (75%, 45%) with a scripted invulnerable beat at each — that is the same shape as a turn-based phase transition triggered by a damage threshold.
- The damage floors (cannot be driven below 65% before Phase 2, below 35% before Phase 3) are an explicit overkill clamp, which is a rule that already reads as a discrete-state constraint rather than a real-time one.
- The elemental mark is a debuff applied on an unblocked hit that ticks a fixed percentage of the boss's ATK until removed — an applied-on-damage stacking DoT, with a defined removal condition.
- Shielding is a binary immunity to mark application rather than a percentage reduction: block the hit and the debuff never applies at all. That threshold behavior is unusually clean for discrete turns.
- The resistance model is per-damage-type multiplicative reduction, publicly known before the fight, growing at each phase transition. It is a knowable damage-type matchup that rewards loadout planning, not reaction.
- The weekly 4-variant element rotation is deterministic variant seeding on a fixed cycle — a boss slot that presents one of four known configurations per week.
- Infusion works by keeping the same intent skeleton and swapping element plus rider payload, which is intent re-skinning: the telegraph and the base shape persist across phases while the attached effect changes.
- Several attacks are natively multi-hit with fixed counts: Seismo-Waves (3 shockwaves at declared descending multipliers), Tremors (5 shockwaves plus 1 large), Decimating Rockfall (2 detonations), Fractal Lance (4 lances x 5 volleys), Moonlit Wave's Frostspike pulses (3). These map to multi-hit intent values directly.
- The King's Rebuke is roar-telegraphed before it resolves — an explicitly announced big AoE that the player has time to prepare for, which is structurally a charged intent.
- Burrow is a boss-off-the-field beat during which a field hazard ticks and the boss returns with a positional payoff — a leave-and-return state.
- Moonlit Wave's kill-denial (damageable but not killable, with one hit banked for after resurfacing) is a discrete rule already, not a timing one.
- The absence of adds means the whole encounter is single-target, so nothing about the fight depends on managing a board of enemies.

### Resists translation
- Positional intent selection is the core of the fight's decision layer and has no analogue without a player position. 'Attack from the front or the back, never the side' — and the Phase 3 tactic of baiting Tail Slam to delay Thunder Sphere and Fractal Lance — both cease to mean anything when there is no facing or standing location.
- The dash-i-frame taxonomy (which attacks can be dodged through and which explicitly cannot) is a frame-timing distinction. In discrete turns there is no difference between an attack you sidestep and one you must out-walk.
- Nearly all of the arena's pressure is spatial: telegraphed ground markers, a safe center versus a safe edge, orbiting Hydro orbs, rotating Electro cones, meteor drop zones, homing projectiles you outrun by running the arena perimeter. These are movement puzzles whose difficulty lives entirely in geometry.
- Displacement effects — the upward launch on the Hydro version of Earthen Skull, the knockback on Tremors — punish by taking control away in real time; there is no equivalent cost when the player has no position to lose.
- Mark cleansing depends on Genshin's aura system: the mark lasts 'as long as the character is affected by that element,' and is removed by self-applying a reacting element. Aura decay is a real-time clock, and the reaction matrix (Anemo clears all, Pyro clears Hydro/Cryo/Electro, Hydro clears Pyro/Cryo, Electro clears Cryo) is a second simulation layer beneath the fight.
- Continuous DoT ticking (13.3% ATK per tick from marks, burning marks, ice-shard fields, orbiting Hydro orbs) is metered against real elapsed time, so its severity is a function of how long you linger in a zone — a quantity that discretizes only by picking an arbitrary conversion rate.
- The boss's own unseen invulnerability frames during infusion punish players for continuing to attack through an animation. Wasted damage due to animation timing has no turn-based equivalent.
- Hitbox scale matters: the strategy notes explicitly reward abilities with large hitboxes because Azhdaha's body is enormous, and reward bows and catalysts for safe uptime at range. Hitbox and range are geometric properties.
- The four-character party layer — swapping to a shielder before a telegraph, rotating a healer in, holding a Burst — is a resource system running underneath every one of the fight's decisions, and it is not the same resource as a hand of cards or an energy pool.
- The whole fight is deliberately built around uptime under pressure: the tension is whether you can keep attacking while dodging, which is a continuous-attention problem, not a decision-per-turn problem.

## La Signora — "The Fair Lady," Eighth of the Fatui Harbingers (Trounce Domain: Narukami Island: Tenshukaku; domain subtitle "Memories: Duel to the Fiery Death"). Second form is titled Crimson Witch of Embers / Rosalyne-Kruzchka Lohefalter.
*Element: Two-element boss on a hard swap. Phase 1 is Cryo (Delusion-powered Harbinger form, 50% Cryo RES). Phase 2 is Pyro (Crimson Witch of Embers, 70% Pyro RES). Each phase resists its own element, so the fight is explicitly built around the player bringing the opposing element to each half. Drops span both (Shivada Jade and Agnidus Agate; exclusives Molten Moment, Hellfire Butterfly, Ashen Heart).* · *Entered the weekly (Trounce Domain) rotation in Version 2.1, released 2021-09-01, as part of the Inazuma Archon Quest finale. Domain: Narukami Island: Tenshukaku, subtitled "Memories: Duel to the Fiery Death," unlocked after completing Archon Quest Chapter II Act III, "Omnipresence Over Mortals." Requires AR 35/40/45/50 for the four difficulty tiers (recommended levels 60/70/80/90). Exclusive drops: Molten Moment, Hellfire Butterfly, Ashen Heart; gem drops Shivada Jade and Agnidus Agate. For pool-modeling: she is one of the older weekly bosses — Inazuma-era, the second two-element hard-swap weekly after Childe — and has remained in the permanent Trounce rotation since 2.1 with no reported mechanical rework.*

### Phases
- **Phase 1 — La Signora, Cryo Harbinger** — trigger: Fight start. Opens deterministically with Ice Shards + Frozen Wheel (5 shards in a tight spread, then a curving wheel, 37.5% ATK each) — a scripted opener every single run.
  Elemental identity: pure Cryo, 50% Cryo RES, and an arena-wide Sheer Cold clock ticking 3.33%/sec that at full gauge chews 3% Max HP + 150 HP per second. Rotation shape: a mid-range zoning loop built from Frostblade Hailstorm (75% ATK, leaves Frosted Floor), Ice Flower (three delayed bombs, 37.5% ATK each), Biting Shards (5 sequential homing spears, 25% ATK each), The Frozen Void (a marked spear, 50% ATK on impact, then a 4-second delayed detonation for 75% ATK), and Snowfall Strike (50% ATK burst, with a teleport-to-you if you are out of range, so range is not a safe answer). Nearly every attack leaves Frosted Floor (6.25% ATK Cryo DoT), so the ground is progressively converted into a hazard layer. Attacks also feed the Sheer Cold gauge directly — 2% for weak hits (Ice Flower, Biting Shards), 5% for strong hits (Snowfall Strike, Ice Shards, Frozen Wheel, Hailstorm, Frozen Void, Tsaritsa's Benevolence), 2.5% per Frosted Floor DoT tick. Timer-based escalation: if the fight runs long she uses The Tsaritsa's Benevolence — six icy spears that surround the player character and converge, 100% ATK plus Frosted Floor. Punish window on the boss side: Pain for Pain — any single instance of DMG exceeding 30% of her Max HP has all the excess nullified, then she detonates for 125% ATK Cryo around her AND instantly fills every player's Sheer Cold gauge. This is a burst cap that converts overkill into a survival threat.
- **Carmine Chrysalis — the cocoon interphase** — trigger: HP gate: at 20% of Phase 1 HP. She relocates to the arena center and encases herself in a large ice cocoon.
  State change, not a damage race in the normal sense. She gains a Frozen (Cryo) elemental shield (60 shield strength) and becomes a stationary shield-break puzzle rather than a duelist. Sheer Cold accumulation is DOUBLED for the duration. Her moveset collapses to a rump of three abilities — Biting Shards, Ice Flower, and The Tsaritsa's Benevolence — with the first two upgraded: enhanced Biting Shards fires 9 spears in quick succession with much stronger homing and now leaves Frosted Floor where it lands; enhanced Ice Flower periodically drops single flowers at scattered points across the whole arena rather than three at once. The counter mechanic: Crimson Lotus Moths spawn periodically around the arena and can be picked up, imbuing the active character's next attack with a strong 8GU Pyro application that does far more to the cocoon than any ordinary hit. Elemental reactions, Pyro especially, speed the break; other elements are heavily deprioritized against the shell. Breaking the cocoon ends Phase 1 outright.
- **Phase 2 — Crimson Witch of Embers, Pyro** — trigger: Cocoon destroyed. A cutscene plays; she repositions to the arena center with the player placed at a distance. Grace period: she will not attack until the player closes distance or strikes her first.
  Full mirror-flip of Phase 1 with a fresh, larger HP pool (wiki stat ratios put Phase 2 at roughly double Phase 1's HP scaling) and 70% Pyro RES. Sheer Cold is replaced by Blazing Heat, same 3.33%/sec accrual and same 3% Max HP + 150 HP per second drain at full; Frosted Floor is replaced by Embered Earth (6.25% ATK Pyro DoT). Gauge feeding mirrors exactly: 2% weak (Hellfire Onslaught, mini-tornadoes), 5% strong (Crimson Lotus Moth, Decimating Lash, The Witch's Wrath, Fireball, main tornado), 2.5% per Embered Earth tick. Rotation shape is more aggressive and longer-ranged than Phase 1: Decimating Lash is a four-variant whip (single melee; a two-lash melee combo usually chained off a Warp; a longer single frontal strike; and a triple-split long-range AoE that then erupts from the floor for 75% ATK and burns the ground). Warp is a repositioning dash that deals 37.5% ATK on contact and trails Embered Earth, and it is her setup tool into melee combos. Fireball (62.5% ATK, rolling, leaves a burning lane) is a scripted follow-up to either The Witch's Wrath or Hellfire Onslaught — a recognizable two-beat combo. The Witch's Wrath is the heavy: wings unfurl, waves of molten rock rain down at 100% ATK each plus Embered Earth. Hellfire Onslaught is the chip: a flock of small moths in front of her, 25% ATK. Separately she summons individual homing Crimson Lotus Moths (37.5% ATK). Pain for Pain returns with a TIGHTER cap — 20% of Max HP instead of 30% — with the same nullify-the-excess, 125% ATK Pyro nova, and instant full Blazing Heat on all players. Her whip also one-shots Geo constructs on contact, deleting the standard 'hide behind a wall' answer.
- **Whirling Blaze — the untargetable enrage** — trigger: Dual trigger. Fires immediately once her Phase 2 HP reaches 20% (and she is not mid-attack), OR on a soft-enrage timer if the fight drags while she is below 50% HP.
  She moves to the arena center and transforms into a roaming blazing tornado, 75% ATK on contact, leaving Embered Earth along its path. Every ~3 seconds it sheds 4 mini-tornadoes (37.5% ATK each), one of which always tracks the player. She is fully untargetable for the whole duration — a hard DPS lockout, not a damage window. On expiry she spreads her wings at arena center for a final burst plus another 4 mini-tornadoes and applies a flat +50% Blazing Heat. The intended survival play is to park next to an Eye of Frost for the duration, or tank it through a shield. The fight ends when her Phase 2 HP is exhausted after she becomes targetable again.

### Signature mechanics
- Elemental identity inversion across phases: Cryo Harbinger with 50% Cryo RES, then Pyro Witch with 70% Pyro RES. The boss resists what she deals, so the player's answer element has to hard-swap mid-fight; the drop table (Shivada Jade + Agnidus Agate) advertises the split.
- Pain for Pain — a per-instance damage cap that nullifies overkill. Phase 1 caps at 30% of her Max HP per hit, Phase 2 tightens to 20%. Exceeding it wastes the excess AND triggers a 125% ATK elemental nova plus an instant full temperature gauge on every player. This is the defining anti-burst mechanic: it punishes the single biggest number you can produce and converts it into a survivability tax.
- The temperature clock: Sheer Cold in Phase 1, Blazing Heat in Phase 2. Both accumulate passively at 3.33%/sec regardless of what you do, and at cap drain 3% Max HP + 150 HP per second. It is a permanent second resource bar running in parallel to HP, and it is fed by taking hits (2% weak / 5% strong) and by standing in hazard ground (2.5% per tick).
- Persistent ground conversion: Frosted Floor (P1) and Embered Earth (P2), both 6.25% ATK elemental DoT, left behind by nearly every attack she uses. The arena degrades over the course of the fight — the longer the fight runs, the less clean floor exists, and hazard ground both damages and accelerates the temperature clock.
- Carmine Chrysalis: a mandatory shield-break interphase at 20% Phase 1 HP. A Cryo elemental shield (60 strength) on a stationary target, breakable meaningfully only with Pyro, while the temperature clock runs at double rate and her surviving moves are upgraded versions of themselves.
- Crimson Lotus Moths as a pickup-fueled counter-token: during the cocoon they spawn around the arena, and grabbing one imbues the next attack with a strong 8GU Pyro application specifically effective against the shell. The same moths reappear in Phase 2 as an enemy projectile — the resource in one phase is a threat in the next.
- Whirling Blaze as an untargetable enrage state — a scripted DPS lockout on a dual HP-and-time trigger, ending in a flat +50% Blazing Heat spike.
- Teleport/Warp as an anti-kiting tool. Snowfall Strike closes the gap in P1 if you are out of range; Warp in P2 both repositions her into melee combos and deals damage on contact.
- Scripted openers and combo chains: Ice Shards + Frozen Wheel always opens the fight; Fireball is a reliable follow-up beat to The Witch's Wrath or Hellfire Onslaught; the two-lash whip combo usually chains off Warp.
- Soft-enrage timers in both phases: The Tsaritsa's Benevolence (six converging spears, 100% ATK) in P1 if the fight runs long, and Whirling Blaze in P2 if the fight runs long below 50% HP.

### Arena gimmicks
- Four Hearts of Flame (Phase 1) at the arena corners: standing near one drains Sheer Cold at 12.5%/sec. A positional safe-zone system layered over an arena the boss is actively converting into hazard ground.
- Four Eyes of Frost (Phase 2), spawning at the same corner positions, functioning identically against Blazing Heat at 12.5%/sec drain.
- Consumable-vs-sustained tension on those objects: they have HIT-count durability (8 hits) rather than an HP pool, so fast multi-hit characters shatter them incidentally. Destroying one releases a wave that fully empties the temperature gauge and clears all hazard floor in that quarter of the arena — a strong one-shot cleanse — but the object then takes a long time to respawn, potentially leaving the player with no safe corner. The optimal play is deliberately NOT breaking them until the cleanse is needed.
- Arena quartering: each corner object owns one quarter of the arena for hazard-clearing purposes, so the cleanse is spatial rather than global.
- The arena physically widens for Phase 2 while her attacks simultaneously gain range and AoE — the space grows but the safe fraction of it shrinks.
- Geo constructs are destroyed in a single hit by her Phase 2 whip, invalidating the standard 'block with a wall' counterplay.
- Phase 2 opens with a scripted non-aggro window: after the transition cutscene she stands at center and will not act until approached or attacked — a free setup beat.
- Crimson Lotus Moths spawn around the arena as collectible objects during the Carmine Chrysalis interphase, making the shield-break a fetch-under-fire loop rather than a pure damage check.
- Known co-op quirk: after she is defeated in the Crimson Witch phase, Blazing Heat continues to accumulate for respawning players, and dying to it can eject the player to the door if the Trounce Blossom is unclaimed.

### Translates naturally (observations, not proposals)
- Pain for Pain is already a per-instance damage cap with a punish rider — it reads almost natively as a rule that clamps incoming damage per hit and retaliates when the clamp is exceeded. The cap tightening from 30% to 20% between phases is a clean per-phase parameter change.
- Sheer Cold / Blazing Heat is a second resource bar that fills on a fixed schedule and overflows into per-turn HP loss. A gauge that ticks up every turn regardless of player action, accelerates when the player takes hits, and only drains through a positional or spent-resource choice is a shape turn-based games already express.
- Frosted Floor and Embered Earth are persistent, accumulating DoT sources — a stacking damage-over-time layer that grows with fight length is a standard turn-based construct.
- The Carmine Chrysalis is a shield with an explicit break condition (Pyro / the moth pickup) and a stat block (60 shield strength) — a gated shield state requiring a specific counter type is directly expressible.
- Crimson Lotus Moths are a spawned pickup that buffs the next attack. A consumable token granting a one-shot damage-type conversion maps cleanly onto a per-turn resource.
- The two HP gates (20% of Phase 1 into the cocoon, 20% of Phase 2 into Whirling Blaze) are literal HP-threshold transitions and need no reinterpretation.
- The soft-enrage timers — Tsaritsa's Benevolence in P1, Whirling Blaze in P2 below 50% — are turn-count-based escalations, which is exactly how turn-based enrages already work.
- The scripted opener (Ice Shards + Frozen Wheel every run) and the fixed follow-up chains (Witch's Wrath or Hellfire Onslaught into Fireball) are already deterministic sequences, which is the same object as a fixed intent rotation.
- The elemental resistance inversion (50% Cryo RES then 70% Pyro RES) is a per-phase resistance profile, trivially statable.
- The Whirling Blaze untargetable window is a scripted no-damage-dealt duration — a stretch of turns where the boss cannot be hit is a familiar phase-transition device.
- The Hearts of Flame / Eyes of Frost hit-count durability (8 hits, not an HP pool) is count-based rather than damage-based, which is closer to turn-based accounting than to real-time HP.

### Resists translation
- The Hearts of Flame / Eyes of Frost proximity drain is fundamentally spatial and continuous — 12.5% per SECOND while near an object at a specific arena corner. There is no position axis in a turn-based fight to express 'near', and the drain rate versus the 3.33%/sec fill rate is a real-time economy that has no natural turn granularity.
- Arena quartering: the cleanse from destroying a corner object only clears hazard floor in that quarter. Both the quartering and the notion of which quarter you happen to be standing in are pure geometry.
- The consume-vs-preserve tension on the corner objects depends on real-time respawn delay — the reason not to break one early is that it takes a while to come back and you may be caught with no safe corner. Respawn measured in wall-clock seconds does not survive conversion to turns without inventing a cooldown that changes the feel.
- Dodgeable projectile patterns are the bulk of her kit and are execution-defined, not decision-defined: the tight five-shard spread, the curving Frozen Wheel, the enhanced nine-spear Biting Shards (the wiki's own advice is to walk side to side and specifically NOT to dash), and the six converging spears of Tsaritsa's Benevolence. In real time these are skill checks on movement; a turn-based frame can only re-express them as probability or as a binary block, which loses the mechanic.
- Delayed-detonation timers — The Frozen Void's spear exploding 4 seconds after impact, and Ice Flower's bombs going off 'after a short period' — are anticipation windows measured in seconds. Turning them into 'detonates in N turns' is a coarse approximation of a real-time telegraph.
- The Whirling Blaze tornado is a moving hazard that sheds four mini-tornadoes every ~3 seconds, one of which always homes on the player. Both the roaming and the shed cadence are continuous-space, continuous-time.
- Snowfall Strike's conditional teleport ('if the player is too far away, she teleports close first') encodes an explicit distance check, and Warp's contact damage plus Embered Earth trail encodes a path through space. Both are anti-kiting tools that only exist because kiting exists.
- The four Decimating Lash variants are distinguished chiefly by range and AoE shape — melee, longer frontal, triple-split long-range — distinctions that collapse into each other without a positional axis.
- The whip destroying Geo constructs in one hit is a counterplay-invalidation aimed at a specific real-time defensive object (a placed physical wall) that has no obvious turn-based analogue.
- Crimson Lotus Moth pickups require physically moving to and collecting a spawned object under fire — the cost of the resource is positional risk, not a turn or a card.
- The 8GU Pyro application on the imbued attack is gauge-unit elemental-aura math (application strength and aura decay over real seconds), a system with no turn-based counterpart at all.
- The Phase 2 non-aggro opener is defined by the player's physical approach — she waits until you close distance or strike. Without distance, only the 'or strike first' half of the trigger survives.

## Magatsu Mitake Narukami no Mikoto ("Raiden no Inazuma Tono" / the Raiden Shogun weekly boss), fought in the Trounce Domain End of the Oneiric Euthymia, Mt. Yougou, Narukami Island, Inazuma
*Element: Electro (mono-element, no infusion cycle). Every hit in every phase is Electro DMG or Electro DoT. Her only defensive element interaction is the Baleful Shadowlord Electro shield, which per the wiki shield table is 96 shield units and is weakest to Cryo, Pyro, and Dendro (Electro itself is near-useless against it; Superconduct/Overload framing in community guides comes from the same weakness table).* · *Entered the weekly Trounce Domain rotation in Version 2.5 (February 2022), alongside Yae Miko's debut; the wiki Change History lists 2.5 for both the boss and the domain. Access requires completing Raiden Shogun's Story Quest, Imperatrix Umbrosa Chapter Act II: Transient Dreams. Domain unlock tiers are AR 35/40/45/50 at recommended levels 60/70/80/90. Reward pool for pool-modeling: the boss-exclusive talent drops Mudra of the Malefic General, Tears of the Calamitous God, and The Meaning of Aeons, plus Vajrada Amethyst gems; claimable once per weekly reset at 30 Original Resin for a player's first three weekly-boss claims that week and 60 Original Resin after that. As a permanent Trounce Domain it has never rotated out, so it is always in pool.*

### Phases
- **Opening / stance assignment** — trigger: Fight start. She spawns in one of two stances at random: Musou Isshin (sword) or Engulfing Lightning (polearm).
  Range-gated opener, not a fixed script. In Musou Isshin she opens with Cross-Slash or Slash Combo 1 if the player is close, and always with Unbending Lightning (three discs then a slow horizontal blade) if the player is far. This is the fight's only 'read the distance' opener; from here she drops into a stance rotation with no scripted turn order.
- **Normal state - Musou Isshin Stance (sword)** — trigger: Entered at fight start or whenever she casts Thunderbolt (a 16.6% ATK frontal poke that also leaves periodic ground-strike thunderbolts running as a lingering hazard).
  Melee-pressure rotation. Core kit: Slash Combo 1 (six hits, 8.3/8.3/16.6/8.3/8.3/16.6% ATK, the 4th having outsized range), Slash Combo 2 (dash in, circular slash, blink behind, 8.3%x2 then 16.6% then 33.3%), Cross-Slash (dash + two crossing slashes, 16.6/8.3/58.3%), Unbending Lightning (ranged discs 33.3% each + horizontal blade 50%). Two set-piece abilities live only here: Illusions (splits into four copies, one 'special' copy identifiable because it fires two crossing lightning blades instead of one; three hits kill a copy; killing the special one stuns her; killing the copy opposite the special one makes it explode for 33.3%; the whole sequence repeats three times if the special is never found) and Musou no Hitotachi (vacuum push-out, Electro seeds rain for 66.6% on landing and leave 12.5% Electro DoT fields, then a freeze-in-place flurry for 33.3%x4 + 100% ATK). Punish/enrage flavor: a positional Follow-Up if the player is behind her after Slash Combo 1, Cross-Slash, or Stun Recovery, a 50% ATK flurry in this stance.
- **Normal state - Engulfing Lightning Stance (polearm)** — trigger: Entered whenever she casts Eyes of Stillness (16.6% ATK frontal poke, then persistent eyes spawn around the arena that flurry for 5.6% ATK x4 shortly after appearing).
  Telegraph-by-position rotation. Her teleport tells the pattern outright: teleport to the player's LEFT is always Slash Combo 3 (three slashes then a large surrounding lightning AoE, 16.6% per hit); teleport to the player's RIGHT is always Lightning Summon (polearm spin into a large 66.6% ATK AoE that dash i-frames do not beat). Also Slash Combo 4 (spin, dash in, 16.6/8.3/16.6/25%, opening hit undodgeable by dash) and Lightning Blade (33.3% upward slash launching a 33.3% travelling vertical blade), commonly chained off Slash Combo 3. The Magatsu Electroculi adds spawn only in this stance.
- **Baleful Shadowlord** — trigger: Gauge, not HP. The Ominous Destiny meter under her HP bar caps at 900: +20 every 2 seconds passively, +40 whenever she lands a hit on the player (that source is limited to once per 2s). At 900 she transforms. There is no HP threshold anywhere in this fight.
  Hard defensive lockout plus a resource-attrition layer. She gains an Electro shield and, per the wiki state table, a large global RES bonus (+210%) while shielded, so the phase is a shield-break check rather than a DPS race. The shield self-drains 8 GU (~8.3%) every 10 seconds, so it eventually expires on its own; breaking it early is the whole optimization. Attrition: active characters lose 20 Energy every 5 seconds, while triggering elemental reactions on her generates 3 non-elemental particles (1.5s cooldown), an explicit drain-and-refund loop. Her moveset is entirely replaced: Slash Combo 1 (front-facing: two slashes + a delayed bolt, 16.6/25/16.6 then 33.3 on the bolt), Slash Combo 2 (rear-facing punish: 66.6% turnaround slash + lightning along its path + bolt), Downward Slash (marked AoE with 8.3% DoT tick during the charge, then 116.6%), Lightning Blade (Enhanced) (two 25% slashes launching five vertical blades at 25% and one slow horizontal at 50%), Single Slash (sheathed-stance 50% around her), Slash Flurry (a far-range gap-closer, so staying close denies it entirely), Clap (tracking hands, 50%), Puppet's Shadow (a homing shadow doing repeated 8.3% contact damage that then becomes a smashing hand, 16.6% on appear + 33.3% on land), Baleful Vajra's Destructive Waves (three center totems, 25% on landing, then expanding rings at 50% with all three firing simultaneously at the end), and 'The Final Calamity' (see signature mechanics).
- **Shield-break stun / recovery window** — trigger: Baleful Shadowlord shield fully depleted (by damage or by its own decay).
  The fight's designed burst window. She exits Baleful Shadowlord, fully recharges all party members' Energy and special Energy, and is stunned for a period with lowered RES (the wiki state table lists Stunned at -50% RES). She then re-enters the normal two-stance loop and the Ominous Destiny gauge starts refilling, so the whole cycle repeats indefinitely. Two smaller stun windows exist inside the normal state as well: destroying the special Illusion (Musou Isshin) and destroying a single center Magatsu Electroculus (Engulfing Lightning) each stun her, with Stun Recovery dealing 8.3% ATK around her on wake-up.

### Signature mechanics
- Ominous Destiny gauge -> Baleful Shadowlord transformation. A second resource bar under HP, 900 cap, filling on a timer (+20/2s) AND on the boss connecting with the player (+40, 2s internal cooldown). The phase transition is therefore purely time-and-punishment driven, never HP-gated: taking hits accelerates the dangerous phase.
- Two-stance normal state with an explicit switch move. Musou Isshin (sword) and Engulfing Lightning (polearm) are separate movesets; she flips between them only by casting Thunderbolt (-> sword) or Eyes of Stillness (-> polearm), and each switch move also leaves a persistent arena hazard behind (recurring ground thunderbolts, or spawning eyes).
- Melee-only reactive shield with escalating counterattack. Melee attacks raise a shield that blocks all incoming melee damage. After 4 successful blocks a counterattack can fire, with probability = 10% x successful-block count, escalating each block; the block counter resets 1.25s after a block if not continued, so fast attack strings are punished and slow ones are not. Ranged attacks bypass the mechanic completely.
- Positional Follow-Up punish. Standing behind her shortly after Slash Combo 1, Slash Combo 3, Cross-Slash, Lightning Summon, Lightning Blade, or Stun Recovery makes her spin and add an extra attack (50% ATK flurry in sword stance; a slash plus ground explosions, 25% each, in polearm stance).
- Illusions add-spawn with a 'find the real one' identification puzzle. Four copies, each dying to 3 hits. The special copy is identifiable only by its tell (it fires two lightning blades in a cross pattern instead of one); killing it stuns the boss. Killing the copy diametrically opposite the special one instead makes that copy explode for 33.3%. Unresolved, the sequence loops three times.
- Magatsu Electroculi timed bomb adds (Engulfing Lightning only). Basic version: one orb at arena center, 8 hits to break; breaking it stuns her for an extended period, ignoring it detonates for 116.6% ATK. Enhanced version: three orbs around the arena plus a vacuum push, 6 hits each, each marking a large zone and charging before detonating for 133.3% ATK across its marked area, with repeated hits subject to damage-sequence falloff.
- Baleful Shadowlord Electro shield as a decaying timer rather than an HP pool. 96 shield units, self-draining ~8.3% per 10s, weakest to Cryo/Pyro/Dendro. Break it and you get a full party Energy refund plus a stunned, RES-reduced boss; ignore it and the phase simply times out on its own.
- Energy drain / reaction-refund economy. In Baleful Shadowlord the active character loses 20 Energy every 5s, while reactions on the boss return 3 non-elemental particles (1.5s cooldown). It is the only Genshin boss whose signature mechanic attacks the player's resource generation directly rather than their HP.
- 'The Final Calamity' scripted insta-kill with a build-the-safe-zone counter. She blinks out, fully drains all party Energy AND special Energy, and drops a Flowers of Remembrance orb. The orb is charged by elemental attacks: one Electro hit fills it completely, one non-Electro elemental hit adds 26% regardless of gauge units, with a 0.5s charge cooldown that is separate from normal elemental application ICD. Fully charged, it spawns a protective shield bubble. She then marks an unprotected player with the Eye of Stormy Judgement and performs a horizontal slash that freezes players and kills the active character outright, ignoring shields and all defensive stats. Protected characters take exactly 1 True Electro DMG instead. Dash i-frames do not work; Elemental Burst i-frames and Mona's Mirror Reflection of Doom i-frames do, and Hu Tao C6 / Kuki Shinobu C6 survive it. Character-swapping immediately before the slash can fail to register protection and still kill.

### Arena gimmicks
- Fixed circular dream-arena (End of the Oneiric Euthymia) with no platform destruction, no phase-change terrain, and no environmental cover. All 'terrain' is boss-generated and temporary.
- Safe-radius geometry on Musou no Hitotachi: the attack pushes the player out of melee, blankets the field with Electro seeds and their 12.5% DoT pools, then freezes and shreds inside its AoE, but the outer rim of the arena is entirely unaffected. It is a stand-at-the-edge check.
- Persistent stance-linked field hazards: Thunderbolt leaves periodic ground-striking bolts running afterward; Eyes of Stillness spawns eyes at points around the arena that flurry shortly after appearing, and their spawn placement tracks the player's movement path (circling tightly makes them spawn off-path).
- Magatsu Electroculi as arena bombs with marked detonation zones. The enhanced three-orb version explicitly paints large ground markers and charges for an extended period, turning the floor into three overlapping deny-zones with one shrinking safe pocket, entered via a vacuum push that repositions the player first.
- Baleful Vajra's Destructive Waves: three totems planted at arena center emitting outward-expanding Electro rings that must be jumped or dashed through, culminating in all three firing a wave simultaneously. A pure rhythm/traversal gimmick; Venti's wind current trivializes it by removing the player from the ground plane.
- The Flowers of Remembrance orb is a player-built safe zone rather than a hazard: a single point in the arena that must be charged with elemental damage under time pressure, after which standing inside its shield bubble is the only survivable position on the field. Everything outside is an insta-fail zone for The Final Calamity.
- Ley Line Disorder (domain-level rule, not a per-attack mechanic): Ominous Destiny accumulation and its acceleration on player hits, plus the Baleful Shadowlord Energy drain and RES gain, are codified as the domain's standing disorder.
- Trounce Domain framing: unlimited attempts, but rewards claimable once per weekly reset at 30 Original Resin for the first three weekly-boss claims and 60 thereafter.

### Translates naturally (observations, not proposals)
- The Ominous Destiny gauge is already a turn counter in disguise: +20 per 2 seconds is a flat per-tick accrual, and +40 on landing a hit with a 2s internal cooldown is 'gains extra charge when its attack connects.' A gauge that fills on a fixed schedule and accelerates when the player fails to mitigate is the standard shape of a countdown-to-transformation.
- The whole fight is gauge- and mechanic-gated with zero HP thresholds. Nothing about phase order depends on damage dealt, so a translation never has to reconcile HP-gate skipping with burst damage.
- The two-stance normal state maps directly onto discrete intent tables: two movesets, and a specific, visible move (Thunderbolt / Eyes of Stillness) that performs the swap and telegraphs it a turn ahead. The switch move also deals small damage, so the 'transition turn' is already a low-value intent.
- Position-based tells in Engulfing Lightning are literal deterministic intent flags: teleport left ALWAYS means Slash Combo 3, teleport right ALWAYS means Lightning Summon. That is a telegraph-then-resolve pair with no reaction-time component once the flag is read.
- The melee-block shield is an interaction rule, not an action-economy one: block melee, and after 4 blocked hits a counterattack fires at 10% per accumulated block. Escalating counter-probability per attack in a window is a clean 'attacking a guarded target risks retaliation' rule, and the ranged-bypass carve-out is a legible counterplay lane.
- Magatsu Electroculi are hit-count-gated timed adds (8 hits for the single orb, 6 for each enhanced orb) that either explode for large damage or, when killed, stun the boss. Add spawns with a hit-count HP proxy, a detonation timer, and a stun payout are already turn-structured.
- Illusions is an add-spawn identification puzzle with fixed hit costs (3 hits per copy) and a tell that distinguishes the correct target. Killing the correct add stuns the boss; killing the wrong one (the opposite copy) punishes with an explosion. Choose-the-right-target-among-identical-adds is a standard turn-based construction, and the three-repetition loop is already a bounded number of attempts.
- The Baleful Shadowlord shield is a decaying block value (96 units, ~8.3% lost per 10s) with element-typed vulnerabilities and a scripted payout on break (full Energy refund + stun + RES down). A shield that ticks down on its own, breaks early to specific damage types, and rewards the break with a free burst window needs almost no reshaping.
- Energy drain (-20 per 5s) versus reaction-driven refund (+3 particles per reaction, 1.5s cooldown) is a resource-attrition duel that already reads as a per-turn drain with an earn-it-back valve.
- The Final Calamity is a multi-turn scripted mechanic: strip the player's resources, spawn an object, require a specific quality of damage to charge it within a window (Electro completes it instantly, non-Electro contributes 26% per hit), and then deliver an unavoidable effect that only the object's shield answers. Charge-the-thing-or-die is one of the most portable set-pieces in the fight.
- The Electro-vs-non-Electro charge asymmetry on the orb (1 hit vs ~4 hits) is a discrete counting rule, not a physics interaction, and the 0.5s charge cooldown is explicitly decoupled from normal elemental ICD.
- Post-break stun windows recur three separate ways (special Illusion, Electroculus, shield break), all resolving to the same designer object: a fixed-length vulnerability window with a RES reduction and a wake-up AoE (Stun Recovery, 8.3% ATK) that punishes overcommitting into the last moment.

### Resists translation
- The Follow-Up punish is purely positional: it fires only if the player is standing behind her within a short window after specific moves. Turn-based combat has no facing or standing-behind state to check, so the trigger condition has no native referent.
- Musou no Hitotachi's entire counterplay is spatial radius: the outer rim of the arena is completely safe. A turn structure has no concept of 'stand further out', so the attack collapses to either unavoidable or free.
- Dash i-frames, and the specific exceptions to them, are the fight's core defensive language. Several moves are called out precisely because dash i-frames do NOT work on them (Lightning Summon, Slash Combo 4's opener, The Final Calamity), and the interesting texture of the encounter is knowing which. Without a real-time dodge verb there is nothing for those exceptions to be exceptions to.
- Baleful Vajra's Destructive Waves is a jump/dash rhythm test against expanding rings, including a triple-simultaneous final wave. It is a traversal timing minigame with no state to model in turns beyond 'take damage or don't'.
- The Eyes of Stillness spawn logic reacts to the player's movement path (running in tight circles makes eyes spawn off-path). Exploiting movement-tracking AI has no analogue when the player never occupies a position.
- The melee shield's 1.25s block-counter reset means slow attacks are strictly safer than fast ones. Attack speed is not a dimension of turn-based combat, so the risk/reward that makes this shield interesting flattens out entirely.
- The Illusions' 'stand near any illusion to take repeated damage' proximity DoT, and the invisible no-damage explosion on the special illusion that nonetheless triggers hit-related effects, both depend on continuous spatial adjacency and on a hit-event that carries no damage and no element.
- The Final Calamity's insta-kill is deliberately shield-piercing and stat-ignoring: it kills through any shield and any defensive stat, and is answered only by the orb's specific shield or by a narrow set of i-frame sources. A card game generally has no 'ignores all mitigation, only this one specific effect answers it' primitive that isn't just an unfun coin flip, and the known escapes (burst i-frames, Mona's skill, Hu Tao C6, Kuki C6) are all real-time or character-specific exploits.
- The character-swap timing failure on The Final Calamity (swapping right before the slash may fail to register shield protection and kill anyway) is an engine timing artifact, not a designed rule.
- The Ominous Destiny gauge is wall-clock driven, not action-driven: +20 per 2 real seconds regardless of what the player does. In a turn structure, turn length is player-controlled, so the same rate produces wildly different pacing depending on how much the player does per turn. Any translation must pick a substitute clock, and that choice changes the fight's difficulty curve.
- The randomized starting stance and the range-dependent opener (close = Cross-Slash, far = Unbending Lightning) mean the fight has no fixed opening sequence, which is at odds with the readable, memorizable opening turn that turn-based bosses usually rely on.
- Poise damage / stagger interruption is a real mechanic here (high-poise attacks stun her more easily, and she has distinct light and heavy staggered states plus an 'Interrupted' state). There is no turn-based equivalent to a continuous poise meter competing against the boss's animation commitment.

## Guardian of Apep's Oasis (two named forms: "Warden of the Last Oasis" for Phase 1, "Warden of Oasis Prime" for Phase 3). Trounce Domain: The Realm of Beginnings, Apep's Resort, Desert of Hadramaveth, Sumeru.
*Element: Dendro-aligned, but split by phase. Phase 1 (Warden of the Last Oasis) has NO elemental damage type — every listed attack is Physical; its Dendro identity is purely defensive (70% Dendro RES). Phase 2 adds are all Dendro-typed (50% Dendro RES). Phase 3 (Warden of Oasis Prime) is fully Dendro on offense — every attack deals Dendro DMG — while keeping 70% Dendro RES. Net: the fight is a Dendro boss that is highly resistant to Dendro, so the player's Dendro is a liability lane, not the answer.* · *Entered the weekly (Trounce Domain) rotation in Version 3.6 — the wiki Change History tag is 3.6, and 3.6 launch coverage confirms it as that version's new weekly boss. Domain: The Realm of Beginnings, in Apep's Resort, Desert of Hadramaveth, Sumeru. Unlock is quest-gated behind Nahida's Story Quest (Sapientia Oromasdis Chapter Act II: Homecoming), specifically after completing "What Shape Does the Self Hold" — the quest in which the Proliferating Organisms are first previewed. Four difficulty tiers at AR 35/40/45/50 (recommended levels 60/70/80/90). Exclusive drops: Worldspan Fern, Primordial Greenbloom, Everamber; also Midlander billets and Nagadus Emerald. For pool modeling: this is the second Sumeru-region weekly boss added after Shouki no Kami (3.2) and the All-Devouring Narwhal (3.5), and it is the only weekly in that cluster whose middle phase removes the boss from the field entirely.*

### Phases
- **Phase 1 — Warden of the Last Oasis (corrupted form)** — trigger: Fight opener. Runs until the boss's HP bar is depleted to zero; depleting it does not kill the boss, it forces the transition to Phase 2.
  Standard aggro-chase boss with a physical-only kit and no elemental application at all. Stat profile: HP ratio 18, ATK ratio 6, 70% Dendro RES, no weak point. Turn-pattern shape a designer would recognize: a repositioning opener, a melee rotation with a variable-length combo, and two telegraphed area punishes. (a) Emerging Strike — submerges, reemerges at the player's current position, double front-limb swipe, 100% ATK Physical; this is its gap-closer/reposition move and functionally re-centers the fight on the player. (b) Body Sweep — full-body spin over a large area, 90% ATK Physical; the wide-ring answer to melee stacking. (c) 4-Part Combo — swipe / slam / sweep / slam at 50/50/80/90% ATK, and notably it can terminate early after part 2 or enter starting from part 2, so the combo is a variable-length chain rather than a fixed script. (d) Remote Crusher — plants 4 limbs into the ground; they resurface at the player's location and slam successively, 70% ATK each; this is the signature Phase 1 tell, a delayed multi-hit ground-target that is dodgeable because it telegraphs its landing zone. (e) Triple Slam — returns to arena center, slams three times at 70/70/90% ATK, each slam emitting a wave of homing spiky orbs at 70% ATK (final wave 80%); this is the arena-wide reset/punish window, and the only Phase 1 move that adds a projectile layer on top of the melee. (f) Tail Sweep — a positional counter that only fires if the player is behind it, two swipes at 70/90% ATK. Punish windows are the recovery frames after Body Sweep and after the Triple Slam sequence. No enrage, no HP-gate sub-triggers, no adds, no shield state in this phase.
- **Phase 2 — Revitalizing Phase / defense of the Heart of Oasis** — trigger: Automatic on Phase 1 HP depletion. The boss leaves the field entirely; there is no boss body to attack. The HP bar is replaced by a cleansing-progress meter that starts at roughly 27%.
  A pure horde-defense interlude with a two-directional meter — the only phase in the fight with a lose condition that is not player death. The player never fights the boss; they fight waves of Proliferating Organisms while a stationary objective (the Heart of Oasis) is the real HP pool. Meter economy: killing a Preliminary Proliferating Organism grants +1.75%; killing any specialist organism grants +11%. Losses: a Preliminary's melee or ranged attack drains 0.6% (1.2% if that Preliminary is buffed by a Provender); a Pernicious reaching the Heart and self-destructing drains 14%. Reaching 100% advances to Phase 3; reaching 0% fails the challenge outright. Wave composition is typically three or four Preliminary Proliferating Organisms, occasionally escorted by one specialist. Specialist roster and their roles: Protective — grants nearby Preliminaries a Dendro shield conferring full damage immunity, and that shield refreshes to full durability every 0.5 seconds, so it cannot be broken by DPS; the Protective itself must die to strip it (a hard kill-priority gate). Provender — buffs up to 3 nearby Preliminaries (raising their damage and their meter drain), and while channeling that buff it gains 9900% RES to all damage types plus high interruption resistance, i.e. it is effectively invulnerable while buffing; the counterplay is indirect, since it loses 40% of its Max HP each time a buffed Preliminary dies. Predatory — consumes a nearby Preliminary to enter an enhanced state, then chases the player with a rapid bite chain up to 8 hits at 100% ATK Physical each (a single bite outside that state is 50% ATK); it is a player-pressure add, not a Heart-pressure add. Perch — spawns barriers in front of or around the player purely to restrict movement, deals no damage, and teleports to the player if they get too far away; it is a mobility-denial add. Pernicious — the timer add: rolls straight at the Heart on spawn and self-destructs on arrival for 100% ATK Dendro and -14% progress; killing it instead refunds 20 Energy to every party member. Crucially, Pernicious does NOT spawn at all until the message 'Deadlier creatures incoming...' appears, which only fires if the player is taking too long — this is a genuine soft-enrage timer layered on top of the meter.
- **Phase 3 — Warden of Oasis Prime (revived form)** — trigger: Cleansing progress reaching 100% in Phase 2. The boss returns transformed and Dendro-infused; fought to death for the kill.
  Same skeleton as Phase 1 with the physical kit rewritten into Dendro and an added ranged/zoning layer plus a scripted arena-wide execute. Stat profile: HP ratio 16 (slightly lower than Phase 1), ATK ratio 10 (substantially higher), still 70% Dendro RES. (a) Emerging Strike — same submerge-and-reemerge-at-player reposition, now a body sweep for 70% ATK Dendro. (b) Triple Airstrike — marks a large ground area and calls down elemental energy three times, 100/70/70% ATK Dendro; a telegraphed zone-denial move with a visible pre-marker. (c) Helix Shower — fires a large volume of double-helix projectiles at the player, 50% ATK Dendro each; sustained chip pressure that forces continuous movement rather than a single dodge. (d) Double Swipe — proximity punish, two body swipes at 80/90% ATK Dendro, fires only if the player is close. (e) Aftershocks of the Apocalypse — the phase-defining scripted sequence and the reason people lose this fight. The boss performs Emerging Strike to return to arena center, then leaps up and down repeatedly; each landing expands an area of Dendro-corrupted ground that applies rapid DoT at 10% ATK per tick. During this windup exactly 3 Shields of Revival's Hymn form on the arena. It then releases a finisher for 400% ATK Dendro across the entire arena. Standing inside a Shield of Revival's Hymn negates both the floor DoT and the 400% finisher; being outside one at high level is a wipe. This is a hard survival check with a fixed, readable script — a forced-response window rather than a damage race.

### Signature mechanics
- Two-body identity with a non-combat interlude between them: the fight is Warden of the Last Oasis -> Revitalizing Phase -> Warden of Oasis Prime. Depleting the Phase 1 HP bar does not kill anything; it hands the fight to a completely different mode where the boss is off-field.
- Element swap across the transformation: Phase 1 deals 100% Physical damage on every listed move; Phase 3 deals 100% Dendro on every listed move. The boss's own 70% Dendro RES is constant across both, so its defensive element never matches its offensive one until Phase 3.
- Bidirectional objective meter replacing the HP bar (Phase 2). Cleansing progress starts at ~27%, gains 1.75% per Preliminary kill and 11% per specialist kill, and loses 0.6%/1.2% per Preliminary hit on the Heart and 14% per Pernicious detonation. 100% advances; 0% is an outright challenge failure independent of party HP.
- Unbreakable refreshing shield gated behind a kill-priority target: the Protective Proliferating Organism grants damage immunity to nearby Preliminaries with a shield that fully refreshes every 0.5 seconds. No amount of damage strips it — the correct answer is to redirect all damage onto the shield source.
- Invulnerable-while-channeling support with an indirect kill condition: the Provender gains 9900% RES to all damage types and high interruption resistance while buffing, and can only be worn down by killing the Preliminaries it has buffed (each buffed death costs it 40% of Max HP).
- Soft-enrage spawn escalation: the Pernicious Proliferating Organism only enters the spawn table once 'Deadlier creatures incoming...' fires, which is triggered by taking too long. Slow play converts the phase from attrition into a hard timer.
- Risk/reward kill on the timer add: killing a Pernicious before it reaches the Heart restores 20 Energy to all party members, so the most dangerous add is also the party's burst-economy refill.
- Player-targeted mobility denial as a distinct add role: the Perch deals zero damage and exists solely to spawn movement-blocking barriers around the player, teleporting to them if they disengage.
- Add self-cannibalization: the Predatory consumes a friendly Preliminary to enter its enhanced 8-hit chase state, trading horde bodies for a single high-pressure threat.
- Scripted arena-wide execute with a supplied counter: Aftershocks of the Apocalypse — expanding Dendro DoT floor, 3 Shields of Revival's Hymn spawned during the windup, then a 400% ATK Dendro full-arena finisher that only the shields negate.
- Ground-emergence as the boss's universal reposition verb: both Phase 1 and Phase 3 open sequences by submerging and reemerging at the player's position (or at arena center for Triple Slam / Aftershocks), which is how the fight resets spacing between rotations.
- Positional conditionals rather than pure randomness: Tail Sweep fires only if the player is behind the boss (Phase 1); Double Swipe fires only if the player is close (Phase 3).

### Arena gimmicks
- The Heart of Oasis: a stationary objective at the arena's focus during Phase 2 that functions as the real health pool. Adds path toward it; damage it takes is expressed as loss of cleansing progress, not player HP.
- Fail-state zone semantics: Phase 2 is the only segment where the challenge can be lost without the party dying — cleansing progress hitting 0% ends the run.
- Shields of Revival's Hymn: exactly 3 protective bubbles that spawn on the arena floor during the Aftershocks of the Apocalypse windup. They are the only safe geometry against both the expanding Dendro floor DoT and the 400% ATK finisher, and they are limited in number, which matters in co-op.
- Expanding corruption floor: during Aftershocks, each of the boss's landings grows the area of Dendro-affected ground applying rapid 10% ATK DoT ticks, progressively shrinking neutral space until only the shields remain safe.
- Perch-generated movement barriers: player-targeted terrain spawned mid-combat in front of or around the player, purely obstructive, forcing repositioning inside an otherwise open arena.
- Ley Line Disorder (domain-level rule) explicitly restates the Revitalizing Phase objective — protect the Heart of Oasis, progress rises on kills and falls when the Heart is attacked — and notes that some defeated enemies restore Energy to characters (the Pernicious's 20 Energy refund).
- Domain gating: The Realm of Beginnings is a Trounce Domain with four difficulty tiers (AR 35/40/45/50, recommended levels 60/70/80/90), so all of the above scales rather than being a single fixed encounter.
- No boss weak point: the wiki entry marks weakpoint = no for both Warden forms, so there is no aimed-shot break mechanic anywhere in the fight.

### Translates naturally (observations, not proposals)
- Phase 2's cleansing meter is already a turn-economy resource. It has a starting value (~27%), discrete positive increments tied to kills (1.75% / 11%), discrete negative increments tied to enemy actions (0.6% / 1.2% / 14%), a win threshold (100%) and a loss threshold (0%). This is arithmetic on a shared counter, which is exactly what a turn-based system already tracks.
- Kill-priority puzzles survive translation intact. The Protective's refreshing-every-0.5s immunity shield is, in effect, 'these enemies cannot be damaged while X lives' — a condition that reads identically whether time is continuous or discrete.
- The Provender's indirect kill condition (invulnerable while channeling; loses 40% Max HP whenever a buffed minion dies) is already an if-then rule chained off another entity's death, with no timing component.
- Positional conditionals map to state checks: Tail Sweep 'if the player is behind it' and Double Swipe 'if the player is close' are behavior branches on a single boolean, which is the same shape as intent selection conditioned on player state.
- The 4-Part Combo's variable length — it may end early after part 2, or begin at part 2 — is naturally a multi-attack intent with a variable number of instances, a pattern turn-based combat represents routinely.
- Aftershocks of the Apocalypse is structurally a telegraphed multi-turn windup: a visible ramp, a stated massive payoff (400% ATK arena-wide), and a supplied counter that must be taken before the payoff resolves. The 'stand in one of exactly 3 shields' constraint is a limited-slot answer to an announced attack.
- The soft enrage is already discrete and event-driven: a message fires when the player is too slow, and only then does a new, more dangerous enemy type enter the spawn table. That is a threshold-triggered escalation, not a continuous ramp.
- The Pernicious's dual outcome — 14% meter loss if it connects, 20 Energy to the whole party if killed first — is a clean risk/reward choice per instance, resolvable once per unit rather than continuously.
- The two-body structure (Phase 1 HP bar depleted -> interlude -> Phase 3 as a different statline and element) is a straightforward phase-gate with a stat and damage-type swap: HP ratio 18/ATK 6 physical becomes HP ratio 16/ATK 10 Dendro.
- Wave composition is already enumerable — typically three or four Preliminaries, occasionally plus one specialist — which is a spawn table, not a spatial flow.

### Resists translation
- Everything about the ground-emergence verb. Emerging Strike's identity is that the boss disappears and resurfaces at wherever the player currently is; without continuous positioning there is no 'wherever the player is,' so the move collapses into an ordinary hit with no distinguishing character.
- Remote Crusher, the Phase 1 signature, is a delayed telegraph whose entire counterplay is reading the marked landing zones and physically leaving them before the successive slams land. Its skill expression lives in the gap between telegraph and impact, which a discrete turn boundary compresses to zero.
- The Shields of Revival's Hymn depend on there being exactly three finite locations and a scramble to occupy one. In co-op the tension is that shields are contested; with a party abstracted away from position, 'get inside one' has no cost and no failure mode.
- The expanding Dendro corruption floor is a continuously growing area — its threat is that safe ground shrinks over time. Discrete turns can model 'take DoT' but not the shrinking-space pressure that makes the expansion legible.
- Helix Shower's 'countless projectiles, 50% ATK each, requiring constant movement' is defined by volume and evasion over time rather than a countable number of hits; the real quantity is how long the player stays in the stream.
- Predatory's enhanced state — chasing the player while biting up to 8 times — is a pursuit mechanic. The 'up to 8' is a function of whether the player outruns it, not a fixed instance count.
- Perch's barriers are pure spatial obstruction that deal no damage whatsoever. An enemy whose only effect is 'you cannot walk there' has no expression at all in a system with no walking; it would have to be reinvented as something else entirely.
- Adds physically pathing toward the Heart of Oasis is the source of Phase 2's tension — the player is choosing what to intercept and how far it has traveled. Without travel distance, a Pernicious is simply 'an enemy that costs 14% in N turns,' losing the interception geometry that defines it.
- Provender's buff aura and Protective's shield aura are both proximity-scoped ('nearby' Preliminaries, up to 3 for Provender), so which minions are affected is a live spatial question that changes as units move.
- Triple Slam's homing spiky orb waves layer a projectile threat on top of a melee slam simultaneously; the two threat channels overlap in time rather than sequencing, which a single resolved intent flattens.
- The boss's 70% Dendro RES only bites because Dendro-based teams are the natural first answer to a Dendro boss surrounded by Dendro adds at 50% RES. That tuning is an anti-synergy against a specific team-building habit; the pressure it creates depends on the surrounding character system, not on the fight's turn structure.

## Shouki no Kami, the Prodigal — enemy entity name "Everlasting Lord of Arcane Wisdom" (Scaramouche / the Balladeer / Wanderer), Trounce Domain: Joururi Workshop, Chinvat Ravine, Avidya Forest, Sumeru
*Element: Multi-element, Electro-dominant. The enemy is tagged with five damage types: Anemo, Cryo, Electro, Hydro, Pyro (no Dendro, no Geo). Phase 1 is a rotating multi-element showcase (Cryo/Pyro arms, Hydro/Anemo vortexes, Electro set-pieces) with only 50% Electro RES and 0% RES to everything else. Phase 2 collapses toward Electro as the identity (30% global RES, 90% Electro RES base) with Cryo/Hydro/Pyro/Anemo relegated to hazard-litter moves. Player-side, the fight is built to be answered with Electro (the matrices), and in Phase 2 with Cryo/Dendro/Pyro (the adds' weakness) — a rare case where the boss's own element and the counter-element are the same.* · *Entered the weekly Trounce Domain rotation in Version 3.2 (released 2 November 2022), alongside the Sumeru Archon Quest that introduced the fight. Domain: Joururi Workshop, Chinvat Ravine, Avidya Forest, Sumeru — unlocked after completing Chapter III Act V, 'Akasha Pulses, the Kalpa Flame Rises' (final quest 'Where the Boat of Consciousness Lies'). Difficulty tiers at AR 35/40/45/50 with recommended levels 60/70/80/90. Exclusive drops: Puppet Strings, Mirror of Mushin, Daka's Bell (Wanderer/Nahida-tier talent materials), plus Midlander weapon billets and Vajrada Amethyst / Vayuda Turquoise / Varunada Lazurite gems. For pool-modeling: it is a permanent addition, was the third Fatui-adjacent two-phase weekly after Childe and La Signora, and was the first weekly boss whose Phase 1 to Phase 2 cutscene had a skip dialog in addition to a skip button (a distinction that held until Version 3.4).*

### Phases
- **Phase 1 — Enshrined Torso (altar platform)** — trigger: Encounter start. Boss is a fixed, non-ambulatory upper body mounted on the altar; the arena is a small circular platform.
  Stationary turret boss with a two-tier intent table. FILLER ROTATION (priority 3-4, cooldowns 7.5-22.5s, with shared-cooldown groups of 10s and 15s locking out sibling variants): Arm Slam (left/right variants — 75%/100% ATK Pyro or Cryo slam, then a delayed cross-arena sweep for 100% ATK) and Arm Combo (a sustained palm spray for 100% ATK every 0.1s while the arm tracks across the arena, then an off-arm slam for 66% ATK). Both filler moves guarantee a large Energy Block drop — the boss pays the player resource for surviving its basic attacks. MID-TIER: Shard Rain (a rain of 100% ATK Electro shards, then two homing prisms at 50% ATK; drops 2 small Energy Blocks) and Waterspout (three Anemo vortexes that vacuum the player in for 33% ATK per 0.5s, then convert into roaming Hydro waterspouts dealing 50% ATK per 0.5s; each leaves a small Energy Block on expiry). SET-PIECES (priority 1, i.e. highest priority, on 75-110s cooldowns with 25-65s initial cooldowns — roughly one every 60-90 seconds, twice-registered as variants 1 and 2): Electrostreak, a full-arena floor beam dealing 50% ATK Electro every 0.5s capped by a 100% ATK arena-wide shockwave, and Laser Array, a ring of rotating cannons firing sweeping 100% ATK Electro beams. Both drop a large Energy Block. Elemental identity is genuinely rotational here: Cryo/Pyro on the arms, Hydro/Anemo on the vortexes, Electro on the set-pieces. Damage is available on the torso at all times — the matrix loop is optimization, not a gate.
- **Phase 1 sub-state — Suppression (one Electro matrix lit)** — trigger: Player charges the Neo Akasha Terminal with Energy Blocks (small = 50%, large = 100% of the meter) and stands on one of the two Electro matrices to activate it.
  A partial-disable state, not a free window. The boss loses most of its rotation but retains a one-handed punish: Partial Paralysis Recovery, in which it raises a hand and calls down thunderbolts for 66% ATK Electro — some strikes are random and each of those seeds a small Energy Block, some track the player directly and seed nothing. This is a deliberately generous state: the punish move feeds the player the very resource needed to complete the combo. Hard timer: if the second Electro matrix is not lit within roughly 15 seconds, the boss shrugs off suppression and resumes its normal rotation. The Electro matrix carries a 75-second cooldown (versus 10s for Cryo/Pyro and 15s for Anemo/Hydro), so a failed chain costs the player over a minute.
- **Phase 1 sub-state — Overload Paralysis (both Electro matrices lit)** — trigger: Second Electro matrix activated while Suppression is still running.
  Full stun. The boss stops attacking entirely and the torso becomes a free-hit punching bag for a fixed window (community guides report roughly 20 seconds; the wiki does not publish a figure). This is the fight's designed burst window in Phase 1. Note that Phase 1 has no shield and no damage gate — the paralysis is a pure uptime and safety reward layered on top of an always-damageable boss, which is unusual for a Genshin weekly.
- **Phase transition — Ascension cutscene** — trigger: Phase 1 HP pool depleted. Phase 1 and Phase 2 are separate stat blocks with separate HP pools (roughly a 20:18 HP ratio between them), not one continuous bar.
  A hard, skippable cutscene break. The boss connects to its lower body, gains legs and two additional arms, and the arena opens from the small altar platform to a large hall. Nothing carries over mechanically: the matrix system is gone, the Neo Akasha Terminal's role changes from matrix-activator to weapon, and the Energy Block economy is re-priced. Designer-relevant: this is two distinct encounters stapled together, not an escalation of one.
- **Phase 2 — Shielded ambulatory god (large hall)** — trigger: Immediately after the transition cutscene. Boss spawns with its shield up.
  Mobile, arena-controlling, Electro-primary boss with a persistent shield state (+200% RES on top of a 30% global / 90% Electro base, i.e. 230% global and 290% Electro while shielded — effectively damage-immune to direct player attacks). Player damage in this state is not the win condition; it is fuel. The intent table is position-gated in a way Phase 1's was not. FILLER: Triple Earthshock (priority 5, 12.5-20s CD, only within 40m of arena center) — a three-beat combo of stomp, arm slam, arm slam for 75%/75%/100% ATK Electro, the last beat throwing an expanding 50% ATK ring, with telegraph circles on each beat. Thunder Lance (12.5-17.5s, two range-gated variants for 0-25m and 25-40m) drives two pairs of lances in for 100% ATK on landing plus a lingering 25% ATK per 0.5s hazard field. Eruption Trails (17.5-22.5s, one variant gated to 40-70m and a 0-150 degree arc) marks a cross on the floor and runs a Hydro trail and an Anemo trail along it for 75% ATK each. Thunder Sphere is a pure punish intent: it only fires when the player is behind the boss (>135 degrees), whereupon it turns and shoots five 50% ATK Electro orbs — an explicit anti-backline check. Double Charge (42.5-47.5s, 22.5-27.5s initial) is the gap-closer, two consecutive body-checks for 50%/62.5% ATK Pyro and 50% ATK Cryo, leaving burnt or frosted trails that tick 25% ATK per second. SET-PIECES: Cosmic Bombardment (priority 3, 50-60s CD, 42.5-50s initial) — an orb array bombards a large marked strike zone for 100% ATK Electro per shot, then the whole arena is raked with vertical beams at 100% ATK each; drops one small and one large Energy Block. Bomb Scatter (35-45s / 75-90s variants, 35s shared CD) litters the floor with Cryo, Hydro and Pyro bombs (50% ATK on landing) then rains sparks at 100% ATK; each bomb is a 2 GU elemental shield that can be popped with the right reaction, converting a hazard into a large Energy Block, or left to detonate for 100% ATK. ANTI-KITE LEASH: two intents exist purely as repositioning — 'Return to Center + Bomb Scatter' fires when the player is more than 20m from center, and 'Return to Center + Triple Earthshock' when more than 40m out. The boss teleports/relocates to center rather than allowing an out-of-range stall. Meanwhile the Neo Akasha Terminal auto-fires at the shield continuously, and the player charges its aimed shot (small Blocks 25%, large 50%; hits on the boss also spit 3-4 orbs worth 2.5% each on a 3.5s internal cooldown) to land the heavy shield-breaking shot. Four-Leaf Sigils now co-spawn with Energy Blocks as traversal aids.
- **Phase 2 sub-state — Setsuna Shoumetsu (shield-break enrage / insta-fail check)** — trigger: Shield depleted. This is a mechanic gate, not an HP gate — breaking the shield is what starts the sequence.
  A 40-second hard-fail countdown wrapped around an add-clear puzzle. The boss ascends to charge its finisher and deploys 4 Nirvana Engines at arena center: one grounded engine (Electro shield, 5 GU) firing bullet volleys at 25% ATK, and 3 airborne engines (Electro shield, 1 GU) periodically beaming the player for 37.5% ATK. The player's Terminal charge is reset to 0% the instant charging begins — a deliberate resource wipe so the meter must be rebuilt from scratch under fire. Each destroyed Nirvana Engine drops a small Energy Block; the engines are weakest to Cryo, Dendro and Pyro, so this is a typed add-clear race. Fail state: at 40 seconds Setsuna Shoumetsu lands and instantly kills the active character, ignoring DEF and ignoring shields. Immediately before impact the game strips all party Energy and Special Energy and freezes the active character in place — a forced no-input lock, so the kill is unavoidable by normal play (a known exception exists using invulnerability frames from a held Mona skill, which requires bursting about 4 seconds after the chain visual appears to break the freeze). In Co-Op only one player dies; the rest take 100% ATK Electro.
- **Phase 2 sub-state — Toppled / Paralyzed (success on the check)** — trigger: Player refills the Neo Akasha Terminal from Nirvana Engine drops and lands its aimed shot on the boss's chest weak point before the 40s timer.
  The boss is toppled and immobilized for 30 seconds and its chest hitbox becomes the vulnerable spot: RES against attacks that connect with the chest drops by 170% (30% global / 90% Electro becomes -140% global / -80% Electro, i.e. a damage multiplier well above 2x), while attacks that miss the chest get no bonus at all. On paralysis the whole party is granted full Energy, so the state is explicitly designed as a synchronized full-team burst window. Separately, once the shield is down the boss bleeds HP passively from the Terminal's auto-fire, and an aimed shot landed while it is unshielded removes 20% of its Max HP outright. The phase then loops: reshield, chip, break, Setsuna check, topple, burst — repeating until the Phase 2 HP pool is gone.

### Signature mechanics
- Neo Akasha Terminal as a player-operated second actor. A friendly turret NPC that is charged by a resource (Energy Blocks) the boss itself drops, and whose function is re-defined at the phase break: in Phase 1 it is the key that activates floor matrices; in Phase 2 it is the weapon that auto-fires at the shield, the tool that lands the heavy shield-breaking aimed shot, the tool that topples the boss during Setsuna Shoumetsu, and the source of passive HP drain once the shield is down. Its charge is priced differently per phase (P1: small Block 50%, large 100%; P2: small 25%, large 50%, plus 2.5% per orb from hitting the boss on a 3.5s internal cooldown).
- Boss-fed resource economy. Nearly every major attack is annotated with what it drops: Arm Slam / Arm Combo / Electrostreak / Laser Array each spawn a large Energy Block, Shard Rain spawns two small, each expiring Waterspout leaves one small, Triple Earthshock / Double Charge / Eruption Trails spawn a small, Cosmic Bombardment spawns one small and one large. The boss literally pays the player for eating its rotation, so the fight's tempo is a loop of survive-collect-spend rather than pure damage racing.
- Two-matrix Electro overload combo (Phase 1). Six elemental matrices are embedded in the platform floor. Lighting ONE Electro matrix applies Suppression — a partial disable in which the boss retains a single-handed thunderbolt punish that itself seeds more Energy Blocks. Lighting the SECOND within roughly 15 seconds overloads him into full paralysis and a free burst window. Miss the chain and the Electro matrix goes on a 75-second cooldown, versus 10s for Cryo/Pyro and 15s for Anemo/Hydro. It is a two-step combo with a tight middle window and a punitive miss cost.
- Shield-as-gate, not shield-as-HP (Phase 2). The shield grants +200% RES (230% global / 290% Electro), which is not a damage sponge but a soft immunity. It is depleted by the Terminal, not by the player's characters — the player's damage output during this state exists only to generate charge orbs. Breaking it does not reward damage; it triggers the Setsuna Shoumetsu sequence.
- Setsuna Shoumetsu — a 40-second insta-fail countdown gated behind a typed add-clear. Success and failure are both dramatic: succeed and the boss is toppled for 30 seconds with -170% RES on its chest hitbox plus a full-party Energy refund; fail and the active character dies outright through DEF and shields, after having all Energy stripped and being frozen in place. It is the only weekly-boss mechanic in Genshin that is a scripted unavoidable kill rather than a large damage number.
- Charge-meter wipe on transition. The moment Setsuna Shoumetsu begins charging, the Neo Akasha Terminal's progress is reset to 0%. The player must rebuild the entire meter from Nirvana Engine drops while under fire from those same engines.
- Typed adds with elemental weakness. 4 Nirvana Engines: 1 grounded (5 GU Electro shield, bullet volleys at 25% ATK) and 3 airborne (1 GU Electro shield, beams at 37.5% ATK). All are weakest to Cryo, Dendro and Pyro, and each drops a small Energy Block on death — so the add wave is simultaneously the threat and the only source of the resource that answers it.
- Chest-hitbox vulnerability. The -170% RES during paralysis applies only to attacks that connect with the chest; attacks landing anywhere else on the model get no discount at all. Damage location, not just damage timing, is the reward.
- Floor-corruption damage-over-time. Phase 1 Cryo and Pyro attacks paint tiles with Raw Frost (5% ATK Cryo per 0.5s) and Remnant Flame (15% ATK Pyro per 0.5s), cleansable only by the opposite matrix (Pyro clears Frost, Cryo clears Flame). Phase 2 Thunder Lance leaves 25% ATK per 0.5s zones and Double Charge leaves burnt/frosted trails ticking 25% ATK per second.
- Reaction-defusal of hazards into rewards. Phase 2 Bomb Scatter litters the floor with Cryo, Hydro and Pyro bombs, each a 2 GU elemental shield. Pop one with the correct elemental reaction and it becomes a large Energy Block; ignore it and it detonates for 100% ATK. The same object is a threat or a reward depending on the player's element coverage.
- Position-gated intent selection with an anti-kite leash. The Phase 2 AI table gates moves on distance and angle: Thunder Lance has separate 0-25m and 25-40m variants, Thunder Sphere only fires when the player is more than 135 degrees behind the boss, Bomb Scatter forks into a 'Return to Center' version past 20m, and Triple Earthshock forks into a 'Return to Center' version past 40m. Kiting is answered with a scripted relocation rather than allowed.
- Explicit priority-and-cooldown AI table. The published logic is: fire the highest-priority ability whose cooldown is up and whose range/angle requirement is met, breaking ties at random, with shared cooldown groups locking sibling variants (Arm Slam group 10s, Arm Combo group 15s, Bomb Scatter group 35s, Eruption Trails 20s, Thunder Lance 20s, Triple Earthshock 15s). Set-pieces sit at the top priority with 50-110 second cooldowns and long initial cooldowns, giving a predictable slow-heartbeat cadence under the filler.

### Arena gimmicks
- Two physically different arenas, swapped at the phase break by cutscene. Phase 1 is a small circular altar platform (the Lucent Altar) where the boss is a fixed torso overlooking the player; Phase 2 is a large open hall where the boss walks, charges and repositions. The arena change is the phase transition.
- Six elemental matrices set into the Phase 1 floor, activated by standing on them with a charged Terminal. Pyro clears Raw Frost from the ground; Cryo clears Remnant Flame; Anemo raises an Upcurrent (a vertical wind current that can dodge Electrostreak entirely); Hydro creates a continuous healing field; and two separate Electro matrices form the suppression/overload combo. Each activation also emits 5 elemental particles of its type, so the matrices double as a party Energy faucet. Cooldowns: Cryo/Pyro 10s, Anemo/Hydro 15s, Electro 75s. All matrices cease to exist in Phase 2.
- Floor-state corruption zones. Raw Frost and Remnant Flame tiles persist on the Phase 1 platform until cleansed by the opposite matrix, progressively shrinking safe standing room on an already small platform. Phase 2 has no cleanse tool — Thunder Lance impact fields and Double Charge burn/frost trails simply have to be walked around.
- Neo Akasha Terminal as a fixed environmental installation rather than a party member. It exists in both arenas, must be fed physical pickups, and its aimed shot is a manual aim at a specific weak point on the boss model.
- Four-Leaf Sigils spawning alongside Energy Blocks in Phase 2 only, giving the player grapple-style traversal to reach pickups across the much larger hall — a traversal system bolted onto a boss arena to make its size survivable.
- Setsuna Shoumetsu as an insta-fail zone in time rather than space: a 40-second window during which the whole arena becomes a kill-on-expiry field. There is no safe tile — only the mechanic solves it.
- Defusable hazard litter: Bomb Scatter's Cryo/Hydro/Pyro bombs are environmental objects with their own 2 GU elemental shields, convertible into resources by reaction or left as timed mines.
- Waterspout vacuum fields in Phase 1: three vortexes that forcibly pull the player toward them while ticking Anemo damage, then convert into roaming Hydro columns — forced movement on a small platform that already has corrupted tiles.

### Translates naturally (observations, not proposals)
- The fight is already two separate stat blocks with separate HP pools and a hard cutscene between them — Phase 1 and Phase 2 do not share a bar, share almost no moves, and re-price the shared resource. It is structurally two encounters, which is the shape a two-act boss card already has.
- The AI is published as a priority-plus-cooldown table: fire the highest-priority ability whose cooldown is up and whose gating condition is met, ties broken at random. That is an intent-selection algorithm stated in the same terms a turn-based designer would state it.
- Shared cooldown groups (Arm Slam 10s, Arm Combo 15s, Bomb Scatter 35s) are lockouts on families of moves, not individual moves — the same idea as an intent bucket that cannot repeat.
- Long-cooldown set-pieces with long initial cooldowns (Electrostreak, Laser Array, Cosmic Bombardment at 50-110s with 25-65s initial) produce a slow, predictable heartbeat under the filler rotation, which reads directly as a telegraphed big-intent every N turns.
- The Energy Block economy is a counter the boss itself increments as a side effect of specific attacks, with the drops explicitly enumerated per move (large Block from Arm Slam, two small from Shard Rain, and so on). Resource generation is already attached to enemy actions rather than to player actions.
- The Neo Akasha Terminal is a charge meter with named thresholds and phase-specific pricing (50/100 in Phase 1, 25/50 in Phase 2). It is spent to trigger a discrete effect and then it is empty.
- The two-Electro-matrix overload is a two-step combo with a stated window between the steps (~15 seconds) and a stated failure cost (75-second lockout). The suppression state has its own distinct behavior rather than being a pure stun, so the middle of the combo is playable.
- Setsuna Shoumetsu is a countdown to a lethal effect that is cancelled by satisfying a condition. A countdown intent that resolves fatally unless answered is one of the most portable boss mechanics that exists.
- Wiping the Terminal's charge to 0% when Setsuna begins is a resource reset tied to a state transition — clean and discrete.
- Draining all party Energy immediately before the lethal hit, and refunding all party Energy the instant the boss is toppled, are both symmetric resource swings attached to state changes.
- Nirvana Engines are a fixed-count add wave (4) with typed vulnerability (Cryo/Dendro/Pyro), individual damage profiles, and a resource drop on death. Adds that both threaten and fund the answer to themselves.
- The shield is a RES modifier, not a hit-point pool — a multiplicative damage-reduction state that a separate actor depletes over time, with a heavy manual shot to accelerate. Chip-versus-burst on a shield timer is standard turn-based grammar.
- The paralysis vulnerability is a flat RES swing (-170%) for a fixed duration (30 seconds), which is a vulnerability debuff with a duration.
- The 20%-of-Max-HP loss from an aimed shot landed while unshielded is a percentage-max-HP execute effect independent of player damage stats.
- Raw Frost, Remnant Flame, Thunder Lance fields and Double Charge trails are all persistent damage-over-time surfaces that require a specific cleanse (the opposite-element matrix) rather than time. DoT plus a typed cleanse is directly expressible.
- Bomb Scatter bombs are timed objects that can be destroyed by a correct elemental answer for a reward or ignored for a penalty — a per-object choice with an explicit right answer, which maps onto a per-turn threat you either handle or eat.

### Resists translation
- Almost every player-side solution is a physical location. The matrices are floor tiles you must stand on, in a specific one of six spots, on a platform that is simultaneously being covered with damaging tiles. The whole Phase 1 puzzle is 'be in the right square', with no abstract equivalent that preserves the tension.
- The chest-hitbox rule. The -170% RES applies only to attacks that connect with the chest and grants nothing to attacks that hit elsewhere. This is sub-model hit detection on a boss the size of a building, and there is no turn-based notion of 'where on the enemy your attack landed'.
- Manual aiming. The Terminal's shield-breaking shot and the topple shot are aimed shots at a weak point, with the aim itself being the skill expression. A meter that fills and then fires is trivial to model; a meter that fills and then must be aimed is not.
- Continuous-time damage ticks. 100% ATK every 0.1 seconds from the Arm Combo palm spray, 50% ATK per 0.5s from Electrostreak, 25% ATK per 0.5s from Thunder Lance, 5%/15% ATK per 0.5s from floor corruption. These are duration-and-overlap problems, and the interesting part is how long you are in the stream, not whether you are hit.
- Position-and-angle gated AI. Thunder Lance forks at 25m, Bomb Scatter and Triple Earthshock fork into Return-to-Center variants past 20m and 40m, Thunder Sphere only fires when the player is more than 135 degrees behind. The boss's intent is a live function of continuous player coordinates, and the anti-kite leash only makes sense because kiting is possible in the first place.
- Verticality. The Anemo matrix's Upcurrent dodges Electrostreak by getting the player off the ground entirely, and the airborne Nirvana Engines require ranged answers because they are in the air. Height is a whole axis a turn-based board does not have.
- Forced movement. Waterspout vortexes vacuum the player toward them; Double Charge is a two-beat body-check that closes distance. Both are about being moved against your will through space.
- The elemental reaction system underneath Bomb Scatter. The bombs are 2 GU elemental shields defused by reactions, which depends on aura gauge units, decay rates, application intervals and reaction types — a real-time chemistry model, not a keyword match.
- Four-Leaf Sigil traversal exists solely to make a large arena crossable at speed. It has no meaning where there is no arena to cross.
- The Setsuna Shoumetsu fail state is a scripted unavoidable kill: Energy stripped, character frozen in place, DEF and shields ignored, and the only known escape is a frame-perfect invulnerability window from a specific character's held skill. Both the input lock and the i-frame counterplay are artifacts of a real-time engine; in a turn-based frame the kill would simply be unconditional, which loses the entire drama of the escape.
- The Phase 1 paralysis window's value is uptime — how much of your rotation you can physically execute in ~20 seconds of freedom, including animation cancels and swap timings. A turn-based window grants a fixed number of turns and does not reward execution speed at all.
- Terminal charge orbs from hitting the boss are governed by a 3.5-second internal cooldown, which caps how fast fast-hitting characters can generate resource. Internal cooldowns exist to fix a problem that only appears when attack rate is continuous.

## All-Devouring Narwhal (吞星之鲸 / "Star-Swallowing Whale") — Fontaine Weekly Boss, Trounce Domain "Shadow of Another World"
*Element: Hydro-primary in the outer fight; Electro-primary in the interior fight. Composite damage profile is Electro + Hydro + Pyro. Resistance flips per phase: the Narwhal body carries 70% Hydro RES; the interior Dark Shadow carries 70% Electro RES. Neither form has a designated elemental weakness — this is a resist-check boss, not a weakness-check boss.* · *Entered the weekly boss rotation in Version 4.2 "Masquerade of the Guilty," released 2023-11-08 — Fontaine's weekly boss and the first weekly boss built around the Arkhe (Pneuma/Ousia) system. Trounce Domain: "Shadow of Another World," located in Salacia Plain, Court of Fontaine region. Gated behind completion of the Archon Quest "Finale" (Fontaine Chapter, Act V), which is a heavier story prerequisite than most weekly bosses carry. Four difficulty tiers at AR 35/40/45/50 (recommended levels 60/70/80/90). Exclusive drops: Lightless Silk String, Lightless Eye of the Maelstrom, Lightless Mass; plus Midlander Billet and Varunada Lazurite (Hydro gems). For pool-modeling: this is a late-Fontaine-era boss, so any rotation model treating weekly bosses as uniformly available should account for its story gate and its 4.2 floor.*

### Phases
- **Phase 1 — Surface Rotation (Narwhal / Hydro)** — trigger: Encounter start. Runs by default and is the fight's home state; the boss returns here after every interior excursion.
  Uptime-gated burrow/emerge loop rather than a fixed intent rotation. The Narwhal submerges into the seabed and is untargetable while under; it surfaces to execute one telegraphed traversal attack, then re-submerges. The designer-recognizable shape is: long invulnerable travel beat -> single surfaced attack + brief exposed window -> re-submerge. Player damage is therefore squeezed into punish windows after each attack resolves, not distributed evenly.

The surfaced attack pool (wiki names are unofficial, damage figures are %ATK scalings):
- Leap: erupts and slams at the player for 100% ATK Hydro, seeding delayed ground cracks that detonate for 50% ATK Hydro. A long variant continues swimming forward and dives back down for another 100% ATK hit plus a second crack wave — effectively a two-beat combo version of the same opener.
- Penetrate: cracks telegraph under the player's feet, then the Narwhal spears up through the floor for 100% ATK Hydro, followed by several 50% ATK Hydro splashes. This is the reactive/punish attack — it targets where you are.
- Dive: descends from above for 100% ATK Hydro plus trailing 50% ATK splashes.
- Starfall Shower: arena-wide crystal rain, each impact 30% ATK Hydro. Chip/pressure filler, not a burst.
- Astral Downpour: light beams fall and persist, 50% ATK Hydro on appearance plus 12.5% ATK Hydro damage-over-time to anyone standing inside. Repeated on-appearance instances are throttled to 25% ATK by the engine's Damage Sequence anti-stacking rule. This is the fight's only true lingering DoT field.
- Eye of Maelstrom: spawns a destructible hazard object at arena center (see arena gimmicks).

Throughout this phase a hidden Fury meter accumulates toward the forced phase transition (see signature mechanics). Phase 1 is not HP-gated at all — it ends on the meter, not on a health threshold.
- **Phase 2 — Devoured / Interior (Dark Shadow / Electro)** — trigger: Hard gate on the Fury meter reaching 120 points. Not optional, not dodgeable, not HP-linked. Whatever the player was doing is interrupted and the party is swallowed into the Narwhal's belly.
  A full scene swap into a second arena with a second boss on its own health bar (rendered as a white bar beneath the Narwhal's). The outer Narwhal is inert and unreachable for the duration; the interior fight is the only thing happening.

The Dark Shadow (revealed in later story content to be the form of Surtalogi) fights as a greatsword duelist with a summon-based punish:
- Greatsword Strike, four variants that chain into freely-ordered combos: clockwise sweep (100% ATK Electro) leaving a delayed space-crack detonation for 50% ATK; counterclockwise sweep (80% ATK Electro) with the same 50% ATK delayed crack; downward cleave (90% ATK Electro) followed by a rip-from-the-ground follow-up for 50% ATK; and a thrown spinning greatsword (100% ATK Electro) along a line. The consistent grammar is: immediate hit now, second smaller hit on a delay — every melee variant plants a deferred detonation.
- Falling Thunder: waves of lightning periodically land around the arena for 50% ATK Electro each. Ambient, always-on chip pressure independent of the melee rotation.
- Eye of Maelstrom (interior version): the Shadow begins visibly charging Pneumousia energy inside a dark/purple sphere. This is the phase's designated interrupt window (see signature mechanics). If it completes uninterrupted, it resolves into one of three add-spawn payloads: 2 Geovishaps that leap-slam for 100% ATK Pyro each; 4 Consecrated Fanged Beasts spawning at arena corners that roll at the player for 50% ATK Electro; or 4 Hat Jellyfish at the corners firing bubbles for 50% ATK Hydro. Regardless of which payload fires, the Shadow then teleports to the player's position and follows up with another 100% ATK Electro greatsword sweep — so a missed interrupt costs adds plus a guaranteed positional ambush.

The Dark Shadow is the only weekly boss entity in the game with a Weak Point, making precision aiming meaningfully rewarded here in a way it is not on the Narwhal itself.
- **Phase 3 — Paralysis Payout Window** — trigger: Defeating the Dark Shadow inside the belly.
  The reward beat. The party is expelled back to the surface arena and three things fire at once: the Narwhal instantly loses 20% of its Max HP as a scripted deduction, it enters a Paralyzed state (stationary, surfaced, fully exposed — no burrow, no attacks), and every party member is refilled to maximum Energy. This is the fight's designated burst window and its resource-restoration beat rolled into one. Structurally the interior phase is not a punishment, it is the intended damage engine: the Narwhal's health is meant to be removed in 20% chunks by clearing its interior, with surface DPS as supplement.
- **Phase 4 — Cycle Repeat (Discounted Interior)** — trigger: Fury refilling to 120 after a prior devour. Repeats indefinitely while the Narwhal is above 20% Max HP.
  The loop re-runs, but the interior sub-boss is discounted: from the second devour onward the Dark Shadow spawns at half HP. The fight therefore accelerates rather than escalating — later interior phases are shorter and cheaper while paying the same 20% Max HP + full Energy dividend. Fury itself also runs hotter over time (its accrual rate doubles at the 80-second mark and never reverts), so devour cadence tightens as the fight goes on. This is the closest thing the encounter has to an enrage: not bigger numbers, but a compressing cycle.
- **Phase 5 — Sub-20% Endgame (Devour Lockout)** — trigger: Narwhal's HP dropping below 20% of Max HP.
  The devour mechanic switches off permanently. No further interior phases, no further 20% scripted deductions, no further Energy refills. The fight collapses into a straight surface damage race against the Phase 1 attack pool with no more resource payouts — the player must finish the last fifth of the health bar on whatever they have left. The Fury meter keeps filling but no longer resolves into anything. Design intent is clear: the interior loop is the intended way to strip the boss down, and the final 20% is the part you are expected to pay for out of pocket.

### Signature mechanics
- Fury / Hostility meter — the fight's spine, and a genuinely unusual gate because it is neither HP-based nor purely timed. It fills from three independent sources simultaneously: 1 point per second for the first 80 seconds of the fight, then 2 points per second thereafter (a permanent rate doubling, never reverting); +1 point per 1% of Max HP the Narwhal loses to damage; and +30 points each time the player destroys an Eye of Maelstrom. At 120 points it forces the devour. The player therefore controls the pace of phase transitions through their own damage output and their own hazard-clearing choices — dealing damage and cleaning up the arena both actively summon the next phase.
- Devouring — the phase transition itself is the boss eating the party. It is mandatory, unavoidable, and relocates the entire encounter into a second arena with a second boss on a second HP bar. Uniquely, the interior fight is the primary damage-dealing avenue against the outer boss: clearing the belly deducts a flat 20% of the Narwhal's Max HP by script, independent of any damage the player dealt in there.
- Arkhe (Pneuma/Ousia) counter-check, used twice with different payloads. On the surface, an Arkhe-aligned hit destroys an Eye of Maelstrom in one strike instead of the 8 regular hits it would otherwise take. In the belly, an Arkhe-aligned hit landed on the Dark Shadow while it is charging its Pneumousia sphere both cancels the attack and strips 25% of the Shadow's Max HP. This is a hard 'correct damage type answers the telegraph' check layered on top of the normal elemental system, and it gates a meaningful chunk of the fight's efficiency behind bringing the right kind of unit.
- Per-phase resistance inversion — 70% Hydro RES on the Narwhal, 70% Electro RES on the Dark Shadow. The same encounter punishes two different damage types in its two halves, so a team optimized for one half is taxed in the other. No compensating weakness exists in either form.
- Submerge-gated targetability — the Narwhal is only damageable while surfaced. Its burrow/emerge cycle, not its health, determines when the player's damage windows exist, which makes the surface fight an uptime-management problem rather than a rotation-execution problem.
- Delayed-detonation grammar on the Dark Shadow — every melee greatsword variant plants a deferred explosion (a 'crack in space') that resolves a beat after the initial hit, so the interior fight constantly overlays a second, later damage layer on top of the immediate one.
- Interior sub-boss discounting — the Dark Shadow spawns at half HP on the second and every subsequent devour, so repeated cycles get progressively cheaper to clear.
- Weak Point on the Dark Shadow — the only weekly boss encounter in the game where a weak point exists, and it exists only on the interior form, not the titular boss.

### Arena gimmicks
- Two entirely separate arenas within one encounter. The outer arena is a seabed platform in the Primordial Sea that the Narwhal treats as swimmable terrain — it burrows into the floor and surfaces through it as if it were water. The interior arena is a void-like space inside the creature's belly with its own enemy roster and its own boss. The Narwhal's HP bar remains visible during the interior fight with the Dark Shadow's white bar rendered beneath it, so the player watches both layers at once.
- Eye of Maelstrom (surface) — a destructible hazard object spawned at arena center. It exerts a vacuum field, slowly dragging nearby characters inward while it charges, then detonating for 50% ATK Hydro. It dies to 8 regular hits or to a single Arkhe-aligned hit. Critically, destroying it grants the Narwhal +30 Fury, so removing the hazard is a deliberate tradeoff against pulling the forced phase transition forward. There is no free option: eat the explosion and the pull, or accelerate the boss.
- Astral Downpour zones — persistent light pillars that damage on appearance and then tick 12.5% ATK Hydro on anyone standing inside them. Standing-in-the-bad ground denial, the only lingering damage-over-time field in the fight. The engine's Damage Sequence rule throttles repeated on-appearance hits from 50% down to 25% ATK, an explicit anti-overlap rule to stop stacked pillars from one-shotting.
- Starfall Shower — arena-wide crystal rain with no safe-spot structure, used as blanket pressure rather than as a positional test.
- Corner spawn points (interior) — the Dark Shadow's uninterrupted charge spawns 4 Consecrated Fanged Beasts or 4 Hat Jellyfish at the four corners of the belly arena, converging or firing inward on the player. The Geovishap variant instead spawns 2 that leap-slam directly onto the player's position. All three payloads end identically with the Shadow teleporting to the player for a follow-up sweep.
- Delayed 'cracks in space' (interior) and cracks in the ground (surface) — both arenas use the same telegraph language of a marked patch of floor that detonates on a timer after the attack that created it, forcing the player to track past attacks as future hazards.
- No insta-fail zones, no corruption meter, no shields requiring a specific counter to break. The Arkhe checks are efficiency gates and interrupt windows, not impassable shields — the Eye can be killed with 8 ordinary hits and the Shadow's charge can simply be dodged and endured. Nothing in the fight is strictly unclearable without Arkhe.

### Translates naturally (observations, not proposals)
- The Fury meter is already a discrete accumulator with a named threshold. Its damage-fed component (+1 per 1% Max HP lost) is a clean 'the boss charges faster the harder you hit it' counter, and its Eye-destruction component (+30) is a clean 'clearing this add costs you meter' tradeoff. Both read as counter-on-boss mechanics with no adaptation needed.
- The devour is deterministic, not random, and the player has partial control over when it fires. Phase transitions the player can see coming and can accelerate or stall are natively legible in a turn economy.
- The 20% Max HP scripted deduction on clearing the interior is a flat, non-damage-derived health removal — a payload delivered by completing an objective rather than by dealing damage.
- The Paralyzed state after each interior clear is a stun window plus a full resource refill, both of which are ordinary turn-based states (boss skips turns; player regains energy/resources).
- The Arkhe interrupt on the charging sphere is exactly the shape of an intent that must be answered with a specific damage type within a window, with a defined success payload (cancel plus 25% Max HP loss) and a defined failure payload (add spawn plus follow-up hit).
- Every greatsword variant's delayed 'crack' detonation is a damage-next-turn intent queued alongside a damage-now intent — a familiar two-layer intent structure.
- Falling Thunder is undifferentiated per-turn ambient chip that does not need a telegraph.
- The three interrupt-failure payloads (2 Geovishaps / 4 Fanged Beasts / 4 Jellyfish) are a randomized add-spawn table with distinct elemental identities, already discrete.
- The interior sub-boss having a separate HP bar that resets to half on subsequent visits is a re-summonable sub-encounter with persistent state between visits.
- The per-phase resistance flip (70% Hydro out, 70% Electro in) is a straightforward per-phase damage-type resistance modifier.

### Resists translation
- The submerge/emerge untargetability is a real-time uptime problem. On a turn grid it flattens to 'the boss is invulnerable this turn,' which discards the skill expression of tracking the dive, predicting the surfacing point, and holding burst — the actual content of the surface fight.
- The Fury meter runs on wall-clock seconds (1/sec, doubling to 2/sec at the 80-second mark), blended on a continuous axis with damage-derived accrual. There is no non-arbitrary seconds-to-turns conversion, and the 80-second acceleration point in particular has no honest turn equivalent — any chosen number is a designer's invention, not a translation.
- The entire spatial layer has no board to live on: the Eye's vacuum pull dragging you toward the center, the four-corner convergence of the Fanged Beasts and Jellyfish, the line-shaped thrown greatsword, standing out of the Astral Downpour pillars, and the Shadow's teleport-to-your-position follow-up are all position-dependent and lose their identity without positioning.
- Arkhe alignment is a party-composition prerequisite decided before the fight starts, not an in-combat action. A deck assembled over a run has no equivalent of the pre-fight roster slot that guarantees access, so any port either makes the counter always-available (removing the check) or makes it a draw-dependent gamble (changing it from a skill check to a variance check).
- The Dark Shadow's Weak Point is an aim-precision check. There is no turn-based analogue for aiming.
- The Damage Sequence falloff (repeated on-appearance hits throttled from 50% to 25% ATK) is a real-time anti-overlap rule that exists because multiple simultaneous instances can physically stack in space. In a turn structure the overlap it guards against cannot occur, so the rule has nothing to do.
- The interior phase is a full scene swap — a distinct arena with its own boss, its own enemy roster, and its own elemental identity, presented simultaneously with the outer boss's HP bar. Preserving the 'fight inside a fight' framing, rather than treating it as merely a second phase of the same fight, is a presentation problem more than a mechanics problem.
- The dramatic beat of the devour is that it interrupts you mid-action — it eats your setup, your burst window, your carefully timed rotation. Turn-based combat resolves actions atomically, so a forced transition can never interrupt anything, and that specific tension is unrecoverable.

## The Knave (Arlecchino) — archive name "Cinder of Two Worlds' Flames," title "Peruere." Weekly/Trounce boss of the Scattered Ruins domain, Mont Esus East, Liffey Region, Fontaine.
*Element: Pyro (Balemoon Bloodfire flavor). Every damaging ability in both phases is Pyro; there is no elemental rotation or infusion cycle. Defensively she carries 70% Pyro RES in both phases (all other RES at baseline), so Pyro teams are taxed but not walled. Her secondary damage channels are non-elemental: Corrosion (raw HP drain that pierces shields) and the player-side Scarlet Nighttide counter, which is True Physical DMG. She also has a weakpoint — only the second weekly boss to have one, after the Dark Shadow inside the All-Devouring Narwhal.* · *Entered the weekly Trounce rotation in Version 4.6, live April 24, 2024 — the same version that released playable Arlecchino. Domain: Scattered Ruins, a permanent Trounce Domain in Mont Esus East, Liffey Region, Fontaine. Unlocked by completing Arlecchino's Story Quest (the act 'When the Hearth-Flame Goes Out', concluding with 'Into the Sunlight') or via the Adventurer Handbook; minimum AR 40, with AR 40/45/50 difficulty tiers at recommended levels 60/70/80/90. For pool-modeling: she is the ninth weekly boss added, slotting in between the All-Devouring Narwhal (4.2, Fontaine) and the Natlan-era additions, and she is the second weekly boss ever to carry a weakpoint. Boss-exclusive drops are Fading Candle, Silken Feather, and Denial and Judgment; she also drops Midlander billets and Agnidus Agate. She has received no reported mechanical rework since 4.6 — the wiki change history lists only the 4.6 entry. Note that at least one widely-cited third-party guide (Game Rant) states Version 4.5 and describes 70% HP as the phase-2 transition; both are wrong, and I have used the wiki plus the 4.6 patch coverage over them.*

### Phases
- **Phase 1 — Duelist Opening (100% to 70% of the Phase 1 HP bar)** — trigger: Fight start. Phase 1 is a large HP pool (internal ratio 48) paired with a low ATK ratio (10) — a long, low-pressure runway.
  Baseline rotation is four short, self-contained melee intents that resolve in one or two hits each and always close distance to the player: Slash and Hook (horizontal slash into a scythe hook-pull, 20% ATK + 20% ATK), Scythewheel (thrown vertical spinning scythe, 50% ATK, ticking repeatedly on contact), Dashcleave (gap-close plus one slash, 70% ATK), and Reaper Dive (rises and dives with the scythe, 100% ATK). None of these consume her own HP and none apply Bond of Life yet, apart from the leash counter. Openers are slow and heavily telegraphed; the punish window is the recovery after each committed swing. The defining non-attack behavior in this window is Ranged Immunity: whenever she is not mid-attack and the player is at distance, she DODGES all incoming attacks outright — a hard damage-nullify state, not a resistance. Damage is only bankable in melee. This is the 'learn the leash' phase; there is no enrage timer and no add spawns anywhere in the fight.
- **Phase 1 Enraged — Fall of Darkness, Rise of Destruction (70% HP gate of the Phase 1 bar onward)** — trigger: Hard HP gate at 70% of Phase 1 Max HP. Announced by a voice line ('With the fall of darkness, destruction shall rise!' / 'The countdown has begun.'). This is an in-phase escalation, NOT the phase-2 swap — several third-party guides mislabel 70% as the phase transition.
  Three simultaneous changes. (1) Ranged Immunity converts from dodge to BLOCK: attacks at range are still nullified, but if enough are blocked in quick succession she punishes with a dash-in wing slash (70% ATK, Bond of Life tagged) — the leash grows teeth. (2) Every baseline intent grows a follow-up tail, converting one- and two-hit intents into 5-hit strings: Slash and Hook chains into 30% ATK x3 (reduced by Damage Sequence falloff), then 50% ATK, then 60% ATK; Scythewheel fires twice and adds a dashing grasping hand (70% ATK, BoL); Dashcleave adds a hooking pull (80% ATK); Reaper Dive adds a wing slash (70% ATK, BoL). (3) Three new intents unlock: Agile Leapslash (evasive left/right hops into an 80% ATK dive that chains directly into Scythewheel), Sundering Convergence (marks 2 crosses on the ground; after a delay 2 blades slash the marked zones for 50% ATK x2 BoL, then a large-area 100% ATK BoL follow-up), and the phase's namesake channel Fall of Darkness, Rise of Destruction — a scripted setpiece that applies Bond of Life to the WHOLE party at once and grants her 20% DMG Reduction for its duration. The first activation is always followed by Purgatorial Dance (leaps onto a hanging thread, throws 2 phantoms at 50% ATK BoL each, then a wing-and-scythe horizontal slash of 70% + 17.5% ATK into a 70% ATK BoL backward pull). The second activation onward is always followed by Bloodtide Banquet (charge-up, then a leap that scatters sharp threads which deal 70% ATK on contact and detonate for 100% ATK BoL over a large area) which is itself always followed by Reaper Dive. Bloodtide Banquet is the fight's designated interrupt check: a Charged Attack imbued with Scarlet Nighttide during it cancels the attack and deals a flat 11.2% of her Max HP as True Physical DMG. Miss the check and the detonation can outright kill an under-healed active character.
- **Phase 2 — Cinder of Two Worlds' Flames (triggers at 10% of the Phase 1 bar)** — trigger: Hard HP gate: Phase 2 begins the moment she is driven to 10% of her Phase 1 HP. She does not die at 0% of bar one — the remaining 10% is consumed by the transition, and she then presents a fresh, smaller HP pool (internal HP ratio 27 vs 48) with a markedly higher ATK ratio (14 vs 10). Corrosion currently on the party is cleared by the transition; Bond of Life stacks are NOT — they carry through.
  Opens with an unavoidable-if-unread punish: an immediate plunge onto the player for 100% ATK BoL, quoted by guides as a one-shot on squishy units. She now wields two Balemoon Bloodfire scythes and gains spider-leg strikes; the design shape moves from 'committed swings with punish windows' to 'long chained strings that self-link with almost no recovery.' Core rotation: Dual Scythe Combo (thrown spinning scythe at 12.5% ATK ticking on contact, then 30/40/40% ATK slashes, extending on proximity into a leaping slash and a hanging-thread dive for 40% ATK x2 and 80% ATK); Dual Scythewheel (both scythes thrown, 70% ATK, then a spider-leg dash for 70% + 50% ATK, able to chain straight into the 3rd hit of Dual Scythe Combo); and Turning Slash, a positional reaction — if the player gets behind her at close range she spins for 50% ATK x2 and immediately branches into Dual Scythe Combo if you are near or Dual Scythewheel if you are far. Two big-signal intents replace the phase-1 setpieces: Bloodbeam (charged laser at the player, 100% ATK BoL, chainable up to 3 times; closing distance is the counterplay) and Destruction Upon Destruction (flies up, fires 2 waves of 3 homing projectiles at 15% ATK BoL each, seeds 6 orbs around the arena perimeter, plunges for 100% ATK BoL, then the orbs detonate for 200% ATK BoL apiece — the single largest damage instance in the fight). Bloodtide Banquet returns rebuilt: it first applies Bond of Life to the entire party, then rolls one of two variants — Version 1 (ground-punch summoning Balemoon Bloodfire in an inner circle, a ring, and a wider circle for 75/75/100% ATK BoL, i.e. a nested safe-ring puzzle) or Version 2 (a domain projected around her body dealing 40% ATK x6 then 100% ATK BoL with a slightly wider final hit). Both variants remain interruptible by a Scarlet Nighttide Charged Attack for the same 11.2% Max HP True Physical payoff. Ranged Immunity is not listed for this phase; pressure comes from string density rather than from denial of chip damage.

### Signature mechanics
- Bond of Life as the fight's economy. Certain attacks (icon-tagged on the wiki) cost her 1.5% of her own Max HP to unleash — a self-damage cost that cannot push her below roughly 10% Max HP. On connect she is refunded 3% Max HP (net +1.5% lifesteal on a successful hit, net -1.5% on a whiff), and the struck character gains a Bond of Life equal to 10% of her ATK. Bond of Life is a healing-hostage debuff: incoming healing pays down the Bond before it restores any actual HP.
- Bond of Life as a damage amplifier. If a Bond-tagged attack hits a character who ALREADY carries a Bond of Life from any source, that attack deals 250% of its original damage. The fight therefore punishes stalling: refusing to heal off the Bond turns her entire tagged moveset into a 2.5x-damage set. Character talents and weapons that self-apply Bond of Life create the same vulnerability.
- Corrosion rider. While a Bond of Life applied by her is active, the character also suffers Corrosion, draining 1.25% Max HP + 200 every 2.5 seconds. Corrosion bypasses shields entirely, which is the reason shielders are considered off-meta for this fight and dedicated healers are near-mandatory. Bonds acquired purely from non-Knave sources do not carry Corrosion — but a Bond that she topped up at any point carries Corrosion until the ENTIRE Bond is cleared, because the game does not track Bond value by source.
- Scarlet Nighttide — the player-side counter charge. Clearing a Bond of Life (from any source) imbues the character's next Charged Attack with Scarlet Nighttide, dealing bonus True Physical DMG equal to 1.8% of her Max HP. This makes healing the offensive play: the heal-clear is not just defense, it mints the counter resource. Reports indicate up to two charges can be held at once.
- The interrupt check. A Scarlet Nighttide Charged Attack landed during Bloodtide Banquet (either the Phase 1 thread version or either Phase 2 variant) cancels the attack outright and deals an additional 11.2% of her Max HP as True Physical DMG — a ~6x payoff over the normal Nighttide hit. This is the fight's designed skill expression: the heal/clear/charge loop must be timed to be ready exactly when the banquet telegraph fires.
- Ranged Immunity leash. Out of attack animations and at range, she nullifies incoming damage — dodging above the 70% gate, blocking below it. Blocking enough attacks in quick succession triggers a counterattack dash and wing slash (70% ATK, Bond-tagged). She is a melee-mandatory duel by construction; ranged/off-field damage cannot be traded from safety.
- Self-consumption / lifesteal loop. She is the rare Genshin boss that pays her own HP to attack and heals off connecting hits — 'Healing' is listed as one of her four archive abilities. Every missed Bond-tagged attack is net HP loss for her; every landing one is net gain plus a stacking debuff on the party.
- Fall of Darkness, Rise of Destruction as a mandatory scripted channel with a defensive buff attached: party-wide Bond of Life application plus 20% DMG Reduction on her for the channel's duration, with a fixed successor script (first cast → Purgatorial Dance; second cast onward → Bloodtide Banquet → Reaper Dive).
- Weakpoint. She carries an exploitable weakpoint, only the second weekly boss to do so after the All-Devouring Narwhal's Dark Shadow.
- Damage Sequence falloff. Several of her multi-hit strings (Slash and Hook's 30% ATK x3, Sundering Convergence's 50% ATK x2, Bloodtide Banquet Version 2's 40% ATK x6) are explicitly reduced by the game's repeated-hit falloff rule — the listed motion values are the ceiling, not the realized total.
- No adds. Across both phases there are zero summons, zero minion spawns, and no third-party entities. All pressure comes from her own moveset and from the Corrosion drain.

### Arena gimmicks
- Scattered Ruins is a Trounce Domain: a single enclosed, flat, ruined-courtyard arena. There are no platform changes, no phase-driven terrain shifts, no insta-fail zones, and no environmental corruption fields. The arena is deliberately plain — the encounter's complexity is entirely in the rule layer and the moveset.
- The real 'gimmick' is the Ley Line Disorder attached to the domain, which is the Bond of Life / Scarlet Nighttide loop stated as a domain-level rule rather than an enemy ability: she applies Bonds of Life; clearing one arms a Scarlet Nighttide on the next Charged Attack; that Charged Attack can interrupt some of her attacks including Bloodtide Banquet; her HP-consuming attacks restore her HP and deal much more damage against a target already carrying a Bond.
- Telegraphed ground-mark zones function as the only spatial hazard layer. Phase 1 Sundering Convergence paints 2 crosses that erupt on a delay before a large-area follow-up; Phase 1 Bloodtide Banquet seeds sharp threads across the floor that damage on contact and then detonate; Phase 2 Bloodtide Banquet Version 1 builds a nested circle/ring/wider-circle pattern out of Balemoon Bloodfire, which is a safe-ring reading puzzle rather than a simple dodge.
- Phase 2 Destruction Upon Destruction converts the arena perimeter into a hazard: 6 orbs are seeded around the edge and each detonates for 200% ATK, so the outer ring of the arena becomes temporarily lethal and the fight is pushed inward.
- Phase 2 Bloodtide Banquet Version 2 projects a damaging domain centered on her body — an inverted safe zone where distance is the answer, opposite to Bloodbeam where closing distance is the answer.
- The Ranged Immunity leash acts as a soft arena boundary: the outer ring of the arena is a no-damage zone for the player in Phase 1, which functionally shrinks the usable combat space to melee range around her.
- There are no shields requiring a specific element or reaction to break. Her only defensive gates are the 70% Pyro RES tax, the ranged dodge/block state, and the 20% DMG Reduction during Fall of Darkness, Rise of Destruction.
- Domain access tiers: AR 40/45/50 with recommended levels 60/70/80/90, gated behind Arlecchino's Story Quest (Act 'When the Hearth-Flame Goes Out', unlocking after 'Into the Sunlight') or the Adventurer Handbook.

### Translates naturally (observations, not proposals)
- Bond of Life is already a turn-based debuff in all but name: a stacking negative-HP pool that intercepts healing before it does anything, with no positional or timing component. It maps cleanly onto a persistent stack counter that must be spent down.
- The 250% amplifier against a target that already carries a Bond is a conditional damage multiplier keyed off a visible debuff stack — exactly the shape of an intent that reads differently depending on player state.
- Corrosion is a percentage-of-max-HP-plus-flat drain on a fixed cadence that ignores shields. Its only real-time property is the 2.5-second clock; the effect itself is a shield-piercing damage-over-time tick.
- Her self-damage-to-attack economy (1.5% Max HP spent, 3% refunded on connect, floored near 10% Max HP) is a deterministic resource ledger. Whiffing costs her HP and landing gains it — a hit/miss-conditional self-buff/self-damage swing.
- The heal-to-arm loop is a genuine two-resource tension: healing is both the only way to remove the debuff and the only way to generate the offensive counter (Scarlet Nighttide). That is an economy decision, not an execution one.
- Phase transitions are pure HP gates at fixed thresholds (70% of the Phase 1 bar for escalation, 10% of the Phase 1 bar for the phase swap), with no timer component. The Phase 2 bar being a separate, smaller pool with higher ATK is a straightforward second-form statline.
- The successor scripting is deterministic and readable in advance: Fall of Darkness first cast always leads to Purgatorial Dance; second cast onward always leads to Bloodtide Banquet, which always leads to Reaper Dive. That is a fixed intent queue.
- The 20% DMG Reduction during Fall of Darkness is a timed defensive buff on a known intent — a 'do not dump damage this turn' signal.
- The Bloodtide Banquet interrupt is a discrete counter-check: hold the right resource, spend it on the right intent, cancel the intent and land a flat percentage-of-max-HP hit. The 11.2% Max HP payoff versus 1.8% for an unbanked Nighttide is a clear reward curve for correct timing.
- 70% Pyro RES is a flat elemental tax, and the True Physical typing on Scarlet Nighttide is an explicit bypass of it — a resistance profile plus a designated resistance-ignoring counter.
- Damage Sequence falloff on repeated hits is already a diminishing-returns rule on multi-hit sequences.
- Phase 1's escalation is legible as intents growing tails: each opener gains 2-3 additional hits after the 70% gate. Same intent name, longer resolution.
- The absence of adds and of environmental hazards means the encounter's entire state space is boss HP, boss phase, per-character Bond stacks, Corrosion ticks, and Nighttide charges — a compact, fully enumerable state.

### Resists translation
- Ranged Immunity is a real-time proximity state with no turn analogue: she nullifies ALL damage while the player is at distance and not mid-animation. In a turn-based frame, 'distance' is not a continuously varying quantity the player occupies, and the dodge/block distinction (dodge above 70%, block below) exists only to communicate escalation through animation.
- The counterattack triggered by 'enough attacks blocked in quick succession' depends on wall-clock attack density, not on a discrete count of player actions. Turn structure has no notion of 'in quick succession.'
- Nearly every big intent resolves as a telegraph-then-delayed-eruption spatial puzzle — Sundering Convergence's two marked crosses, the thread grid that detonates on a fuse, Bloodtide Banquet Version 1's nested circle/ring/wider-circle safe bands, Destruction Upon Destruction's perimeter orb seeding. The player skill being tested is reading an area diagram and standing somewhere else; there is no 'somewhere else' in a turn-based abstraction.
- The Bloodbeam versus Bloodtide Banquet Version 2 pair is deliberately contradictory in a way that only reads spatially: one is answered by closing distance, the other by fleeing her body. Collapse distance and both counterplays become the same non-choice.
- Turning Slash is a facing-and-proximity reaction — it fires only if the player is behind her at close range, and it branches into different follow-ups by distance. Facing is not modeled in turn-based combat.
- Contact-tick damage from the spinning Scythewheel and Dual Scythe Combo's thrown scythe deals damage repeatedly while the player is physically overlapping the projectile. The damage total is a function of how long you stand in it.
- The interrupt window is animation-scoped: the Scarlet Nighttide Charged Attack must land DURING the Bloodtide Banquet windup, not before and not after. Turn-based timing can express 'the turn before it resolves' but loses the sub-second commitment and the failure mode of being caught mid-animation with the charge unbanked.
- Corrosion's 2.5-second tick runs on wall-clock time and therefore punishes slow, deliberate play — the exact opposite of a turn-based game, where deliberation is free.
- Corrosion drains the entire party, not just the on-field character, which presupposes a 4-slot standby roster taking passive damage. There is no off-field-but-still-alive state to drain in a single-character turn structure.
- The Phase 2 opening plunge is designed as an unread-punish: it lands immediately on the transition and one-shots unprepared units. Turn-based phase transitions grant an implicit read of the new state before anything resolves, so the trap disarms itself.
- Healing to clear a Bond of Life is a continuous-magnitude operation — you heal a specific numeric amount and any overflow becomes real HP. Turn-based healing tends to be discrete and lumpy, which changes whether partial clears are ever a meaningful choice.
- The weakpoint is a positional hitbox that must be aimed at, which has no turn-based equivalent beyond a generic 'bonus damage' flag.

### Phases
- **Phase 1 — Grounded Swordsman** — trigger: Fight start. Runs until the boss first casts Severing Primal Fire (not an HP gate — no documented HP threshold; it is a scripted rotation entry, capped at 3 total uses across the whole fight).
  Grounded, melee-anchored, single-sword. Elemental identity: mostly Pyro chip with two designated abyssal (Physical + Attrition) burst tools.

FILLER ROTATION (low-threat, no Attrition): Single Slash (lunge, 50% ATK Pyro, scorches), Double Slash (two arcing swings, 30% + 30% ATK Pyro, scorches), Downward Cleave (60% ATK Pyro, scorches). These are the beat-keepers between the real intents.

FIXED COMBO: Abyssal Orbs — 4 large orbs fired in succession, each 40% ATK Physical + 10 Attrition stacks + scorch. This attack ALWAYS chains into Downward Cleave. A designer would read it as a hard-scripted two-intent chain: multi-hit DoT-applier followed by a guaranteed heavy hit.

PUNISH-WINDOW INTENT A — Wave Blast: boss plants its sword, self-applies a Void Ward with 50 durability, and channels repeating expanding flame waves (20% ATK Pyro per wave). The channel ends by lobbing a large abyssal orb that briefly vacuum-pulls the player in, then detonates for 60% ATK Physical + 30 Attrition. If the 50-durability ward is broken during the channel: attack is interrupted, boss loses 10% of its Max HP, and it is paralyzed for a stun window.

PUNISH-WINDOW INTENT B — Summon Tenebrous Mimiflora: blankets the arena in dark fog (heavy visibility loss) and spawns 3 Tenebrous Mimiflora adds, each carrying a 3-durability Void Ward. Adds periodically fire Abyssal Spheres (5% ATK Physical each, notably NO Attrition). After a delay the boss marks the player's position and a giant hand erupts for a 2-hit 30% + 60% ATK Physical with 20 + 30 Attrition. Kill all 3 adds first: attack interrupted, boss loses 10% Max HP, paralyzed. The fog is separately cleared by having a Masters of the Night-Wind character, Mavuika, or Pyro Traveler on-field in Nightsoul's Blessing.

Shape: two threat archetypes alternating with cheap filler — a 'break the shield' check and a 'clear the adds' check, both of which pay out identically (10% boss Max HP + free-damage stun).
- **Transition — Severing Primal Fire (arena-collapse cutscene-attack)** — trigger: Boss decides to cast it (no published HP gate). First cast is the Phase 2 entry. Usable a maximum of 3 times per fight, so it also fires twice more later, inside Phase 2.
  A three-beat scripted sequence, not a rotation move:
1) Abyssal flame rain across random arena locations, each drop 15% ATK Physical + 5 Attrition.
2) After a few seconds, larger meteorites join the rain, each 35% ATK Physical + 15 Attrition.
3) The boss rises, raises its sword, and delivers a massive downward cleave for 120% ATK Pyro that shears off part of the arena floor.

Each use permanently shrinks the playable arena. A player who falls with the collapsing floor is teleported back to arena center and the active character loses 80% of Max HP — a positional near-death penalty, not a killing blow.

The first cast is the phase gate: afterward the boss gains wings, hovers past the arena's edge, fights with dual swords, and unlocks its Phase 2 kit.
- **Phase 2 — Winged Dual-Sword / Aerial** — trigger: Entered immediately after the FIRST Severing Primal Fire. Persists to death; the remaining two Severing Primal Fire casts occur inside this phase and shrink the floor further.
  Airborne, ranged-forward, dual-wielding. Elemental identity: explicit Pyro-sword / abyssal-sword alternation, so a single attack now delivers both an elemental hit and a Physical+Attrition hit. The boss is still meleeable except during specific attack and channel animations, when it drifts out of reach.

ROTATION TOOLS:
- Dual Sword Blast: plunges both swords, sending a quake in a straight line forward. Pyro sword 25% ATK Pyro; abyssal sword 25% ATK Physical + 5 Attrition. Both quakes scorch their lane. Follow-up: sweeping abyssal beam from its mouth, 50% ATK Physical + 5 Attrition.
- Dual Sword Slash: strict 3-beat sequence — Pyro sword, then abyssal sword, then both simultaneously. Each slash 25% ATK of its type, scorches, and launches a matching wave for 15% ATK. Only the Physical hits add Attrition (10 stacks).
- Darkscale Shower: barrage of scales landing in front of it, each 15% ATK Physical + 5 Attrition. Coverage attack with gaps.
- Cloudform: a slash-sandwich. ### Signature mechanics
- ATTRITION — the fight's identity mechanic. Dark-purple attacks apply N stacks on hit (N varies per attack, from 5 up to 60). Stacks pool; once 0.5s passes without a new stack landing, the pool resolves and the active character loses HP equal to (1% Max HP + 50) PER STACK. It is HP loss, not damage: it bypasses shields entirely and scales off the victim's own Max HP, so bulk is not a defense. Every single-target and AoE threat in the kit is really a stack-delivery vehicle; the raw ATK% numbers are secondary.
- VOID WARD — a typed-counter immunity shield rather than an HP pool. While up, the wearer takes zero damage. Durability drops by 1 per elemental hit and by 3 per Nightsoul-aligned hit — a 3x efficiency multiplier that hard-favors Natlan/Nightsoul characters. Durability tiers in this fight: boss Phase 1 (Wave Blast) 50, boss Phase 2 (Annihilate) 40, Nightsoul Totem 24, Tenebrous Mimiflora 3. Because it counts hits rather than damage, hit-rate beats hit-size, and rapid Pyro reapplication (Burning) is a known cheap solvent.
- THE INTERRUPT-PUNISH CONTRACT — every gated channel resolves the same way. Break the ward (or kill all the adds) before the cast completes and you get three things at once: the incoming nuke is cancelled, the boss self-inflicts 10% of its Max HP, and it enters a paralysis stun for a free-damage window. Fail and eat a 30-to-60-stack Attrition bomb. There are four such gates in the fight (Wave Blast, Summon Tenebrous Mimiflora, Annihilate, Starscourge), so a clean run strips ~40% of the boss's Max HP through the gate mechanic alone. The boss's own HP is the currency it pays for failed casts.
- SCORCHED GROUND — nearly every attack leaves persistent hazard on the ground it struck, in one of two flavors that tick every 0.5s: Pyro scorch (10% ATK Pyro) or Abyssal Flame scorch (5% ATK Physical + 2 Attrition stacks). This is what makes the shrinking arena bite — the floor is progressively both smaller and dirtier, and the abyssal flavor is a second, ambient Attrition faucet independent of getting hit.
- DUAL-SWORD ELEMENTAL SPLIT (Phase 2) — the boss stops choosing between Pyro and abyssal and starts alternating them within a single attack. Dual Sword Blast fires both quakes at once; Dual Sword Slash runs Pyro → abyssal → both. Practically, each Phase 2 rotation delivers an unavoidable-ish elemental component plus a Physical component carrying Attrition.
- ADD-SPAWN GATE — Summon Tenebrous Mimiflora is the only add phase: 3 elites with trivially thin 3-durability wards, whose real cost is that they occupy the player during a fog blackout while a delayed marked-ground execute charges up.
- SCRIPTED USE COUNTERS — the two biggest set pieces are budgeted, not rotational. Severing Primal Fire fires at most 3 times (first = phase gate, all 3 = arena collapse stages). Starscourge fires exactly once per battle. The fight has a fixed, countable structural spine underneath the free-form rotations.
- FOUR-VOICE POSSESSION (flavor, but mechanically legible) — the boss speaks in the voices of four dead Pyro Archons (Xbalanque, Cochanina, Xumucul, Ndenge), and each attack is consistently assigned to one voice: Xbalanque owns the big Abyss set pieces (Summon Mimiflora, Wave Blast, Severing Primal Fire, Annihilate), Xumucul the aggressive slashes and Starscourge, Cochanina the Abyssal Orbs and Cloudform, Ndenge the Single Slash and Dual Sword tools. A designer could read the movepool as four sub-personalities sharing one body.

### Arena gimmicks
- PROGRESSIVE FLOOR COLLAPSE — the arena is destructible in exactly 3 stages, one per Severing Primal Fire cast. The playable space only ever shrinks; there is no restoration. An achievement (Die, or Go Parry II) exists specifically for killing the boss at the smallest arena size, confirming the collapse is a designed difficulty axis rather than set dressing.
- FALL PENALTY, NOT FALL DEATH — falling with the collapsing floor teleports the character back to arena center and strips 80% of their Max HP. Survivable in principle, effectively a death sentence in practice; a positional insta-punish that sidesteps the normal damage/shield system.
- SCORCH ZONE ACCUMULATION — the shrinking floor fills with two grades of persistent hazard (Pyro tick vs Abyssal tick that also feeds Attrition). Space denial and space reduction compound.
- FOG BLACKOUT — Summon Tenebrous Mimiflora engulfs the arena in dark fog that severely cuts visibility, hiding both the adds and the telegraph for the incoming ground-hand execute. Uniquely, the counter is roster-based rather than skill-based: it clears whenever a Masters of the Night-Wind character, Mavuika, or the Pyro Traveler is on-field in Nightsoul's Blessing.
- NIGHTSOUL TOTEM CAGE (Starscourge) — 3 totems form a circular boundary the player physically cannot exit, converting the arena into a tiny forced-DPS box that ticks 30% ATK Physical + 10 Attrition until you cut your way out. Each totem broken refunds the whole party's Energy, so the cage is simultaneously the punishment and the resource pinata.
- VACUUM FIELDS — the boss has two pull effects: the Wave Blast orb briefly sucks players toward its impact point before detonating, and Starscourge opens a vortex that drags the player to arena center. Both are forced-repositioning tools that set up the follow-up hit.
- AERIAL / OUT-OF-REACH STATE — after the Phase 2 transition the boss gains wings and hovers beyond the arena's edge. It remains meleeable most of the time, but specific attack and channel animations put it out of melee range, intermittently taxing melee-only teams.
- NO ADDED LEY LINE DISORDER — worth noting for pool modeling: the Stone Stele Records 'Ley Line Disorder' entry is not a modifier at all, it is just a restatement of the Attrition and Void Ward rules. The fight is unmodified; all difficulty is intrinsic.

### Translates naturally (observations, not proposals)
- Attrition is already a card-game status effect wearing an action-game costume. It is a stacking counter applied in discrete amounts by named attacks, it resolves on a delay rather than on impact, its magnitude is (1% Max HP + 50) per stack, and it explicitly pierces shields. The 'block does nothing here' clause is precisely the kind of tension a turn-based game uses to stop block-stacking from solving everything.
- Void Ward is a hit-count shield, not a damage-pool shield: 1 durability per elemental hit, 3 per Nightsoul hit. That counts actions, not numbers, which is exactly the unit a turn-based game already thinks in. The published durabilities (50 / 40 / 24 / 3) are already a difficulty ladder.
- The interrupt-punish contract is a fully-formed gated intent: a multi-turn charge with a visible payload, a stated break condition, and a triple payout on success (cancel the nuke, boss loses 10% Max HP, boss is stunned). It even has a built-in fail-forward, since surviving the failure just means eating a big Attrition dump.
- Several attacks are already fixed intent chains rather than free rotations. Abyssal Orbs ALWAYS chains into Downward Cleave. Dual Sword Slash is a strict Pyro → abyssal → both sequence. Cloudform is a rigid A-B-A (slashes → roaming cloud → same slashes). Each is a legible multi-turn pattern a player can learn and plan against.
- The set pieces are already budgeted with hard use counts — Severing Primal Fire exactly 3 times, Starscourge exactly once — which reads as scripted turn markers rather than probabilistic intent rolls.
- Scorched Ground behaves like a persistent per-turn chip status, and the two grades map cleanly onto 'plain recurring damage' versus 'recurring damage that also feeds the stacking DoT counter'.
- The add wave has a clean structure: 3 minions with deliberately thin (3-durability) shields, on a timer, where clearing them cancels the boss's queued execute. Kill-the-adds-or-eat-the-nuke is native turn-based vocabulary.
- Destroying a Nightsoul Totem instantly refunds the party's full Energy — a resource refund attached to clearing a threat, which is a resource-economy interaction rather than a positional one.
- The Phase 2 Pyro-sword/abyssal-sword split is a two-track alternation: the boss reliably delivers one elemental-typed hit and one Physical hit that carries the DoT counter, per action.
- The boss paying 10% of its own Max HP on every failed cast means the fight already tracks boss HP as a mechanical currency, not just a life total.
- Phase entry is event-driven rather than HP-driven (the first Severing Primal Fire), which is a cleaner trigger to author than an HP gate and needs no threshold-crossing edge cases.

### Resists translation
- Essentially the entire threat budget is spatially dodgeable in real time — sprints, i-frames, spacing, and standing in the gaps of Darkscale Shower. Strip positioning and every attack becomes unconditional, so raw ATK% values will not transfer; they were balanced against an expected hit rate well under 100%.
- The shrinking arena is the fight's signature and has no direct turn-based analogue. Three permanent floor-collapse stages express escalating pressure purely through geometry — a resource a card game does not have.
- The 80% Max HP fall penalty is a pure positional punish with no attack, no telegraph you can respond to except by moving, and no damage-type. It is a movement mistake tax.
- The fog blackout is an information-denial mechanic. It works because the player's perception of the board is imperfect; in a turn-based game where the board state is fully visible by construction, hiding things is a UI lie rather than a mechanic.
- Both vacuum fields (Wave Blast orb, Starscourge vortex) are forced-movement setup tools whose entire purpose is to place your body somewhere before the follow-up lands.
- Void Ward durability drains per HIT, and hit frequency is a real-time property — which is exactly why Burning's rapid reapplication is a known cheap solvent. Converting to a per-action budget changes which strategies solve the gate, because a turn-based system has no notion of ticks-per-second.
- Attrition's resolution rule — 'if no more stacks are accumulated after 0.5 seconds' — is a real-time coalescing buffer. It means an unbroken barrage keeps deferring the payout into one large hit. Any turn-based version has to pick a resolution point and thereby loses the mechanic's characteristic 'stop getting hit or the bill compounds' texture.
- Scorched Ground ticks on a 0.5s clock while you occupy the space; both its damage and its duration are functions of dwell time, not turn count.
- The Nightsoul Totem cage's threat is that you are physically walled in. Without a movement axis, 'cannot leave the circle' has nothing to restrict.
- The Nightsoul 3x ward-efficiency rule and the Natlan-roster fog clear both reach outside the fight into an external character-resource system (Nightsoul's Blessing) and a specific regional roster. They are team-composition gates expressed through an overworld mechanic, not through anything the fight itself grants.
- The Phase 2 aerial state matters only because melee weapons have physical reach; 'the boss is temporarily too high to hit with a claymore' does not survive the removal of a range axis.
- The boss's four-voice attribution is per-attack audio flavor with no mechanical hook — it is characterization delivered through a channel (voice lines fired mid-animation) that a turn-based presentation layer does not naturally carry.
