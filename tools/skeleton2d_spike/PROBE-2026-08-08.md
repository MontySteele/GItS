**Question** (BACKLOG `EB-42`, the one link `d69b7a0` could not test offline):
does a Godot-native `Skeleton2D` + weighted `Polygon2D` rig **seat as creature
Visuals in live combat**, and are the RenderingServer RIDs — invalid under the
headless dummy renderer — valid under the real one?

**Answer: YES, on every leg.** It seats, the RIDs are valid, the polygons are
visible in tree, the AnimationTree drives the bones, and the deformation
reaches the screen. No engine error came from the rig.

## What was observed

**1. It seats.** `CreateFromScene` returned a real `NCreatureVisuals`, BaseLib
converted the `Node2D` root and added `FormVfx`, and the named-node contract
(`%Visuals` / `Bounds` / `%CenterPos` / `IntentPos`) survived:

```
[INFO] [EB42] seated rig from res://kokomi/model/combat_visuals.tscn: NCreatureVisuals
[INFO] [EB42] tree-entered: root=NCreatureVisuals/Node2D name=KokomiCombat inTree=True
[INFO] [EB42] tree-entered: rig_found=True children=[FormVfx:Control,Visuals:Node2D,
       Bounds:Control,CenterPos:Marker2D,IntentPos:Marker2D,AnimationPlayer:AnimationPlayer,
       AnimationTree:AnimationTree]
```

**2. The RIDs are valid under the real renderer.** This is the reading the
headless run could not give:

```
[INFO] [EB42] t+1s: SKELETON bones=5 skeleton_rid=RID(123591978909696) rid_valid=True
       canvas_item_rid_valid=True
[INFO] [EB42] t+1s: POLY LayerLegs  verts=30 bones=4 skeleton_resolves=True
       canvas_item_rid_valid=True visible_in_tree=True texture=(560, 677) modulate_a=1 z=0
[INFO] [EB42] t+1s: POLY LayerHips  verts=28 bones=4 ... canvas_item_rid_valid=True visible_in_tree=True
[INFO] [EB42] t+1s: POLY LayerChest verts=28 bones=4 ... canvas_item_rid_valid=True visible_in_tree=True
[INFO] [EB42] t+1s: POLY LayerHead  verts=26 bones=4 ... canvas_item_rid_valid=True visible_in_tree=True
```

Note the ordering, because it is the same trap the spike already paid for once:
at creation time and at `tree_entered` the skeleton reports `bones=0`
(`get_bone_count()` refuses outside the tree) while its RID is **already**
valid. The bone count becomes 5 on the next frames. Read the rig one frame
late, not at construction.

**3. The AnimationTree drives the bones.** Bone rotations move between samples
one second apart — the idle clip is running on the seated rig, not frozen:

```
t+1s  Hips 0.0106  Chest -0.0176  Neck 0.0000  Head  0.0377  Legs 0.0000
t+2s  Hips 0.0068  Chest  0.0272  Neck 0.0000  Head -0.0158  Legs 0.0000
t+4s  Hips 0.0118  Chest  0.0128  Neck 0.0000  Head  0.0075  Legs 0.0000
```

**4. The deformation reaches the screen** — the render-side half, which bone
numbers alone do not prove. Frame-differencing the idle burst over a tight crop
of the head (the rig's longest lever) against a same-size control patch of bare
floor:

| crop | median Δ summed luma per 140 ms frame | max |
|---|---|---|
| head (rig pixels) | 412 629 | 724 474 |
| floor (control) | **0** | **0** |

The control patch is pixel-identical frame to frame, so the scene carries no
ambient motion and every changed pixel in the head crop is the rig. Same shape
on the hurt burst (head median 509 487, control 0). This is a mechanical
statement about pixels moving and nothing else — no claim about how it looks.

**5. Nothing broke.** `godot.log` for the whole session holds **four** `ERROR`
lines, all of them the probe reader's own premature `GetBoneCount()`
(`Condition "!is_inside_tree()" is true`) — an apparatus defect, not the rig's.
Zero engine errors, zero warnings and zero exceptions attributable to the
`Skeleton2D`, the `Polygon2D` skinning, or the pack mount. `CreatureAnimationRouter`
took the spine-less path as expected (`Visuals.HasSpineAnimation` false), the
state-display band drew below the creature normally, and combat played through
a card, a hit taken and a turn boundary with the rig on screen throughout.
