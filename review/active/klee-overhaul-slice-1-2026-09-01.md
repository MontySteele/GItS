# Klee overhaul, slice one: the rules, the starter, and 28 cards (Paper stage)

Written 2026-09-01 against the ruled brief (`klee-brief-2026-09-01.md`, all
nine picks ruled). This is the first Paper artefact under the three-stage
gate. It is the card list a prototype can be built from. **No number in it
is a claim.** Numbers are placeholders so the cards can be played; the
Balance stage prices them later, with the measurement law.

## 1. Why a slice and not the whole sheet

The brief's seven rules have never been played. Sixty cards written against
untested rules would bake in whatever is wrong with them. So slice one is
the smallest pool that exercises every rule and every loop: the ten-card
starter plus 28 pool cards, enough for an act-one run where each loop is
draftable and each defence shows up. The other thirty-odd cards, the
Hexerei readers, the four Personals and the stand-ins wait for the rules to
survive the Prototype gate.

**What the slice must show:** a player who has never read the brief can
find "plant, wait, boom" in fight one; the cook-or-cash decision is felt at
least once per fight; Sparks read as a reward and never as a second energy
bar; and being squishy is a problem with three answers rather than a death.

## 2. The rules, one line each (the brief's §3, unchanged)

1. **Bomb N**: a numbered charge on an enemy. Grows by 2 at the start of
   Klee's turn. Never goes off by itself.
2. **Set off**: only a card that says it. Every Bomb on the target goes off
   one at a time, each a Pyro hit for its size, before the rest of the card.
3. **Jump**: a Bomb whose enemy dies moves to a random living enemy at its
   current size.
4. **Spark**: 1 per explosion. Some cards cost Sparks instead of energy. No
   cap. Gone at end of combat.
5. **Pyro**: her Attacks and her explosions are ordinary Pyro hits, so every
   shared reaction applies with nothing printed.
6. **Mine N**: a Bomb that also goes off when its enemy attacks her, before
   the hit. Cooks like any Bomb.
7. Nothing fires by itself.

Keywords with tooltips: Bomb, Set off, Spark, Mine. Pick 5 raised the
keyword limit for exactly this.

## 5. What the engine has to do (the C# build list)

This is the whole of the new machinery. Anything not here is a card.

- **Bomb** as a per-enemy stack with a size; growth at the start of her
  turn (2, plus Explosives Workshop, replaced by Alice's Recipe).
- **Set off** as a card keyword: resolve each Bomb on the target as a Pyro
  hit for its size, one at a time, before the card's own damage. For
  random-target Attacks, per target hit.
- **Jump** on enemy death, not on Set off.
- **Spark** minted per explosion; the Spark alt-cost badge already exists
  behind the Spark alt-cost switch and is reused; no cap; reset at end of
  combat.
- **Mine** flag on a Bomb; fires before the enemy's attack lands on her.
- **Bomb payload** (Jumpy Dumpty: on explode, Mine 3 on every enemy).
- Two counters, both per turn: Bombs that went off; Bombs that reacted.
  Grounded reads last turn's first counter.
- **Merge** (Careful Arrangement), **remove for Block** (Sorry, Jean...),
  **total size set off** memory (Big Badda Boom), **double** (The Big One),
  **play again once** (Perfect Timing), two Power hooks (Chained Reactions,
  Sparks 'n' Splash), and **Vermillion Pact**, which lets the Set off Attack
  apply the reaction the explosion just consumed. Vermillion Pact is the one
  item on this list that touches shared reaction code; if it costs more
  than a day it drops out of slice one and is tested in slice two.
- **No automatic detonation** anywhere under the switch: whatever the
  shipped Klee mod fires at start of turn is off for the prototype.
- **UI**: the Bomb badge on the enemy (size, a fuse mark for a Mine) and the
  Spark counter. Nothing else.

All of it goes behind the prototype switch, C# first, per the ruled process
(change 7). The shipped 79-card Klee stays in the release build untouched.
The Python sim is not brought up for slice one.

## 6. The Prototype gate, and who plays

1. Opus builds the switch-gated prototype from §3 to §5 and the prototype
   surface rows, and runs the soak and the lints.
2. Seats play first: Opus, GPT and Qwen each play an act-one run on the
   build with this 28-card pool as Klee's only reward pool. They catch
   soft-locks, text that lies, and cards nobody plays, before your time is
   spent. That is the ruled limit on your playtesting (change 2).
3. Then you play: one act-one run, three or four fights including an elite.
   This is the "first build of a kit's rules" gate.
4. You answer four questions, in a sentence each:
   1. Did you want to cook, and did it feel like a bet?
   2. Did Spark cards ever feel like a second energy bar instead of a reward?
   3. Was being squishy interesting, or just death?
   4. Which card did you never want to play?

A pass is not a number. It is your four answers plus the seats' defect
list. A fail on question 1 or 3 goes back to the brief's rules, not to the
card numbers.
