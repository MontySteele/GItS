## 2. What the round found

**The hypothesis, read.** *Reader rows:* the natural seat was offered Well
Laid and declined it, reading its bare-morning face as self-contradictory
(`EB-539`); no Tide Wall or Tide Chart was offered. Not reached. *The two
halves:* Flank "excellent, but its Plan is a bet on next turn's intents";
Feigned Retreat's carry-out printed damage twice and no Block, so the seat
read "its two halves point in opposite directions" (`EB-545`, a pin: the
Block is either unpaid or unprinted). Pincer "never presented a decision":
3 twice now or 3 three times later "is a 3-damage difference on a card
too small to change any turn". Round 18's assembled seat called Pincer the
best of its five. Two seats, opposite reads, and the difference is the
deck around it; held.

**The engine is a tempo loan, said in the kit's own terms.** The assembled
seat: "richest when you are ahead and unusable when you are behind. From
floor 5 on I was under the damage curve and the correct play was
repeatedly block with three Defends and write nothing." That is the
brief's §3 weakness stated back by a seat that reached it at 2 HP, and the
same seat played Nereid's Ascension in three fights and declined it in
three, "a live call and it went both ways". Turn one of fight one "already
posed the kit's thesis: 21 face damage now versus Nereid's doubling
engine". The natural seat: the face-or-plan choice "was live on nearly
every turn and the right answer genuinely moved with the board"; Plan
ordering (Exposed Flank before Oath, 10 for 7) legible off the carry-out
line "the 10 is damage; the clause asked for 7", called "the best writing
on any screen".

**A rule the page does not print: a carry-out is not a hit.** Skittish gave
no Block to a body hit by two carry-outs and 6 Block to a plain Strike on
the same enemy, same fight. The engines say so on purpose (a planned
clause is not a card play, the same reading as Klee's Set off, `EB-490`),
and Klee's tip says it; Kokomi's does not (`EB-538`). "Either a defect or a
large undocumented advantage of planning into blockers": the latter, and
the tip owes the sentence.

**The starter, both lanes.** "Defend and Strike are dead weight, no Plan
line, no element, played only as filler"; "Strike asks nothing; it is the
reason my defensive turns had no damage attached". Battle Plan's now-line
"strictly dominated by its own Plan line", a dominated option printed
beside the good one. Recorded; R254 and R257 stand.

**Companions and reactions.** Assembled: six offered, two taken (Heizou,
Kirara), one Companion play in three of seven fights; a Fire Potion used
to set up Vaporize left no Pyro aura and nothing says which sources apply
an element (`EB-544`); the reaction rules "a large body of printed rules I
could not use". Natural: five offered, two taken, Bennett's Pyro into
Vaporize "the run's most enjoyable discovery, made at the draft"; the
Vaporize that left a Hydro aura was predicted by the gloss and settled by
HP (`EB-410`). Eleven rounds, one shape: the second element is the draft's
largest decision.

**Defects, seven rows.** An enemy letter reused by a spawned replacement,
against the printed invariant (`EB-541`). Raw localisation keys in Flank's
carry-out line (`EB-542`). Neow's Talisman's Strike half never landed on
the kit's Strikes (`EB-543`, `EB-409`'s family). A Skill's damage is not an
Attack and the faces do not say so (`EB-540`). The map's deck census drops
a Power played that fight (`EB-447`, cited). The carry-out and the Skittish
rule (`EB-538`), Well Laid's bare face (`EB-539`), Feigned Retreat's Block
(`EB-545`).
