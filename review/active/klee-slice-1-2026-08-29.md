## 1. The question this slice is asking

R213 E2, in full:

> **E2 — Reopen "Sparks mean free Attack": YES. Likely upstream of Klee's
> Attack-spam identity.**

The finding it answers is [USER]'s, from the 2026-08-26 exploratory playtest
(mod `0.2-1159`, raw notes `review/active/kokomi-playtest-notes-2026-08-26.md`).
Two lines carry it. R213's own summary of the run says Klee plays as
**"attack-spam Ironclad with less complexity"**. The run notes themselves say:

> *"Klee's cards mostly strike me as boring flavors of 'attack and/or bomb' —
> compared to Ironclad, who has a similar intended attack-focused playstyle,
> she seems much less interesting."*

### What the engine says, and it is sharper than the complaint

The Spark rule is three lines of `tier0/engine/combat.py`, and they are
**automatic and unconditional**:

```
if (card.type == "attack"
        and state.player.sparks >= spark_threshold(state)):
    return 0
```

…and, at play time, the bank is debited without being asked:

```
if (card.type == "attack" and cost == 0
        and p.sparks >= spark_threshold(state)
        and card.cost != 0 and card.cost != "X"):
    p.sparks -= spark_threshold(state)
```

So the bank has exactly one destination, it is chosen for the player, and it
is chosen the moment they play any Attack. There is no timing choice (the
discount lands on whichever Attack goes first), no targeting choice, no
conversion, and no way to forgo it. Measured against **LAW D2** —

> **D2 — Player-controlled leverage.** Every persistent resource and every
> automatic engine must feed a decision the player can steer: timing,
> targeting, placement, acquisition, conversion, or forgoing. "Watch it rise
> until the number is large" is not a decision.

— the base Spark rule feeds none of the six verbs. It is not even "watch it
rise": it is *watch it rise and then be spent for you*. That is the strongest
statement of E2 the repo can make, and it is a fact about the engine rather
than a taste claim.

### What already exists, so that this slice does not rebuild it

The Spark sink is **already built and already shipped** — this slice is not
inventing expenditure. `spend_spark` resolves in both engines; a short bank
pays nothing (never a partial spend); `combat.spark_cost` derives a
playability gate from the printed op so the price shown and the price paid
cannot drift apart; and the price must sit at top level or it is not a cost
line. Three shipped Uncommons print it (R211's W3 window):

| shipped sink | body | what the bank buys |
|---|---|---|
| `powder_charge` | Spend 2 Sparks. Detonate, +4. | a detonation |
| `hold_the_line` | Spend 2 Sparks. Block 5; +6 if the enemy intends to attack. | defence |
| `smoke_and_sparks` | Spend 2 Sparks. Apply 3 Vulnerable. | a debuff |

All three are **Skills**. That is the gap this slice is standing in: after
W3, Klee's *Skills* have a Spark decision and her *Attacks* still have none —
and "attack spam" is a complaint about her Attack turns. The sinks also charge
**2**, priced against True Spark Knight's reduced threshold ("2 is exactly one
free Attack under True Spark Knight"), not against the base rule E2 reopened.

### The hypothesis under test

> A resource that only subsidises Attacks feeds one decision — attack, or
> don't. A Spark that is **priced, thresholded, chosen against another
> outcome, or spent on something that is not an Attack** feeds a decision the
> player steers. If none of the four shapes changes how a turn plays, the
> answer to E2 is that the automatic free Attack was right and the rule closes
> again.

The three charter questions that bind are **D2** (does the bank feed a
steerable decision), **D3** (does the payoff carry a binding price), and
**D8** (does a Spark line change the *shape* of a turn rather than only its
number). The slice does not settle any of them by argument. It builds four
shapes, puts each on the same board as its shipped twin, and lets the funnel
ask.

### The one thing this slice deliberately does not touch

**The automatic free Attack itself stays exactly as it is.** Making it opt-in
would be a change to Klee's core mechanic (`klee-character-design.md` §3,
"at **3 Sparks**, next Attack costs 0 (consumes 3)"), which is a character
rule and not a card, and it cannot be quarantined on a card surface at all.
Every arm below is a **card**, and every arm is engineered to sit beside the
automatic rule without altering it. Where that took care, §2 says so.

That restraint is itself put to the gate as overall question 4.

## 2. The four arms, and how they would print

Four rows on `docs/prototype-surface.yaml`, compiled only into a dev build. A
shipped mod does not contain the classes at all, so there is no id a normal
game could be talked into granting.

### Arm 1 — the Attack pays the bank instead of being paid by it

| | |
|---|---|
| id | `proto_spark_priced_strike` |
| name | **Second Helping** |
| cost | 0, Attack, Uncommon |
| prints | *Spend 3 **Sparks**. Deal `{Damage}` damage twice.* (6 × 2) |
| shipped twin | `flame_on_the_wick` — *Flame on the Wick*, 0, Attack, Uncommon, 6 damage, **bank untouched** |

Same cost, type, rarity, target and damage figure as the twin. Two things
differ and they are one idea: the card charges the bank, and it hits twice for
having done so.

**Why this is the arm that speaks to "attack spam".** With three Sparks banked
and this card plus any paid Attack in hand, the turn has an order that
matters and cannot be dodged. Play the paid Attack first and the automatic
rule makes it free and eats all three Sparks — and this card is now unplayable.
Play this card first and it takes the three Sparks itself — and the other
Attack costs full price. **Two Attacks, one bank, and the player picks which
one the bank belongs to.** That is D2's *timing* and *forgoing* on the Attack
half of her kit, which is precisely the half W3 left without a decision.

**Why it needs no rule change, and this is engine-verified rather than
assumed.** A printed-**0**-cost Attack never debits the bank: `play_card`'s
debit branch is guarded by `card.cost != 0`, a guard that has been there since
R39 and R34 for their own reasons. So at bank 3 this card costs 0 (it always
did), takes no automatic debit, and its own top-level `spend_spark 3` is the
only thing that moves the meter. The automatic rule is untouched, and the
card sits beside it rather than against it.

**Doctrine it claims to follow, quoted.**
- **D2**: *"Every persistent resource and every automatic engine must feed a
  decision the player can steer: timing, targeting, placement, acquisition,
  conversion, or forgoing."* — timing and forgoing, on the Attack half.
- **D3**: *"Defence and engine advancement may share a card, but not both at
  full rate without a binding cost: … a card or resource spent …"* — the
  resource is spent, at top level, gated.
- LAW, Klee: *"X-cost attacks are exempt from spark-spend"* — untouched; this
  card is not an X card, and the exemption is not widened.

### Arm 2 — a bar to bank to, and the bank pays for what it used to read for free

| | |
|---|---|
| id | `proto_spark_threshold_draw` |
| name | **Rummage** |
| cost | 1, Skill, Uncommon |
| prints | *Draw 1 card. If you have 3 or more **Sparks**: spend 3 **Sparks** and draw 2 cards.* |
| shipped twin | `eager_to_help` — *Eager to Help*, 1, Skill, Common, draw 2 if you have any Spark, otherwise draw 1, **bank untouched** |

Both of the twin's numbers, 1 and 2, are unmoved. Two things differ and they
are one idea: the bonus now **costs** the Sparks it reads, and the bar moves
from "any Spark at all" to **the free Attack's own threshold**, so that the
bar and the price are the same fact and the card competes with the rule
directly.

`eager_to_help` is the purest "watch it rise" card on the sheet — it looks at
the bank, takes nothing, and pays more for the bank existing. This arm asks
whether the same bonus is more interesting when it is bought.

**Doctrine it claims to follow, quoted.** **D2** — *conversion*, and
*acquisition* on the card the extra draw buys. **D3** — *"a resource spent"*,
and *"a future draw or deck cost"* read the other way. **D5**: *"Any added
line of text must alter a decision."* — the added clause is a price, which is
the only kind of line that always does.

### Arm 3 — two prices for one card

| | |
|---|---|
| id | `proto_spark_mode_bombs` |
| name | **Bag of Tricks** |
| cost | 0, Skill, Uncommon |
| prints | *Choose one: Place 1 Bomb dealing 5 \| Spend 3 **Sparks**: Place 3 Bombs dealing 5.* |
| shipped twin | `pop` — *Pop!*, 0, Skill, basic, place 1 Bomb dealing 5 |

The cheap mode is the shipped twin printed alone, exactly — so the pair asks
what the second mode is worth and nothing else. The expensive mode is
`bomb_voyage`'s body verbatim (3 Bombs dealing 5), which ships at **2 energy**;
here the bank buys those two energy instead. Every number on the face is
lifted off a shipped face; nothing is picked.

**What the bank buys that it has never bought before: placement.** D2 lists
placement as a steerable verb, Klee is the only character in the roster who
has one, and Sparks have never touched it. This is also the arm that most
directly changes the *shape* of a turn rather than its size: three Bombs are
delayed damage on a board the player has to survive to collect, so taking the
expensive mode is a decision about the next turn, not this one.

**Doctrine it claims to follow, quoted.** **D2** — *placement*. **D3** —
*"mutually exclusive outcomes"*, named in the clause as a legal binding price,
and *"a resource spent"*. **D8**: *"Archetypes differ in how turns and drafts
unfold … the route, cadence, constraint, targeting, transformation, control or
economy must differ."* — the bank crosses from the Spark plan into the
Demolition plan, which is a route change, and `powder_charge` is the only
existing bridge in that direction.

### Arm 4 — the bank leaves the Attack economy altogether

| | |
|---|---|
| id | `proto_spark_burst_conversion` |
| name | **Slow Burn** |
| cost | 1, Skill, Uncommon, `skill_tag` |
| prints | *Spend 3 **Sparks**. Gain 5 Block. Gain 10 Burst Energy.* |
| shipped twin | `combustion_study` — *Combustion Study*, 1, Skill, Common, `skill_tag`, Block 5 and **3 Burst Energy**, bank untouched |

Cost, type, tag and the Block figure are unmoved. The Burst rider stops being
free and starts being bought, and it pays more for being bought.

Klee has three meters — Sparks, Bombs, Burst — and **Sparks and Burst have
never interacted in either direction.** Sparks buy Attacks; Burst fills from
Skills and reactions and casts the kit Burst. This arm is the only shape in
the slice where the bank buys something that is not damage, not defence and
not a Bomb: it buys *progress toward the one Klee payoff that is not an
Attack at all*.

**Doctrine it claims to follow, quoted.** **D2** — *conversion*. **D3** —
*"a resource spent"*. **D8** — *"the route, cadence, constraint, targeting,
transformation, control or economy must differ"*; this is economy. LAW, Klee:
*"The Burst (Sparks 'n' Splash) is kit, not loot: never draftable, granted to
hand on meter fill, casting empties the whole meter"* — untouched. This card
moves the meter; it does not grant, alter or draft the Burst.

### The one price, and the one number that is not a shipped number

Every arm that charges Sparks charges **three**. That is an attribution call,
not a balance one: four arms differing in shape *and* in price would leave
nothing about a turn attributable to the shape. Three is also **lifted, not
picked** — it is the free Attack's own threshold (`klee-character-design.md`
§3, "at **3 Sparks**, next Attack costs 0"), and E2's question is precisely
whether the bank is worth more as something other than that. A price of 2 —
the three shipped sinks' price — would have been a trade against True Spark
Knight's reduced threshold rather than against the rule under test. Charging
exactly the rule's price is what makes each pair a clean question.

Every other number on every face is lifted off a shipped face. **The one
exception is arm 4's `10`, and it is derived rather than picked**: the Burst
meter is 40 and a `skill_tag` card pays 5 (klee §3), so 3 Sparks buying 10 is
exactly **two skill-plays of Burst progress** — a rate, not a taste. It is
used once.

### What the engine would have to grow

Almost nothing, which is the point of standing on W3's work.

- **`spend_spark`** — built, shipped, printed by three Uncommons. No change.
- **`spark_at_least_three`** — arm 2 needs a Spark threshold predicate, and
  the sheet has only `has_spark` (`sparks > 0`). The parameterised family
  already exists and already contains the exact sibling: `charge_at_least_`,
  `fanfare_at_least_`, `encore_at_least_`. A `spark_at_least_` prefix on that
  same rail is the smallest possible addition, and it is quarantined the way
  `spend_charge` was — no shipped row uses it, so every drafted number in the
  world is byte-identical with and without this branch.
- **`choose_one`** — arm 3 uses the shipped modal grammar (`ModalChoice`,
  fixed and proven live at EB-150). No change.
- **Everything else** — `damage`, `draw`, `block`, `place_bomb`,
  `burst_energy` — shipped ops, unchanged.

**One known gap, disclosed rather than buried, and it is arm 3's.** The
choose-a-card screen has **no per-mode playability** (found by Kokomi slice 2
and filed there). Arm 3's expensive mode is offered whatever the bank holds.
It does not pay out on a short bank — the emitted body returns and the sim
raises the same refusal at the same point — but the face cannot grey the mode
out. On these boards the bank is set so it never fires. As a shipped shape it
would be a D4 defect, and arm 3's acceptance would inherit that defect as a
prerequisite.

## 3. The names, and what they lost to

Provisional names are mine under R212's ladder (R179), and these are all
provisional — they are deleted with the rows when the slice closes.

**Second Helping. Rummage. Bag of Tricks. Slow Burn.** Ordinary card names,
Mondstadt- and Klee-flavoured (she is a child with a satchel and an appetite),
and none of them says spend, threshold, mode, price, conversion or prototype.
The reason is Kokomi slice 1's and has not changed: a blind QA grader reads
printed card titles, and a title that names the experiment tells the grader
which arm they are holding, which is the one thing a blind grade cannot
survive.

Two collisions were checked and both bit. **"Bombs Away"** was the first
choice for arm 3 and already appears in `docs/klee-cards.yaml`. **"Powder
Keg"** was the second and sits one word from the shipped `powder_charge`
(*Powder Charge*), which is one of the three sinks this slice is standing
beside — two Spark-spending cards a syllable apart would be a legibility
defect of our own making. `docs/reserved-card-names.txt` and all three card
sheets plus `mondstadt-companions.yaml` were grepped for the four names that
survived; none appears anywhere.

The **turn ids** (`klee-slice1-t01` … ) are opaque for the same reason — they
are printed into the packet. The filenames name the arm, because only the
tooling and the packet-writer read filenames.
`understudy/turns/klee-slice-1/MANIFEST.md` is the map.
