### 1.3 GPT's proposal (summarised faithfully; the full text is [USER]'s)

A hierarchy: **"Salon and Companions are Furina's cast. Spotlight directs
the cast. Encore pays for and protects the performance. Successful
performances create Fanfare, which unlocks the finale."**

1. Salon members are the *resident troupe*; Companions are the *guest cast*.
   Both hang off the same performance hooks.
2. Remove Center Stage. Spotlight chooses **Salon** (the front member's next
   performance is Featured) or **Guest Cast** (the first Companion played is
   Featured).
3. Spotlight is a **persistent state**, not a generated selector card:
   chosen at combat start, and moving it costs Encore.
4. Encore is the immediate tactical resource, shown beside HP. Furina cards
   spend it to move Spotlight, command another performance, repeat the
   Featured performance, or add a rider.
5. Fanfare **replaces** the separate 70-point Burst meter. Spotlighted
   performances generate it; full = Burst; casting empties it. The Burst is
   a qualitative **"Grand Performance"** (e.g. both lanes Featured for a
   round), not "+1 per X".

Archetypes stay three: Resident Troupe / Guest Cast / Conductor. The starter
kit teaches the whole sentence — one deterministic Salon deployment, one
generated Companion, one Encore generator, one cheap Encore spend, and the
relic supplies the persistent Spotlight interface. Prototype the starter,
two members and a few Companions first. The decisive test: *"Did the player
plan who would receive Spotlight, and did spending Encore on that
performance feel meaningfully different from keeping it for defense?"*

### 3.1 LAW amendment 1 — replaces LAW.md:74-75

Current text:

```
- **Spotlight/empowerment boosts numbers only, never turn-economy effects.**
  (principles §2.2a, v1.10)
```

Replacement (GPT's wording, adopted verbatim):

```
- **Spotlight is a designation, not a universal numeric multiplier.** A
  Featured performance may generate Furina's Burst resource and receive
  effects explicitly authored by Furina's kit. Spotlight alone may not
  duplicate a card, reduce its cost, add draw, or amplify nonnumeric
  Companion text. Explicit bounded Furina cards and her Burst may create
  additional Featured performances. (principles §2.2a, v1.10; R213 E4)
```

Note what the new text is *stricter* about and what it relaxes. It relaxes
"numbers only" — a Featured performance may now receive a Furina-authored
non-numeric rider. It tightens everything else: the four turn-economy verbs
the old guard banned (duplicate, cheapen, draw, amplify companion text) are
now banned **from the designation itself** rather than from empowerment
generally, and they remain reachable only through a printed Furina card.
That is why the old guard cannot simply be kept beside it: "boosts numbers
only" and "receives effects explicitly authored by Furina's kit" contradict
each other, and the second is the one E4 needs.

## 4. Architecture

### 4.1 The shared performance hook

**The event.** One new engine event, `performance`, with a payload of
`(lane, source_card, actor)`. It fires in exactly two places, and GPT's step
2 builds exactly these two:

- **Salon lane** — fires once when a Salon member resolves its slot act at
  the start of Furina's turn. Today that path is the tick in
  `SALON_MEMBERS` (`tier0/constants.py:301-310`), priced at
  `SALON_TICK_ENCORE_COST = 1` per member (`:316`) and paying
  `SALON_TICK_BURST = 2` (`:319`). The hook wraps the existing tick; it does
  not replace it.
- **Guest Cast lane** — fires once when a Companion card resolves. That is
  the same predicate the selector already uses,
  `c.is_companion and not c.kit_card` (`tier0/engine/effects.py:1808`).

Everything else in E4 hangs off this one event. That is the point of doing
it first: until both lanes emit the same event, "choose a lane" is a choice
between one implemented thing and one imagined thing, which is GPT's
Constraint 3.

**What "Featured" does, per lane.** A `performance` event whose `lane`
matches the live Spotlight designation is **Featured**. Featured is a
per-performance property, bounded once per turn by §3.2.

**Which body is Featured, exactly.** The lane names a half of the cast, and
each half resolves to one body by a rule that already ships:

- **Salon lane → the FRONT member**, `p.salon[0]`, the head of the FIFO
  queue. That is the same position `salon_bow` takes, the same position a
  deploy landing on a full stage displaces, and the same position the
  `leftmost_salon_member_*` predicates read
  (`tier0/engine/effects.py:1928-1949`). With `SALON_MEMBER_SLOTS = 3`
  (`tier0/constants.py:313`) up to three members act each turn; exactly one
  of them — the front one — is Featured, and the other two tick normally.
- **Guest Cast lane → the FIRST Companion played this turn.** Same
  once-per-turn bound, and it is the wording GPT's proposal already uses.

**This is what finally gives `change_the_bill` a reader.** The card rotates
the front member to the back (`docs/furina-cards.yaml:472-473`,
`{op: salon_rotate}` + `{op: salon_perform}`), and the richness brief's own
standing complaint about it is that nothing cares:
"No card anywhere on her sheet cares which member is at the front. So 'why
would I play this' is the correct reaction, and it is a hole we already know
about" (`review/active/richness-playtest-brief-2026-08-26.md:100-105`).
Under E4, which member is at the front decides which act gets Featured, and
the rotate becomes the Salon lane's aiming verb. That is a hole E4 closes
without adding a card, and it is evidence for the architecture rather than a
convenience of it.

**Salon lane, Featured — my reading:** *the front member performs its BOW
act in place of its tick, and is not displaced.* The numbers already exist,
ratified, per member (`tier0/constants.py:301-310`, ratified R187):

| member | tick (today) | bow (today) |
|---|---|---|
| crabaletta | 6 damage | 14 damage |
| usher | 3 Block | 9 Block |
| chevalmarin | 2 damage + Hydro | Hydro to ALL + 3 Encore |

So Featuring the Salon lane is a real, legible, *already-priced* upgrade —
roughly 2.3× on Crabaletta, 3× on the Usher, and a categorical change on
Chevalmarin (single-target aura → all-enemy aura plus Encore back). It
invents no breakpoint: every number is lifted off a shipped face, per the
doctrine-seat rule.

**Alternatives, if the seat or [USER] returns it:**
(a) the Featured member acts **twice** (tick, then tick) — simpler, but it is
"+1 per X" wearing a costume and it doubles Chevalmarin's aura, which is a
turn-economy effect in aura clothing;
(b) the Featured member's act gains the Focus bonus at a better rate
(`SALON_FOCUS_PER = 10`, `:311`) — but that is a numeric multiplier, exactly
what §3.1 retires;
(c) the Featured member's act is free of upkeep (`SALON_TICK_ENCORE_COST`
waived) — cheap and legible, but it *reduces* the Encore tension the slice
is trying to create.

**Guest Cast lane, Featured — my reading:** *a Featured Companion play mints
Fanfare and receives a Furina-authored rider printed on the card that
Featured it.* The rider is not the +50% (retired, §3) and is not a fixed
engine constant; it is whatever the Furina card that set or moved the light
printed. The default rider for the prototype, again lifted off a shipped
face, is Chevalmarin's bow rider: **apply Hydro** — which is Furina's
skill-grade cadence problem solved through the cast rather than through her
own tags.

**Alternatives:**
(a) the Featured Companion is played **twice** — explicitly banned by §3.1
("may not duplicate a card");
(b) the Featured Companion costs 1 less — banned by §3.1;
(c) the rider is a fixed engine-level buff (Weak, Vulnerable) rather than
card-printed — legal, but it re-centralises the thing E4 is decentralising,
and D4 wants the consequence readable off the card at the decision point.

**Why these two outcomes are "meaningfully different"** (GPT's test): the
Salon-Featured outcome is a *magnitude* change to a body already on the
board and already paid for; the Guest-Featured outcome is a *cadence and
application* change to a body you are choosing to play this turn. One
rewards having built a stage; the other rewards holding a card. They are not
two ways to produce meter.

### 4.2 Persistent Spotlight

**Combat-start choice.** The lane is chosen once, at combat start, through
machinery that already ships on both sides:

- **Mod (C#):** `CardSelectCmd.FromChooseACardScreen`, which
  `EtherealSpotlight.OnPlay` already drives
  (`klee-mod/KleeCode/Cards/Furina/SpotlightCards.cs:52-70`), with the two
  option models `CenterStageOption` / `GuestCastOption` (`:77`, `:109`)
  re-labelled to the two lanes. Note the comment at `:44-51`: the options
  must be combat-scoped owned instances created through
  `combatState.CreateCard`, or the screen softlocks. That trap is already
  documented and already solved; E4 must not re-derive it.
- **Sim (tier0):** the modal chooser seam, `_chosen_mode` at
  `tier0/engine/effects.py:2571`, the surface R192's Deep Breath prototype
  established and R194 ruled. It is **the only shipped modal card**
  (`effects.py:2558-2563`) and the module says so as a discipline, not an
  accident. E4 is the second use, and the reason it qualifies is the reason
  named there: the pilot must be able to distinguish the modes. Under E4 it
  can — the two Featured outcomes are different ops, not different sizes of
  one op.

Two consequences worth stating out loud. First, the seam's switch is a
`POLICY_VERSION` event when flipped on (`effects.py:2580-2591`), so a lane
choice the pilot actually *makes* renumbers every tier0.5 arm; behind the
E4 flag it stays at the staged fixed index, which is index 0 and is the
degenerate case of the policy. Second, the combat-start choice is a screen
before the first draw, which is a place no Furina screen exists today; it is
the one genuinely new UI surface in the whole architecture.

**Encore-priced moves.** Moving the light mid-combat is a card, and the price
rides the field that already exists: `encore_cost`, a **playability gate,
never an overdraw** — `tier0/engine/combat.py:168` refuses the play when
`state.player.encore < card.encore_cost`, and `combat.py:315-320` spends it
on resolution. `tools/gen_prototype_cards.py`'s field whitelist already
knows `encore_cost` is load-bearing and not an effect
(`docs/current/OPERATIONS.md`: "load-bearing: `encore_cost` changes
playability without being an effect"). So an Encore-priced Spotlight move is
`{op: spotlight_designate, ...}` on a card carrying `encore_cost: N` — no
new engine primitive at all.

**Click-on-rail, and what it would need.** Letting the player re-aim the
light by clicking the stage rail is the interaction [USER]'s "status bar"
language points at, and it is an **unverified interaction**: nothing in the
mod today accepts a click on a VFX node as a game action. It would need
(i) a hit-testable node on the rail — `SalonVisualsBridge` builds
`Node2D` displays with `RemoteTransform2D` tracking, not input-consuming
controls; (ii) a command path from that click into `SpotlightSystem.Designate`
that survives co-op ownership checks; (iii) an answer to how the blind-play
harness drives it, since `understudy/blindplay` resolves **printed names**
only and a rail click has no printed name. That third one is not a nicety:
an interaction the harness cannot express cannot be graded by the funnel, and
would silently exempt itself from the process R213 built. **Attempt it
second**, after the card-priced move has a verdict.

### 4.3 Fanfare becomes the Burst meter

**The two legs, and their rates.**

| leg | fires | rate | bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 — a Featured performance resolves | on the `performance` event when `lane == spotlight` | **2** | once per turn |
| L2 — Encore deliberately spent through a Furina `Direct` effect | on `encore_cost` spend, and on `spend_encore` from a Furina card that commands a performance | **2** | once per turn |

Both rates are **2**, lifted off the shipped face
`FANFARE_PER_SPOTLIGHT_CARD = 2` (`tier0/constants.py:199`) — the existing
"a directed play pays the meter" rate. No breakpoint is invented.

L2 is deliberately narrow: `encore_cost` (the gate) and a Furina card's
explicit `spend_encore`, **not** `spend_encore_or_hp`'s overdraw half and
**not** Salon upkeep, which is automatic and therefore not direction. Per
GPT: "Fanfare evidence that the player directed the cast, not a general
activity odometer."

**The meter size is DERIVED, not picked.** Under R212 a derived-not-picked
number with a one-way error direction, an archive scope and one constant is
Claude's to ship, disclosed. Here is the arithmetic.

*Step 1 — what cadence ships today.* Income per player turn under today's
constants, for a mid-fight Furina with two members on stage:

| source | arithmetic | per turn |
|---|---|---|
| Salon ticks | `SALON_TICK_BURST` 2 × 2 members | 4 |
| Skill tags | `BURST_PER_SKILL_TAG` 5 × ~1 skill-tagged play | 5 |
| Encore spend | `BURST_PER_ENCORE_SPENT` 1 × upkeep `SALON_TICK_ENCORE_COST` 1 × 2 members | 2 |
| Reactions | `BURST_PER_REACTION` 5, intermittent | 0 (floor) – 2.5 (one every other turn) |
| **total** | | **11 – 13.5** |

Against `burst_max: 70` (`tier0/content/characters/furina.yaml:14`) that is
**70 / 11 ≈ 6.4 turns** at the floor and **70 / 13.5 ≈ 5.2 turns** with
occasional reactions. Call the shipped cadence **one Burst per ≈5–6 player
turns**. (Assumption named: two members on stage. R177's finding — "half of
all upkeeps already arrive unable to fund a member" — pushes the true
Salon-tick term *below* 4, so this estimate is if anything fast, and the
error direction is stated below.)

*Step 2 — what the new economy pays.* Exactly two legs, each bounded once
per turn, each paying 2: **4 per turn**, hard ceiling, no variance from
skill tags or reaction luck.

*Step 3 — the meter that preserves the cadence.* 4 per turn × 5 turns =
**20**.

*Step 4 — the sanity check against precedent.* **20 is Kokomi's shipped
meter** (`tier0/content/characters/kokomi.yaml:25`, `burst_max: 20`). So the
derived value is a size the roster already ships and the C# already renders,
not a new breakpoint. Klee ships 40 (`klee.yaml:11`) and Furina 70; 20 is the
low end of the existing range, which is correct for a meter with two bounded
legs instead of four unbounded ones.

**DERIVED: `burst_max: 70 → 20` for Furina, one constant, archive scope =
every Furina Burst number.** *Error direction, one-way:* if 20 is wrong it is
wrong **slow** — a player landing only one leg per turn fills in 10 turns
rather than 5, which under-delivers the Grand Performance rather than
over-delivering it. A meter that arrives too rarely reads as "the finale
never came" in a blind-play record and is caught by the funnel; a meter that
arrives too often reads as "nothing else mattered" and would corrupt the
slice's own question. The safe error is the one the instrument sees.

**Cast empties it.** Unchanged: `tier0/engine/combat.py:380`,
`p.burst_energy = 0`, and the LAW clause at 176-178. Overflow lost at cast,
re-granted on refill, carries Retain. E4 changes what fills the meter and
nothing about what emptying means.

**Grand Performance.** The Burst is not a number. It is: **both lanes are
Featured for one round.** The Salon lane's front member bows in place *and*
the next Companion played is Featured, simultaneously, for the round.

That state already exists in shipped C#. The Curtain Never Falls prints it
(`UpgradedStarterRelics.cs:399-405`): "Center Stage and Guest Cast are both
always active." E4 takes the relic's both-halves-live state and makes it the
Burst's payload — one round of it, on demand, instead of permanently and for
free.

**What The Curtain Never Falls becomes.** Under E4 its current text *is* the
Burst, permanently, which is not a relic upgrade — it is a deletion of the
character's finale. It has to become something else, and that is a design
pick, not a derivation. See §9 **P3**.

**What happens to the ~31 Fanfare-reading cards.** See §7.

### 4.4 Encore display

**Count, not a fill bar** — Encore is uncapped (`tier0/constants.py:165`) so
a bar has no denominator, and the shipped runway exists precisely because
someone already discovered that (`SalonVisualsBridge.cs:572-578`: "the old
denominator was a display-only 20 -- a lie twice over").

**The shape:** an integer beside HP, with **pips** for the low counts where
"how many more upkeeps can I pay" is actually a decision — one pip per point
up to a small ceiling, then the bare number. This is the Regent's-stars
reading of [USER]'s note: stars are a *count* of discrete things, not a
fraction of a maximum.

**The slot.** The centered-overhead creature-space slot is the
cross-character **Burst** indicator by LAW ("gauge skins are unique per
character"), and under E4 the merged meter is what renders there — so
Encore cannot go overhead. It goes on the **HP row**, beside the HP number
and Block shield, which is where the player already looks for "what stands
between me and damage" and is exactly what Encore is
(`resources.absorb_into_encore`, after Block and before HP, LAW:192-194).

**The runway becomes a tooltip.** The arithmetic
`SalonVisualsBridge.RefreshRibbon` already computes —
`segmentCost = members × TickEncoreCost`, `turns = encore / segmentCost`
(`:589-595`) — is the right *answer* in the wrong *place*: it is a
second-order derived quantity displayed as the primary one. It moves to the
hover tooltip on the Encore count: "N turns of upkeep at the current stage."
Whether the ribbon survives beside it is §9 **P4**.

## 6. The testable slice

GPT's six-step order, with flags, tests and the pre-registered question.

### 6.1 Flags

The repo's prototype-flag convention is a **compile-time MSBuild property
plus an id prefix**: `dotnet build klee-mod/KleeCode -p:PrototypeCards=true`,
every row's id starting `proto_`, and the classes not compiled at all in a
release build (`docs/current/OPERATIONS.md`, "Prototype surface (`EB-147`)").
E4 follows it and adds one master switch and five sub-flags underneath:

| flag | step | scope | default |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FURINA_E4` | master | C# compile symbol, defined only under `-p:PrototypeCards=true`; sim-side module constant in the E4 module, not `constants.py` | OFF |
| `FURINA_E4_HOOK` | 2 | the `performance` event fires; nothing reads it | OFF |
| `FURINA_E4_LANE` | 3 | persistent lane, combat-start choice, `encore_cost` moves | OFF |
| `FURINA_E4_METER` | 4 | Fanfare and Burst are one meter; Grand Performance | OFF |
| `FURINA_E4_STARTER` | 5 | the §5.2 starter deltas | OFF |
| `FURINA_E4_MIGRATE` | 6 | the ~31 readers move to the merged meter | OFF |

**An honesty note that has to be in the packet, not discovered later.**
Staging a prototype *card row* bumps no stamp, because prototype rows never
enter tier0's card index (`OPERATIONS.md`). Steps 2–4 are **engine**
behaviour, not rows. A default-off flag in the engine bumps no stamp *while
it is off*; **turning any of them on is a `CONSTANTS_VERSION` event** and, for
step 3's modal seam, a `POLICY_VERSION` event as well
(`tier0/engine/effects.py:2580-2591`). Every measured number taken with a
flag on is taken in a new world and is not comparable to the standing
baseline. That is not a reason to avoid the flags; it is a reason to say so
before the first table is quoted.

### 6.2 The six steps

**Step 1 — write E4 and its doctrine gate.** *This packet, plus §10.* Test:
the seat answers FOLLOWS or REQUIRES_MODIFICATION per item and names the
clause. Proves: the architecture does not contradict the charter before
anything is built.

**Step 2 — the smallest shared performance hook.** `FURINA_E4_HOOK`. One
Salon action and one Companion play emit the same `performance` event.
Test: a scenario at the real game (the `EB-146` harness) where one Salon tick
and one Companion play both appear in the event log with the correct `lane`;
plus a tier0 unit test that the event fires exactly once per member act and
exactly once per Companion resolution. **Proves:** the two lanes are the same
kind of thing. Nothing is Featured yet and no number moves — this step is
deliberately invisible in play, which is what makes it safe to land first.

**Step 3 — persistent Spotlight.** `FURINA_E4_LANE`. Combat-start choice
through `FromChooseACardScreen`; the designation persists; a card with
`encore_cost: N` moves it. Test: a staged turn where the lane is set at
combat start and a second staged turn where moving it is affordable but
contested. **Proves:** [USER]'s "should not require playing a card every
single turn" is satisfied, and that a move is a real decision rather than a
formality. Falsified if the Encore price is never worth paying in any staged
board, which would mean the lane choice is de facto permanent.

**Step 4 — merge Fanfare with Burst; add Grand Performance.**
`FURINA_E4_METER`. Two legs at 2, meter 20, cast empties, Burst = both lanes
Featured for one round. Test: a scenario that fills the meter from the two
legs only and confirms no other event moves it (the negative test is the
important one — HP loss, upkeep and absorption must all read zero); a staged
turn on a full meter where the Grand Performance round is the decision.
**Proves:** the finale is a state the player steered into, and the meter is
evidence of direction rather than of activity.

**Step 5 — the starter-deck boards.** `FURINA_E4_STARTER`. The §5.2 deltas
staged as prototype rows. Test: the funnel — blind staged turns, the
four-question form, closeness per R213 F, two graders, replay of every graded
line. **Proves:** the starter teaches the sentence. This is the first step
whose output is a grade rather than an assertion.

**Step 6 — migrate nothing else until independent playthroughs answer the
D2 question.** `FURINA_E4_MIGRATE`, and it stays off until then. Test: none
yet, by construction.

### 6.3 The decisive question, as a prediction slate

Per `EXPERIMENTS.md`: **DRAFTED by Claude** from written design intent,
committed as its own commit labelled DRAFTED **before any run**, [USER]
countersigns in batch or vetoes within five days, grade goes in blind, and
every slot names the decision its outcomes would change.

The question, GPT's, unaltered: *"Did the player plan who would receive
Spotlight, and did spending Encore on that performance feel meaningfully
different from keeping it for defense?"* It splits into two gradeable slots.

**Slot 1 — direction.** *Predicted:* on the step-5 boards, a majority of
blind forms name the lane designation as a reason for the line taken, in
q1's own prose, unprompted. *Falsified if:* no form mentions the lane; **or**
forms mention it only as forced ("there was only one Companion"), which is
the §2.2 collapse rule surviving the redesign under a new name.
*Decision it changes:* falsification returns step 3 — the lane goes back to
being a per-turn card, or the combat-start choice acquires a mid-combat
contest it currently lacks.

**Slot 2 — the Encore tension.** *Predicted:* on a matched pair of boards
differing only in whether Encore is best spent commanding a Featured
performance or held as buffer, `closeness` SURVIVES on both — the gap between
the top two lines on the pilot's own EV surface does not show one line
overwhelmingly dominating (R213 F). *Falsified if:* closeness fails on
either board; **or** both graders take the same line on both boards, which
would mean the two uses of Encore are not actually in tension.
*Decision it changes:* falsification returns §4.3's L2 leg — either the
Direct-spend leg is not carrying its half of the meter, or the cheap spender
of §5.2 is priced wrong.

**Who grades:** the Codex seat, blind, per `EB-149`, two graders per turn.
The reason it is the seat and not fresh Claude is §8.

**Registered blind spot.** Every board in step 5 is staged, and a staged turn
cannot show whether a lane chosen at *combat start* still felt planned at
turn six. That is a whole-fight question, and it belongs to the automatic
post-ADVANCE gate — whole-fight blind play on a dev build, `EB-188`'s door.
The slate does not pretend to answer it.

## 7. Blast radius

**Tests.** 37 test files reference salon, 32 fanfare, 18 spotlight, across a
190-file suite; 16 / 10 / 6 of those pin the plan name as a quoted string
literal (`review/active/eb81-furina-remedy-options-2026-08-12.md:227-229`).
E4 touches all three names. The string-literal subset is the expensive half:
a rename or an archetype fold breaks them mechanically, not semantically.
One test dies outright rather than being updated —
`test_every_point_past_block_prints_exactly_one_fanfare`
(`tier0/constants.py:196`), which asserts the invariant §3 retires.

**The re-baseline.** Any landed step is the **whole twelve-arm table**, never
the three Furina rows — "a bump makes every arm of the table discontinuous —
for every character, not just Furina" (`…:225-227`). Steps 2–4 each carry a
`CONSTANTS_VERSION` bump when their flag flips on, and step 3 carries a
`POLICY_VERSION` bump as well if the modal seam is switched on. Under R207
that is one window per step **where attribution matters**, and here it does:
the whole question is which of the four mechanics the merge fixed.

**The ~31 Fanfare-reading cards.** 31 cards touch fanfare, 14 exclusively, 6
of those 14 at rare (`…:120`, `:128-129`). Twelve are tagged
`[fanfare, generic]` and are therefore offered to salon decks too (`:130-131`).

**Prototype default: behind the flag they read the merged meter unchanged.**
The reader grammar does not change — `bonus_formula: 1_per_4_fanfare` and
`if: fanfare_at_least_N` (`docs/furina-cards.yaml:20-21`) both take a
non-negative integer off the player, and the merged meter is a non-negative
integer on the player. Every reader compiles and every reader reads. What
changes is the **rate and the ceiling**: a meter that fills to 20 on two
bounded legs is a different distribution from one that decays 20% a turn and
caps at half of max HP, so a `fanfare_at_least_20` gate that used to open
sometimes may now open only at the finale. That is a real balance change and
it is exactly why migration is step 6 and not step 4: the readers are
*correct* under the merge and *mispriced* by it, and pricing them is a sheet
edit — a `CONSTANTS_VERSION` event under LAW's material-card-sheet-edit rule
— that should happen after the architecture has a verdict, not before.

**What retires with the merge.** `FANFARE_DECAY_FRACTION` and
`decay_fanfare`; `gain_fanfare_floor` / `raise_fanfare_cap` and the two
printed keywords; `FANFARE_CAP_FRACTION`; register lints `R6` and `L12`
(`tools/lint_furina_registers.py`), which exist only to police those
keywords. Nothing on any shipped sheet carries `raise_fanfare_cap` today
(`STATE.md`: "Furina's pool carries **zero** `raise_fanfare_cap` riders"), so
that half is already free.

**Co-op.** Both of Furina's ally-coupled mechanics are **retired by §3 item
6**, not deferred: partner-coupled Fanfare dies with the HP-loss leg, and
cross-player selector passing has no selector card to pass. Nothing replaces
them in E4, so after this she has no co-op-specific mechanic at all — a real
loss against the kickoff's Appendix A.4 lineage, stated plainly rather than
left to be discovered. It cannot be settled by a test either way: there is no
automated co-op backstop for anything needing a live `CombatState`
(`klee-mod/KleeTests/README.md`), so every co-op defect here would be
play-derived. Authoring a replacement is future design space and is
deliberately outside this packet.

## 8. Independence

The direction in §1.3 and §1.5 is **GPT-co-authored with [USER]**. The
specifics in §4–§6 — the two Featured outcomes, the derived meter, the flag
ladder, the slate — are **Claude-authored**. Under R217 C independence is by
model FAMILY, author against grader, and the roles are fixed at two: Claude
authors, GPT grades and reviews (`OPERATIONS.md`, "Doctrine seat protocol").
There is no third family and none is being added.

That splits this packet's independent reader in two, and the split is not
optional:

- **§3's E4 rule rows** — the ruling text, what it retires, the two LAW
  amendments. GPT co-authored the direction and wrote the §3.1 wording, so
  the seat cannot be the independent reader here: it would be reviewing its
  own text. **A fresh Claude session, with no part in this drafting, is the
  independent reader on the E4 rule rows.**
- **§4–§6's specifics** — Claude authored them, so **the Codex seat (GPT) is
  the independent grader there**, and it is the seat that runs the doctrine
  gate at §10 and grades the step-5 forms.

**This is a case-specific allocation, not a general rule.** "Fresh Opus
grades" is never written as the house rule and must not be inferred from
this packet: the house rule is R217 C's model-family separation, and it
happens to point at a fresh Claude here only because GPT authored the rows
in question. Where Claude authors, GPT grades — which is the ordinary case
and the case for everything the slice actually builds.

**`authored_by:` consequences.** Every prototype row this slice stages
