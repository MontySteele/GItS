### 1.3 GPT's proposal (summarised faithfully; the full text is [USER]'s)

A hierarchy: **"Salon and Companions are Furina's cast. Spotlight directs
the cast. Encore pays for and protects the performance. Successful
performances create Fanfare, which unlocks the finale."**

1. Salon members are the *resident troupe*; Companions are the *guest cast*.
   Both hang off the same performance hooks.
2. Remove Center Stage. Spotlight chooses **Salon** (the front member's next
   performance is Featured) or **Guest Cast** (the first Companion played is
   Featured).
3. Spotlight is a **persistent state**, not a generated selector card:
   chosen at combat start, and moving it costs Encore.
4. Encore is the immediate tactical resource, shown beside HP. Furina cards
   spend it to move Spotlight, command another performance, repeat the
   Featured performance, or add a rider.
5. Fanfare **replaces** the separate 70-point Burst meter. Spotlighted
   performances generate it; full = Burst; casting empties it. The Burst is
   a qualitative **"Grand Performance"** (e.g. both lanes Featured for a
   round), not "+1 per X".

Archetypes stay three: Resident Troupe / Guest Cast / Conductor. The starter
kit teaches the whole sentence — one deterministic Salon deployment, one
generated Companion, one Encore generator, one cheap Encore spend, and the
relic supplies the persistent Spotlight interface. Prototype the starter,
two members and a few Companions first. The decisive test: *"Did the player
plan who would receive Spotlight, and did spending Encore on that
performance feel meaningfully different from keeping it for defense?"*

## 3. E4 — ruling text, DRAFT for [USER]'s countersign

> **R2xx — E4: Furina's four mechanics become one sentence.**
>
> **The architecture.** Salon members and Companion cards are one cast.
> Spotlight designates which half of the cast is Featured. Encore pays for
> and protects the performance. **Only a successful Featured performance
> creates Fanfare. One natural and one Encore-commanded Featured performance
> may pay each turn.** Fanfare **is** Furina's Burst meter; a full meter
> grants her Burst, and the Burst is a Grand Performance rather than a
> number.
>
> Fanfare therefore has exactly one cause, not two. Encore creates
> additional Fanfare by successfully commanding another performance, never
> by leaving the counter: a command that finds no eligible performer pays
> nothing.
>
> **What this reopens.** R177's ruled direction — "rebalance the weak Furina
> plans… NOT option B1 (fold plans into salon)" — is **overturned on the
> fold**. E4 is B1, done deliberately and under R213's authority rather than
> as a balance move. R177's measured finding that the stage outruns its fuel
> **stands** and constrains §4.3. The kickoff's deliberate separation of
> Encore and Fanfare as two independent counters
> (`docs/current/characters/furina-kickoff-v0.1.md` §4, "Encore is Furina's
> only managed resource" / "Fanfare is a global pool") is **retired**: they
> are one loop with one meter.
>
> **What E4 explicitly RETIRES.** Each of these is named so it cannot ride
> into the new architecture unexamined:
>
> 1. **"All Encore loss is progress."** `FANFARE_PER_HP_LOST`,
>    `FANFARE_PER_ENCORE_ABSORBED` and Salon upkeep stop generating. The
>    design invariant "every point of damage past Block prints exactly 1
>    Fanfare" (LAW; `tier0/constants.py:196`) is retired with them, along
>    with the test that asserts it. **`FANFARE_PER_ENCORE_SPENT` retires as
>    an independent leg too**: under the single cause above, Encore spent
>    pays only through the performance it successfully commands, and a spend
>    that commands nothing pays nothing.
> 2. **"Spotlight means +50%."** `SPOTLIGHT_BASE_MULT = 1.5` is retired as
>    the meaning of a designation. A Featured performance receives what
>    Furina's kit explicitly authors for it and nothing by default.
> 3. **Center Stage as a mode.** The two-mode Spotlight (R41, LAW "Character
>    identity — Furina") is replaced by two lanes of one cast: **Salon** and
>    **Guest Cast**. Furina's own cards stop being a Spotlight target.
> 4. **The Ethereal selector card.** The starting relic stops adding a card
>    to hand each turn and instead supplies the persistent Spotlight
>    interface. "Spotlight should not require playing a card every single
>    turn" ([USER]) is satisfied structurally, not by making the card
>    cheaper.
> 5. **Fanfare decay, floor and cap machinery.** `FANFARE_DECAY_FRACTION`,
>    `gain_fanfare_floor`, `raise_fanfare_cap`, `FANFARE_CAP_FRACTION` and
>    the `Fanfare +X` / `Fanfare Cap +X` keyword pair are retired: a Burst
>    meter does not decay, has no floor, and its ceiling is the meter size.
>    Register lint `R6` and `L12`, which exist to police those keywords,
>    retire with them.
> 6. **The two co-op provisions that depend on the retired machinery.**
>    Cross-player selector passing (kickoff §3.1: "In co-op, the selector may
>    instead be passed to a teammate, who applies it to one of their own
>    cards") is retired, because there is no selector card to pass — the
>    lane is a persistent state on Furina. Partner-coupled Fanfare (kickoff
>    §4: "partner HP/Encore flux counts toward Fanfare") is retired, because
>    HP loss and Encore flux stop generating for Furina herself under item 1
>    and cannot survive as a partner-only leg. **Both are retired, not
>    deferred**, and neither is replaced by this packet. Furina's co-op
>    identity is thereby left with no ally-coupled mechanic at all, which is
>    a real loss against Appendix A.4's mandate and is called out as such;
>    what replaces it is future design space and is not E4's to author.
>

> **Two LAW amendments** (exact replacement text at §3.1 and §3.2 below).
>
> **What the animation contract keeps.** All three contracted binding points
> (LAW: "Visuals bind to funnels, never to values") survive E4 unchanged:
> **Salon = 3 slot-index-keyed slots, duplicates legal** — E4 moves no slot

### 3.1 LAW amendment 1 — replaces LAW.md:74-75

Current text:

```
- **Spotlight/empowerment boosts numbers only, never turn-economy effects.**
  (principles §2.2a, v1.10)
```

Replacement (GPT's wording, adopted verbatim):

```
- **Spotlight is a designation, not a universal numeric multiplier.** A
  Featured performance may generate Furina's Burst resource and receive
  effects explicitly authored by Furina's kit. Spotlight alone may not
  duplicate a card, reduce its cost, add draw, or amplify nonnumeric
  Companion text. Explicit bounded Furina cards and her Burst may create
  additional Featured performances. (principles §2.2a, v1.10; R213 E4)
```

Note what the new text is *stricter* about and what it relaxes. It relaxes
"numbers only" — a Featured performance may now receive a Furina-authored
non-numeric rider. It tightens everything else: the four turn-economy verbs
the old guard banned (duplicate, cheapen, draw, amplify companion text) are
now banned **from the designation itself** rather than from empowerment
generally, and they remain reachable only through a printed Furina card.
That is why the old guard cannot simply be kept beside it: "boosts numbers
only" and "receives effects explicitly authored by Furina's kit" contradict
each other, and the second is the one E4 needs.

### 3.2 LAW amendment 2 — Burst kit-scope, amends LAW.md:144-146

Current text:

```
- **Neutral action-energy / draw / thin are legal enabler-grade companion utility**
  (one-shot, Exhaust-gated, costed) — the pool must let any character draft a
  kit-gap fix. **Burst-meter (`burst_energy`) generation stays character-kit-scoped**
  and must never be cheaply repeatable from companions. (principles §4.7, §2.4)
```

Replacement — the first two sentences are unchanged; one clause is appended:

```
- **Neutral action-energy / draw / thin are legal enabler-grade companion utility**
  (one-shot, Exhaust-gated, costed) — the pool must let any character draft a
  kit-gap fix. **Burst-meter (`burst_energy`) generation stays character-kit-scoped**
  and must never be cheaply repeatable from companions. **Furina's meter is
  the narrow case (R213 E4): only a successful Featured performance generates
  it. One natural and one Encore-commanded Featured performance may pay each
  turn. A Companion card generates none by being played; a Companion Featured
  by Furina's kit generates it as a Featured performance. Encore spend, HP
  loss, automatic Salon upkeep, Encore absorption and Skill tags feed her
  meter not at all.** (principles §4.7, §2.4; R213 E4)
```

This is the clause GPT's Constraint 1 says resolves the companion Burst-law
question, and it resolves it in the direction the existing law already
points: the meter stays kit-scoped because the *kit* is what Features the
Companion, not the Companion.

**Why this wording and not the previous one.** The version this replaces said
the meter "is generated by exactly two things — a Featured performance
resolving, and Encore deliberately spent through a Furina card that commands
a performance". The doctrine seat's G2 verdict caught the contradiction
between that and the ruling's own prose; the first revision fixed it by
making both legs explicit, and the seat's return then closed it properly by
**collapsing the two legs semantically**. There is one cause. The
Encore-commanded leg is not a second source: it is the same Featured
performance, arrived at by paying Encore instead of by the turn's natural
cadence. The 2 + 2 per-turn ceiling is preserved exactly; what is removed is
the possibility of Encore paying the meter without a performance happening.
That makes the LAW sentence **literally true as written**, which is what G2
asked for.

**What is NOT amended.** The kit-Burst clause at LAW.md:176-178 — "never
draftable, granted to hand on meter fill, casting empties the whole meter
(overflow lost at cast), re-granted on refill, carries Retain" — is
unchanged and E4 obeys it: the merged meter is granted on fill and emptied
on cast, exactly as `tier0/engine/combat.py:156-170` and `:380` already do.

## 4. Architecture

### 4.1 The shared performance hook

**The event.** One new engine event, `performance`, with a payload of
`(lane, source_card, actor)`. It fires in exactly two places, and GPT's step
2 builds exactly these two:

- **Salon lane** — fires once when a Salon member resolves its slot act at
  the start of Furina's turn. Today that path is the tick in
  `SALON_MEMBERS` (`tier0/constants.py:301-310`), priced at
  `SALON_TICK_ENCORE_COST = 1` per member (`:316`) and paying
  `SALON_TICK_BURST = 2` (`:319`). The hook wraps the existing tick; it does
  not replace it.
- **Guest Cast lane** — fires once when a Companion card resolves. That is
  the same predicate the selector already uses,
  `c.is_companion and not c.kit_card` (`tier0/engine/effects.py:1808`).

Everything else in E4 hangs off this one event. That is the point of doing
it first: until both lanes emit the same event, "choose a lane" is a choice
between one implemented thing and one imagined thing, which is GPT's
Constraint 3.

**What "Featured" does, per lane.** A `performance` event whose `lane`
matches the live Spotlight designation is **Featured**. Featured is a
per-performance property, bounded once per turn by §3.2.

**Which body is Featured, exactly.** The lane names a half of the cast, and
each half resolves to one body by a rule that already ships:

- **Salon lane → the FRONT member**, `p.salon[0]`, index 0 of the single
  company order defined under "the clarification the rule needs" below. That
  is the same position `salon_bow` takes, the same position a deploy landing
  on a full stage displaces, and the same position the
  `leftmost_salon_member_*` predicates read
  (`tier0/engine/effects.py:1928-1949`). With `SALON_MEMBER_SLOTS = 3`
  (`tier0/constants.py:313`) up to three members act each turn; exactly one
  of them — the front one — is Featured, and the other two tick normally.
- **Guest Cast lane → the FIRST Companion played this turn.** Same
  once-per-turn bound, and it is the wording GPT's proposal already uses.

**This is what finally gives `change_the_bill` a reader.** The card rotates
the front member to the back (`docs/furina-cards.yaml:472-473`,
`{op: salon_rotate}` + `{op: salon_perform}`), and the richness brief's own
standing complaint about it is that nothing cares:
"No card anywhere on her sheet cares which member is at the front. So 'why
would I play this' is the correct reaction, and it is a hole we already know
about" (`review/active/richness-playtest-brief-2026-08-26.md:100-105`).
Under E4, which member is at the front decides which act gets Featured, and
the rotate becomes the Salon lane's aiming verb. That is a hole E4 closes
without adding a card, and it is evidence for the architecture rather than a
convenience of it.

**Salon lane, Featured — the seat's rule, adopted verbatim in substance:**
*the Featured front member performs its special act and **rotates to the
back**.* Three things travel with it and none of them is optional:

- **It is NOT a `final_bow` event.** The member stays on stage. The engine's
  `salon_final_bow` emit (`tier0/engine/effects.py:1404`) does not fire, and
  **bow-triggered Powers do not fire**: `salon_bow_block` and
  `salon_bow_encore` (Stagehands, Curtain Call B / R85,
  `effects.py:1392-1402`) read the departure, not the performance, and a
  member who did not depart has not paid their price.
- **The act bodies are TEMPORARY PROTOTYPE INPUTS, labelled UNPRICED in this
  cadence.** The seat's return is exact about why, and it is the reason this
  section was returned:

  > "'Bow in place' is not already priced. The current bow is explicitly a
  > final displaced payoff. It removes the oldest member and triggers every
  > bow-dependent Power. … Calling the existing numbers 'ratified' does not
  > make them transferable to a free, repeatable, once-per-turn action that
  > leaves the member in play. In particular: Crabaletta becomes a repeatable
  > 14-damage engine. Usher becomes repeatable 9 Block. Chevalmarin refunds 3
  > Encore repeatedly, potentially erasing the intended Encore tension."

  So the table below is **input, not price**. The R187 signing
  (`tier0/constants.py:280-292`) signed 6/14, 3/9 and 2/+3 **as a tick-and-
  displaced-bow pair**, and nothing in it transfers to a repeatable
  in-place act.

| member | tick (shipped) | bow body (shipped) | as a Featured act |
|---|---|---|---|
| crabaletta | 6 damage | 14 damage | **UNPRICED** in this cadence |
| usher | 3 Block | 9 Block | **UNPRICED** in this cadence |
| chevalmarin | 2 damage + Hydro | Hydro to ALL + 3 Encore | **UNPRICED**; the Encore refund is the specific hazard — a repeatable +3 erases the tension Slot 2 exists to grade |

Pricing them is step-4 work under the flag and its numbers are graded by the
funnel, not asserted here. The one thing the packet does assert is the
**shape**: the Featured act is categorically different from a tick, and it
costs the front slot.

**The clarification the rule needs and the return did not give (Claude's,
and it is a real fork).** Once a Featured performance rotates, "front" and
"oldest-by-deploy" **diverge**. `_deploy_salon_members`'s docstring says
deploying into a full stage "bows the OLDEST member OUT"
(`effects.py:1418-1425`) while its code pops `p.salon[0]`; today those are
the same thing only because nothing but `salon_rotate` ever reorders the
queue. Under E4 a rotation happens every turn the Salon lane is Featured, so
the two orders come apart on turn one and every positional reader has to be
told which one it reads.

**My reading: there is ONE order, and it is the rotation order.**

1. The Salon is a single ordered company. **FRONT = index 0** of that order.
2. The Featured act is performed by the front member, who then goes to the
   back.
3. **Deploying into a full stage bows out the FRONT** — so the rotation is
   also the player choosing who leaves next.
4. `change_the_bill`, `salon_bow`, `salon_perform` and every
   `leftmost_salon_member_*` predicate read **that same order**, unchanged.

This is not a new invariant — **it is the shipped one**, stated out loud.
`_op_salon_rotate`'s own docstring already says the rotate "buys exactly one
thing — which performer the FIFO end offers next, to `salon_bow`, to a deploy
landing on a full stage, and to the `leftmost_salon_member_*` reads"
(`effects.py:1935-1946`). "Oldest" in the deploy docstring is a description of
the un-rotated case, not a second key; there is no deploy-age field on the
queue and E4 does not add one. **This is therefore a documentation fix
inside E4's scope, not a rule change** — and it turns the Featured rotation
into a second copy of a verb the player already learned from
`change_the_bill`, which is the D4 argument for it.

*The alternative, and why it is worse:* keep displacement keyed to **deploy
age** and let the two orders diverge — add an age key, let the front be
whoever the rotations left there, and let deploys still evict the eldest.
That fails D4 ("at the decision point the player can perceive and forecast
the consequences that matter") on its face: the stage would render one
order while resolving another, so "who leaves when I deploy" becomes
unreadable off the board exactly when Featured rotations are churning it.
It also splits `salon_bow` from deploy displacement, which the shipped
`_op_salon_bow` docstring names as the whole reason it takes leftmost —
"the player already knows which member is next out, because the deploy rule
taught them". Two orders means the deploy rule teaches nothing.

*Against the animation contract:* **a re-render, not a contract change.**
The contracted point is "Salon = 3 slot-index-keyed slots (duplicates
legal)" — the slots are keyed by **index**, not by member identity, so
re-drawing which member occupies which index moves no slot, adds no slot and
adds no member-to-slot assumption. The shipped `salon_rotate` already does
precisely this and the bridge already renders it. No breach, and no
stop-work.

**Alternatives to the Featured act itself, if [USER] returns it:**
(a) the Featured member acts **twice** (tick, then tick) and does not rotate
— simpler, but it is "+1 per X" wearing a costume, it doubles Chevalmarin's
aura, and it costs the player nothing positional, so the lane stops being a
place where order matters;
(b) the Featured member's act gains the Focus bonus at a better rate
(`SALON_FOCUS_PER = 10`, `:311`) — a numeric multiplier, exactly what §3.1
retires;
(c) the Featured member's act is free of upkeep (`SALON_TICK_ENCORE_COST`
waived) — cheap and legible, but it *reduces* the Encore tension the slice
is trying to create;
(d) the Featured member performs its act and **is displaced** — i.e. the old
"bow" reading. This is the one the return rejected, and the rejection is
about repeatability: a payoff that removes its own source is priced as a
final payoff and cannot be the lane's every-turn body.

**Guest Cast lane, Featured — the seat's rule:** *a Featured Companion play
mints Fanfare and applies **Hydro**, and the rider has exactly one owner —
the **relic**.* The return is explicit that the rider needed an owner and
that the first prototype is not where that gets to be flexible:

> "The Guest rider needs one owner. … For the first prototype, make Hydro a
> fixed relic-authored Guest rider. Spotlight-movement cards should only
> move Spotlight."

So, for the prototype: **the rider is a fixed, relic-authored Hydro
application.** `ethereal_spotlight` — which under §5.2 already becomes the
persistent Spotlight interface — is the single place the Guest rider is
printed and the single place a reader looks for it. Hydro is lifted off a
shipped face (Chevalmarin's aura rider, `tier0/constants.py:308-310`) and is
Furina's skill-grade cadence problem solved through the cast rather than
through her own tags.

**Spotlight-movement cards only move Spotlight.** A lane-move card carries
`{op: spotlight_designate, ...}` and an `encore_cost`, and it prints no
rider. The packet's earlier proposal — *"the rider is whatever the Furina
card that set or moved the light printed"*, a per-moving-card payload — is
**recorded as later design space and is not built**. Its problem is that it
gives one effect several owners: the rider would depend on which card last
touched the light, which is a fact the board does not show at the moment the
Companion is played, and the slice would then be grading a rider-selection
minigame instead of the lane choice.

**Alternatives:**
(a) the Featured Companion is played **twice** — explicitly banned by §3.1
("may not duplicate a card");
(b) the Featured Companion costs 1 less — banned by §3.1;
(c) per-moving-card rider payloads — the deferred design space above; not the
first prototype, by the return.

**Why these two outcomes are "meaningfully different"** (GPT's test): the
Salon-Featured outcome is a *magnitude* change to a body already on the
board and already paid for; the Guest-Featured outcome is a *cadence and
application* change to a body you are choosing to play this turn. One
rewards having built a stage; the other rewards holding a card. They are not
two ways to produce meter.

### 4.2 Persistent Spotlight

**Combat-start choice.** The lane is chosen once, at combat start, through
machinery that already ships on both sides:

- **Mod (C#):** `CardSelectCmd.FromChooseACardScreen`, which
  `EtherealSpotlight.OnPlay` already drives
  (`klee-mod/KleeCode/Cards/Furina/SpotlightCards.cs:52-70`), with the two
  option models `CenterStageOption` / `GuestCastOption` (`:77`, `:109`)
  re-labelled to the two lanes. Note the comment at `:44-51`: the options
  must be combat-scoped owned instances created through
  `combatState.CreateCard`, or the screen softlocks. That trap is already
  documented and already solved; E4 must not re-derive it.
- **Sim (tier0):** the modal chooser seam, `_chosen_mode` at
  `tier0/engine/effects.py:2571`, the surface R192's Deep Breath prototype
  established and R194 ruled. It is **the only shipped modal card**
  (`effects.py:2558-2563`) and the module says so as a discipline, not an
  accident. E4 is the second use, and the reason it qualifies is the reason
  named there: the pilot must be able to distinguish the modes. Under E4 it
  can — the two Featured outcomes are different ops, not different sizes of
  one op.

Two consequences worth stating out loud. First, the seam's switch is a
`POLICY_VERSION` event when flipped on (`effects.py:2580-2591`), so a lane
choice the pilot actually *makes* renumbers every tier0.5 arm; behind the
E4 flag it stays at the staged fixed index, which is index 0 and is the
degenerate case of the policy. Second, the combat-start choice is a screen
before the first draw, which is a place no Furina screen exists today; it is
the one genuinely new UI surface in the whole architecture.

**Encore-priced moves.** Moving the light mid-combat is a card, and the price
rides the field that already exists: `encore_cost`, a **playability gate,
never an overdraw** — `tier0/engine/combat.py:168` refuses the play when
`state.player.encore < card.encore_cost`, and `combat.py:315-320` spends it
on resolution. `tools/gen_prototype_cards.py`'s field whitelist already
knows `encore_cost` is load-bearing and not an effect
(`docs/current/OPERATIONS.md`: "load-bearing: `encore_cost` changes
playability without being an effect"). So an Encore-priced Spotlight move is
`{op: spotlight_designate, ...}` on a card carrying `encore_cost: N` — no
new engine primitive at all.

**Click-on-rail, and what it would need.** Letting the player re-aim the
light by clicking the stage rail is the interaction [USER]'s "status bar"
language points at, and it is an **unverified interaction**: nothing in the
mod today accepts a click on a VFX node as a game action. It would need
(i) a hit-testable node on the rail — `SalonVisualsBridge` builds
`Node2D` displays with `RemoteTransform2D` tracking, not input-consuming
controls; (ii) a command path from that click into `SpotlightSystem.Designate`
that survives co-op ownership checks; (iii) an answer to how the blind-play
harness drives it, since `understudy/blindplay` resolves **printed names**
only and a rail click has no printed name. That third one is not a nicety:
an interaction the harness cannot express cannot be graded by the funnel, and
would silently exempt itself from the process R213 built. **Attempt it
second**, after the card-priced move has a verdict.

### 4.3 Fanfare becomes the Burst meter

**One cause, two payments per turn.**

| what pays | fires | rate | bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| a successful Featured performance | on the `performance` event when `lane == spotlight` and the performance actually resolves | **2** | the turn's **natural** Featured performance: once |
| a successful Featured performance **commanded by Encore** | the same event, on a performance a Furina card paid Encore to cause | **2** | the turn's **commanded** Featured performance: once |

Both rows are the **same event**. The table has two rows because the ceiling
is 2 + 2, not because there are two sources: the second row is what the first
row looks like when the player paid for it. The rate is **2**, lifted off the
shipped face `FANFARE_PER_SPOTLIGHT_CARD = 2` (`tier0/constants.py:199`) —
the existing "a directed play pays the meter" rate. No breakpoint is
invented.

**Encore never pays the counter directly.** A Furina card that spends Encore
to command a performance pays the meter *through the performance it caused*.
If the command finds no eligible performer — an empty stage, no Companion in
hand, the once-per-turn commanded bound already consumed — **it pays
nothing**, and that is the intended feel: the Encore is spent on the cast,
not on the gauge. `encore_cost` on a card that does not command a performance
(a lane move, for instance) mints nothing at all. Neither does
`spend_encore_or_hp`'s overdraw half, nor Salon upkeep, which is automatic
and therefore not direction.

This is the seat's collapse, and its whole point is legibility:

> "Only a successful Featured performance creates Fanfare. One natural and
> one Encore-commanded Featured performance may pay each turn. That preserves
> the intended 2 + 2 ceiling while giving Fanfare exactly one comprehensible
> cause. Encore creates additional Fanfare by successfully commanding another
> performance, not merely by leaving the counter."

**The meter size: `20 — declared prototype seed; cadence unresolved until
whole-fight play.`** That is the seat's label and it is the one the packet
carries. The arithmetic below is real and is kept, but it computes a
**ceiling, not expected income**, and the return says why that matters:

> "Twenty is a prototype seed, not a derived shipping value. The arithmetic
> uses the hard ceiling of both legs firing every turn. … The staged boards
> can test the Burst decision by starting near full. Only complete MCP
> combats can determine whether it arrives every five turns or every ten."

So 20 is **not** claimed as a derived-under-R212 shipping constant. It is the
seed a prototype starts from, and the number that replaces it — if one does —
comes out of whole-fight play, not out of this section. Here is the
arithmetic that produced the seed.

*Step 1 — what cadence ships today.* Income per player turn under today's
constants, for a mid-fight Furina with two members on stage:

| source | arithmetic | per turn |
|---|---|---|
| Salon ticks | `SALON_TICK_BURST` 2 × 2 members | 4 |
| Skill tags | `BURST_PER_SKILL_TAG` 5 × ~1 skill-tagged play | 5 |
| Encore spend | `BURST_PER_ENCORE_SPENT` 1 × upkeep `SALON_TICK_ENCORE_COST` 1 × 2 members | 2 |
| Reactions | `BURST_PER_REACTION` 5, intermittent | 0 (floor) – 2.5 (one every other turn) |
| **total** | | **11 – 13.5** |

Against `burst_max: 70` (`tier0/content/characters/furina.yaml:14`) that is
**70 / 11 ≈ 6.4 turns** at the floor and **70 / 13.5 ≈ 5.2 turns** with
occasional reactions. Call the shipped cadence **one Burst per ≈5–6 player
turns**. (Assumption named: two members on stage. R177's finding — "half of
all upkeeps already arrive unable to fund a member" — pushes the true
Salon-tick term *below* 4, so this estimate is if anything fast, and the
error direction is stated below.)

*Step 2 — what the new economy pays at its CEILING.* One cause, two payments
per turn, each paying 2: **4 per turn**, and that is the **hard ceiling** —
what a player collects on a turn where the natural Featured performance
resolves *and* a second one is successfully commanded. It is not expected
income. A turn with no eligible performer, no Companion in hand, or no Encore
to spend pays 2, or 0.

*Step 3 — the meter that preserves the cadence at that ceiling.* 4 per turn ×
5 turns = **20**.

*Step 4 — the sanity check against precedent.* **20 is Kokomi's shipped
meter** (`tier0/content/characters/kokomi.yaml:25`, `burst_max: 20`). So the
seed is a size the roster already ships and the C# already renders, not a new
breakpoint. Klee ships 40 (`klee.yaml:11`) and Furina 70; 20 is the low end
of the existing range, which is correct for a meter with one bounded cause
instead of four unbounded legs.

**PROTOTYPE SEED: `burst_max: 70 → 20` for Furina, behind
`FURINA_E4_METER`, one constant, archive scope = every Furina Burst number.**
*Error direction, one-way:* if 20 is wrong it is wrong **slow** — a player
landing only the natural performance fills in 10 turns rather than 5, which
under-delivers the Grand Performance rather than over-delivering it. A meter
that arrives too rarely reads as "the finale never came" in a blind-play
record and is caught by the funnel; a meter that arrives too often reads as
"nothing else mattered" and would corrupt the slice's own question. The safe
error is the one the instrument sees.

**Cadence is a whole-fight question and this packet does not answer it.**
Staged boards cannot: a staged turn has no turn one to have started from, so
"every five turns or every ten" is unanswerable on the surface step 5
measures. What the staged boards *can* do — and what they are for — is start
the meter **near full** and test the **Burst decision** itself: is spending
the Grand Performance round the interesting call, and against what. The
arrival rate is graded by the mandatory whole-fight gate at §6.2, and 20
moves or stays on that evidence.

**Cast empties it.** Unchanged: `tier0/engine/combat.py:380`,
`p.burst_energy = 0`, and the LAW clause at 176-178. Overflow lost at cast,
re-granted on refill, carries Retain. E4 changes what fills the meter and
nothing about what emptying means.

**Grand Performance — the seat's exact wording (P5 option 3), adopted:**

> **"Gain one Featured entitlement for each lane; each is consumed by the
> next eligible performance and expires at the end of Furina's next turn."**

Two entitlements, one per lane. Each is spent by the **next eligible
performance in its lane** — the Salon entitlement by the front member's next
act, the Guest entitlement by the next Companion played — and whatever is
unspent **expires at the end of Furina's next turn**.

**Why the timing had to be made exact.** The packet's earlier wording was
"both lanes are Featured for one round", and the return names the defect:
"'One round' is ambiguous because Salon acts before Furina can cast the
Burst." Salon members resolve at the **start** of Furina's turn, so a
Burst cast on the turn it fills arrives after the Salon lane has already
acted; "for one round" leaves it undecidable whether that lane's round was
the one just spent or the one to come. An **entitlement consumed by the next
eligible performance** has no such gap: the Salon half is simply held until
the members next act, which is the following turn's start, and both halves
expire together at the end of that turn.

The both-halves-live *state* already exists in shipped C# — The Curtain Never
Falls prints it (`UpgradedStarterRelics.cs:399-405`): "Center Stage and Guest
Cast are both always active." E4 takes that state and makes it the Burst's
payload: two entitlements, on demand, instead of permanently and for free.

**What The Curtain Never Falls becomes — retired under the flag (P3, the
seat's pick 4).** Under E4 its current text *is* the Grand Performance,
permanently, which is not a relic upgrade — it is a deletion of the
character's finale. The seat's pick, and the reason:

> "P3: 4 — retire the upgraded relic's current effect and author its
> replacement after the base loop passes. 'Opening Grand Performance' may
> waste the Salon half before a member is deployed."

That objection kills the packet's own earlier read (option 2, "each combat
opens with one round of Grand Performance") on the same timing fact the
entitlement wording fixes: at combat start the stage is **empty**, so the
Salon entitlement has no eligible performance to be consumed by and expires
having done nothing. So: **behind `FURINA_E4_METER` the relic's current
effect is retired, and its replacement is authored after the base loop
passes** — not in this packet, and not before there is a loop to build a
relic on top of. [USER] countersigns the retirement; the replacement is a
later authoring step with its own pick list.

**What happens to the ~31 Fanfare-reading cards.** See §7, and the triage plan at §7.1.

### 4.4 Encore display

**Count, not a fill bar** — Encore is uncapped (`tier0/constants.py:165`) so
a bar has no denominator, and the shipped runway exists precisely because
someone already discovered that (`SalonVisualsBridge.cs:572-578`: "the old
denominator was a display-only 20 -- a lie twice over").

**The shape:** an integer beside HP, with **pips** for the low counts where
"how many more upkeeps can I pay" is actually a decision — one pip per point
up to a small ceiling, then the bare number. This is the Regent's-stars
reading of [USER]'s note: stars are a *count* of discrete things, not a
fraction of a maximum.

**The slot.** The centered-overhead creature-space slot is the
cross-character **Burst** indicator by LAW ("gauge skins are unique per
character"), and under E4 the merged meter is what renders there — so
Encore cannot go overhead. It goes on the **HP row**, beside the HP number
and Block shield, which is where the player already looks for "what stands
between me and damage" and is exactly what Encore is
(`resources.absorb_into_encore`, after Block and before HP, LAW:192-194).

**The runway becomes a tooltip.** The arithmetic
`SalonVisualsBridge.RefreshRibbon` already computes —
`segmentCost = members × TickEncoreCost`, `turns = encore / segmentCost`
(`:589-595`) — is the right *answer* in the wrong *place*: it is a
second-order derived quantity displayed as the primary one. It moves to the
hover tooltip on the Encore count: "N turns of upkeep at the current stage."

**The ribbon retires as a primary display** — the seat's P4 pick 2: "count
and pips beside HP; runway in the tooltip; retire the second primary
display." Two primary displays of one resource is [USER]'s legibility
complaint in miniature, and the runway is the derived half, so it is the half
that becomes a hover. This is eyes-on taste and therefore still [USER]'s to
countersign (§9); it is recorded here as the shape the slice builds unless
[USER] says otherwise.

|---|---|---|---|

| `FURINA_E4_LANE` | 3 | persistent lane, combat-start choice, `encore_cost` moves | OFF |
| `FURINA_E4_METER` | 4 | Fanfare and Burst are one meter; Grand Performance | OFF |
| `FURINA_E4_STARTER` | 5 | the §5.2 starter deltas | OFF |

behaviour, not rows. A default-off flag in the engine bumps no stamp *while
it is off*; **turning any of them on is a `CONSTANTS_VERSION` event** and, for
step 3's modal seam, a `POLICY_VERSION` event as well
(`tier0/engine/effects.py:2580-2591`). Every measured number taken with a
flag on is taken in a new world and is not comparable to the standing
baseline. That is not a reason to avoid the flags; it is a reason to say so
before the first table is quoted.

### 12.3 Closure 1 — Grand Performance refill

**Closed:** Burst-granted Featured entitlements generate **no** Fanfare. Only
the turn's **natural** Featured performance and the turn's **Encore-commanded**
Featured performance pay the meter, at 2 each, exactly as §4.3's table already
bounds them. A Burst-granted entitlement causes a Featured performance; that
performance resolves, applies its lane's Featured outcome, and pays **0**.

**One performance, one entitlement.** A single performance consumes exactly
one entitlement and pays for exactly one. It can never be simultaneously the
natural performance and the commanded one, and it can never spend both lanes'
Burst entitlements.

**Order of consumption when several are live** — natural first, then
Encore-commanded, then Burst-granted:

1. the turn's **natural** entitlement (pays 2),
2. the turn's **Encore-commanded** entitlement (pays 2),
3. a **Burst-granted** entitlement (pays 0).

*Why that order and not the reverse.* The two paying entitlements are the
scarce ones: the natural one exists once a turn and the commanded one costs
Encore. The free Burst-granted one exists only inside the finale round. If the
free entitlement were consumed first, a Grand Performance round would **waste
the paying entitlements** — the player would spend Encore commanding a
performance the Burst had already paid for, and the round that is supposed to
be the payoff would silently be the round the meter stopped filling. Draining
the paying ones first means a Grand Performance round refills the next meter
at exactly the same rate as an ordinary round, and the extra performances are
pure surplus, which is what a finale should feel like.

**Reason, one line:** the finale must not be able to fund itself, and the free
entitlement must not be allowed to eat the paid one.

**Alternative, named and rejected:** Burst-granted entitlements pay. Rejected
because the finale would fund itself — two extra Featured performances at 2
each return 4 of the 20 the cast just spent, so the Burst becomes a partial
refund of its own cost, which is the "watch it rise until the number is large"
loop D2 rejects, wearing a payoff's costume.

### 12.4 Closure 2 — Guest Hydro resolution

**Closed, target:** the rider applies to **the enemy the Companion play is
aimed at** — literally `state.card_aim`, the single creature R210 binds at the
top of `resolve_card` before any op runs (`tier0/engine/effects.py:4190`; the
bind is `bind_card_aim`, `:454-470`; the read is `_pick_targets(state,
"enemy")`, `:535-553`). The rider is therefore spelled `{op: apply_aura,
element: hydro, target: enemy}` and needs **no new targeting primitive**: it
is the same aim every other aimed op on that Companion already uses, which is
the whole point of R210's one-aim-per-play rule.

**Closed, fallback — and a correction to the brief's own reading.** The brief
proposed falling back to "the enemy the Spotlight last recorded". **The
Spotlight records no enemy.** `_op_spotlight_designate`
(`tier0/engine/effects.py:1809-1841`) writes `p.spotlight` with a *category* —
`p.character_id` or `C.SPOTLIGHT_GUEST_CAST` — and emits
`spotlight_designated(character=…, mode=…)`. There is no creature anywhere in
the designation, so that fallback cannot be built. The fallback the engine
already knows how to express is the one `_pick_targets` writes down:

- **In tier0 there is no untargeted case at all.** `resolve_card` binds an aim
  on **every** play, targeted or not, before the first op. A Block-only
  Companion, a self-buff Companion and an `all_enemies` Companion all have a
  bound `card_aim`. `target: enemy` therefore resolves for every Companion the
  sim can play, and the only null is an **empty board** (`bind_card_aim`
  returns `None` when `living_enemies` is empty), on which the rider does
  nothing.
- **In the mod there is.** `CardPlay.Target` is `Creature?`, and the generated
  C# throws on null for aimed bodies
  (`klee-mod/KleeCode/Cards/Furina/Generated/ApplauseLine.cs:68`,
  `Breathless.cs:63`, and every other `ArgumentNullException.ThrowIfNull` row),
  so a non-targeted Companion genuinely has no aim on that side. **The stated
  fallback is therefore a mod-side rule**, and it is the branch tier0 already
  writes for "no card play in flight": **the lowest-HP living enemy**
  (`_pick_targets`, `:557-563` — `min(living, key=lambda e: e.hp)`, or
  `state.rng.choice(living)` when `force_random_targeting` is on). Not a fresh
  random roll: lowest-HP keeps both engines' rider on the same creature under
  the harness's own targeting identity, which is what makes a sim/mod
  divergence on this rider **detectable** instead of ambient.

  **That asymmetry is itself part of the contract:** step 2's parity test must
  include one non-targeted Featured Companion and assert both engines put the
  Hydro on the same creature. It is the exact place a rider that "obviously
  follows the card" would diverge silently.

**Closed, ordering:** Hydro lands **after** the Companion's own effects have
fully resolved. The rider is a follow-up appended at the end of the play,
never a pre-emption.

**Reason, one line:** the Companion's own element and any reaction it causes
resolve on the board the Companion's text describes, and only then does
Furina's rider add hers — so the rider can never change what a Companion card
does, only what happens next.

**Alternative, named and rejected:** Hydro lands **before**. Rejected on D4 —
"At the decision point the player can perceive and forecast the consequences
that matter". A pre-emptive Hydro would silently rewrite the Companion's own
printed outcome: a Pyro Companion's face says Overload against a dry enemy and
would instead read Vaporize; a Cryo Companion's face says Frozen and would
instead read Frozen-off-a-Furina-applied-Hydro. The card the player reads and
the card the player plays would stop being the same card. After-resolution is
also the only order that composes with `_element_for`'s standing rule that
"Companion cards are exempt from cadence entirely: what a companion applies is
the sheet's explicit call" (`effects.py:571-573`) — the sheet's explicit call
resolves first and keeps its meaning.
