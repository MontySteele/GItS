# Klee round seventeen: the kit's identity cards are blank until a second placer exists

Written 2026-09-05. The first round under the Prototype loop
(`operations/stage-gate.md`, *The loop inside Prototype*): one hypothesis,
an assembled deck and a natural run, the smallest intervention. Two blind
Opus seats on `0.2.2674+proto` (main `6e873961`, the three pool passes and
the round-16 fixes in), rows on `docs/prototype-surface.yaml`. Records:
`review/qa/klee-round-17-2026-09-05/opus-arm-act1.md` (lane 1, assembled)
and `opus-natural-act1.md` (lane 2, natural). Prototype stage, Guardrail-7.
This round was graded against the seat brief's amended rubric (decisions
judged across the fight and the draft); rounds 1 to 16 were not.

## 1. The hypothesis

"Klee can build around preserving one large Bomb, and that changes which
rewards she wants; the relic's one Spark per explosion pays Spray more
directly, and Cook's compensation (Grounded's quiet-turn Spark, Stoke the
Fuse's conversion) is enough for the plan to be worth choosing."

**Assembled deck** (lane 1, seed `P7B233U14YC5`, Ascension 0): Grounded,
Sparks 'n' Splash, Long Fuse, Fish Blasting and Stoke the Fuse added to the
starter. 117 of 120 actions, floor 12, six of six fights including the
four-Gardener elite, 22 of 62 HP. **Natural run** (lane 2, seed
`TSRRAR5Q4U3K`, Ascension 1): 114 of 120, floor 11, five of five including
the Skulking Colony elite, 9 of 62 HP.

## 2. What the round found

**The identity cards are blank without a placer, and the starter has one.**
The assembled seat held Stoke the Fuse unplayable in fights 1, 3 and 4 and
Sparks 'n' Splash in fights 1 and 5: "the kit's identity card is a blank
three fights out of six", because the deck as dealt had one Bomb source
(Jumpy Dumpty) in seventeen cards. The natural seat's first Klee decision
was fight 1 turn 3, "reachable only because both Bomb placers came from
the Neow bundle"; on the starting deck alone "the first Bomb card I saw
was Ka-pow!, a detonator with nothing to detonate." Turn one of fight one
presented no kit decision on either lane, the seventh round to say so.
Both seats say the problem vanished the moment a second placer was
drafted (Mine Toss, Dodoco Cover). This is an access finding, not a card
finding, and it is the pick (§5).

**The hypothesis, read.** Preserving one large Bomb was the winning plan
where it was possible: fight 3 of the assembled run ("the lethal on turn 3
was obvious because of the two cards I spent turn 2 on"), the natural
run's elite (Bomb 20 held under a 20-a-turn cap while a Retained Ka-pow!
waited as "a guaranteed detonator for a pile I could not otherwise be sure
of popping"), and Stoke the Fuse's one live play, six Sparks into +18. But
the Sparks that paid it came from Mines going off on the enemy's turn, not
from the cook, and the assembled seat named the relic's incentive as the
kit's open contradiction: "Grounded and the whole Bomb archetype are at
war", the Power that pays for the engine turns itself off when the engine
runs, and "the fix lives on a card you may never be offered" (Kaeya's
clause). The natural seat, playing Spray by draft, ended fight 2 with four
Sparks and no sink until floor 8. **Verdict: partly supported.** The Cook
plan is real and the seats reach for it; the relic pays it through Mines
rather than through holding, and Grounded is the card that says so.

**The rounds' best decisions, named by where they were made.** On the
turn: reaction target-and-order (Superconduct over Melt into a Plating
wall, Overloaded into three rats, "opposite answers, decided by what was
on the board"); "don't hit that enemy" twice against Skittish Gardeners and
once against Thorns, ending turns with nothing played and winning for it
("I have not seen another deckbuilder make pass the strong play this
legibly"). Earlier in the fight: the Bomb plant two turns before the
lethal. At the draft: Shinobu as the only second element on the screen,
Mine Toss over Dahlia, Fwoosh! and Dig In as Spark sinks noticed eight
floors late, and the Neow bundle (energy-priced over Spark-priced, "the
single most load-bearing decision of the act"). The rubric change did its
job: neither seat filed a planned lethal as a dead turn.

**What the pool pass displaced, first reading.** Long Fuse: "never a
decision", the rising cost reads as "play it now" every time, so its
Retain is "a lie told by the card frame"; the seat did not weigh that a
held turn buys +4 Bomb for +1 energy, which the face does not say. Fish
Blasting: "good immediately", played for the kill and the AoE, Confiscated
"did what it said". Rapid Fire (pre-pass): "never once earned 2 energy",
random targeting against a deck that loads one body; the card the seat
most wanted to remove. Run Away!'s rider "almost never turned on" because
Mines fire on the enemy's turn. Big Badda Boom under the elite's cap
"indistinguishable from two Strikes". None of these is a row; each is a
candidate for the smallest intervention in §4.

**Mines are the kit's best card by both seats' account.** One energy, every
enemy, no when-hit power, the enemy kills itself before it swings, and
against Skittish "hitting a gardener protects it from its own Mine", so
the right play was not to attack.

**Companions and reactions.** Assembled seat: six offers, took Shinobu and
Kaeya, and every reaction card it faced after that was priced with the
glossary's help; but Dahlia (Hydro) was passed twice "priced blind" because
the glossary defines a reaction only once the element is held. Natural
seat: five offers, took Albedo, declined Barbara, Charlotte and Gorou, "the
only second element I was ever offered", and played eleven floors without
a reaction while the reaction block was "the largest piece of text in the
game and I never used it once" (`EB-428`, cited a third time; the
reward-screen half of it is new and widens the row).

**Defects, three rows.** Enemy numbering re-counts mid-turn and a targeted
Melt hit the wrong body (`EB-496`). Explosive Ampoule dealt 10, not 15,
into a Vulnerable enemy while the glossary says every hit (`EB-497`).
Shinobu's face printed "Deal 8" at 29 of 62 and dealt 13, the below-half
clause unpreviewed where Weak and reactions are (`EB-498`). Not rows:
Plating's tick read 8, 8, 7 (the seat unsure of the round boundary);
Ravenous reads singular and both survivors ate (base-game text).

## 5. The pick

**Pick 1 (A): the starter's second placer.** Seven rounds have read turn
one as generic and this round's assembled deck shows the kit's payoff
cards blank without one. R242 ruled the four-four-two starter; the new
fact that reopens it is the assembled-deck read (Stoke the Fuse and Sparks
'n' Splash dead in five of twelve fights between them on the starter plus
themselves). Options:

1. **Pop! (0 energy: Place a Bomb 5) replaces one Strike** [default]: the
   starter keeps ten cards and two kit cards become three; turn one has a
   placer half the time instead of a quarter; nothing else moves.
2. Ka-pow! becomes a flex basic ("Place a Bomb 4, or Set off"): no card
   count moves, one basic gains a choice; a bigger rules change on the
   starter's only detonator.
3. Leave the starter; make Pop! and Mine Toss Neow-bundle guarantees or
   raise their offer weight in act 1: an access fix off the starter.
4. Leave everything: the first placer is the draft's first decision.
