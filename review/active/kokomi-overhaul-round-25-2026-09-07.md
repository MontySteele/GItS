# Kokomi round 25: two natural drafts without the cap; the enemy's printed intent is what decides whether writing everything is free or chosen

Written 2026-09-07, night. Two blind Opus seats, both natural drafts, on
`0.2.3018+proto` (main `0ab28596`: pool pass three, `EB-655`, and the cap
retired, R266). Budgets 240 acts and 9000 s, both lanes disposable. Records:
`review/qa/kokomi-round-25-2026-09-07/opus-lane1-natural.md` and
`opus-lane2-natural.md`. Prototype stage: nothing here is a measured number
(Guardrail 7), and no pick is asked.

## 1. The hypothesis and the question

From the plan: on a safe turn with three or more Plan-capable cards and no
cap, a seat that inspects the hand names at least one turn where it played a
face now instead of writing it; a seat that stops inspecting names none and
calls the turn free. The debrief asked each seat, verbatim: on the turns
where you wrote two or more Plans, did you inspect the hand and choose to
prepare everything, or recognise an off-turn and stop inspecting.

## 3. The reads

**1. The debrief.** Both seats: both behaviours happened, and both name the
same decider, the enemy's printed intent. Lane 1: "the thing that decided
which was almost always the enemy's printed intent"; it wrote everything on
the boss's no-damage Debuff turns (turns 5 and 11) and stopped inspecting on
a threshold (boss turn 4: four Plan-capable cards, zero written, because 15
immediate damage crossed Plow 150 and cancelled a 22), on an enemy Block
intent (fight 3 turn 2), and when a relic had already made every Block card
dead (fight 7 turn 2). Lane 2: it inspected and wrote all three on boss
turn 3 with Weak on itself and only 12 incoming ("my cards were cheap today
and expensive tomorrow", 35 damage the next morning), and it stopped
inspecting at fight 6 turn 3 "off a single number, my HP against the intent
total". Each named turn matches its fight section.

**2. Faces played now while a Plan line was open.** Many, and the reasons
are card-shaped rather than "I had to": Slack Water nine times across both
lanes because Weak has to land before the hit; Coral Bulwark five times
because the Block was for this turn; Kurage's Oath three times to kill a
body now; Flank twice, once into a live Electro aura, once for 14 on the
body that had to die; Opening Gambit once for an exact lethal. Feint is the
pass-three case working as written: lane 1 played it face-up four times
"because a Plan had already carried out that turn, so its immediate half was
already the 10-damage branch", and calls that "the nicest tension in the
kit"; lane 2 names it the card it was happiest to draw. Riptide against a
debuff did not come up. Read the Field's immediate half was never chosen in
lane 2's eleven fights: "10 Block for 1 energy beat 5 Block every time the
Block could arrive a turn late" (§5, watch item).

**3. All-written turns, free or chosen.** Lane 1 wrote every Plan-capable
card on four turns: two free (boss turns 5 and 11, no-damage intents, "the
only chosen part was the ordering"), two chosen (elite 1 turn 3, the
immediate Block worth nothing against a Status intent; fight 7 turn 1,
Weak-to-ALL planned so three Casket hits stripped three Slippery bodies in
one beat, "the best-feeling turn of the run"). Lane 2: one free (fight 6
turn 1, "I had noticed a free one"), three chosen (boss turn 3 under Weak,
fight 10 turn 1 under an escape clock, fight 11 turn 1 under a damage cap).
Lane 1's summary: "where the kit is at its best is not 'write everything',
it is 'write these two, in this order'." Lane 2's: free "where the board was
doing nothing to me", chosen where "something printed on the screen gave me
a reason to prefer tomorrow's energy to today's."

**4. Battle Plan:** not taken by either seat. **5. Nereid's:** lane 2 took
it at the boss reward and aimed the doubled first Plan on purpose both
times, writing Opening Gambit first so the doubled entry was the Vulnerable
and the rider. **6. Dusk:** not taken. **7. The cap sentence** is absent from
both records; no seat mentions a limit.

## 4. What this says about R266

The turn the cap was written against, every Plan in hand written at once,
still exists, and it is still common: both seats called it free on the
boss's no-damage turns, and lane 2 called fight 6 turn 1 free with 12
incoming that its Block covered. What the round shows is narrower than the
plan's hypothesis asked: under pressure the faces priced the choice by
themselves, card by card, and Feint made a prepared turn worth exploiting;
on a safe turn with three Plan-capable cards, nothing but Block competed
with writing another Plan in either record. So the cap stays retired, the
faces do the work where there is pressure, and the safe turn's competition
is the read the next lane is for: immediate draw, a filter or an attack
against one more Plan, kept separate from "I need Block now."
