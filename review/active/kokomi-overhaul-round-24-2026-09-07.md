## 1. The hypothesis

"With a deck that can write three or more Plans in a turn, the lane under
the cap names the cap as a choice, not a wall." The cap engaged four times
(4, 4, 3 and 3 written; 2 carried out each morning; the badge kept the
rest) and the seat named it as **both, in that order**: a wall for three
occurrences, because nothing printed it, and a choice once noticed ("it
changed my play twice: I stopped writing a fourth Plan, and I passed on
Battle Plan at the elite reward because I no longer trusted stacked Plans").

## 2. What the round found

**The cap is silent, and the panel says the opposite.** The Bake-Kurage
panel prints "holds any number of Plans and carries them out in the order
written; the number on the Plan badge is how many are written, not a
limit", and under the lane rule the jellyfish carried out exactly two a
morning and kept the rest: "No screen anywhere printed a cap, a reason, or
even an acknowledgement that something written had not fired." The seat's
own verdict: "either the cap is real and the panel's sentence is false, or
the panel is right and the carry-out is dropping Plans." The cap is real
(`EB-650`'s scenario proved it the same evening) and the sentence is false
under it. That is `EB-653`: the rule must print where it binds before it
can be read as a choice.

**Once seen, the cap did what R265's pick imagined.** Priority became a
decision: which two of four written Plans go first is the order written,
and the seat re-ordered on purpose after the third occurrence; the fourth
Plan stopped being written; a draft pick was refused on it. That is the
positive read the cap was put in a lane to find, and it arrived only after
the seat reverse-engineered the rule from the badge. A read against a
printed rule is still owed.

**The kit's standing decision held on this deck too.** "One big decision,
asked on every turn of every fight: play this card now, or write it on the
jellyfish for next turn", with the first turn of the first fight already
presenting it (a face-up and a planned version of the same card in hand
against a telegraphed 8 and a debuff). Well Laid rewriting its own number
and the carry-out log's "the clause asked for 7" were named the best-written
things on the screen.

**Smaller.** The Casket's "deals 2 Hydro damage" dealt 3 under Vulnerable;
no card or relic face in the game folds the enemy's Vulnerable, so this is
noted and not a row. Yae Miko's Sakura took 10 HP off an enemy with no line
in the log, and the Electro-Charged it set off showed on the body as
`Poison` (`EB-410`, already open); the summon's silent hit is `EB-654`.

## 5. Defaults applied (D and E), disclosed

**The carry-out rule stays undecided, and the cap stays a lane rule.** R265
said the rule is decided on the round's reads. The reads so far: round 23's
order riders made a free turn a sequencing puzzle without the cap; this
lane's cap made priority a decision but only after the seat inferred a rule
the screen denied. A rule read against a false sentence is not a read of
the rule. So the cap is not adopted tonight, the pool-first path stands,
and the re-ask trigger is the cap lane in §4 item 3 on a build that prints
it. That is a D default under R265 pick 1 and is applied without a pick.
Items 1 and 2 are E defaults (a printed rule, a log line). No shipped
number moves.
