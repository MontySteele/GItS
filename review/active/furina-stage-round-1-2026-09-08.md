## 2. What every seat said first: the stage is not printed

Nothing on the blind-play page names a performer, a seat or a bar. The bridge
publishes pets and the page draws Kokomi's one; Furina's three have no
renderer. So in some 550 actions no seat ever knew who was on stage or what a
bar held. Each learned the roster from one glossary line (the Bow's) and
inferred bars by firing readers and reading the result backwards. Every
finding below is read through that hole, and the round's first job is to
close it (`EB-735`).

The second finding rides on the first: the page's combat header still prints
the shipped Fanfare meter, the shipped Burst meter and `Encore: 0`, beside a
glossary that calls Fanfare the bar. One word, two resources, the shown one
dead. The draft was mixed by construction (batch one swaps fourteen rows), so
seats were offered Encore cards they could never pay, a Fortissimo Guard that
never fired on a summon, and a Casting Call whose gloss describes the old
three-member Salon. That is `EB-736`, and it takes the shipped Furina rows out
of the arm's offer until `EB-726` retires them.

## 3. The hypothesis, read

**Turn one of fight one was a wager the seat could name.** Two of three seats
said so in their own words: the natural seat chose Curtain Rise with Spend as
a probe and then Regal Bearing's Weak over Stage Presence's Block, and the
Expend seat chose Standing Ovation "hoping the bar existed" over a safe
double-Block. The Preserve seat also sequenced its turn as a probe. In all
three the interesting part was reverse-engineering the header, and the
competent part was the card decision. That is a pass on the brief's test,
qualified by §2.

**A second performer was wanted, and for reasons.** All three seats said yes,
and each gave the reasons the brief predicted: absorption (the elite turn
with an empty stage took 17, the next with a body took 2), the reserve
against one's own Spend cards, and the bows on Let the People Rejoice. None
could confirm having one, and none wanted a *particular* one, because none
could see one.

**Spend at a small bar read three ways.** The natural seat called it a play
("a designed release valve": full rider plus a bow, at the cost of the thing
protecting you, and the Elite made it pay the next turn). The Expend seat
called it a loophole ("a sacrifice you are rewarded for", the only cost the
downstream empty stage). The Preserve seat called it a loophole that then
stopped working: Curtain Rise at 7 after two Refills. That last is the rule
working unseen: Raise loads the back seat, three Corpse Slugs kill three
1-bar performers in one enemy turn with no bow and no line, and the next
Spend plays at base. The brief's "cast is chaff" failure mode was observed,
and so was its guard question. No lever moves on it before the stage is
printed and a seat can see what it spends.

**The three read as nothing.** Not as three, not as one Osty three times: "one
anonymous pool with three names". Every seat said the Bow line draws three
distinct performers and the screen shows none, and two said the fix is
printing, not more differentiation.

## 5. One rule clarified, E default

Brief rule 3 said a newcomer "performs its act that same turn", and both
engines read it as an act on arrival. Three seats saw every summon deal
damage and apply Hydro on play with nothing on its face, and a summon turn
performed twice. The brief meant the end-of-turn act with the others, so the
summon turn is not empty. Rule 3 now says so, and `EB-738` removes the
arrival act in both engines. Applied as an E default (a wording the engines
misread, not a rule change); [USER] vetoes.
