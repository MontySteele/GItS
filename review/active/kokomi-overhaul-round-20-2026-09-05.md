## 2. What the round found

**No reader row was drafted, and the seat that was offered two said why.**
Lane 1 was offered Song of Pearls and Treatise at one reward and took a
vanilla 10-damage Companion: "I'd written a Plan on 5 of 13 turns. That
was my error, not the card's, but it means the Plan-payoff cards are
competing against raw damage and losing at the moment of choice." Lane 2
was offered none. **The hypothesis is not supported on either lane,** and
the reason is the one round 18 named: a reader pays a deck that already
plans on most turns, and neither natural deck did (lane 2 wrote no Plan in
fights 2 and 3 at all). The loop's reading is that the shelf is a
second-half shelf, drafted after the deck's Plan density is real, and the
seats' drafts do not reach that density in act 1. Held; nothing moves.

**The Moon Overlooks the Waters deletes the question.** Lane 2, after
Moon+ landed from an event: "writing is never worse than playing, so the
tension the kit is built on stops being asked; the strongest card I drew
and the one that flattened my turns." Round 17's assembled seat had read
the same card as the deck's engine ("Battle Plan under the Moon refunds
itself, the best of the six"). Two seats, one card, two verdicts, and both
are right: it is the Rare that breaks rule 2 (a Plan does nothing now), and
a rule-breaking Rare that removes the kit's decision for the rest of the
fight is C1's own sentence. The smallest intervention is a number, not a
cut: the now-copy at half, or the effect for a fixed number of turns as
Nereid's once was. Read at the door before it moves; §4.

**The elite beaten with zero Plans written.** Lane 2's Byrdonis: "four
turns, Bake-Kurage on the field the whole time, the box reading Nothing is
planned every turn; against one big body with growing Strength every Plan
line I owned was worse than its printed line; one elite turn was five
starter cards with no kit content at all." Round 19's tempo-loan reading
from the other side: on the board where the loan cannot be repaid, the kit
is its basics.

**The decision, both lanes.** "Spend this card now for X, or hand it to the
jellyfish for roughly 1.5 X next turn", re-priced every turn by the
printed intent; lane 1 answered the same card opposite ways in fight 3 and
the elite; lane 2 answered Kurage's Oath four different ways. Turn one of
fight one presented it on both lanes. Multiple Plans in one turn works and
the screen numbers them in order, "and nothing said so beforehand": the
box and the glossary say "the Plan", singular, and lane 2 wrote one at a
time for four fights on that wording (`EB-563`).

**Companions and the second element.** Lane 1: took a vanilla Companion
over the readers. Lane 2: seven offered, one taken; "three of the seven
keyed off resources the kit never gave me": Freminet needs Frozen, Heizou
needs Swirls, Gorou's pair need other Companion cards. "A companion card
that only pays off if you already have companion cards is a dead first
offer, and that shape showed up three times." That is `EB-249`-`EB-251`'s
question (the companion slate's offer shape) with a third reading behind
it; carried to the companion workshop, not this packet.

**Legibility.** War Council's face contradicts itself on aura (`EB-561`).
Whether the Casket's Hydro leaves an aura, "the single fact I most wanted
and never got" (`EB-562`). The Plan count wording (`EB-563`). The map's
deck list printed Stone Cracker's combat-only upgrades as permanent
(`EB-447`, cited; the row is full). Infested's "summons something" is
base-game text and the one deliberate blank; "unpriceable in a kit whose
whole verb is plan around it", recorded. The carry-out log praised again
by both seats: "clause number, rider and total kept separate; I never had
to guess where a number came from."
