# Doctrine read: Klee pool pass two, seven Spark sinks, against the kit charter and Regent's Stars

Each numbered arm below is ONE new card. For each, in order, give FOLLOWS or
REQUIRES_MODIFICATION and name the clause (C1 to C6) you ruled against, and
quote the line and the arithmetic (energy, Sparks, Block, damage, cards,
turns) that breaches it, in one sentence. A verdict with no clause named is
not a verdict. Read adversarially: the author is a different model family and
the owner wants the row that should not reach a tester named. Do not soften.
Name clauses, not remedies: a number, a face or a rewritten row you volunteer
is DISCARDED.

Why this read exists. Five seat reads and the owner's own run (rounds 23, 24,
25 and the run of 2026-09-08) agree that Spark never binds: a natural deck
ends fights on 3 to 5 Sparks having spent none, and a deck built to spend it
peaked at 8 while taking damage. The owner ruled on 2026-09-08 (R270): Spark
is a currency, income stays as it is, and the pool gets interesting things to
buy with it, "the relevant comparison here is Regent." This pass is those
things. It goes to this door BEFORE it is built.

## The charter

- **C1 (owner, 2026-09-04).** "I don't want us to end up gaming the metric
  of 'can an agent beat the game' by printing overly strong cards." A row
  that removes a losing line the kit is meant to keep, instead of pricing
  it, is out of charter.
- **C2 (LAW D3, binding prices).** A benefit carries a binding price. There
  must be a real turn in which the card is worse than a plain card of its
  cost. Repeatable Energy or draw at Uncommon or below is priced by
  something past the card's own Energy cost. A Spark price counts as a price
  only if the bank it draws on is one the deck can run short of: read it
  against the income figures below, not against an empty bank.
- **C3 (LAW D2, player-controlled leverage).** The card's value is decided
  by a choice the player makes (spend now or bank; which turn; which hit to
  block into), not by a number that rises while you watch.
- **C4 (Klee brief section 3 rule 7).** Nothing fires by itself: no
  start-of-turn detonation, no automatic free attack, no "at 3 Sparks".
  Every explosion is a Set off card the player played, a Mine answering an
  attack on her, or her Rare.
- **C5 (Klee brief section 9, "the second energy pool", as reframed by
  R270).** Sparks that only buy more Attacks are the named failure. A sink
  in this pass must be one the player sometimes declines: it competes with
  the other sinks for the same bank, and there is a turn on which holding
  the Sparks for a Set off is right. A row that is always played the moment
  it is affordable is out of charter.
- **C6 (not strictly better).** A row may not be a strictly better version
  of a pool row or a base-game card at the same or lower rarity and cost.
  Reference points, base game: Defend (1: 5 Block), Shrug It Off (1: 8
  Block, draw 1), Blur (Uncommon, 1: 5 Block kept), Skim (Uncommon, 1: draw
  3), Adrenaline (Rare, 0: 2 Energy, draw 2, Exhaust), Seeing Red, Echo
  Form (Rare, 3). Reference points, Regent's Stars (decompile, v0.111.0):
  Cloak of Stars (Common, 0 Energy, 1 Star: 7 Block), Particle Wall
  (Uncommon, 0 Energy, 2 Stars: Block, returns to hand), Reflect (Uncommon,
  1 Energy, 3 Stars: Block, blocked damage reflected this turn), Alignment
  (Uncommon, 0 Energy, 2 Stars: gain Energy), Guiding Star (Common, 1
  Energy, 1 Star: 12 damage, draw 1 next turn), Child of the Stars (Power:
  whenever you spend Stars, gain Block for each Star spent), Neutron Aegis
  (Rare Power, 1 Energy, 5 Stars: Plating). Regent's cheapest sink is 1
  Star, its median printed price 3, and its starter income is 3 Stars per
  fight plus 2 per Venerate play.

## The engine (as it stands today, 2026-09-08)

A Bomb is a numbered charge on an enemy; every Bomb grows by 4 at the start
of Klee's turn and never goes off on its own. Only a card that says Set off
makes Bombs go off, each a Pyro hit for its own size. A Mine is a Bomb that
also goes off when its enemy attacks her, before the hit. Spark: each Bomb
that goes off gives 1 Spark; she starts every combat with 1; every Hexerei
companion card played gives 1 (1 more on a reaction, 1 more if upgraded, 3
at most from one play); no cap; lost after combat. A card with a Spark
price spends Sparks instead of Energy, and the price is a badge, not a line
of rules text. Energy 3, HP 62. Starter: four Strike, four Defend, Jumpy
Dumpty (1 Energy, Innate: Bomb 8; when it goes off, a Mine 3 on ALL
enemies), Ka-pow! (0 Energy, Retain: Set off, 4 damage). Relic: Pounding
Surprise (the Spark-per-explosion rule).

Income, measured on the seats. Round 23 natural lane: fights ended at 3, 2,
3, 5, 3, 3 Sparks, two spent all run. Round 25 lane 1 (natural): ended on
3, 5, 5, none spent. Round 25 lane 2 (built to spend, three sinks): the bank
ran 1 to 8 across seven fights and never failed a price. A single Set off on
a two-charge board mints 2.

Spenders on the pool today (45 rows), price in Sparks: Fwoosh! (Common, 0
Energy, 1: Set off, 6 damage), Pocket Match (Common, 0, 1, Retain: Set off,
5), Tinder Toss (Common, 0, 1: twice, Set off a random enemy and 4 damage),
Quick Fuse (Common, 0, 1: Bombs on the enemy grow 3, Set off), Dig In
(Common, 0, 1: 8 Block), Powder Charge (Uncommon, 0, 1: Bomb 6), Bang Bang!
(Uncommon, 0, 2: Set off, 8, Bomb 4), Fireworks Show (Uncommon, 0, 2: Set
off ALL), Sugar Rush (Rare, 0, 2, Exhaust: 2 Energy, draw 1), Stoke the
Fuse (Uncommon, 0, X: largest Bomb grows 3 per Spark). Ten of 45; nine of
them are Attacks or Bomb setup, one is Block, one Energy at Rare. Generators
beyond the relic: Grounded (Uncommon Power: 4 Block and 1 Spark a turn while
a Bomb is out), Catalytic Converter (Uncommon Power: 1 more Spark when a
Bomb reacts), Flash Point (Uncommon Attack: 1 Spark and a card on a
reaction), and every Hexerei companion card.

Block on the pool today: Dig In (above), Careful Now (Uncommon, 1: Block
equal to your largest Bomb, up to 10), Sorry, Jean... (Common, 0: remove a
Bomb, Block equal to its size), Run Away! (Common, 0: 3 Block, 7 if a Bomb
went off this turn), Dodoco Cover (Common, 1: Bomb 4, 5 Block), Grounded.
Round 25's finding on the defence: on the four turns it failed, the missing
thing was the Block card in hand or the Energy, never the Spark or the Bomb.

## The arms

1. **Blast Shield** -- Uncommon Skill, 0 Energy, 2 Sparks. Gain 6 Block.
   Return this card to your hand. Upgrade 8 Block. Particle Wall's shape:
   playable again in the same turn for 2 more Sparks, so a bank of 8 is 24
   Block on the turn it is needed, and 0 when the bank is 1. Compare Dig In
   (1 Spark: 8, once), Defend, Blur. Read C5 against a card that is always in
   hand once drawn, and C2 against 3 Block per Spark with no ceiling but
   the bank.
2. **Return to Sender** -- Uncommon Skill, 1 Energy, 2 Sparks. Gain 8
   Block. This turn, damage this Block absorbs is placed on the attacker as
   a Bomb. Upgrade 11 Block. Reflect's shape with Klee's verb: blocking a
   12 with it puts a Bomb 8 on the attacker, which grows 4 at dawn. Compare
   Jumpy Dumpty (1 Energy: Bomb 8, no Block), Dodoco Cover (1: Bomb 4 and 5
   Block), Shrug It Off. Read C3 for whether choosing which hit to block
   into is the player's, and C6 against Dodoco Cover at the same Energy.
3. **Bottomless Bag** -- Common Skill, 0 Energy, 2 Sparks. Draw 2 cards.
   Upgrade draw 3. A plain sink at Regent's Quasar price. Compare Skim
   (Uncommon, 1 Energy: draw 3) and read C2 against 0 Energy for 2 cards
   when the bank is 5, and C5 against whether 2 Sparks is ever better held
   for two Fwoosh! plays.
4. **Once More!** -- Uncommon Skill, 0 Energy, 3 Sparks. Return the last
   Set off card you played this combat to your hand. Upgrade 2 Sparks.
   The detonator-draw answer: the scarce thing round 25 named is a Set off
   card in hand, and Sparks are the surplus, so the surplus buys the
   detonator back. Dead on a turn with no Set off played yet; Ka-pow! is
   Retained so it is rarely the one returned. Compare a base-game
   recursion at Uncommon and read C4 (it returns a card; it fires
   nothing) and C5 (3 Sparks is three Fwoosh! plays).
5. **Sparkling Burst** -- Uncommon Skill, 0 Energy, 3 Sparks. Gain 1
   Energy. If a Bomb went off this turn, gain 1 more. Upgrade 2 Sparks. Not
   Exhaust. Alignment's shape with an ordering rider: played after the
   detonation it is 2 Energy for 3 Sparks, and the detonation is what
   minted them. Compare Sugar Rush (Rare, 2 Sparks, Exhaust: 2 Energy, draw
   1) and read C2 and C5 against a repeatable Energy card at Uncommon,
   and C1 against the loop "Set off, mint 2, Sparkling Burst, 2 Energy,
   Set off again".
6. **Blast Goggles** -- Uncommon Power, 1 Energy. Whenever you spend
   Sparks, gain 2 Block for each Spark spent. Upgrade 3. Child of the
   Stars. With it, Fwoosh! is Set off, 6 damage and 2 Block; Stoke the
   Fuse on a bank of 6 is 18 Bomb growth and 12 Block. Read C1 and C6
   against Child of the Stars and against the existing spenders becoming
   defence cards for free, and C3 for whether the Block follows a choice.
7. **Blazing Delight** -- Rare Power, 2 Energy, 5 Sparks. At the start of
   your turn, gain 1 Energy and draw 1 card. The top of the price ladder:
   5 Sparks is a fight's income for a natural deck, and Sparks do not carry
   between fights, so it is bought mid-fight off this fight's explosions or
   not at all. Compare Echo Form, Demon Form and Neutron Aegis, and read
   C1 against a combat-long Energy engine and C5 for whether holding 5
   Sparks through two turns of Set off offers is a decision.

Seven verdicts, each with its clause and its breaching line, then two
sentences: after this pass, is there a turn on which two Spark sinks in hand
compete for one bank, or does income still outrun every price on the pool.
Under 700 words.
