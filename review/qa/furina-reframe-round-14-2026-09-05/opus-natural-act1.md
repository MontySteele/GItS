## Fight 1 — Fuzzy Wurm Crawler [A], 55 HP (floor 1)

Opening hand: Ethereal Spotlight, Charlotte — First-Person Shutter, Injury,
Soloist's Solicitation, Aria of Recompense, Stage Presence. Enemy intent: attack
for 4.

**Turn 1** — Ethereal Spotlight → Soloist's Solicitation on A → Charlotte —
First-Person Shutter → Aria of Recompense. The real decision was the first card
and it was a good one: Ethereal Spotlight costs **0 energy but 2 Encore**, and I
opened with exactly 2 Encore, so "light the Companions for the whole combat" and
"keep the pad that absorbs damage" were the same two pips. I paid, and the proof
came back on the screen — Charlotte's face rewrote itself from "Gain 4 Block. At
the start of your next turn, gain 4 Block" to **6 and 6** the moment the
Spotlight landed. *Rejected:* holding the Spotlight to keep the Encore, because
the buff is priced once per combat and this was a slow fight; and Stage Presence,
because Charlotte's 6+6 beat its 6 for the same energy.

What I could **not** work out on turn 1: Charlotte printed "*Performs a member*
— No member on stage: performs nobody. Deploy a member first, or this card is
only its own line", and nothing in my hand or in the glossary said **which card
deploys**. So I played a Companion card knowing it was half a card and not
knowing whether the missing half was in my deck at all.

**Turn 2** — An Invitation → Lisa — Violet Arc → Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire
on A → Salon Début. Enemy was Empowering, so block was worthless and the whole
turn was about damage. An Invitation gave Lisa (apply Electro to ALL, draw 1);
playing her put an Electro aura up, and Chevreuse's face immediately grew a line
it had not carried a second earlier: "*Reaction preview: Overloaded* — Pyro meets
Electro: 6 damage to ALL enemies and 1 Weak on the reacted enemy". That preview
is the best piece of writing in the kit — it turned an ordering question into a
readable one. 49 → 33, exactly the 10 + 6 the screen promised. Salon Début then
deployed **Mademoiselle Crabaletta**, who hit for 6 Hydro and paid 1 Encore, and
the Salon keyword finally explained the whole machine. *Rejected:* Stage Presence
and Regal Bearing, both dead against a Buff intent; and Rapturous Applause,
because at 8 Fanfare its "1 per 10 Fanfare" pays 0.

**Turn 3** — Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire on A → Soloist's Solicitation on A.
This is the turn the fight was decided on, and the decision had been made on
turn 2: the enemy was wearing the Hydro aura Crabaletta left behind, so
Chevreuse's preview read **Vaporize, 1.5×**. 10 × 1.5 = 15, plus Crabaletta's 6
off the Companion play = 21, leaving exactly 6 for Soloist. An exact-lethal
three-card line, and every number on it had been printed to me before I
committed. *Rejected:* Salon Début for a second Crabaletta and Aria for Encore —
both are investments and the board was dying this turn.

Damage taken: **0**. Fanfare peaked at 4.

## Fight 2 — Wriggler ×4 [A] 19, [B] 18, [C] 21, [D] 17 (Dense Vegetation, floor 2)

I chose the event's **Rest** branch ("Heal 23 HP. Fight some enemies") over
"Trudge On" (76 gold, lose 8 HP) because a fight is another card reward and
another Companion offer, and I wanted the kit data more than the gold.

**Turn 1** — An Invitation (→ Gorou — Inuzaka All-Round Defense) → Ethereal
Spotlight → Aria of Recompense → Gorou on A → Stage Presence. Spotlight had to
go **before** anything spent Encore: I had exactly 2, and a deployed member eats
1 per performance, so a single perform would have locked the Spotlight out for
the fight. That ordering constraint is real and it is not printed anywhere; I
worked it out from the two numbers. Gorou hit for **12** (printed 8, lit ×1.5)
and gave 6 Block — which retired my worry from fight 1 that the Spotlight was
not touching damage. *Rejected:* Soloist on A — 6 into a 7-HP body left it alive
and cost me the 6 Block that made the turn free; taking 0 damage and keeping
5 Encore was worth more than leaving a Wriggler at 1.

**Turn 2** — Salon Début → Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire on C → Charlotte —
Snappy Silhouette on D. My hand held **no block at all**, and the board was
about to hit for 16, so there was no defensive line to weigh: the only question
was ordering. Crabaletta's deploy-performance landed on C (it picks its own
enemy at random) and left a Hydro aura, which made Chevreuse read Vaporize for
exactly 15 into C's 15 HP. Then Charlotte — Snappy Silhouette, which was a
genuine gamble I want on the record: I could have killed A (1 HP) for certain
with Soloist, or played Charlotte for 2 Vulnerable + a draw + a **random**
6-damage performance with a 1-in-3 chance of killing A anyway. I took the
gamble; Crabaletta hit A and killed it. It could as easily have wasted the turn,
and the screen was honest that it would be random.

**Turn 3** — Charlotte — First-Person Shutter → Regal Bearing on D. **The dead
turn of the run.** Both Wrigglers were Empowering, so nothing was incoming; my
hand was Charlotte — First-Person Shutter, Regal Bearing, and Stage Presence ×2
— four block cards and an Injury against a board that was not attacking. I had
3 energy and could find only two cards worth playing, and one of them (Charlotte)
was worth playing solely because a Companion play makes Crabaletta perform. The
perform printed the best line in the log: "**Crabaletta** hit Wriggler (2) for
4 Hydro … *(dry: it could not pay its Encore, so it acted at three-quarters)*" —
6 → 4, with the reason attached.

**My own mistake, undeclared by nobody but me:** I put Regal Bearing's 1 Weak on
D, a Wriggler that was Empowering, not attacking. The Weak keyword says plainly
"One stack falls off at the end of each of its turns", so it expired on D's buff
turn and reduced nothing. The screen told me; I did not read it carefully enough.

**Turn 4** — Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire on B → Charlotte — Snappy Silhouette
on D → Soloist's Solicitation on D. Board clear, computed before the first card:
B at 14 with a Hydro aura dies to Vaporize (10 × 1.5 = 15); with B dead, only D
is left, so Crabaletta's "random" targeting becomes **deterministic**, and the
rest of the line is arithmetic — perform 4, then 2 Vulnerable, then perform
6 (4 × 1.5), then Soloist 9. Killing B first was what made the randomness go
away, and noticing that was the fun part of the fight. *Rejected:* Rapturous
Applause, and the Flex Potion, both unnecessary once the line closed.

Damage taken: **14**, all on turn 2, all because the hand had no block in it.
The board's status cards left **Infection** ("Unplayable. At the end of your
turn, if this is in your Hand, take 3 damage") in my draw pile.

## Fight 4 — Shrinker Beetle [A], 40 HP (floor 4)

Intent turn 1 was a strong debuff; it landed as **Shrink -1 — "While Shrinker
Beetle is alive, your Attacks deal 30% less damage."**

**Turn 1** — Rapturous Applause → Salon Début → Charlotte — Snappy Silhouette on
A. Here I made the opposite Spotlight call to fights 2 and 3, and it was the
most interesting decision of the run: the Spotlight relic hands you a fresh
Ethereal Spotlight **every turn**, so lighting is never now-or-never, and with no
damage incoming I chose to keep the 2 Encore so both of this turn's performances
would be full strength. Rapturous Applause went first so its Fanfare would be on
the board before the members performed — and the second performance came back
**10** (6 base, +1 for holding 10 Fanfare, ×1.5 for Vulnerable), against the
first one's 6. *Rejected:* Ethereal Spotlight, Stage Presence and Regal Bearing,
all three for the same reason — nothing was coming in and none of my three
Companion cards were in hand to be lit.

**The one refusal of the run**, and it was a good one. At 0 Encore I tried
`play "Ethereal Spotlight"` to see what would happen, and got:
`'Ethereal Spotlight' cannot be played right now: you have no Encore, and this
costs 2.` The next turn the card sat in my hand with **CANNOT BE PLAYED: you
have no Encore, and this costs 2** printed under it, and a turn later, at

**Turn 2** — An Invitation (→ Diona — Icy Paws) → Aria of Recompense → Diona →

is explicitly "not an Attack"**, so Crabaletta ignored the debuff entirely while
Soloist's face fell from 6 to 4. The kit let me route around a debuff by using
the half of my damage the debuff could not name, and the printed text was
sufficient to see that coming. *Rejected:* Stage Presence — 6 block against 7,
when 9 Encore was already going to eat the leak.

**Turn 3** — Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire on A, alone. The Beetle was at 7 with
a Hydro aura; Interdiction printed **5** (7 base, ×0.7 Shrink) and Vaporize made
it 7. One card, exact lethal, one energy — set up by the aura Crabaletta had
left the turn before.

Damage taken: **0** (one point leaked past block and Encore ate it).

6. 40 → 0. I spent the Energy Potion to close it in one turn rather than eat 9
damage and spend another turn — a real cost/benefit, decided on the printed
numbers.

Damage taken: **0**. Two turns.

board into a new one in a single beat, which was a genuine surprise and a fair
one — "Upon dying, summons… something" was printed from turn 1.

**Turn 4** — Crashing Waves → Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire on B. Four bodies at
12/9/10/8; Crashing Waves' 9 to all killed two of them, Interdiction's 11 killed

random targeting stops being random.**

Damage taken: **1**. Rewards included the relic **White Star** ("Elites drop an

## The kit, after 6 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Four kinds, and three of them are good.

1. **Encore is one pool doing three jobs, and that is the kit's best decision.**
   It absorbs damage after Block; it pays 1 per member performance, or the member
   acts at three-quarters; and Ethereal Spotlight costs 2 of it for a
   combat-long +50% on every Companion card. Every fight opened with the same
   live question — light the Spotlight now, or keep the members at full strength
   — and I answered it **differently in different fights for printed reasons**:
   paid in fights 2 and 3 (Companion cards in hand, an AoE that killed more
   bodies at 15 than at 10), held in fights 4 and 5 (no Companions in hand, a
   damage debuff that made performances my best damage), paid instantly in the
   elite (empty stage, so the price was zero). *Decided on the turn.*
   It also has a **hidden ordering rule** I had to derive rather than read: the
   Spotlight must be played before anything spends Encore, because one
   performance drops you from 2 to 1 and locks it out for the fight.

2. **Elemental sequencing.** Crabaletta leaves Hydro; both Chevreuse cards are
   Pyro; so a Companion play vaporizes the aura the previous performance left and
   leaves a fresh one behind. Fight 5 turn 2 was four cards chained on that
   alternation for 40 damage, and the *Reaction preview* lines meant I was
   reading it rather than guessing. Crashing Waves' "+5 if the enemy has an
   elemental aura" put the same question on a non-Companion card. *Decided on
   the turn, enabled at the draft.*

3. **Killing down to one body to make the random targeting stop being random.**
   A performing member "picks its own enemy at random". Twice — fight 2 turn 4,
   elite turn 4 — the whole turn was built around killing the other bodies with
   aimed cards *first* so the unaimed performances could only land where I wanted.
   This is the most interesting thing the Salon does and it is entirely emergent.
   *Decided on the turn, off a plan laid the turn before.*

4. **The draft.** Ring of Bursting Grenades (a Companion body on an AoE) and
   Crashing Waves changed what my turns looked like more than any card play did;
   fight 3 turn 1 and elite turn 4 were both won by a draft pick two floors
   earlier. Rapturous Applause at Neow is why Universal Revelry was worth taking
   at the end.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Stage Presence** (Gain 6 Block) and **Soloist's Solicitation** (Deal 6) are
  vanilla and never asked me anything. Soloist was still the card that closed
  three fights, because the kit's actual damage is lumpy and it was the small
  exact number; Stage Presence I played twice in six fights and skipped it in
  a dozen turns.
- **Regal Bearing** (3 Block, 1 Weak) I would cut. Its Weak is applied on my turn
  and falls off at the end of the enemy's next turn, so against any Buff or
  status intent it does literally nothing — which is most of what act-1 enemies
  do on the turn you have spare energy. I wasted it once for exactly that reason.
- **Injury** and **Infection** are dead cards by construction, which is fine.
- **Fight 2 turn 3 was fully automatic**: a hand of four Block cards against a
  board of Buff intents, 3 energy and only two cards worth playing. Nothing was
  traded off. That is the one turn in eighteen with no decision in it, and its
  cause is that the starter deck's defence is undifferentiated — four cards that
  all say "gain block" and nothing that says "gain block *and*".
- **An empty stage makes every Companion card half a card.** Salon Début is the
  only Deploy in the deck, so on 4 of 18 turns the Companion cards in my hand
  were vanilla — and on fight 1 turn 1 and elite turn 1 that was the *opening*
  hand, which is the worst place for it.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

- **Two meters appeared that no card prints.** From fight 4 onward the state
  block carried `Fanfare Cap Bonus: 8` and `Fanfare Floor: 8`, each annotated
  "the game's data feed carries this meter's amount only: no maximum, and no rule
  for how it is spent". They appeared the turn after I played Rapturous Applause,
  whose face says only "**Fanfare +8**". So the card is not a one-time +8: it
  appears to set a floor Fanfare cannot decay below, which is a *much* better
  card than the one printed and I only inferred it from watching Fanfare sit at
  8–10 for three fights instead of decaying to 0. Either the face or the meter is
  lying about what the card does.
- **Amber — Explosive Puppet did not light.** Every other Companion card visibly
  rewrote its numbers when the Spotlight was up (Charlotte 4→6, Chevreuse 7→10,
  Ring 10→16, Gorou 8→12). Amber printed "deal 8 Pyro damage to ALL enemies"
  before and after lighting, and the damage it actually dealt (18 = 8 × 1.5
  Vaporize × 1.5 Vulnerable) is consistent with 8, not 12. Either its damage is
  not "printed damage" for the Spotlight's purposes, or it is a bug; nothing on
  the screen distinguishes those.
- **Fight 1 turn 1, I did not know which card deploys.** Charlotte's face told me
  "Deploy a member first" and no screen I could reach told me *what deploys*
  until I happened to draw Salon Début on turn 2. For a first fight, that is the
  one keyword chain the kit does not close.
- **The Salon keyword is 90 words long and arrives all at once**, on the screen
  where Salon Début first appears, and it is load-bearing in every clause (front
  member, Evoke on a full stage, random targeting, "not an Attack and not a hit",
  +1 per 10 Fanfare, three-quarters when dry). I read it three times. It is
  *accurate* — every clause of it paid off later — but it is a wall.
- **Evoke never happened to me.** The keyword ("The member performs and leaves.
  Its Fanfare bonus counts 3 times and it prints 5 Fanfare") was on my screen
  from fight 1 turn 2 onward, and with one Deploy card in the deck I could never
  fill the stage to trigger it. A third of the Salon's printed rules were
  unreachable for the whole act.
- **A played Power vanished from the deck snapshot.** The floor-8 deck list on
  the map omits Rapturous Applause, which I played on turn 1 of that very fight;
  Prima Donna, a Power I never played, is listed. Cosmetic, but it made me
  double-count my own deck.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Regal Bearing** — see (b); its Weak is timed so that it is dead
whenever you have the spare energy to play it. (Injury is beneath the question.)

Happiest: **Chevreuse — Ring of Bursting Grenades**. It is the card where every
system in the kit lands on one line at once — 10 printed, 15 lit, 16 with
Fanfare, 24 into Vulnerable, and its own Pyro sets up the *next* performance's
Vaporize while its Companion body makes that performance happen. Runner-up:
**Aria of Recompense**, entirely because of the two-turns-later feeling of
watching a "dry" 4 become a 10.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

Yes, but a thin one, and it nearly hid. The decision was Ethereal Spotlight —
a 0-energy card whose price is the 2 Encore you start the combat with, which is
also the pad that keeps you off HP. That is a genuine trade and I thought about
it. But it resolves to "yes, play it" almost immediately once you notice it
costs no energy, and the rest of turn 1 was three one-cost cards into three
energy with no cut to make. The turn's *real* content — that Charlotte was
half a card because I had no member and no way to know which card would give me
one — was a confusion, not a choice.
