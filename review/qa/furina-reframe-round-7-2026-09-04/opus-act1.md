## Fight 1 — Nibbit, 43 HP

**Turn 1** (3 energy; Nibbit intends Attack 12).
Played **Freminet — Pers, Deploy!** (6, applies Cryo), then **Soloist's
Solicitation** ×2 (6 + 6). 18 damage, took 12.
*Rejected:* **Stage Presence** for 6 Block instead of the second Solicitation —
an even 6-for-6 trade on paper, but against a single attacker with 43 HP the
damage shortens the fight and the Block does not. So there was a decision here,
and it was a thin one.
The turn's real content was that **Ethereal Spotlight sat in my hand unplayable**:
"CANNOT BE PLAYED: you have no Encore, and this costs 2", and nothing in my
opening hand made Encore. The starter relic hands me a card every turn that the
starter hand cannot pay for.

**Turn 2** (Nibbit 25/43, Cryo Aura 2, intends Attack 6 + Defend).
This is where the kit arrived. Played **Aria of Recompense** (1) → 5 Encore, then
**Salon Début** (1) → Crabaletta deployed and performed for 6 Hydro **into the
Cryo aura**, which printed back:

> **Crabaletta** hit Nibbit for 6 Hydro, and left no aura on it.

and Nibbit came out of it wearing **Frozen 1**. Then **An Invitation** (0) → drew
Sayu — Yoohoo Art: Fuuin Dash.
Then **Ethereal Spotlight** (0 energy, 2 Encore) and **Sayu** (1). The screen then
told me exactly what the Spotlight did *not* cover:

> **Guest Cast 1 (buff)** — Companion cards are Spotlighted: 50% stronger printed
> damage and Block. **No member is one.**

That line is the best-written thing in the kit. Sayu at 4 became 6, Frozen
Shattered for 6, and Crabaletta performed a second time off the Companion play.
Nibbit ended the turn on **1 HP** — I had predicted exactly 1 before playing, so
the arithmetic on screen is trustworthy.
*Rejected:* skipping the Spotlight to keep 4 Encore as a damage cushion. I paid 2
Encore for +2 damage and information, against an enemy threatening 3 (halved by
Frozen). Real choice, small stakes.

**Turn 3.** **Sayu** for 6 through 5 Block. Kill.
*Rejected:* Soloist's Solicitation, which also killed it — genuinely no decision.

**Reward:** took Chevreuse — Ring of Bursting Grenades (2 energy, 10 to ALL,
Pyro) over Florid Cadenza / Applause Line / Dinner Service, because 10 AoE on
floor 1 outclasses three cards that all scale off Fanfare I did not yet make.
This was the reward screen where I first saw the promised fourth Companion slot,
and it did print a Companion in it.

## Fight 2 — Twig Slime (S) 8, Leaf Slime (M) 34, Leaf Slime (S) 13

**Turn 1.** **Chevreuse** (2) killed Twig outright and left both Leaf Slimes on
Pyro; **Soloist's Solicitation** (1) finished Leaf Slime (S) at 3.
*Rejected:* Stage Presence for 6 Block. Killing the 3-damage attacker removes the
damage *and* a body; 6 Block removes the damage once. Clear decision, clearly
resolved.
Also recorded here: Chevreuse is a Companion card and my stage was empty, and the
screen performed nobody — the empty-stage rule works as printed, and the Salon
does **not** persist between fights.

**Turn 2** (Leaf Slime M 24/34, Pyro Aura 1, attacks 8).
**An Invitation** (0) → Sayu — Muji-Muji Daruma. Then **Salon Début** (1) →
Crabaletta performed **dry**:

> **Crabaletta** hit Leaf Slime (M) for 4 Hydro … (dry: it could not pay its
> Encore, so it acted at three-quarters).

Then **Sayu — Muji-Muji Daruma** (1), which is both 4 Block now + 4 Block next
turn *and* a Companion play that performed Crabaletta a second time; then
**Soloist's Solicitation** (1).
*Rejected:* **Regal Bearing** (3 Block + 1 Weak) for the same energy. Sayu gave 8
Block across two turns and a free 4-damage member performance; Regal Bearing gave
3 Block and shaved 2 off one hit. Not close.
The Pyro/Hydro Vaporize fired inside this and the enemy fell from 24 to 8 on 14
printed damage — the screen showed the reaction but I could not reconcile the
extra points to a printed number.

**Turn 3.** **Freminet — Pers, Deploy!** (6 + a Crabaletta performance) killed it.
*Rejected:* Chevreuse — overkill for 2 energy on an 8-HP body.

**Reward:** took **Lasting Impression** (1, gain 4 Encore, Exhaust) over Top
Billing / The House Holds Its Breath / Sucrose. Reason from play, not theory: two
fights in, my members had performed **five** times and paid for **none** of them,
and my starter relic's card had been unplayable in most hands. I was buying the
resource the kit kept telling me I did not have.

## Fight 3 — Shrinker Beetle, 38 HP

**Turn 1** (intent: apply a Debuff — no incoming damage).
**Aria of Recompense** (1) → 5 Encore; **An Invitation** (0) → Neuvillette;
**Ethereal Spotlight** (0, −2 Encore); **Neuvillette** (1, 5→7 spotlighted);
**Soloist's Solicitation** (1).
*Rejected:* every Block card in hand. The intent was a debuff, so Block was worth
exactly nothing — the intent line made that a free read, and the turn had a real
shape because of it.

**Turn 2.** The debuff landed: **Shrink −1 — While Shrinker Beetle is alive, your
Attacks deal 30% less damage.** And the deck re-priced itself in front of me:
Soloist's Solicitation now *printed* "Deal 4 damage", Lynette printed 7 Block
instead of 5. The printed number folds Spotlight, Shrink and Dexterity together
and shows you the real one. That is the second genuinely good piece of writing
here.
Played **Salon Début** (1) — Crabaletta performed dry into the Hydro aura — then
**Chevreuse** (2), whose face had already updated to *"Reaction preview: Vaporize
— Pyro meets Hydro: this hit deals 1.5x damage and consumes the aura."*
The pair killed a 25-HP enemy from full in one turn.
*Rejected:* Chevreuse first, then Salon Début. Order mattered and the screen told
me why: deploying first means the Chevreuse play *also* performs the member. That
is the best decision the kit asked me for all round, and it was legible.

**Reward:** took **Fischl — Nightrider** (7 damage, Electro) — the only third
element on offer, and a Companion, so it doubles as a member trigger.
Its keyword box said: *"**Hexerei** — A Companion card that prints the word, and
**Klee herself**. Some are Klee's own, some are not. Cards of hers pay when you
play one."* I am not playing Klee. I took the card without ever learning what
that sentence meant for me, and nothing later explained it. See (c).

## Fight 4 — Twig Slime (M) 26, Flyconid 48

**Turn 1.** **Aria** (1) → 5 Encore; **Fischl** (1) on Flyconid; **Soloist** (1).
Took 11, and the numbers proved the Encore rule: HP fell only 6, so 5 of the 11
went into Encore first. Encore-as-shield is real and silent — no line announced
it, the pool simply emptied.
*Rejected:* Regal Bearing (3 Block + Weak on Flyconid). I chose 5 Encore over
3 Block + a 25% shave, because Encore carries to next turn and Block does not.

**Turn 2** — the best turn of the run.
Flyconid wore **Electro Aura 1** from Fischl. I played **Lynette — Enigmatic
Feint** (1) to *Swirl* that aura onto **all** enemies, then **Chevreuse** (2) —
whose preview now read *"Overloaded — Pyro meets Electro: 6 damage to ALL enemies
and 1 Weak on the reacted enemy."*
Both enemies were carrying Electro, so **Overloaded fired twice**: Twig 26 → 4,
Flyconid 35 → 13, both Weak, and 5 Block on me. I had worked that line out from
the printed text before playing it and it landed exactly.
*Rejected:* Chevreuse + Stage Presence (16 to all, 6 Block, no Weak). The Swirl
line traded 1 Block for roughly 12 extra damage and two debuffs. This is the one
turn where the kit's elemental layer paid a *designed* dividend rather than an
incidental one.

**Turn 3** (I was Vulnerable 3; incoming 16 + 12 = 28 against 43 HP).
**An Invitation** (0) → Lynette — Bogglecat Box; **Salon Début** (1) → deploy
performance; **Bogglecat Box** (1) → draw 2 *and* a second Crabaletta
performance. The two member hits landed **one on each enemy** — random targeting
— and the Twig Slime happened to die. Then **Fischl** (1) killed Flyconid.
*Rejected:* Stage Presence + Regal Bearing for 9 Block and a Weak, which would
have left both enemies alive into a Vulnerable turn. Correct call, but note the
line only worked because the random member targeting happened to go my way.

**Reward:** **Diona — Signature Mix** (2 Weak to ALL, 4 Block at the start of each
of two turns, Exhaust) over three cards that all needed a Spotlight economy I
could not fund. At 43/89 with elites four floors up, I bought the defensive one.

## Fight 5 — Mawler, 72 HP

(Between fights: Whispering Hollow — paid 41 gold for two potions rather than
9 HP for a transform, because HP was the scarce resource and gold had no shop in
front of it. Then a rest site: **Rest** for 26 HP over Smith, at 43/89 with an
Elite three floors ahead.)

**Turn 1.** **An Invitation** (0) → Freminet — Pressurized Floe: Backstroke; then
**Backstroke** (2, 10 damage + 6 Block) and **Soloist** (1).
*Rejected:* **Chevreuse** for the same 2 energy and the same 10 damage. I held it
deliberately, because Chevreuse is the card that wants an aura already on the
body and Backstroke came with 6 Block attached. That is a real cost/benefit the
kit put in front of me.

**Turn 2** (Mawler 56, intent: Debuff, so no Block needed).
**Salon Début** → **Fischl** → **Freminet — Pers, Deploy!**, three Companion
plays chaining three Crabaletta performances, deliberately ordered so each
element landed on the aura the previous one left: Hydro → Electro (Electro-
Charged) → Hydro → Cryo (Frozen) → Hydro. 25 damage and two lasting debuffs.
*Rejected:* Diona, for defence on a turn where the intent was a debuff.
**The disagreement between screen and outcome:** the reaction is called
**Electro-Charged** in the keyword box, and the status it actually applied to
Mawler printed as **"Poison 4 (debuff) — At the start of its turn, loses 4 HP,
then reduce Poison by 1."** Same numbers, different name. Nothing on the screen
connects "Electro-Charged" to "Poison", and I only knew they were the same thing
because I had just played the Electro card.

**Turn 3** (Mawler 27, Poison 3, intends 21; I was Vulnerable).
Two lines were available and I picked the one the *status text* made lethal:
**Chevreuse** (Vaporize, 15) + its Crabaletta performance (4, dry) + **Soloist**
(6) = 25, leaving Mawler on **2 HP** with **Poison 3** — which fires at the start
of its turn, before it acts. It died to the poison and never swung.
*Rejected:* Aria first for 10 Encore, which would have made Crabaletta perform
wet for 6 and given me a 9-point cushion — but left Mawler alive on 6 to land its
21. I chose the kill-by-tick over the shield, and it worked; the printed poison
text was precise enough to bet the turn on.

**Reward:** **Endless Waltz** (2, a power: adds Crabaletta *and* Gentilhomme
Usher, and Salon Member numbers are 3 higher) over Stagehands / Usher the Waves /
Chevreuse — Vanguard's Valor. I picked the engine piece over the 0-cost glue, on
the grounds that every Companion card I already owned would get bigger. See (c)
for what it actually did.

## Fight 6 — Elite: Byrdonis, 82 HP (Territorial: +1 Strength every turn)

**Turn 1.** **An Invitation** (0) → Shinobu; then **Fischl** (Electro) →
**Freminet — Pers, Deploy!** (Cryo onto Electro = **Superconduct**, 2 Vulnerable
on the elite) → **Diona** (2 Weak + 4 Block for two turns).
*Rejected:* Shinobu — Sanctifying Ring, 2 energy for 3 damage and 4 Block, a bad
rate against one 82-HP body. And rejected Regal Bearing's 1 Weak, strictly worse
than Diona's 2.
Deliberately front-loading Superconduct so the *next* turn's big cards would land
into Vulnerable.

**Turn 2.** **Endless Waltz** (2) then **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint** (1).
This is where the kit contradicted itself in a small, checkable way. Endless
Waltz says *"Salon Member numbers are 3 higher"* and adds two members, and
members perform as they join. The join performances used the **un-bumped**
numbers:

> **Crabaletta** hit Byrdonis for **4** Hydro … (dry …)
> **the Usher** gave you **2** Block … (dry …)

and then Lynette's Companion play, one beat later, performed Crabaletta for
**6** — 9 base at three-quarters, i.e. *with* the +3. Same card, same turn, two
different member numbers, and nothing on screen says the bonus lands after the
deploy. 15 damage and 11 Block; the elite's 3×3 bounced off entirely.
*Rejected:* Endless Waltz + Soloist (9 damage into Vulnerable, no Block).
Choosing Lynette instead got *more* damage (a member hit at ×1.5) **and** 5
Block, purely because the stage was populated first. Best structural decision the
kit offered.

**Turn 3** (Byrdonis 51, Strength 2, no aura left).
**Potion of Binding** (Weak + Vulnerable to all) → **Chevreuse** (10 base,
Vaporize ×1.5, Vulnerable ×1.5 ≈ 22) → **Soloist** (9). 31 damage in one turn,
51 → 20, and the Usher's performance gave 4 Block.
*Rejected:* holding the potion. Against a body that gains 1 Strength per turn and
had 51 HP left at my ~18/turn, the arithmetic said I would lose the race by two
turns; the potion converted the fight. This was the only turn all round where I
felt the run was actually on the line.

**Turn 4.** **Chevreuse** (10) + its Crabaletta performance (6) + **Soloist** (6)
= 22 against 20 HP. Kill.
*Rejected:* Aria first to make the member perform wet — 10 + 9 = 19, one short of
lethal. The difference between winning this turn and eating a 6×3 was a single
point of arithmetic that the printed numbers let me do correctly.

**Reward:** relic Oddly Smooth Stone, Skill Potion, 37 gold, and the card **High
Tide** (1 energy, "Deal 15 damage, already including Fanfare", Hydro, **Elemental
Skill**). Taken over Prima Donna / Ebb and Flow / Lynette — Bogglecat Box on raw
rate. This screen is also where a **whole mechanic appeared for the first time**:
*Burst Energy*, *Elemental Skill*, and a *Burst card* that lands in your hand
when a meter I have never seen fills. Six fights in, on a card reward, is where
the game first mentioned it. See (c).

## The kit, after 6 completed fights and one stopped mid-way

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Three kinds, and they are all the same underlying trade in different clothes.

1. **Ordering, because deploy-then-Companion is worth more than the reverse.**
   Fight 3 turn 2 and fight 6 turn 2 both turned on putting a member on stage
   *before* spending the Companion card, so the Companion play performs the
   member as well as itself. In fight 6 that choice was worth a member hit at
   ×1.5 Vulnerable **plus** 5 Block over the naive line. This is the kit's best
   decision and it is legible from the *Deploy* and *Companion* boxes alone.
2. **Element sequencing.** Fight 4 turn 2 — Swirl the Electro onto both bodies so
   one Pyro card Overloads twice — is a play I constructed from printed text
   before I saw it work. Fight 5 turn 2 chains Hydro→Electro→Hydro→Cryo→Hydro so
   every hit lands on a fresh aura. The "Reaction preview" line on the card face
   is what makes this reachable; without it the six reactions would be memory
   work.
3. **Encore: shield now, or performance later.** Every Aria is this question.
   Fight 5 turn 3 was the sharpest form: 10 Encore and a 9-point cushion, versus
   no cushion and a kill-by-poison. That is a genuine fork with the numbers on
   screen to settle it.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Soloist's Solicitation.** Six damage, one energy, no element, no Companion
  trigger. I played it eight times and rejected nothing to do so. It is the card
  that fills the turns the interesting cards do not.
- **Block cards against a Debuff or Summon intent** were automatic *not*-plays.
  That is fine and is the intent system working — but it means Stage Presence and
  Regal Bearing were dead in roughly a third of my turns and unremarkable in the
  rest. Regal Bearing in particular was never once the right card: Diona,
  Lynette, Sayu — Muji-Muji Daruma and Stage Presence each dominated it in every
  hand where I held both.
- **Ethereal Spotlight, the starter relic's card, most turns.** It arrives every
  single turn and reads "CANNOT BE PLAYED: you have no Encore, and this costs 2".
  I played it twice in six fights. The rest of the time it is a card-shaped
  reminder that I am poor.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

Five things, roughly in order of how much they cost me.

1. **The Salon members choose their own target, and it lost me the only
   performance I ever paid for.** The keyword is honest — "The member picks its
   own enemy at random, never the card's target" — but in fight 7 the game aimed
   a paid 6-damage Crabaletta at a 6-HP minion whose own status line says it
   revives at full HP next turn. The rule is printed; the *outcome* is that the
   Encore economy's payoff is handed to the RNG at exactly the moments a paid
   performance is worth most. In fight 4 the same randomness happened to help.
2. **Endless Waltz's "+3" does not apply to the performances Endless Waltz itself
   causes.** Same card, same turn: 4 Hydro and 2 Block from the join
   performances, then 6 Hydro from a Companion play one beat later. Nothing on
   the screen orders those two effects, and a player reading "Add 1 Crabaletta …
   Salon Member numbers are 3 higher" will price the deploy at 6, not 4.
3. **Electro-Charged applies a debuff called Poison.** The reaction box and the
   status box use different words for the same thing and never connect them.
4. **Burst Energy arrives on floor 9, on a card reward, for a meter I have never
   been shown.** High Tide's keyword box explains Elemental Skill, Burst Energy,
   a Burst card and the "energy past full is lost at the cast" rule — a whole
   subsystem — and no combat screen in six fights had a Burst meter on it. I do
   not know whether Furina has a Burst card, and the run never told me.
5. **Hexerei names Klee in a Furina run.** Fischl — Nightrider and Sucrose —
   Astable Anemohypostasis both print *"Hexerei — A Companion card that prints
   the word, and **Klee herself**. Some are Klee's own, some are not. Cards of
   hers pay when you play one."* I played Fischl five times and never once saw
   the word do anything. Either it is inert here, in which case it is noise on a
   card I am asked to evaluate at a reward screen, or it is not, in which case I
   was charged something the screen never showed me.

   Smaller, same family: **"Oz"** in Fischl's own text ("If Oz is out, he deals 5
   Electro damage") is never defined anywhere, and I never learned how Oz would
   get out.

**(d) The card I never wanted to draw, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted: Regal Bearing** (3 Block, apply 1 Weak). It was strictly
  dominated in every hand I held it in, and I finished the run having never
  played it once. Runner-up is Ethereal Spotlight, but that one is at least the
  visible face of a real resource problem; Regal Bearing is just small.
- **Happiest to draw: Chevreuse — Ring of Bursting Grenades.** It is AoE, it is
  the Pyro that makes Vaporize and Overloaded reachable, it is a Companion card
  so it performs a member on top, and its face rewrites itself to tell you which
  reaction is about to fire. It did the work in fights 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, and in
  fight 6 it did 22 damage off one 2-energy play. Every good turn I had, it was
  either in the turn or being deliberately held for the next one.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Barely, and not one about this kit.** The choice was "third attack, or 6
Block", which is the generic opening decision of any deck-builder — I resolved it
in one line of arithmetic and learned nothing about Furina. Nothing on that
screen made Encore, nothing put a member on stage, and the one card that names
the kit's own mechanics (Ethereal Spotlight) printed a refusal. The kit's first
*real* decision is turn 2 of fight 1, when Aria + Salon Début finally put Encore
and a member in play at once and the Cryo/Hydro reaction lands. So: one turn of
generic filler, then the kit starts.

## Non-blindness declaration

- Commands run in the game: **only** `GITS_LANE=2 python -m understudy.blindplay
  observe` and `GITS_LANE=2 python -m understudy.blindplay act "<command>"`. No
  `harness state`, no `scenario`, no `staged_turn`, no `soak`, no other
  understudy subcommand.
- Other Bash use, all of it my own scratch: one `mkdir -p` and one `echo` to
  create a notes file in the session scratchpad (written once at the start,
  never read back), and `sed -n` filters applied to the *output of my own
  `observe` calls* to re-read one block of a screen without reprinting the whole
  page. `>/dev/null` was used on some `act` calls to suppress their echo. No
  file on disk was read by any of these.
- Tools used: **Bash** (as above) and **Write**, once, for this record.
- **Repo files read: none.**
