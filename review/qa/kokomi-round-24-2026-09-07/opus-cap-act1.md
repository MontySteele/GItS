## Fight 1 — Sludge Spinner [A], HP 37/37

Opened HP 64/80, 3 energy, hand Defend / Read the Field / Feint / Strike / Well Laid.
Enemy intent printed as two parts: Attack 8 **and also** Debuff.

**Turn 1.** Played Feint → Bake-Kurage (Plan: 10 damage), Read the Field →
Bake-Kurage (Plan: 10 Block), then Well Laid on A (2) and Strike on A (6).
*Rejected:* playing Feint face-up for 6 now instead of planning it for 10 next turn,
and playing Defend for 5 Block instead of Strike. I took the 8 to the face on
purpose: the Plan line's own numbers (6→10, 5→10) say the jellyfish pays about 70%
more for one turn of delay, and against a 37 HP body I had the turns to spend. This
is a real turn-1 decision and it is the kit's central one — see (e).

**Turn 2.** The carry-out log printed exactly what I had written:
`Bake-Kurage: Feint, 10 — the 10 is damage. / Sludge Spinner lost 10 HP` and
`Read the Field, 10 — the 10 is Block`. Enemy at 19, I was at Block 10 and had
picked up **Weak 1** from its debuff half. Wrote four Plans — Vanguard (Vulnerable +
Weak), Kurage's Oath (7 to ALL), Ripple (1 energy + 4 Block), Stolen Chapter (draw
4) — and played Defend.
*Rejected:* playing Vanguard face-up now (1 Vulnerable, and the Casket's answering
2) and swinging with Oath for 3, about 5 damage total. Planning it instead promised
the Vulnerable to land *in front of* the planned Oath in the same beat, which is
worth roughly triple. Also rejected holding a Plan back for defence: I had Weak on
me and my attacks were the worse half of my turn anyway.

**Turn 3.** Here is the first place the screen and the outcome disagreed — see the
whole finding in (c). I wrote **four** Plans; **two** carried out:
```
Bake-Kurage: Vanguard, 1 — the 1 is Vulnerable. Inside the same beat:
  Tamakushi Casket 3 on Sludge Spinner, Tamakushi Casket 3 on Sludge Spinner.
Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 10 — the 10 is damage; the clause asked for 7.
```
and the badge then read `Plan 2` with Ripple and Stolen Chapter still queued. Two
sub-findings inside those two lines, both good: the Oath line **volunteers that it
overshot its own clause** ("the 10 is damage; the clause asked for 7") because the
Vulnerable written one Plan earlier landed first — that is the clearest piece of
rules writing I saw all round. And the Casket, whose face says "**deals 2** Hydro
damage", dealt **3**, twice, silently: the Vulnerable scaled it and the relic's face
never says it can.
Enemy at 3. Played Strike for the kill. *Rejected:* nothing — this was the two-turn
plan paying off, not a dead turn. The decision that killed the Sludge Spinner was
made on turn 2 when I chose the order Vanguard-then-Oath.

**Reward:** 13 gold, then Deep Current (1 energy, 6 to ALL) over Riptide (2 energy,
9 to ALL / Plan 13 to ALL) and over the two Companion-count cards, which read as
blanks in a deck with no Companions in it.

## Fight 2 — Corpse Slug (1) [A] 26/26, Corpse Slug (2) [B] 27/27

Both printed `Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it,
becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength`. That single line is the whole fight: it
turns "which one do I kill first" into "when do I want the survivor stunned".

**Turn 1.** Played Vanguard face-up on A (Vulnerable; Casket answered), Strike on A,
Ripple → Bake-Kurage, Tide Chart face-up, Defend.
*Rejected:* planning Vanguard as I had in fight 1. I chose tempo instead because the
face-up line was 3 (Casket, scaled by the Vulnerable it had just applied) + 9
(Strike ×1.5) = 12 immediately onto a 26 HP body, versus 6 next turn. The trade I
was making was Weak — the planned version applies Weak too, and A was the one
telegraphing an attack. I decided a dead-sooner slug beats a weakened one.

**Turn 2.** Picked up **Frail 2**, and the kit handled it beautifully: my hand
reprinted itself, Defend now reading "Gain **3** Block" and Ripple "Gain **1**
Block". No arithmetic asked of me. Energy showed **4/3** — Ripple's planned energy
had arrived.
This was the round's best decision. A at 14, B at 27. Killing A would stun B for a
turn and hand it +4 Strength permanently. I wrote Oath (7 to ALL), then Feint (10 to
front), then Ripple, then Stolen Chapter, and played Defend. The order was the
point: Oath first would take A to 7, Feint second would kill it, and B — already
7 lighter from the same Oath — would eat A on *my* schedule, spend its Stun while
I had a full hand and 4 energy, and never get to use the +4 Strength.
*Rejected:* face-up Oath (3 to ALL) plus Strike, about 9 damage, which kills nothing
and lets B choose the moment. Also rejected killing B first: B was the fatter body
and A was the one with a turn of attack left in it.

**Turn 3.** The plan paid exactly as written — Oath 7 to both, Feint 10 into A,
`Corpse Slug (1) lost 7 HP, and died`, B at 20 with `Intent: Stunned` and Strength 4
it would never spend. Well Laid's face had rewritten itself to **"Deal 8 damage"**
with the note "*2, plus 3 for each Plan the Bake-Kurage carried out at the start of
this turn; it carried out 2*". Played Well Laid (8), Strike (6), Deep Current (6) for
the kill. *Rejected:* nothing again, and again that is the plan paying, not an empty
turn.

**Reward:** Yae Miko — Sesshou Sakura, over Converging Tide, Tide Wall and Flank. I
took it because it printed **Electro**, and every keyword box I had read so far told
me an Elemental Reaction needs a second element and that I had none: *"NO REACTION
IS REACHABLE HERE: Hydro is the only element this screen can supply."* Buying the
second element was the most interesting thing on the screen.

## Fight 4 — ELITE: Skulking Colony [A], HP 75/75

`Hardened Shell 20 of 20 left this turn — Skulking Colony cannot lose more than 20
HP each turn.` This is the best-designed fight I played, because that one line makes
overkill a real cost and turns every turn into a budgeting problem rather than a
maximising one. The badge counts *down* live (`13 of 20 left this turn`), so you can
see the budget you have left mid-turn. Excellent screen.

**Turn 1.** Vanguard face-up on A (Vulnerable), Slack Water on A (damage + Weak,
Casket answers), Deep Current (8 ×1.5), Kurage's Oath → Bake-Kurage.
75 → 55: exactly 20, capped. *Rejected:* Defend instead of the planned Oath. I
chose to eat the 14 (Weak cut it to 10) so that next turn's cap would be partly
filled by a Plan I did not have to pay energy for. That is the specific decision the
shell creates and I liked it.

**Turn 2.** The Oath's 7 arrived and the badge read `13 of 20 left this turn` — the
Plan's damage counts against the *same* turn's cap as my cards. Played Song of
Pearls, Night Watch → Bake-Kurage (**Dusk**), Well Laid (5), Strike (6).
Dusk is a good, distinct piece of the kit: the Weak landed at end of turn, *before*
the enemy swung, so it cut the incoming attack the same turn I paid for it — a Plan
that does not cost you a turn of delay, in exchange for a smaller number. And the
Casket's answering 2 from that Weak fitted inside the cap: 5 + 6 + 2 = 13, exactly
the 13 left. 48 → 35.
*Rejected:* Read the Field → Bake-Kurage for 10 Block next turn instead of Strike.
With a hard 20/turn cap the fight length is set by whether I fill the cap, so
wasting 6 of it to bank Block would have added a whole turn of taking 14s.

**Turn 3.** Played The General's Banner, then Kujou Sara (a Companion, so the Banner
applied Weak, so the Casket answered), then Feint, then Tide Chart. That is the one
place three cards chained off each other's printed text, and it read cleanly.
*Rejected:* planning Feint. Sara's rider is "next turn **your Attacks**", and the
Plan keyword says a carry-out is the jellyfish's, so I judged a planned Feint would
not collect the +5 — and did not test it, which I regret.

**Turn 4.** 35 → 16 (19: my ~13 plus the Electro-Charged tick). My attacks printed
as Strike **11** and Flank **13**, and the page appended an honest disclaimer that
it could not tell me whether the +5 was already inside those numbers. It was.
Flank 13 + Strike 11 killed it. *Rejected:* nothing; this was the four-turn budget
paying out.

**Reward:** 35 gold, Fire Potion, Cloak Clasp, and Shikanoin Heizou — Heartstopper
Strike over Battle Plan. That was probably my worst pick of the round: Battle Plan's
"Plan: Gain 1 Energy and draw 2" is the engine my deck wanted, and I passed on it
partly *because* I had lost confidence in stacking Plans (see (c)). Heizou brought
Anemo, a third element I never got to fire.

Then a chest (Ornamental Fan) and a rest site, where I took Smith and upgraded
Kurage's Oath (Plan 7 → Plan 10) rather than resting at 47/80.

## The kit, after 4 completed fights and one turn of a fifth

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

The kit has one big decision and it is a good one, and it is asked **on every turn
of every fight**: *play this card now, or write it on the jellyfish for next turn?*
Every Plan card prints both numbers side by side, so the exchange rate is legible
without arithmetic — Feint 6→10, Read the Field 5→10, Kurage's Oath 3→7, Stolen
Chapter 2→4. You are buying about 70–100% more effect for one turn of delay, out of
a body that cannot be hit. That is a clean, repeatable, non-obvious trade and it
carried the whole round.

Four decisions were sharper than the baseline, and each was made somewhere different:

1. **Fight 2, turn 2, on the turn: the order of the four Plans.** Oath before Feint
   so that the Oath's 7 splashed the survivor *before* the Feint killed the first
   slug and triggered `Ravenous`. The trade was tempo for control of when the
   survivor got stunned. Ordering Plans is a real second axis on top of writing
   them, and the log prints the order back at you, which is what makes it playable.
2. **Fight 1, turn 2, on the turn: Vanguard written in front of Kurage's Oath.**
   The debuff resolved inside the same beat and the Oath's own line admitted it —
   "the 10 is damage; the clause asked for 7". Sequencing a debuff Plan ahead of a
   damage Plan is the kit's combo and it is discoverable purely from the screen.
3. **Fight 3, turn 2, on the turn: Thorns 2 versus the Plan keyword.** "A carry-out
   is not a hit: no when-hit power fires" makes the jellyfish a *routing* choice and
   not only a timing one. Trading immediacy for immunity to a retaliation power is a
   real decision and it is printed, not folklore.
4. **The elite, across the fight: Hardened Shell's 20/turn cap.** It inverted the
   normal maximise-damage instinct into fill-the-budget-exactly, and made the
   *planned* Oath strictly better than a card, because a Plan spends next turn's
   energy against this turn's cap. Best fight of the round.

At the draft: the Neow bundle choice (three Skills with no kill in them versus an
attack, an energy Plan and a Casket-synergy Power) and the elite reward
Battle Plan-vs-Heizou were both real. Most other card rewards were not — see (b).

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Strike and Defend.** Every turn I had spare energy they were what I spent it on
  and I never once thought about it. Defend also has no upgraded face to read (see
  c), so it is invisible at the Smith too.
- **Ripple.** Its Plan is "gain 1 energy and 4 Block" for 0 — there is never a
  reason to play it face-up, and never a reason not to write it. It is a free
  action, not a decision.
- **Tide Chart.** Same: 0 cost, no downside, always play it.
- **Potions.** I finished holding Weak Potion and Fire Potion unused across an elite
  and five fights. Nothing on any battle screen ever reminded me they existed —
  potions only print in the `## Potions` block, which is easy to scroll past, and no
  fight ever got close enough to losing that I went looking. That is a real "never
  worth playing" and it is a UI cause, not a card cause.
- **The Companion sub-theme was mostly dead weight.** Chain of Command
  ("3 damage for each Companion you played this turn"), Gorou — Crystal Collapse
  ("play a copy of the last other Companion card you played this turn") and Rally
  all appeared in rewards while I owned zero or one Companion. They read as blank
  cards. The General's Banner only did anything on the one turn I happened to hold
  both it and Sara.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

**The big one: the jellyfish does not carry out the Plans it says it will.**
The Bake-Kurage panel prints, in as many words:

> The jellyfish holds any number of Plans and carries them out in the order written;
> the number on the **Plan** badge is how many are written, not a limit.

It carried out **exactly two per turn**, four separate times:

| where | Plans written | carried out | left on the badge |
|---|---|---|---|
| fight 1, turn 2 | 4 (Vanguard, Oath, Ripple, Stolen Chapter) | 2 (Vanguard, Oath) | `Plan 2` |
| fight 2, turn 2 | 4 (Oath, Feint, Ripple, Stolen Chapter) | 2 (Oath, Feint) | `Plan 2` |
| fight 3, turn 2 | 3 (Slack Water, Read the Field, Ripple) | 2 | `Plan 1` |
| fight 3, turn 3 | 1 carried + 2 new (Feint, Stolen Chapter) | 2 (Ripple, Feint) | `Plan 1` |

No screen anywhere printed a cap, a reason, or even an acknowledgement that
something written had not fired. The carry-out log simply lists two entries and the
badge silently keeps the rest. I only noticed on the third occurrence, and once I
had noticed it changed my play twice — I stopped writing a fourth Plan, and I passed
on Battle Plan at the elite reward because I no longer trusted stacked Plans.
Either the cap is real and the panel's sentence is false, or the panel is right and
the carry-out is dropping Plans. Either way it is the one thing in this kit I could
not read off the screen, and it sits on the kit's central mechanic.

Smaller ones:

- **Tamakushi Casket says "deals 2 Hydro damage" and dealt 3** whenever a Vulnerable
  was up. Correct behaviour, undisclosed on the relic's face. The carry-out log does
  print `Tamakushi Casket 3` so it is checkable *after*, never before.
- **Yae Miko's Sakura fires with no printed line at all.** Toadpole (2) lost 10 HP
  between two screens from a card whose face says 4, and the only evidence was a
  `Poison 3` badge. The Elemental Reaction keyword box does warn that a reaction can
  be invisible, but the *damage* going unlogged is separate: every Plan carry-out
  gets a receipt and this end-of-turn trigger gets none.
- **Electro-Charged shows on the body as `Poison`.** The reaction preview calls it
  Electro-Charged and describes "loses 4 HP at the start of its turn, 1 less each
  turn"; the enemy's badge says `Poison 3 — At the start of its turn, loses 3 HP,
  then reduce Poison by 1`. Two names, and two different numbers, for one thing.
- **The reaction preview only appears once the second element is in your hand.**
  Before that every screen said *"NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE"*, which is honest
  and helpful — but it means the entire reaction layer is invisible at the point you
  are drafting toward it.
- **Base Strike and Defend have no upgraded face**: `Upgraded: not shown -- this
  page has no written face for this card`. Every kit card showed me its upgrade.
- **Thorns 2 moved from Toadpole (1) to Toadpole (2)** between rounds with no
  printed line for the transfer; I assume the Empower intent did it.
- **Two-part intents ("Attack 8 *and also* Debuff")** come with a disclaimer that
  the page cannot say which parts resolve or in what order. That is honest, and it
  is also the thing that makes a Plan a gamble in a way the Plan text does not warn
  about — you are committing next turn's damage against an intent you only half see.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Strike**. Six flat damage, no Plan line, no element, nothing to
sequence — in a deck whose whole identity is writing next turn, a card with no next
turn is a blank. I removed one at the shop and would have removed all three.

Happiest to draw: **Well Laid**, easily. It costs 0, and its face *rewrites its own
number in front of you* — "Deal 2 damage" became "Deal 5" became "Deal 8", each time
with the line "*2, plus 3 for each Plan the Bake-Kurage carried out at the start of
this turn; it carried out 2*". It is the card that pays you for having planned well
two turns ago, and it tells you exactly what it is paying and why. If I were keeping
one card from this kit it would be this one. Runner-up: **Night Watch**, for Dusk —
a Plan that resolves before the enemy acts is a genuinely different shape from every
other Plan and I want more of it.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a good one.** Fight 1, turn 1, 3 energy, one 37 HP enemy telegraphing 8
damage and a debuff, and a hand holding both a face-up and a planned version of the
same two cards. Playing Feint for 6 now versus writing it for 10 next turn, and
Read the Field for 5 Block now versus 10 Block next turn, is exactly the kit's
question, asked at full strength on the very first turn, with both numbers printed
side by side so you can answer it without knowing anything else. I chose to take the
8 to the face to buy the bigger turn, and that choice was still paying two turns
later. Very few first turns in this genre are that pointed.

## Non-blindness declaration

Commands run outside the two allowed ones — all through the Bash tool, all against
my own scratch or the shell, none against the game:

- `mkdir -p` twice, in one call: creating
  `review/qa/kokomi-round-24-2026-09-07/` for this record, and the session
  scratchpad directory (which I then never used).
- `echo ok` in that same call, as its success check.
- Shell plumbing wrapped around the two allowed commands and nothing else:
  `&&` chaining of several `blindplay act` calls into one Bash call;
  `>/dev/null` on `act` calls whose JSON I did not need;
  and `| head -N`, `| tail -N`, `| sed -n '<ranges>p'` on the output of
  `blindplay observe` to re-read one block of a screen without reprinting all of it.
  Every byte I read this way came out of an `observe` or an `act`.

Tools used: **Bash** (as above) and **Write** (once, for this file). No Read, no
Grep, no Glob, no Agent, no web access.

No `harness state`, no `scenario`, no `staged_turn`, no `soak`, no other understudy
subcommand. No `TOOL-BLOCKED` screen was hit; no refused command at all until the
budget refusal that ended the round, and no stall.

**Repo files read: none.**
