You are reading the results of round 3 of a card-design experiment and giving
a per-arm judgement. You have no repository access and you do not need it:
everything you need is inline below.

WHAT THIS IS. Eleven staged single turns in a deck-building roguelike, in four
matched GROUPS. Within a group every board is identical -- same HP, Block,
energy, resources, enemies and alternatives in hand -- and the halves differ in
EXACTLY ONE CARD, the one under test. Each group has one SHIPPED half (the card
as it currently exists) and one or two PROTOTYPE halves (an experimental
reshaping of the same card). Three arms are under test:

  ARM 1  the COUNTING BASIS of an Exhaust-scaling attack: does it count the
         cost of the ONE card it exhausts (shipped), or every card exhausted
         during the whole turn (prototype)?
  ARM 2  a companion card that currently gives damage AND Block together:
         does making the two modes MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE change the turn?
  ARM 3  the same card with its Block PRICED INTO THE COST LINE instead
         (higher cost, both effects kept).

The design worry behind all three is SUBSIDIZED DEFENCE: Block that arrives
free on the back of a card you were playing anyway, so that defending never
costs a decision.

HOW EACH TURN WAS GRADED. A fresh, design-blind instance of an independent
model was handed only the packet (printed faces, HP, Block, energy, meters,
enemy intents -- no card ids, no design tags, no repository) and answered four
questions: what did you play, what other line did you seriously consider, what
did your line give up, and would a different enemy INTENT have changed it. A
"no" to the fourth refuses the turn as intent-insensitive -- a turn where the
telegraph did not matter is not evidence about what defence costs.

WHAT IS NEW IN ROUND 3, and why you are being told. Round 2's faces were
defective: a card printed a number that already included a scaling rider and
then asserted the rider again beneath it, so four graders and round 2's own
reviewer counted it twice and read a 9-damage card as 13. That manufactured
lethal lines that did not exist. The face is fixed; the number is 9 and the
face now says so once. Round 2's boards also contained one REAL lethal line
(group B) which has been removed by giving the attacker Block, and group D is
on a new seed whose attacker telegraphs 4 rather than 8, so that the 6-Block
half can fully answer it.

AND THE REPLAY. New this round: after grading, every graded line was REPLAYED
on the live game on its own pinned seed and board, and the authoritative
post-state is given beside each form below. Where a form's arithmetic is
contradicted by the board, that is stated. NOTHING WAS RE-GRADED on the
strength of a replay; the replay is evidence for YOU.

WHAT I WANT FROM YOU, per prototype arm (there are seven prototype halves):

  1. Was the card under test PLAYED, or seriously weighed as the alternative?
  2. Did the prototype form change the KIND of choice the turn asks, compared
     with its shipped half on the identical board? Say how, concretely.
  3. Did the cost BIND -- did paying for the Block actually cost something the
     grader names?
  4. Did any printed text trip the grader up?
  5. RETURN (the arm is not learning anything yet and the board or the card
     needs rework), ADVANCE (this shape is worth pursuing), or ESCALATE (you
     and the grader materially disagree about the game state).

Finish with a summary table of the seven prototype arms and your outcome for
each, and one line of reasoning per row. Be concrete and quote the forms.
Do not rate whether any turn is FUN; that is explicitly not the question.
