## Arm 1 — Second Helping

1. **Card involvement.** Seriously weighed, but not played. Opus considered “**leading Second Helping with the bank instead**,” while Codex considered “**Second Helping followed by Rapid Fire, dealing 28 total damage but gaining no Block**.”

2. **Change in kind of choice.** Yes. The shipped half only asked which Attack should receive the automatic discount. The prototype half added a distinct, player-steered destination: spend the bank to activate Second Helping instead of discounting Rapid Fire.

3. **Did the cost bind?** Yes. Spending the bank on Second Helping gave up the free Rapid Fire. Relative to the replayed 23-damage/5-Block line, the credible alternatives were either 28 damage with no Block or 19 damage while retaining the Block. Thus the destination of the bank concretely traded damage against 5 Block.

4. **Text problem?** Opus miscalculated Second Helping’s line: after the 0-cost Second Helping and 1-cost Kaboom!, one energy remains for Duck and Cover; “Kaboom! plus nothing” is false. Codex’s Second Helping–Rapid Fire alternative is legal. The replay confirms the chosen line, not Opus’s rejected-line arithmetic.

5. **Judgment: ADVANCE.** Both graders recognized the new destination and compared it with the automatic discount. The bank’s destination produced a concrete tradeoff worth testing across a whole fight.

## Arm 2 — Rummage

1. **Card involvement.** Seriously weighed by Opus: “**Rummage… is the only card in hand that could change what the turn is, so I priced it**.” Codex also claimed to weigh it, proposing “**Rapid Fire, Kaboom!, then Rummage instead of Duck and Cover**,” but that proposed line was illegal.

2. **Change in kind of choice.** Yes in principle. The shipped card created an ordering and energy choice—play Eager to Help before consuming Spark to draw two—without itself spending the bank. Rummage instead makes draw a competing destination for all three Sparks, mutually exclusive with the free Attack.

3. **Did the cost bind?** Yes, severely. Opus correctly found that Rummage first leaves only one energy: Rummage plus Kaboom! produces 7 damage and no Block, versus the replayed 23 damage and 5 Block. Spending the bank on draw therefore cost 16 immediate damage and 5 Block on this board.

4. **Text problem?** Yes. Codex misread Rummage’s requirement. After Rapid Fire consumes all three Sparks, Rummage cannot be played; its proposed replacement for Duck and Cover contradicts “Spend 3 Sparks.” The replay does not validate that alternative because Rummage was never attempted.

5. **Judgment: RETURN.** The board prices the prototype against 16 guaranteed damage and defense while offering three unknown, mostly unaffordable draws, and one grader consequently constructed an illegal comparison. This arm needs a board that can expose a real draw destination before whole-fight testing.

## Arm 3 — Slow Burn

1. **Card involvement.** Opus seriously weighed it: “**Slow Burn first. It is the only card that uses the Spark bank for something other than a discount**.” Codex ignored the prototype, considering only “**Kaboom! first and Rapid Fire second**.”

2. **Change in kind of choice.** Yes. The shipped half allocated energy between damage, defense, and incidental Burst gain while Spark still had only the Attack-discount destination. Slow Burn introduced a bank-level choice between that discount and converting all three Sparks into Block and Burst progress.

3. **Did the cost bind?** Yes. Slow Burn plus Kaboom! would retain the same 5 Block but fall from the replayed 23 damage to 7—a concrete cost of 16 damage—in exchange for the Burst gain. The replay confirms that declining Slow Burn left Burst unchanged at 20.

4. **Text problem?** No replay contradicts either chosen form. Opus correctly predicted that declining Slow Burn would leave Burst at 20, and the replay did so. Codex did not misread Slow Burn’s face; it simply failed to discuss it.

5. **Judgment: ADVANCE.** The prototype created a genuine second destination with an explicit immediate cost, while its Burst benefit is inherently forward-looking. Whole-fight play is the appropriate next question, especially because one grader weighed that conversion seriously and the staged-turn replay cannot value its later payoff.
