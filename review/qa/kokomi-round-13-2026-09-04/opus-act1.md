## Fight 1 — Nibbit, 43 HP

**Turn 1** (3 energy; hand Strike ×2, War Council, Change of Plans, Slack Water).
Played **War Council onto the Bake-Kurage** (a Plan), then **Slack Water**, then
**Strike**. Rejected: playing War Council face-up. Its printed lines are "Apply 1
Weak to ALL enemies" now versus "Plan: Deal 5 damage and apply 1 Weak to ALL
enemies" next turn — the Plan line is a strict superset of the face-up line, so
against an enemy with no rush on me the only reason to play it now is to get the
Weak onto the current attack, and Slack Water's own Weak already did that
(intent fell 12 → 9). That is a real decision and it is legible off the card
face alone. Also rejected: **Change of Plans** ("the Bake-Kurage carries out
your first Plan now, Exhaust") to fire War Council the same turn — 2 energy for
7 damage is a worse rate than 2 energy for Slack Water + Strike's 12.

**Turn 2** (Strike ×2, Deep Current, Defend ×2). Played all three attacks.
**No alternative rejected** — no card in the hand had a Plan line, the enemy's
9 was survivable bare, so the turn had exactly one shape. Worth recording: the
turn presented no decision at all.

**Turn 3** (Defend ×3, Kurage's Oath, Slack Water; enemy 6 HP behind 5 Block,
intent Empower). Played Slack Water, Kurage's Oath, Defend. Rejected: planning
Kurage's Oath (3-to-ALL now vs 7-to-ALL next turn) — I wanted the block stripped
this turn, and I rejected the Defend as *useful*, playing it only because the
third energy had nowhere else to go against a buffing enemy. Block whose window
is an enemy turn with no attack in it is dead energy, and the kit gave me no
sink for it.

**Turn 4.** Strike killed it. No decision.

Screen vs outcome: clean all four turns. War Council's Plan carry-out was
printed as 5 and the body lost 7 — the extra 2 is the Tamakushi Casket firing on
the Weak. That is arithmetic I had to do myself; nothing on the screen attributed
it.

## Fight 3 — Shrinker Beetle, 40 HP

**Turn 1** (intent: a strong debuff, no damage). Planned **both** War Council and
Kurage's Oath onto the Bake-Kurage, then Strike. Rejected: playing either
face-up. With nothing inbound, the Plan lines are free upgrades (0→5 and 3→7);
the only cost of planning is a turn of tempo, and a turn of tempo against a
non-attacking enemy costs nothing. This is the cleanest expression of what the
Bake-Kurage is for, and I read it correctly off the card faces with no help.

**Turn 2.** The plans landed for 20 total; the beetle's debuff (**Shrink −1**,
"your Attacks deal 30% less damage") was on me, and — good — **the card faces
in hand repriced themselves**: Deep Current printed 4 instead of 6, Slack Water
2 instead of 4, Strike 4 instead of 6. That is the single best legibility moment
of the round. It also told me for free that Shrink does not touch the Plans,
because Kurage's Oath and War Council are Skills; I never had to guess.

Played **Kujou Sara — Crowfeather Cover** (0 cost, "next Attack deals 4
additional damage and applies Electro") into **Deep Current**, deliberately, to
put Electro onto the Hydro aura the plans had left and see what a reaction does.
It gave **Poison 4**. Rejected: Slack Water + Defend, which was the safe line;
I wanted the reaction on the board and the beetle was already losing.

**Screen vs outcome, second disagreement:** Deep Current printed 4, Sara printed
+4, that is 8, plus the Casket's 2 for the Poison application makes 10 — the
beetle lost **9**. One point unaccounted. I could not close that gap from
anything printed.

Also as the glossary had warned in advance: after the Electro-Charged fired, the
enemy still showed **Hydro Aura 2**, because the Casket's own Hydro tick
re-applied it inside the same beat. The Elemental Reaction blurb predicts this
exact illusion in its own text, which is remarkable and which I would not have
believed without the Poison stack as proof.

**Turn 3.** Strike killed it. No decision.

**Turn 1** (enemy asleep, so free). Vexing Puzzlebox had handed me a free

is the best decision the kit asked me for all round, and every input to it was

**Turn 3** (it woke with Strength 10 and a 23-damage intent). Played War Council

## The kit, after 9 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

The Plan is the kit, and it is a good one. Every Plan card is two cards printed
on one face, and the choice between them is a genuine tempo-for-value trade that
flipped direction on me repeatedly and for readable reasons: I planned War
Council in fight 1 (nothing pressing), played it face-up in fight 2 (three
attackers, Weak worth 25% of 18 right now), planned it again in fight 5 (racing
beat mitigating), and played it face-up in the boss's turn 2 only because
**Change of Plans** let me have both. That is one card generating four different
correct answers in nine fights, which is the strongest thing I can say about
anything here.

The second real axis is **ordering inside a turn**, and it is the best part.
Three separate multipliers stack and all of them reward sequencing: the elite's
Slow (+10% per card played, so cheap cards first and the big Attack last),
Rosaria's Vulnerable (which lands *after* her own damage, so she goes first),
and the Casket's debuff tick (which wants to fire while Vulnerable is up, so
War Council goes last). Turn 2 of the elite — 31 damage out of 3 energy purely
by getting the order right — is the single most satisfying turn I played, and
every number I needed was on a card face.

Third: the elements. Hydro is laid down almost automatically by the deck and the
relic, so the choice is *which* second element to spend and *when* — Lisa's
Electro-to-ALL onto a board my own AoE had just painted (Poison on three bodies
for 1 energy), or Rosaria's Cryo for Frozen, or the boss variant for 2 extra
Vulnerable. Sequencing Deep Current → Lisa is a real, discoverable combo.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

The base cards. **Strike and Defend are 8 of ~22 cards** and neither ever
presented a choice; roughly a third of my turns were "play the attacks, or play
the blocks", decided entirely by the intent number. Fight 1 turn 2, fight 2
turn 1, and every kill-the-last-enemy turn (five of them) had no rejected
alternative at all.

**Change of Plans** was dead in hand in fights 2, 3 and 5 — three times before it
was ever good once. It needs a written Plan to do anything and its own face
never says so; a first-time reader will play it into an empty jellyfish and get
nothing. When it finally fired (boss turn 2) it was excellent, which makes the
dead draws worse rather than better.

**Block generally.** I played a Defend into a non-attacking enemy three separate
times purely because the third energy had nowhere else to go. The kit has no
sink for surplus energy on a quiet turn other than a Plan, and quiet turns are
exactly the turns where I already planned everything plannable.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **The Weak Potion did nothing** (boss turn 5) — consumed, no Weak on any
   enemy, no intent moved. Untargeted use was accepted without complaint.
2. **A reaction can leave no trace whatsoever.** Cubex Construct: Electro-Charged
   fired, Artifact ate the Poison, the Casket's Hydro tick re-applied the aura
   the reaction had consumed. Result: an untouched-looking board. The glossary
   pre-warns about the aura half and not the Artifact half.
3. **Rainbow Ring never fired.** "The first time you play an Attack, Skill, and
   Power each turn" read to me as three independent first-times; it appears to
   require all three, and I own no Power. I planned two turns around Strength
   that never arrived.
4. **Slow boosts card Attacks but not potion damage.** Learned by spending a
   Fire Potion into a 30% multiplier and getting a flat 20.
5. **The end-of-fight carry-out panel is unreliable.** It printed "Kurage's Oath,
   7 — the 7 is damage" beside a body that lost 9, and it omitted the second of
   the two Plans that had just resolved.
6. **Small unexplained arithmetic**: Deep Current 4 + Sara 4 + Casket 2 = 10
   printed, 9 dealt (fight 3); Rosaria 9 + two Casket ticks = 13, 14 dealt
   (elite turn 2). Both one off, in opposite directions.
7. **Three Slimed cards entered my deck permanently** from intents I watched
   resolve, and I never saw a screen name them until the Smith screen eleven
   floors later.
8. **Jean — Gale Blade carries no element tag in its title** (every other
   elemental card prints `[Hydro]`, `[Electro]`, `[Cryo]`); I only learned it is
   Anemo from a reaction-preview line that appeared mid-fight.
9. The **Byrdonis Nest** event offered "Add Byrdonis Egg to your Deck" against
   "+7 Max HP" and **never printed the card's text**. That is an unreadable
   choice; I took the max HP because I could read it.

Against all that, three things are unusually well printed and deserve saying:
card faces **reprice themselves live** under Shrink, Vulnerable and cost
discounts (with an explicit "the cost printed on this card is 1; it is showing 0
here… it is what this card costs now, not what it costs"); reaction previews
**appear only when the reaction is actually reachable**, including a
boss-specific variant; and the glossary predicts its own worst illusion in
advance.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Change of Plans**, on volume — three dead draws to one great one.
Honourable mention to **Defend**, which I played six times as a shrug.

Happiest to draw: **War Council**, easily. It is the card that made me think
every single time it appeared, in a different direction each time, and both its
halves are live. **Rosaria** is the most exciting card and **Lisa** the biggest
single button, but War Council is the one that made the turns.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a genuine one.** Fight 1, turn 1, three energy, one enemy at 43 with a
12-damage intent: War Council face-up (Weak now, cut the 12 to 9) or War Council
onto the Bake-Kurage (nothing now, 5 damage + Weak next turn), with Slack Water
in hand able to supply the Weak either way, and Change of Plans in hand offering
to collapse the delay for 1 energy. I read all of that off the printed faces
with no prior knowledge of the kit, chose the plan, and the outcome matched the
arithmetic exactly. That is as good a first turn as a blind seat can ask for.
The caveat is that it depended on the opening hand containing two Plan cards —
fight 2's turn 1 (three Defends, a Strike, a dead Change of Plans) presented
nothing at all.
