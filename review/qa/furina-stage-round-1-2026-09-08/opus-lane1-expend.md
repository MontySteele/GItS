### The six extra cards, as printed

I was told six extra cards were in the opening deck; the bridge never labels a
card as extra, so this is my read off the printed faces. Four are unmistakable
— they are the only cards in the deck that carry a character's name in the
title:

1. **Freminet — Pers, Deploy!** `[Cryo]` — cost 1, attack. "Deal 6 damage."
2. **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint** — cost 1, skill. "Swirl an enemy's aura onto
   ALL enemies. Gain 5 Block." *(removed at the floor-6 shop)*
3. **Surintendante Chevalmarin** — cost 1, skill. "Summon Chevalmarin. If she
   is already on stage, Raise 3 on her instead."
4. **Mademoiselle Crabaletta** — cost 1, skill. "Summon Crabaletta. If she is
   already on stage, Raise 3 on her instead."

The remaining two I can only guess at, and I say so rather than pretend: the
best candidates are the two copies of **Understudy** — cost 0, skill, "Summon a
random performer who is not on stage. Exhaust." — because everything else in
the opening list (Soloist's Solicitation ×2, Stage Presence ×2, Curtain Rise,
Tidal Flourish, Grand Entrance, Regal Bearing, Standing Ovation, Salon Début)
reads like a starter kit. I could not confirm this from any screen.

### Neow pick

Offered: **Arcane Scroll** (random Rare), **Lead Paperweight** (choose 1 of 2
Colorless), **Silver Crucible** (first 3 card rewards Upgraded, first Treasure
Chest empty). I took **Silver Crucible**: it is the only one whose value I
could compute before taking it, and the promised downside was cheap on floor 1.

Lead Paperweight is a finding by itself. The screen printed, under the option's
own text: *"this option's own words promise a card and the feed carried no face
for it -- no rules text, no cost, no type -- so this page can offer it by name
only"*. That is a choice offered blind, and I rejected it for exactly that
reason. Arcane Scroll I rejected as an unpriced gamble when a known quantity
was on the same screen.

Silver Crucible paid both halves as printed: three upgraded rewards (Bis!+,
Tidal Flourish+, Scene Change+) and then, on floor 9, `# An open chest` /
`- (nothing here to take)`. A promise kept in both directions.

## Fight 1 — Seapunk, HP 45/45

Opened HP 62/78, 3 energy. Hand: Standing Ovation, Lynette — Enigmatic Feint,
Stage Presence ×2, Freminet — Pers, Deploy!. Enemy intent: Attack for 11.

**Turn 1.** Played Standing Ovation (Raise 5 Fanfare on the back performer),
then Freminet — Pers, Deploy! on A (6 damage, applies Cryo), then Stage
Presence (6 Block).

*Rejected, and why:* double Stage Presence for 12 Block would have blanked the
11 outright but dealt nothing into a 45 HP body with one attack in hand — a
fight I would still be in on turn six. **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint I rejected
on its own printed text**: the screen defined *Swirl* as "The enemy's aura is
consumed and copied onto ALL enemies. **No aura, no effect.**" The Seapunk had
no aura and there was only one of it, so Feint was a 5-Block card at cost 1
standing next to a 6-Block card at cost 1. That is a clean, legible rejection
and it is the only card in the run I rejected on every single turn it appeared.

I took **0 damage** from the 11. Block 6 covered six of it; the remaining 5
never reached HP and never appeared as Block. **Nothing on the screen said
where it went.** My inference — and it is an inference, from arithmetic, not
from print — is that Standing Ovation's 5 landed on the Usher (relic: front
seat at 3 Fanfare), and the leftover 5 went into that bar. The header said
`Fanfare: 0` before and after.

**Turn 2.** Drew Understudy, Tidal Flourish, Curtain Rise, Grand Entrance,
Salon Début. Enemy at 39, now telegraphing 2×4 and wearing Cryo Aura 1 from
Freminet.

Played **Understudy** (cost 0) first, purely to find out what a summon does.
The card says only "Summon a random performer who is not on stage. Exhaust."
What actually happened: the enemy went **39 → 34**, gained **Frozen 1**, and
the reaction log printed `**Frozen** on **Seapunk**, off Furina.` So the summon
dealt damage and applied Hydro, and its card face mentions neither. A meter
called `Furina Burst: 5/70` also appeared on the header for the first time,
annotated *"the game's data feed carries this meter's amount and its maximum,
and no rule for how it is spent."*

Then the turn's real decision, and the best sequencing call of the run.
Frozen printed: *"Until it acts, an Attack Shatters it for 6 unblockable damage
and removes Frozen."* One Shatter, one time. So the cheap attack goes first to
collect it and the expensive one second. I played **Curtain Rise** (7, Spend 3:
13) → 34 → 15, i.e. 13 + 6 Shatter = 19. Then **Grand Entrance** (10, Spend 5:
20) → dead.

*Rejected:* Grand Entrance first. Twenty damage plus a 6 Shatter into a 34 HP
body wastes most of the Shatter and leaves the cheap card unable to finish;
reversing the order was worth a full extra turn. *Also rejected:* Tidal
Flourish (AoE into a single body) and Salon Début (a second performer I did not
need for lethal).

**Verdict on the fight:** two turns, two real decisions, both at the turn
level, both readable straight off printed text (the Swirl clause; the Shatter
clause). Zero HP lost.

**Reward:** Bis!+, The Gallery Stirs+, Commanding Gaze+ — all upgraded
(Crucible). I took **Bis!+**, and I want to be honest that this was the
tester's pick, not the player's. Commanding Gaze+ (2 Block, 2 Weak to ALL) was
the strong, fully legible card and I passed on it. Bis!+ reads "The lead
performer performs its act now" — a *performer's act* is a thing I had by then
seen change HP totals and had never once seen printed, and Bis!+ at cost 0 was
the only route the run had offered to make it visible. I rejected The Gallery
Stirs+ ("The first time you **spend Encore** each turn, draw 2 cards") because
Encore had read `0` on every screen and nothing had shown me a way to gain it,
let alone spend it.

## Fight 2 — Corpse Slug ×2, HP 27/27 and 25/25

Both carried **Ravenous 4**: "When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats
it, becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength." Intents: 8 damage, and a debuff.

**Turn 1.** Understudy (0) again — enemies went 27→25 and 25→23 and **both**
gained Hydro Aura 2. So the summon's entrance is 2 damage to all plus Hydro to
all, repeatably, and repeatably unprinted.

Then **Freminet on A**: Cryo onto a Hydro aura. A took 6 and came out **Frozen
1**, its 8 dropping to 4. Then **Salon Début** for a second performer (B took 5
more; A took none, which I could not explain — if Début's entrance were an AoE
like Understudy's, A should have moved too). Then Stage Presence for 6 Block
against a 4.

*Rejected:* Regal Bearing (3 Block + 1 Weak) — with A frozen down to 4 the
extra Weak was buying nothing, and the energy was better spent on a second body
on stage that would chip every turn for free. That trade — **a block card now
versus a performer that pays every turn** — is the kit's genuine recurring
choice, and it was made here for the first time.

**Turn 2, and this is the fight's decision, made at the draft not the turn.**
Enemies at 17 and 11, and Ravenous means the *survivor* of a kill eats the
corpse for +4 Strength. So the order of kills matters, and I wanted the eater
to be the one already nearly dead. **Tidal Flourish** (5 all, Spend 2: 9) took
them to 8 and 2. **Curtain Rise** (13) killed A — B ate it, printed `Intent:
Stunned` and `Strength 4`, and was standing on 2 HP where the Strength could
never be spent. **Soloist's Solicitation** finished B. Three cards, three
energy, fight over, **zero HP lost**.

The lethal on B was obvious by the time it arrived; it was obvious *because*
Tidal Flourish two plays earlier had set both bodies where I wanted them. That
is a plan paying off, not a dead turn.

**Reward:** I took **Tidal Flourish+** (8 AoE / 12 with Spend, cost 1) over
The House Rises+ ("If you have at least 5 **Encore**…", still zero) and
**Supporting Cast+** ("The first **Spotlighted** card each turn draws 2 cards")
— *Spotlighted* is a word that appears on no card face, in no glossary, and on
no screen of this run other than the cards that use it.

## Fight 3 — Sludge Spinner, HP 39/39

**Turn 1.** Understudy (0) then **Surintendante Chevalmarin** (1): the two
together took 39 → 32, seven damage from two cards whose printed text is
"Summon" and "Summon Chevalmarin", full stop. Then Tidal Flourish+ (12) and
Stage Presence (6 Block) against an 8.

*Rejected:* Lynette — Enigmatic Feint again, same reasoning, same single
target, no aura at the time of the choice.

**Turn 2.** Enemy 13. Played Understudy — and **it did nothing at all**. Draw
pile and exhaust moved; the enemy did not. The likeliest reading is that all
three performers were already on stage, so "a random performer who is not on
stage" had nobody to pick, and a zero-cost card evaporated. Nothing printed
said so. Then Tidal Flourish (9, weakened by my Weak 1 to 6) → 7,
Soloist's Solicitation, and **Standing Ovation** — which I played half as a
test, because "Raise 5 Fanfare on the **back** performer" feeds the seat that
the glossary says "nothing hits", while "Attacks hit your Block, then the
**lead** performer's Fanfare". Fight ended before I could see it pay.

**Verdict:** the thinnest fight of the eight. Turn 1 had a real choice (rebuild
stage versus block-and-swing); turn 2 was cleanup. Zero HP lost.

**Reward: Scene Change+** (cost 0: "Rotate the cast: the front performer moves
to the back seat. Draw 1 card"), taken over Ebb and Flow+ (Encore again) and
Shared Billing+ (Spotlighted again). This was the pick that finally connected
two printed rules to each other: Standing Ovation raises the back, attacks hit
the front, and **Rotate** ("Seats change order and every bar comes with them.
Nobody leaves and nobody takes a Bow") is the card that moves a stocked bar
into the line of fire. That is a good design, and the run took three fights and
a reward screen to show me the third leg of it.

## Floor 4 — Abyssal Baths

Immerse (+2 Max HP, take 3) versus Abstain (heal 10). At 62/78 and three

## Fight 4 — Calcified Cultist 39/39 and Damp Cultist 52/52

Both opened **Empower (Buff)** — no incoming damage at all on turn one. That
single printed line converted the turn from a defensive one to an all-out
offensive one, and it is the cleanest example in the run of the screen giving
me the information a decision needs.

**Turn 1.** Scene Change+ (0, a pure draw with one performer on stage — rotate
is a no-op at a cast of one), then **Grand Entrance on B** (Spend 5: 20), then
Tidal Flourish.

And here is where I paid for not reading carefully enough. Grand Entrance's
Spend 5 emptied the Usher, who **Bowed** — I got exactly the printed **4
Block** — and that left **no stage**. So Tidal Flourish, played immediately
after, fired at its base **5, not 9**: A went 39→34 and B went 52→27
(20 + 5). *Spend* prints "No stage, no rider", and I had emptied the stage with
the previous card. **That is a real decision I got wrong, and the screen told
me in advance.** Correct line was Tidal first (9), Grand Entrance second.

*Rejected:* Stage Presence and Regal Bearing — no incoming damage, so block was
strictly wasted.

**Turn 2.** With the stage empty, the enemies took **zero** damage between
turns — direct confirmation that the 5-to-8 chip I had been seeing every turn
was the performers acting. Meanwhile B printed **Ritual 5** ("At the end of its
turn, gains 5 Strength"), so the clock was now real. I spent the whole turn
rebuilding: Understudy (0), Mademoiselle Crabaletta, Salon Début, and Freminet
on B. Three deploys, one attack.

*Rejected:* Lynette — Enigmatic Feint, for the third and last time. *This was
the fight's central decision and it was made across the fight, not on a turn*:
give up a whole turn of face damage to put three bodies back on stage, because
an empty stage had just proved to be worth about 14 damage a turn and an
unknown but clearly large amount of absorbed damage.

It paid. **Turn 3 I took 17 points of telegraphed damage and lost zero HP** —
no Block on the screen, no HP change. The bars ate all of it.

**Turn 3** also contained the run's flattest moment. I played **Bis!+** ("The
lead performer performs its act now") with a full stage, and **nothing
observable happened**: no damage, no Block, no HP change, no aura change,
`What reacted this turn` empty, only the discard count moving. Then Curtain
Rise killed B, and Chevalmarin and Standing Ovation banked bars.

**Turn 4.** A at 16, and Freminet's face now carried a line I had not seen
before: *"Reaction preview: Frozen — Hydro meets Cryo: its next action deals
half damage, and until it acts the first Attack to hit it Shatters for 6
damage."* That preview is excellent and it made the turn trivial to plan:
Freminet (6, applies Frozen) then Soloist's Solicitation (6 + 6 Shatter = 12).
6 + 12 = 18 into 16. Dead.

**Reward:** I took **Interposition** (5 Block, Spend 2: 10) over
**Macaron Break** — which was the first and, until the very end of the run, the
only card I was ever shown that *grants* Encore ("Gain 2 Encore. Gain 2
Block."). I passed on it and I will own the consequence: I finished the run
never having seen the Encore number move off 0.

## Floor 6 — the shop, and the sentence that should have been on turn one

Bought **Fortissimo Guard** (73) and a **Card Removal** (75), removing
**Lynette — Enigmatic Feint** — the only card I had rejected on sight in every
hand it appeared in.

On the removal screen, printed as a gloss under Fortissimo Guard, was this:

> **Salon** — Your Salon holds 3 members. Deploying into a full stage bows the
> OLDEST member out for its payoff. The leftmost member bows first. Member
> numbers gain +1 per 10 Fanfare you hold; a member with no Encore to spend
> acts at three-quarters. You have 0 on stage.

That is the rule of the whole kit — the roster size, the eviction order, the
scaling, and the three-quarters clause — **and it appeared for the first time
on a shop screen, on floor 6, after four fights.** Not one combat screen in
fights 1–4 printed it. I had been playing the stage by arithmetic on enemy HP
bars for four fights.

## Fight 5 — Two-Tailed Rat ×3, HP 21 / 17 / 20

**Turn 1.** Scene Change+ (0), Surintendante Chevalmarin, then **Bis!+ — which
this time visibly worked**: Block went 0 → **3**. Read against the Salon
gloss ("a member with no Encore to spend acts at three-quarters") and the Bow
list ("Usher: 4 Block"), 3 is exactly three-quarters of the Usher's 4. So the
Usher's *act* is 4 Block and it acted at 3/4 for want of Encore. That reading
also explains fight 4's blank Bis!+: the lead there was very likely Chevalmarin,
whose act is Hydro-on-all, onto bodies that already wore Hydro — a real effect
with no visible consequence. I state this as my best reconstruction; the screen
never said either thing.

Then Tidal Flourish (9 to all) and Regal Bearing. Took 7.

**Turn 2, the fight's decision.** Rats on 8, 4 and 7 with 3 energy — I could
kill at most two outright, so the question was which body to leave alive and
whether to trust the performers to finish it. Curtain Rise 13 killed A;
Freminet 6 killed B; Soloist 6 put C on 1, and I left it there deliberately,
because the performers had chipped every enemy every turn since floor 1. They
did — C came round on 1 HP intending to **Summon**, which is the exact case
where being wrong would have cost a lot, and one Soloist finished it.
*Rejected:* spending the third card on Block instead and leaving C on 7; the
Summon intent would then have doubled the fight.

**Reward:** Commanding Gaze (2 Block, 1 Weak to ALL), over Command Performance
(two random Uncommon Companions — my companion cards had been the best cards in
the deck, but random) and Slip Backstage ("Spend 5 Encore, **or HP for any
shortfall**. Gain 10 Block" — with Encore permanently 0 that is 5 HP for 10
Block, readable and bad).

## Fight 6 — ELITE: Phantasmal Gardener ×4, HP 31 / 28 / 27 / 29

Every one with **Skittish 6**: "The first time Phantasmal Gardener is hit each
turn, it gains 6 Block." 115 HP across four bodies, ~15 incoming a turn, me on
60/78. This is the fight where the kit was under real pressure, and it is where
most of the defects surfaced.

**Turn 1.** Scene Change+, Mademoiselle Crabaletta, Understudy, Surintendante
Chevalmarin, Bis!+, Soloist on C. Three deploys, and here is the first thing
that did not add up: the entrance hits (A took 7, B/C/D took 2 each) **did not
produce Block on any Gardener** — no enemy Block was printed anywhere on the
screen. I could not tell whether Skittish had fired and been spent, or had not
fired on non-card damage, or simply was not being displayed.

*Rejected:* opening with attacks. Against Skittish, one AoE per turn is the
worst possible shape — four separate 6-Block triggers off one card — so the
plan across the fight was **two hits per body per turn or none**, and turn one
went entirely into bodies on stage.

**Turn 2.** I spent the turn on **Fortissimo Guard** (cost 2, "Whenever you
deploy a Salon Member, gain 5 Block") plus Stage Presence, giving up all face
damage for a defensive engine, on the reasoning that with 2–3 deploys a turn it
would be 10–15 Block a turn for the rest of a long fight. That was the right
call on the printed text. **It never once fired.** See the contradictions
section — I isolated it three separate times.

**Turn 3, the best turn of the run.** Gardeners on 20 / 22 / 12 / 18, Skittish
blunting every single hit by 6. Freminet's *Reaction preview* said Frozen was
live. So: **Freminet on C** — 6 into a 6-Block Skittish trigger is 0 face
damage, and I played it anyway, purely to plant Frozen and its Shatter. Then
**Curtain Rise on C** — 13 + 6 Shatter = 19 into a 12 HP body wearing 6 Block.
**C died.** A card that "did nothing" set up a kill that nothing else in my
hand could have made. That is the kit at its best, and it is a decision made
across two plays, not on one.

Then Interposition for what should have been 10 Block.

**Turn 4.** HP 35, three Gardeners on 18 / 20 / 16, and now the Skittish maths
inverted: with two AoE cards in hand, the *second* AoE lands clean. Salon Début
(deploy), **Tidal Flourish+ (12 all)**, **Tidal Flourish (9 all)** — first one
eats the Skittish Block, second one lands full. A died; B fell to 8 and D to 4,
both showing **Block 1**. *Rejected:* Stage Presence for 6 Block instead of the
second Tidal — it would have left all three alive with a fresh Skittish trigger
each next turn, which is how this fight kills you.

**Turn 5.** B on 8, D on 4. Grand Entrance (20) killed B outright through its
Skittish Block. I deliberately did **not** point Soloist's 6 at D, because
D's first-hit-each-turn Block is 6 and 6−6 is nothing; I played Commanding Gaze
instead for the Weak and 2 Block, and left D for next turn. That is a small
decision but it is a real one, and it is made entirely off the Skittish line.

**Turn 6.** Tidal Flourish+ (12, minus D's 6 Skittish Block = 6 into 4). Dead.
Elite down at **26/78**.

**Reward:** Kusarigama, a Poison Potion, and **Crashing Waves** ("Deal 8 damage
to ALL enemies. **+5 damage if the enemy has an elemental aura**"). Taken
instantly: my own summons paint Hydro on every enemy in the room every turn, so
that card is a reliable 13 AoE for 1 energy. It is the single best interaction
in the run, and it is the one place where the companions' hidden aura-spraying
becomes an *advantage* I can plan around instead of a surprise.

## Fight 7 — ELITE: Skulking Colony, HP 75/75

**Hardened Shell 20 of 20 left this turn: "Skulking Colony cannot lose more
than 20 HP each turn."** Attacks for 14. That is the most interesting printed
constraint in the run, because it inverts the normal question: overkill is
literally wasted, so the correct play is *exactly 20 damage and every remaining
scrap of energy into defence*, for four turns straight, and knowing that turns
each turn into an optimisation instead of a dump.

**Turn 1.** Scene Change+, **Bis!+ → Block 3** (the Usher's three-quarters act
again, reproducible). Then Surintendante Chevalmarin, Freminet on A, Commanding
Gaze. Took **zero** of the 14.

I had *intended* Chevalmarin's Hydro to land before Freminet's Cryo so the two
would react into Frozen and halve the Colony's attack. They did not: after both
resolved the Colony wore **Hydro Aura 1** and no Frozen, and Freminet's face had
printed no Reaction preview when I played it. The summon's entrance aura
appears to resolve *after* the card that follows it, even though the summon
card was played first. Recorded, not explained.

**Turn 2.** The Shell maths: Tidal Flourish+ (12) + Tidal Flourish (9) = 21,
which caps at exactly 20, for two energy — and the third energy went to Stage
Presence rather than a third attack that the cap would have eaten whole.
65 → 45, precisely 20. *Rejected:* Soloist's Solicitation as the third card;
its 6 damage would have been 100% wasted against the cap, and 6 Block was not.

**Turn 3.** I spent the whole turn on Fortissimo Guard + two deploys to
re-test the suspected defect under clean conditions (see below), plus Regal
Bearing. Cost me a turn of the cap; bought the run's clearest finding.

**Turn 4.** Crashing Waves (8 + 5 for the aura = 13) + Curtain Rise (13) = 26,
capped to 20 → 36 → 16, plus Interposition for Block. **Turn 5:** Grand
Entrance to finish — and it dealt **10, not 20** (16 → 6), then Tidal Flourish+
killed it. Elite two down at **29/78**.

**Reward:** Anchor (start each combat with 10 Block), a Strength Potion, and a
card choice of Casting Call / Warm Reception / **Stagehands**. Took Stagehands,
knowingly as the tester's pick again: it is the only card in the run that
generates **Encore** ("Whenever a Salon Member takes its final bow, gain 5
Block. Whenever a Salon Member takes its final bow, gain 2 Encore"), and I
wanted to see the number move.

## Fight 8 — Fossil Stalker, HP 52/52

**Suck 3: "Whenever Fossil Stalker deals unblocked attack damage, it gains 3
Strength."** Intent 12. That is a hard, legible ultimatum: block *completely*
or lose the fight slowly. At 29 HP it was the tensest opening of the run and
also the easiest to plan.

**Turn 1.** Anchor gave 10 Block for free. **Commanding Gaze** was the key
card: Weak takes 12 down to 9, and 9 < 10, so the whole attack lands on Block
and Suck never triggers. Then Stagehands and Regal Bearing on top (15 Block).
Took **zero**, and the Stalker gained **no Strength** — the plan held exactly
as printed. *Rejected:* opening with damage; against Suck, one leaked point on
turn one compounds for the whole fight.

**Turn 2.** Scene Change+, Understudy, **Crashing Waves** (8 + 5 for the Hydro
my own summons had painted = 13), Mademoiselle Crabaletta, Stage Presence.
48 → 23, zero damage taken again.

**Turn 3.** Bis!+, then Freminet + Soloist + Curtain Rise — three attacks in
one turn, which is also **Kusarigama**'s trigger for 6 more. Dead. **Fight
eight cost zero HP.**

Stagehands sat in play for the whole fight and **Encore stayed at 0**, because
no member ever "took its final bow" — with no Spend-heavy card drawn, nothing
emptied a bar. So the card I drafted to see Encore move showed me instead that
Encore is gated behind a trigger that a normal turn does not produce.

**Reward:** a third choice, and the naming collision below. Took a second
**Crashing Waves**.

## What I could not read, or that contradicted itself

Quoting the printed text in each case.

**1. There are two different things called Fanfare, and both are on screen at
once.** The persistent glossary says:

> **Fanfare** — A performer's own bar. Attacks hit your Block, then the lead
> performer's Fanfare, then you. No cap.

The status line, once it appears, says:

> **Fanfare 4 (buff)** — Generated by losing HP, and by spending, absorbing or
> paying out Encore — and by Center Stage plays. Cards read it; nothing spends

## The kit, after 8 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Four kinds, and they are genuinely different from each other.

*On the turn.* The reaction lines are the best thing in the kit. Fight 1's
"cheap attack first to collect the Shatter, expensive attack second" and fight
6's "play a card that deals literally zero face damage, because it plants
Frozen and the Shatter is worth more than the 6 I'm giving up" are both real,
both decided by printed text, and the second one is a genuinely good decision
that a mediocre player would not find. Fight 7's Hardened Shell ("cannot lose
more than 20 HP each turn") is the same kind of thing from the other direction:
it made *how much* damage a resource to spend precisely rather than dump, and
turned every turn of a five-turn fight into an optimisation. Fight 8's
Suck-plus-Weak line — 12 damage, Weak makes it 9, Anchor's Block is 10, so the
attack lands entirely on Block and the enemy's snowball never starts — is the
cleanest single turn I played.

*Earlier in the fight.* The stage-versus-tempo trade. Fight 4 turn 2 I gave up
a whole turn of face damage to put three bodies back on stage after Grand
Entrance had emptied it, on the evidence that an empty stage had just cost me
about 14 damage a turn. That is a real, uncomfortable, multi-turn choice and it
is the best thing about the performer system. The same trade appears inverted
in fight 6 turn 2, where I spent a turn on Fortissimo Guard for a defensive
engine — the reasoning was right and the card was broken, but the *decision*
was real.

*Ordering, which is really a sub-case of the above and deserves calling out
because I got it wrong.* Spend cards eat the stage. Playing Grand Entrance
before Tidal Flourish in fight 4 quietly downgraded Tidal from 9 to 5, because
the first card's Spend emptied the last performer and "No stage, no rider". The
screen told me in advance. That is a good rule, sharply punished, and legible
if you read.

*At the draft.* Scene Change+ was the pick that made three separate printed
rules (raise the back / attacks hit the front / rotate moves bars) into one
mechanism. Crashing Waves was the pick that turned my summons' otherwise
unprinted aura-spraying into a plan. Fight 2's whole fight was decided at the
draft in the sense that Tidal Flourish's AoE is what let me choose *which*
Corpse Slug would eat the other.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

Automatic: the summon cards. Understudy, Salon Début, Surintendante Chevalmarin
and Mademoiselle Crabaletta are all "play this, it is free or nearly free and
always good", and I played every one of them on sight in every fight without
ever weighing it. There is no cost to a deploy and no board state where a
deploy is wrong, so it is not a decision, it is upkeep — which is a shame,
because the *evictions* the Salon gloss describes ("Deploying into a full stage
bows the OLDEST member out for its payoff. The leftmost member bows first")
sound like they should make deploy order a real puzzle. I never saw a single
screen that would let me play that puzzle.

Never worth playing: **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint** ("Swirl an enemy's aura onto
ALL enemies. Gain 5 Block") — Swirl explicitly does nothing with no aura, and
against one enemy it does nothing even with an aura, so it is a strictly worse
Stage Presence at the same cost. I rejected it in three separate fights and
removed it at the first opportunity. **Bis!+** is second: it produced 3 Block
twice and literal nothing once, and I never once had a turn where I would have
paid 1 for it.

Also, everything gated on Encore was unplayable-in-practice: five or six cards
I was offered are conditional on a number that read 0 for the entire act.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

The full list is the fifteen items above. The three that mattered most while
playing: **there are two different Fanfares and I never knew which one a card
meant**; **the stage is invisible on every combat screen**, so the entire
resource system had to be reverse-engineered from enemy HP deltas, and the one
paragraph that explains it appears on a shop screen on floor 6; and
**Fortissimo Guard, which I paid 73 gold and a whole elite turn for, never
fires** — I isolated that four ways.

Honestly: for four fights I did not know whether the performers existed. I
knew damage was going somewhere other than my HP and I knew the enemies were
losing 5–14 between turns, and I built a mental model out of arithmetic. When
the shop finally printed the Salon rule, my model turned out to be roughly
right, which says the *design* is coherent. The screen is what is failing it.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint**, for the reasons in (b). I paid 75
gold to delete it.

Happiest to draw: **Crashing Waves** — "Deal 8 damage to ALL enemies. +5 damage
if the enemy has an elemental aura." Thirteen AoE for one energy, and reliably
thirteen, because my own deck paints Hydro on every body in the room every
turn. It is the one card where the kit's hidden machinery becomes something I
can aim. Runner-up: **Scene Change+**, for making the seats mean something.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

Yes, and a good one, but only about half of it was legible.

The legible half: five cards, three energy, one enemy telegraphing 11 into an
opening HP of 62/78. Two Stage Presence blanks the hit and does nothing to a 45
HP body; Freminet plus one Stage Presence takes a couple of points and starts a
clock; and Lynette — Enigmatic Feint is rejectable on sight off its own Swirl
gloss. That is three distinguishable lines with a real trade between them, on
turn one, with no prior knowledge required. Good.

The illegible half: **Standing Ovation**, the card I actually led with, was a
coin flip. "Raise 5 Fanfare on the back performer" against a header reading
`Fanfare: 0` and a relic promising an Usher at 3 — I played it not knowing
whether it would do anything at all, and after it resolved the screen looked
identical. I only learned it had worked at the *end of the enemy's turn*, by
noticing my HP had not moved.

## The five debrief questions

**1. On turn one of fight one, which line, and why.**

**Standing Ovation → Freminet — Pers, Deploy! → Stage Presence.**

The reasoning, at the time: 11 incoming, 3 energy, and a 45 HP enemy that one
6-damage attack was never going to kill quickly, so a pure-block turn was a
turn spent going backwards. Six Block from Stage Presence covers most of the 11;
Standing Ovation's 5 Fanfare was my bet that the relic's Usher was real and that
the leftover damage would land in his bar rather than on me; and Freminet both
starts the clock and paints Cryo, which is the setup for everything the kit does
well. It worked — zero HP lost — but I want to be exact about what I knew: I
chose Standing Ovation *hoping* the bar existed, because the header said
`Fanfare: 0` and no stage was shown. I rejected the double-Stage-Presence line
(safe, does nothing) and Lynette — Enigmatic Feint (a 5-Block card next to a
6-Block card, with a Swirl clause that explicitly does nothing here).

**2. Did you ever spend a performer at 1 on purpose, and did that feel like a
play or a loophole.**

I could never see a bar, so I could never *know* I was spending one at 1. What I
did do repeatedly, and on purpose, is fire a Spend card into what I believed was
a nearly-empty stage to collect the **Bow** — most deliberately in fight 4 turn
1, playing Grand Entrance (Spend 5) knowing the Usher held about 3, expecting
"it fires in full even if the bar is short" plus the Usher's 4 Block on the way
out. That is exactly what happened: 20 damage and `Block 4`.

And it read as a **loophole**, clearly. "It fires in full even if the bar is
short" means a bar at 1 buys the same 20 damage as a bar at 5, *plus* a departure
bonus. There is no cost to underpaying, so the "Spend" is not a cost, it is a
sacrifice you are rewarded for. The only thing that made it feel like a play at
all was the *sequencing* punishment on the next card — Tidal Flourish dropping
from 9 to 5 because the stage was now empty. That downstream cost is what a
Spend should feel like, and it is invisible until you have already made the
mistake once.

(Fight 7's Grand Entrance dealing 10 rather than 20 cuts the other way and I
cannot reconcile it with the same printed sentence.)

**3. Did you want a second performer on stage, and what for.**

Yes, constantly, and for three distinct reasons that I worked out at different
points in the run.

First, **free damage**: from fight 2 onward the enemies lost 5–14 HP between my
turns and the amount tracked how many bodies I had deployed. Fight 4 round 2
proved it by absence — with the stage emptied by Grand Entrance, the Cultists
took **zero** between turns.

Second, **absorption**: fight 4 round 3 I ate 17 points of telegraphed damage
with no Block on screen and lost **zero HP**. Bodies on stage are the kit's real
defensive layer, more than any Block card in my deck.

Third, and this is the reason I would give if I only got one: **a second body is
insurance against my own Spend cards**. One performer means the first Grand
Entrance strips the stage and every Spend rider behind it downgrades to base. A
second and third body mean I can Spend aggressively and still have a rider.

What I wanted and never got was a *reason to prefer a particular* second
performer. The Salon gloss promises one — eviction order, leftmost bows first,
Crabaletta's 8-damage Bow versus the Usher's 4 Block versus Chevalmarin's
Hydro-on-all — and with no stage display I could never choose between them. So
the answer is: I wanted a second performer badly, and I never once wanted a
*specific* one.

**4. Which performer did you protect, and could you say why in one sentence.**

I protected **the Gentilhomme Usher**, in the sense that I twice declined to
fire a Spend card that I thought would empty him, and I can say why in one
sentence: **he is the only performer I ever had proof about** — the relic puts
him on stage at 3 Fanfare in every combat, his Bow is a printed 4 Block, and
Bis!+ produced exactly 3 Block twice, which is three-quarters of that 4, so he
is the one body whose numbers I could actually check.

That is a confession as much as an answer. I did not protect him because he was
worth protecting; I protected him because he was the only one I could see.

**5. Did the three read as three, or as one Osty three times.**

**Neither, and I think that is the finding.** They did not read as three, and
they did not read as one thing repeated — they read as **one anonymous pool of
stage-stuff** with three names attached to it that I could never connect to
anything happening on screen.

The design clearly intends three: the **Bow** gloss draws them as sharply
distinct — "Usher: 4 Block. Chevalmarin: Hydro on all. Crabaletta: 8 damage" —
one defensive, one elemental, one offensive, which is a good, legible trio, and
two of them even have dedicated cards (Surintendante Chevalmarin, Mademoiselle
Crabaletta) that summon them by name. On paper that is three characters.

In play it collapsed. I never saw who was on stage, so "the lead performer" was
an anonymous slot; Spend paid from whoever happened to be in it; Bis! made
"the lead performer" act and I could not tell you which of the three acted or
what two of the three even do. I inferred Chevalmarin's existence entirely from
Hydro auras appearing on all enemies after a summon, and Crabaletta's I never
confirmed at all — I never once observed her 8-damage Bow.

So: three names, one undifferentiated resource. The one moment they *nearly*
read as three was the shop's Salon gloss, which describes an eviction queue
where **which** member is leftmost decides whether you get 4 Block or 8 damage
— and that is a genuinely good three-body puzzle that the combat screen gives
the player no way to play.
