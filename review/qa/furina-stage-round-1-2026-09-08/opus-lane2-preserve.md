### The six extra cards, as printed

The brief told me six extra cards were seeded into the starting deck. Reading
the opening deck off the screens, the six that are plainly not generic
Furina basics — the ones carrying a performer's proper name, or a face no other
copy of the deck repeats — are:

1. **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint** — cost 1, skill — "Swirl an enemy's aura onto
   ALL enemies. Gain 5 Block."
2. **Freminet — Pers, Deploy!** [Cryo] — cost 1, attack — "Deal 6 damage."
3. **Gentilhomme Usher** — cost 1, skill — "Summon Usher. If he is already on
   stage, Raise 3 on him instead."
4. **Salon Début** — cost 1, skill — "Summon a random performer who is not on
   stage."
5. **Let the People Rejoice** — cost 2, attack — "Spend all Fanfare on stage.
   Deal that much damage to ALL enemies. Every performer takes a Bow, then
   returns at 1. Exhaust."
6. **Scene Change** — cost 0, skill — "Rotate the cast: the front performer
   moves to the back seat."

I want to be honest that this list is my inference from printed faces, not
something the game told me. Nothing on any screen marked a card as added. The
other candidates I weighed and rejected were Pneuma Refrain, Ousia Surge,
Standing Ovation, Warm Reception and Curtain Rise, all of which appeared in
multiples or read as the character's own spine.

### Neow pick

**Silver Crucible** — "The first 3 card rewards you see are Upgraded. The first
Treasure Chest you open is empty."

Why: it was the only one of the three whose value I could read off the screen
and act on. Arcane Scroll hands you a random rare you do not choose. Lead
Paperweight came with the page's own confession printed under it — *"this
option's own words promise a card and the feed carried no face for it -- no
rules text, no cost, no type -- so this page can offer it by name only"* — which
is asking a blind seat to buy a sealed box. Crucible upgrades three cards I pick
myself, and the empty chest is a cost paid nine floors later. It duly paid: the
chest on floor 9 printed "(nothing here to take)".

## Fight 1 — Seapunk, 45/45

Opened HP 62/78, 3 energy, 11 cards behind the hand.

**Turn 1.** Hand: Standing Ovation, Lynette — Enigmatic Feint, Stage Presence
×2, Freminet — Pers, Deploy!. Intent: Attack for 11.

I played **Standing Ovation** first ("Raise 5 Fanfare on the back performer"),
specifically to watch the counter move, because the header said `Fanfare: 0`
while the relic said I start "with the Gentilhomme Usher in the front seat at 3
Fanfare". It did not move. After the play the header still read `Fanfare: 0`.
That was the first thing this run taught me and it never stopped being true.

Then **Freminet — Pers, Deploy!** on Seapunk (6, applies Cryo) and **Stage
Presence** (6 Block), and ended. *Rejected:* the second Stage Presence, i.e.
12 Block and a fully blanked 11. I rejected it because a 45 HP enemy and a hand
of Block is a fight I lose by attrition, and 6 damage now was worth 5 HP later.

**Outcome disagreed with the screen, in my favour.** I ended the turn with 6
Block against an 11-damage attack and took **zero**. HP stayed 62. Something
absorbed 5 damage and no readout on the page moved to show it. Reading the
glossary — "Attacks hit your Block, then the lead performer's Fanfare, then
you" — the Usher's 3 from the relic plus Standing Ovation's 5 ate it. That is
the whole mechanic working correctly and being completely invisible.

**Turn 2.** Hand: Pneuma Refrain, Gentilhomme Usher, Curtain Rise, Let the
People Rejoice, Salon Début. Intent: 2×4.

I played **Salon Début** ("Summon a random performer who is not on stage") to
find out whether the page would name who arrived. It did not name anyone. What
it *did* print was this, under "What reacted this turn":

> **Frozen** on **Seapunk**, off Furina.

and the enemy went 39 → 34, and a meter appeared in the header that had not
existed a moment earlier:

> **Furina Burst: 5/70** — the game's data feed carries this meter's amount and
> its maximum, and no rule for how it is spent

So a card whose entire printed text is "Summon a random performer who is not on
stage" applied Hydro, triggered an Elemental Reaction, dealt 5 damage, and
switched on a meter — none of which is on its face, and all of which contradicts
the rule the same screen prints: *"An element comes from a CARD that prints one
and from nothing else: a potion, a relic or an enemy applies none unless its own
face says so."*

Then **Curtain Rise** on the frozen Seapunk. 34 → 15, i.e. 19: the printed 13
from "Spend 3: deal 13 instead" plus Shatter's 6. That is the one moment in the
run where a card, a debuff and a printed number all agreed with each other, and
it felt good. Then **Gentilhomme Usher** to put the bar back.

*Rejected:* Let the People Rejoice, because I could not price it — it spends
"all Fanfare on stage" and no screen would tell me what that number was.

**Turn 3.** 15 HP left, enemy buffing and blocking. I played **Ousia Surge**
("Deal damage equal to the lead performer's Fanfare") purely as a probe: it dealt
**3**, which told me the lead's bar was 3, which no page had told me. Then
Soloist's Solicitation ×2 finished it.

**Verdict on the fight:** two real decisions (turn 1's damage-vs-block trade,
turn 2's freeze-then-Curtain-Rise), and the fight's shape came from a plan set a
turn earlier. Turn 3 was a lethal the plan had earned.

**Reward.** Three upgraded cards, and two of the three referenced machinery the
game had never shown me: **Bis!+** ("The lead performer performs its act now" —
nothing had ever told me what a performer's *act* is) and **The Gallery
Stirs+** ("The first time you spend Encore each turn, draw 2 cards" — Encore had
read 0 on every screen and I had never gained, spent or seen any). I took
**Commanding Gaze+** ("Gain 2 Block. Apply 2 Weak to ALL enemies") because it was
the only one I could price. That is the pick, and it is also the finding: two
thirds of my first reward were unreadable.

## Fight 2 — Corpse Slug ×2, 27/27 and 25/25

Both carried "Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it,
becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength." That is a good printed puzzle: killing
one is both a reward and a cost, and the cost is legible.

**Turn 1.** Regal Bearing on A (3 Block, 1 Weak: 8 → 6), Salon Début, Freminet
on A. Salon Début again dealt ~2 to *both* bodies and put Hydro on them, and
Freminet's Cryo then Froze A. *Rejected:* Stage Presence's flat 6 Block, because
Weak on the attacker plus the Fanfare buffer was cheaper than raw Block.

**Turn 2.** The decision of the fight, and it was a real one. A was at 17, B at
21, both Ravenous. I played **Curtain Rise** on A (17 → 4) then **Soloist's
Solicitation** to kill A — deliberately feeding B, because the printed text says
the eater is **Stunned**, and buying a free turn was worth handing B +4 Strength.
It worked exactly as printed: B showed "Intent: Stunned (Stun)" and "Strength 4".
*Rejected:* killing neither and blocking. That was a decision made on the turn,
off text I could read, and it is the best-designed moment the base game supplied.

**Turn 3–4.** Blocked through the stun, then **Curtain Rise killed B from 11**,
which told me the "Spend 3: deal 13" rider had fired. Fight won at 61/78.

**Reward:** took **Tidal Flourish+** ("Deal 8 damage to ALL enemies. Spend 2:
deal 12 instead") over The House Rises+ (gated on "at least 5 Encore", a
resource I still had never seen move) and Supporting Cast+ ("The first
Spotlighted card each turn draws 2 cards" — *Spotlighted* is never defined on
any screen in this run).

## Fight 3 — Sludge Spinner, 39/39

**Turn 1.** All damage: Tidal Flourish+ (12 to all), Soloist, Freminet. 39 → 15.
*Rejected:* Gentilhomme Usher's Raise 3, i.e. 3 points of persistent absorption
against 6 points of damage; in a 39 HP race the damage wins.

**And then the run's central defect surfaced.** Next turn the header read:

> - Fanfare: 3 — defined under *Words on this screen*
> - **Fanfare 3 (buff)** — Generated by losing HP, and by spending, absorbing or
>   paying out Encore — and by Center Stage plays. Cards read it; nothing spends
>   it. It fades by 20% at the start of each of your turns, never below the
>   baseline your cards have built.

while the same screen's glossary still said:

> **Fanfare** — A performer's own bar. Attacks hit your Block, then the lead
> performer's Fanfare, then you. No cap.

Two different things called Fanfare, on one page, at the same time. One is a
per-performer bar that is Raised and Spent and eats attacks. The other is a
player-side buff that "nothing spends", is generated by *losing HP*, and decays
20% a turn. The header number is the second one. It had read 0 for two and a half
fights not because my bars were empty but because **the header never shows the
bars at all**.

**Turn 2 proved it the expensive way.** I played **Ousia Surge** — "Deal damage
equal to the lead performer's Fanfare" — while the header said `Fanfare: 3`. It
dealt **zero**. Enemy stayed at 15/39. The card reads the invisible number, the
header shows the visible one, and they are not the same number. That is one
energy thrown away on a card doing exactly what it says while the screen tells
me something else.

**Turn 3–4.** Blocked, then Curtain Rise + Salon Début took it to 3, and it
**died on its own turn** with me holding 1 energy and no attack played — 3 damage
arriving from something the "What reacted this turn" block did not name.

**Reward:** took **Shared Billing+** for element access, over Ebb and Flow+
("Spend 1 Encore. Gain 4 Encore." — from a pool that had been 0 all run) and
Scene Change+.

## Fight 4 — Calcified Cultist 39/39 + Damp Cultist 52/52

Both opened on Empower, so turn 1 cost me nothing and was a pure draft-and-stage
turn — a real decision made by the *absence* of a threat.

**Turn 1.** Shared Billing+ (cost 0, gains 1 energy, so the turn was 4 cards
deep), Lynette — Enigmatic Feint aimed at A, Salon Début, Standing Ovation,
Soloist. **My Feint did nothing and it was my mistake, printed:** Shared Billing
"Apply Hydro to a **random** enemy" put the aura on B; I aimed the Swirl at A,
which was bare, and "Swirl — The enemy's aura is consumed and copied onto ALL
enemies. **No aura, no effect.**" Two randomised cards that need to chain, with
no way to see the first one's result before committing the second — that is a
real trap and I walked into it.

**Turn 2.** **Pneuma Refrain** ("Gain Block equal to the back performer's
Fanfare") gave exactly **6 Block**, which is how I learned the back bar was 6.
This is the run's recurring shape: the only instrument for reading the stage is
to fire a card at it and infer the number from the result.

**Turn 3 — the best turn of the run, and it was planned.** Both cultists carried
Ritual (A +2 Strength a turn, B **+5**), so time was against me absolutely. I
played **Warm Reception** ("Raise 3 Fanfare on the back performer. Draw 1 card")
*into* **Let the People Rejoice** ("Spend all Fanfare on stage. Deal that much
damage to ALL enemies. Every performer takes a Bow, then returns at 1"), pumping
the pool the second card empties. It hit for **11 to both** (A 14→3, B 46→35),
gave me **4 Block** (Usher's Bow) and put Hydro on everything (Chevalmarin's
Bow) — so the stage was Usher + Chevalmarin, which I only learned by watching
their departure effects land. *Rejected:* Soloist plus two Block cards, taking 5
damage instead of 13. I took the 13 on purpose because Ritual 5 meant a slow
fight was a lost fight.

**Turns 4–7.** Killed A with Freminet, then ground B down while its Strength ran
2 → 5 → 25 and its intent ran 12 → 21 → 26. Finished it at 8 HP with Tidal
Flourish+. Ended 46/78.

**Reward:** **Interposition** ("Gain 5 Block. Spend 2: gain 10 instead").

## Fight 5 — Two-Tailed Rat ×3, 21/17/20

**Turn 1.** Tidal Flourish+ (12 to all), Soloist to kill the middle one, Stage
Presence. *Rejected:* Standing Ovation's bar-building — with three bodies
telegraphing 14, removing a body was worth more than a buffer.

**Turn 2.** Curtain Rise on A (9 HP) — **and it dealt 7, not 13.** A went 9 → 2.
That is the un-Spent number. Which means the printed rule

> **Spend** — Pays from the lead performer. **It fires in full even if the bar
> is short**; an emptied performer takes a Bow. No stage, no rider.

**is false.** The rider does not fire when the bar is short; it silently
degrades to the base number. I had believed that sentence and built a turn on
it, and the turn failed. This is the single most costly disagreement between
printed text and outcome in the run, and it recurred: **Grand Entrance dealt 10
and not 20** on a body at 19 HP two turns later, and **Tidal Flourish+ dealt 8
and not 12** in the same fight, while both fired their upgraded numbers in other
fights where the bar happened to be full.

The consequence is worth stating plainly: **whether my attacks do 7 or 13, 8 or
12, 10 or 20, 5 or 10 Block, is decided every single turn by a number the game
will not show me.** That is not a hard decision, it is a coin I am not allowed to
look at.

**Turns 3–5.** The rats summoned (A summoned D at 19, C intended another), Warm
Reception into Let the People Rejoice only mustered 6 to all because the bars had
been emptied, and I dropped from 42 to 24. Cleared it on turn 5 with **Tidal
Flourish+ into Curtain Rise** — 8 to all killed A and left D on 1, Curtain Rise
finished it. Two-card lethal, set up the turn before.

**Reward:** skipped. My deck was 18 cards and none of Command Performance,
Commanding Gaze (unupgraded — I had the +) or **Slip Backstage** ("Spend 5
Encore, **or HP for any shortfall**. Gain 10 Block") improved it. Slip Backstage
at 24 HP with a permanently-zero Encore pool is a card that reads "pay 5 HP for
10 Block", and I said no.

## Fight 6 (Elite) — Phantasmal Gardener ×4, 28/27/30/26

Every one carried "Skittish 6 — The first time Phantasmal Gardener is hit each
turn, it gains 6 Block." 111 HP across four bodies, 15 incoming, me at 47.

**This was the best-designed fight of the run and the decision was made at the
draft.** Skittish punishes hitting the same body twice and rewards hitting each
body exactly once, which makes **Tidal Flourish+** — one hit, everybody — not
just the best card but structurally the *correct* card. I bought and kept that
card three fights earlier; the elite is where the pick paid.

**Turn 1.** Tidal Flourish+, Commanding Gaze+ (2 Weak to ALL), Lynette Feint (5
Block). It hit for **12 each — 48 damage on one energy** — and I took zero.
*Rejected:* Grand Entrance, 2 energy for one blunted body.

**Turn 2.** A genuine trade, and the first time the Spend rule cut both ways:
**Interposition spends 2 from the lead** to give 10 instead of 5 — but that is
the same 2 that makes Tidal Flourish fire at 12 instead of 8. I chose Stage
Presence (6, no spend) + Pneuma Refrain (which returned **2**, telling me the
bar) over Interposition, deliberately protecting the bar for the next Tidal.
**That is the one decision in the whole run where the Fanfare economy created a
real tension**, and I could only make it because I had spent earlier turns
learning the bar's size by firing probes at it.

**Turn 3.** Warm Reception drew Let the People Rejoice; I spent everything for 6
to all and Usher's 4 Block, dropping to 36. *Rejected:* Standing Ovation and a
Block card, delaying Rejoice one turn for 13 instead of 6 — rejected because
they were gaining Strength each turn and D was at 8, in range.

**Turn 4.** Shared Billing+ → Salon Début (which again dealt ~2 to everything
and killed D) → Curtain Rise → Commanding Gaze+ → Gentilhomme Usher. Killed two.

**Turn 5 — the cleanest play I made.** Two left, 7 and 10, and my bar was in the
*back* seat because Gentilhomme Usher had Raised it there. So I played **Scene
Change** (cost 0, "the front performer moves to the back seat") to swap the
loaded performer into the lead, *then* Tidal Flourish+ — which now had 2 to
spend and fired at **12**, clearing both bodies at once. That is the kit at its
best: a free card, a hidden resource, and a rider, chained on purpose. It is
also the kit at its worst, because I could only do it by having reverse-engineered
where the bars were over six fights.

**Reward:** Kusarigama, a Poison Potion, and **Crashing Waves** ("Deal 8 damage
to ALL enemies. +5 damage if the enemy has an elemental aura") — taken over
Undercurrent and a second Interposition precisely because its number does not
depend on the invisible bar.

## Things I could not read, or that contradicted themselves

**1. Two different mechanics are both called Fanfare, on the same screen.**

> **Fanfare** — A performer's own bar. Attacks hit your Block, then the lead
> performer's Fanfare, then you. No cap.

> **Fanfare 6 (buff)** — Generated by losing HP, and by spending, absorbing or
> paying out Encore — and by Center Stage plays. Cards read it; nothing spends
> it. It fades by 20% at the start of each of your turns, never below the
> baseline your cards have built.

One is spent and absorbs attacks; the other explicitly *cannot* be spent and is
generated by taking damage. The header's `Fanfare: N` is the second. Cards that
say "Fanfare" mean the first. I lost an energy to this on fight 3 and never
fully trusted the header again.

**2. "It fires in full even if the bar is short" is false.** Observed both ways
in the same run: Curtain Rise 13 (fight 2, fight 7) and Curtain Rise **7**
(fights 5 and 8, at 9 HP and 9 HP targets); Tidal Flourish 12 (fights 4, 6) and
Tidal Flourish **8** (fight 5); Grand Entrance 20 (boss turn 2) and Grand
Entrance **10** (fight 5, on a 19 HP body); Interposition 10 (fight 7) and
Interposition **5**, and once **3**.

**3. The stage is never printed.** In nine fights no screen ever listed who was
on stage, in which seat, or with how much Fanfare. I learned the roster only from
Bow effects landing — "Usher: 4 Block. Chevalmarin: Hydro on all. Crabaletta: 8
damage" — and I learned bar sizes only by firing Pneuma Refrain or Ousia Surge at
them and reading the result backwards. **Every card that says "lead performer",
"back performer", "all Fanfare on stage" or "per Salon member" is a card played
blind.**

**4. A third, incompatible stage model, printed on one card only.** From the
Casting Call reward on floor 12:

> *Salon* — Your Salon holds **3 members**. Deploying into a full stage bows the
> **OLDEST** member out for its payoff. The **leftmost** member bows first.
> Member numbers gain +1 per 10 Fanfare you hold; a member with no Encore to
> spend acts at three-quarters. **You have 0 on stage.**

Three members, leftmost-and-oldest, scaling off the *player-buff* Fanfare, and
gated on Encore. Every other screen all run says two seats, front and back, lead
and reserve, with per-performer bars. And it said "You have 0 on stage" in a run
whose relic puts a member on stage at the start of every combat.

**5. Salon Début does four undocumented things.** Its whole face is "Summon a

## The kit, after 9 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Four, and I can name where each was made.

*At the draft:* taking **Tidal Flourish+** on floor 3 decided the elite on floor
11. Four Gardeners with "first hit each turn gains 6 Block" is a puzzle whose
answer is one-hit-per-body, and one card in the game is that. The fight had
almost no turn-level decisions and it was still one of the best fights in the
run, because the decision had already been made three floors earlier and I got to
watch it pay. Removing **Ousia Surge** at the shop was the same kind of choice
made in the other direction — cutting the card I could not read.

*Earlier in the fight:* the **Warm Reception → Let the People Rejoice** sequence
(fight 4 turn 3, fight 8 turn 1). Fill the pool on one card so the next card can
spend it, at the cost of a turn's defence against enemies gaining 5 Strength a
turn. That is a proper two-turn plan with a real price, and it is the thing the
kit is *for*.

*On the turn:* **Scene Change → Tidal Flourish+** at the elite. Rotating a loaded
performer into the lead so a Spend rider could fire is exactly the decision the
Raise-back/Spend-front split is designed to create. It cost 0 energy and won the
fight. It is also the only time in nine fights I was able to make it on purpose
rather than by luck, because it is the only time I happened to know where the
bars were.

*On the turn, from the enemy's text rather than mine:* killing the first Corpse
Slug to buy a Stun (fight 2), and turtling for a turn against Fossil Stalker's
Suck rather than let 1 damage through for a permanent +3 Strength (fight 7).
Both real, both created by the **enemy's** printed rule, not by Furina's.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

Automatic: **every Spend rider.** "Deal 7 damage. Spend 3: deal 13 instead" is
written as a choice and is not one — I never once wanted the 7, and the game
never asked me; it just quietly decided based on a number I could not see. The
same for Interposition's 5-or-10 and Tidal's 8-or-12. Four cards that look like
decisions and are actually a hidden die roll. **Stage Presence** is automatic in
the honest way a Defend is. **Shared Billing+** at cost 0 refunding an energy is
a free click.

Never worth playing: **Ousia Surge** (removed it — 0 and 3 damage from a card
whose number I cannot see). **Scene Change**, in eight of the nine fights, for
the same reason: rotating is only a decision if you know which seat holds what,
and I did not, so seven times out of eight it was a blank 0-cost card I skipped.
And **Pneuma Refrain**, which I ended up playing mostly as a *measuring
instrument* — I played it to learn the back bar's size, and the Block was
incidental. A card being useful chiefly as a diagnostic is a bad sign.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

The nine items above. If I have to pick the one that mattered: **the two
Fanfares, and the fact that the header shows the one the cards do not read.**
Everything else compounds from there — I cannot price Let the People Rejoice, I
cannot aim Ousia Surge, I cannot decide whether to spend on Interposition or save
for Tidal, I cannot tell whether Scene Change helps, because the single number
all of those cards depend on is not on the screen. Second: **"It fires in full
even if the bar is short" is simply not true**, and I built and lost a turn on
believing it.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Ousia Surge**. It is a card that asks me to know something the
game refuses to tell me, and both times I obeyed it I got 3 damage and 0 damage.
I paid 75 gold to delete it and I would do it again.

Happiest to draw: **Tidal Flourish+**. Not because it is strong — because it is
*legible*. 8 or 12 to everything, and even its worse number is a good card. It
won the elite and cleared the last two rats, and it never once asked me to
guess. The runner-up is **Let the People Rejoice**, which is the most exciting
card in the deck — a real payoff, real Bows, a real plan — and which I would put
first if I could ever see what it was going to do before I played it.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a good one, but not the one the kit thinks it was.** With 3 energy
against a Seapunk telegraphing 11, I had 12 Block available in two Stage
Presences, or 6 Block plus 6 damage, or a persistent 5 Fanfare from Standing
Ovation. I chose Standing Ovation + Freminet + one Stage Presence, trading a
fully-blanked turn for 6 damage and a buffer that carries. That is a real
trade — one-turn Block against persistent absorption against tempo — and I made
it on turn one.

But the decision was made on a *guess*, and it succeeded for a reason I could not
see: I ended the turn with 6 Block against 11 damage and took **zero**, because
the invisible bars ate the rest. I did not know that would happen when I chose
it. So: the first turn presented a decision, and gave me no way to check my
reasoning against the result. That is the whole run in one turn.

## The five debrief questions

**1. On turn one of fight one, which line, and why.**

**Standing Ovation → Freminet — Pers, Deploy! → Stage Presence.** Standing
Ovation first, and deliberately first, because the header said `Fanfare: 0` while
my relic said the Usher starts at 3, and I wanted to watch a card that adds 5
Fanfare move the counter. It did not move — that was the first bug-shaped thing
the run showed me and I got it for free by sequencing the turn as a probe. Then
Freminet over the second Stage Presence: 12 Block would have blanked the 11
entirely, but a 45 HP enemy against a hand of Defends is a fight lost to
attrition, and 6 damage now was worth 5 HP later. I took zero damage anyway,
which I had not predicted.

**2. Did you ever spend a performer at 1 on purpose, and did that feel like a
play or a loophole.**

Yes, repeatedly, and it felt like **a loophole — until it stopped working, at
which point it felt like a bug.** The face of Spend says: *"It fires in full even
if the bar is short; an emptied performer takes a Bow."* Read literally, that
makes every Spend rider free — you fire Curtain Rise at 13 with a bar of 1, empty
the performer, and *collect a Bow on top*. I did exactly that in fight 2, killed
an 11 HP Damp Cultist with what I read as a 13 off an empty bar, and wrote in my
head "the Spend riders are not costs, they are free upside."

Then in fight 5 the same card off a similar bar dealt **7**, and in fight 8 it
dealt 7 again after I had spent two full energy raising Fanfare specifically to
make it 13. So: I spent at 1 on purpose, it felt like a loophole, and the
loophole was not real — but nothing on the screen ever told me which world I was
in on any given turn. **A loophole I cannot rely on and cannot see is worse than
either an honest cost or an honest freebie.**

The one time spending at low felt like a genuine *play* was Let the People
Rejoice, where the Bows are the point: emptying everyone to collect Usher's 4
Block and Chevalmarin's Hydro-on-all alongside the AoE is a real decision with a
real cost — the stage comes back at 1 and my damage buffer is gone for two turns.

**3. Did you want a second performer on stage, and what for.**

**Yes, badly, for three concrete reasons — and I could never confirm I had one.**

First, absorption: attacks hit the lead's Fanfare, and a second body means the
reserve's bar survives the turn untouched. Second, Bows: Let the People Rejoice
pays out every performer's departure effect, so two bodies is Usher's 4 Block
*and* Chevalmarin's Hydro-on-all, which is what made fight 4 turn 3 the best turn
of the run. Third — and this only became clear late — Scene Change is a dead card
with one performer and a genuine tactical tool with two, because rotating a
loaded reserve into the lead is what lets a Spend rider fire.

So I played Salon Début whenever I drew it. But I could never verify it. No
screen names who is on stage. I inferred Usher + Chevalmarin only because their
Bows landed together, four fights in. And the card that most wants two
performers — Standing Ovation, "Raise 5 on the **back**" — actively fights the
card that most wants them full — Curtain Rise, "Spend from the **lead**". I spent
two energy on Warm Receptions in fight 8 loading a back seat that Curtain Rise
could not reach, for a 7 instead of a 13.

**4. Which performer did you protect, and could you say why in one sentence.**

**I protected the Gentilhomme Usher, and no, not honestly in one sentence,
because I could never see which seat he was in.**

The intent was clear enough: he is the one the relic gives me at 3 Fanfare every
combat, he is the one whose Bow gives 4 Block, and he is therefore the one I
wanted holding a bar. The elite fight is where I acted on it — I passed on
Interposition's 10 Block and took Stage Presence's 6 instead, specifically so
that the 2 Fanfare Interposition would have eaten stayed in the lead's bar to
make the next Tidal Flourish fire at 12. That is protecting a performer, on
purpose, at a cost.

But "which performer" is a question I was answering by faith. Once Salon Début
put a second body on stage I had no way to know who was leading, who was
reserving, or which of them Scene Change would swap. **The kit asks me to have a
favourite and then refuses to introduce me to the cast.**

**5. Did the three read as three, or as one Osty three times.**

**They read as three on paper and as almost nothing in play.** The Bow glossary
prints three genuinely different identities — *"Usher: 4 Block. Chevalmarin:
Hydro on all. Crabaletta: 8 damage"* — one defensive, one elemental, one
offensive, which is a good spread. And when Let the People Rejoice fired in fight
4 and I got Block *and* Hydro-on-everything in one beat, I could feel two
distinct people leaving the stage. That was the single most characterful moment
of nine fights.

But that is the only channel through which they differ, and it only opens when
they leave. While they are standing there they are interchangeable buckets of a
number I cannot see. Salon Début summons "a random performer" and does not name
them. Scene Change moves "the front performer" and does not name them. Ousia
Surge reads "the lead performer's Fanfare" and does not name them. I met
Crabaletta exactly once, in a glossary entry, and never knowingly had her on
stage in nine fights.

So: three on the card, one in the hand. They are differentiated only at the
moment of departure, which means the kit's whole cast exists as a footnote to a
single card. **Name them on the screen, show their bars, and I suspect the answer
flips — the design underneath is clearly three, and the presentation is
delivering one.**
