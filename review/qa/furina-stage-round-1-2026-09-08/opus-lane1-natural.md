  floor 9 (four Phantasmal Gardeners). The bridge returned

- **Deck at the end** (13 cards): Bis!+, Curtain Rise, Command Performance,
  Freminet — Pers, Deploy!, Grand Entrance, Lynette — Enigmatic Feint,
  Regal Bearing, Salon Début, Shared Billing+, Soloist's Solicitation ×2,

I could not evaluate it at all. It was not a choice I made; it was a choice the

## Fight 1 — Seapunk 45/45

Opened at HP 62/78, 3 energy. Starting hand: Stage Presence, Curtain Rise,
Soloist's Solicitation, Standing Ovation, Regal Bearing. Enemy intent 11.

**Turn 1.** The relic said *"Start each combat with the Gentilhomme Usher in the
front seat at 3 Fanfare"* and the header said **`Fanfare: 0`**. Those cannot both
be true, so my first play was chosen to find out which one was: I played
**Curtain Rise** ("Deal 7 damage. Spend 3: deal 13 instead"). The Seapunk went
45 → 32 and my Block went 0 → 4, which is the Usher's Bow ("Usher: 4 Block").
So the Usher *was* there at 3, the Spend fired, it emptied the bar and the
performer left — and the header still read `Fanfare: 0` before, during and after.

That is the single most useful thing I learned all run and I learned it by
guessing.

Then **Regal Bearing** (3 Block, 1 Weak on the Seapunk) and **Soloist's
Solicitation** (6). *Rejected:* Stage Presence for 6 Block. I rejected it because
Regal Bearing's Weak turned the incoming 11 into 8, so 3 Block plus Weak beat
6 Block outright, and the spare energy bought 6 damage instead of nothing.
That was a real decision and the numbers on the screen were enough to make it.

Took 2 (62 → 60).

**Turn 2.** Drew Freminet — Pers, Deploy! [Cryo], Salon Début, and the enemy
switched to 2×4. I played **Salon Début** — printed face, in full:

> **Salon Début** — cost 1, skill
>     Summon a random performer who is not on stage.

The Seapunk went 26 → 21 and gained **Hydro Aura 2**. Neither the 5 damage nor
the aura is on that card's face. What happened, as best I can reconstruct it, is
that the summoned performer (Chevalmarin, from the Bow glossary's "Chevalmarin:
Hydro on all") arrived and immediately performed. Nothing on the screen said a
performer had arrived, said its name, or said it had acted.

That accident then made my turn. Freminet's face changed the moment the aura
landed, to *"Reaction preview: Frozen — Hydro meets Cryo: its next action deals
half damage, and until it acts the first Attack to hit it Shatters for 6
damage."* So I played **Freminet** (6, Frozen), then **Soloist's** (6 + 6
Shatter). Enemy dead before it acted.

*Rejected:* Stage Presence and Lynette, i.e. blocking the 8. I rejected blocking
because Frozen plus Shatter was exactly lethal and blocking was not. **This was
the best-signposted decision of the run** — the reaction preview appeared on the
card face in the hand, unprompted, at the moment it became true. That is the
right way to print a conditional.

**Judgement on the fight:** two turns, two real decisions (Weak-vs-Block on turn
one, race-vs-block on turn two). The second was set up by a card that did
something its face does not mention.

**Rewards:** 15 Gold, Energy Potion, and a card — Bis!+ / The Gallery Stirs+ /
Commanding Gaze+. I took **Bis!+** (cost 0, "The lead performer performs its act
now"). I took it *because* I could not read it: it was the only card offered that
would tell me anything about the performer system, and it was free to play.
I rejected The Gallery Stirs+ ("The first time you spend Encore each turn, draw
2 cards") because the header had read `Encore: 0` on every screen and I had seen
no way to gain any. I rejected Commanding Gaze+, which was probably the
strongest card of the three, and I would take it if I ran this again.

## Fight 2 — Corpse Slug (1) 27/27 and Corpse Slug (2) 25/25

Both carried *"Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it,
becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength."* That is a good printed rule: it told
me, before I played a card, that the order and *timing* of my kills was the
whole fight. So the fight had a plan in it from turn one.

**Turn 1.** Played **Bis!+** first, free, to read it: Block 0 → 3. So the lead
performer's act, that time, was 3 Block. Then **Soloist's** (6 on A),
**Standing Ovation** ("Raise 5 Fanfare on the back performer"), **Stage
Presence** (6 Block). Block 9 against 8 incoming.

*Rejected:* two Stage Presence for 12 Block. Rejected because two slugs at 27 and
25 need a clock, and pure block gives none. The genuinely interesting rejection
was **Standing Ovation over a fourth block card** — I banked 5 into a bar I could
not see rather than 6 into a Block number I could. I took that on faith in the
glossary line *"Attacks hit your Block, then the lead performer's Fanfare, then
you."*

Took 0. Frail 2 applied.

**Turn 2.** Frail was on me and the screen did something good here: **Regal
Bearing's face changed from "Gain 3 Block" to "Gain 2 Block", and Stage Presence
from 6 to 4.** The card faces carried the debuff. That is exactly right, and it
is the thing the same screen fails to do for Spend cards later.

Played **Curtain Rise** on A (Spend 3 → 13; A 21 → 8), **Freminet** on B (6,
Cryo aura), **Regal Bearing** on B (Weak). *Rejected:* killing A. A was at 21 and
I could have got it to 2 and finished it; I deliberately left it at 8 instead and
put damage into B, because Ravenous meant whichever slug died would hand the
other +4 Strength permanently, and I wanted to choose the *turn* that happened on.

Took 0 HP — and here is the second invisible-bar event: I had 2 Block against a
Weakened 2×2, so 2 should have reached me and my HP did not move. Something
absorbed it and nothing on the screen said what. I believe it was the Fanfare
bank from Standing Ovation. I could not confirm it.

**Turn 3.** A at 8, B at 19, and *both* were attacking (A 3×2, B 8). This is the
turn the plan was for. **Curtain Rise** (Spend 3 → 13) killed A; B ate it, became
Stunned and Strength 4; then **Freminet** and **Soloist's** into B. Took 0,
because a stunned slug does not swing.

This turn had no in-turn alternative worth writing down — and that is the point.
It was the payoff of a decision made two turns earlier, not a dead turn.

**Turn 4.** Hand was **five skills, no attack**: Lynette, Standing Ovation, Stage
Presence, Bis!+, Regal Bearing. B was at 7 with 12 incoming. I could not kill a
7-HP enemy. Played Bis!+ (3 Block), Regal Bearing (Weak on B), Stage Presence,
Lynette; took 0.

**This turn presented no decision at all.** Not "an obvious best line" — no line.
Every card in my hand was a block card and the only question was which four of
five I played. That is a deck shape problem (I was 8 skills to 4 attacks) and it
happened twice in this fight.

**Turn 5.** A meter appeared in the header that had never been there before:

> **Furina Burst: 5/70** — the game's data feed carries this meter's amount and
> its maximum, and no rule for how it is spent

Two Soloist's killed B. Fight over, **0 HP lost across five turns**.

**Rewards:** Tidal Flourish+ / The House Rises+ / Supporting Cast+. Took **Tidal
Flourish+** ("Deal 8 damage to ALL enemies. Spend 2: deal 12 instead") — the only
card of the three with a clock on it. Rejected Supporting Cast+ ("The first
**Spotlighted** card each turn draws 2 cards") partly because **"Spotlighted" is
not defined anywhere on that screen**, and the screen defines eight other words
in a glossary right underneath it.

## Fight 3 — Sludge Spinner 39/39

**Turn 1.** No decision worth the name: hand was Lynette, Stage Presence,
Soloist's, Bis!+, Regal Bearing — one attack again. Played Bis!+ (3 Block),
Regal Bearing (Weak), Stage Presence, Soloist's. Took 0.

**Turn 2.** Weak was on *me* this time, and this is the clearest self-contradiction
I saw all run. On one screen, at one moment, under one debuff:

> - **Soloist's Solicitation** — cost 1, attack / Deal **4** damage.
> - **Curtain Rise** — cost 1, attack / Deal **7** damage. Spend 3: deal **13** instead.
> - **Tidal Flourish+** — cost 1, attack / Deal **8** damage to ALL enemies. Spend 2: deal **12** instead.

Soloist's had been printing 6 and dropped to 4 — it was showing me the
Weak-adjusted number. Curtain Rise and Tidal Flourish+ printed their base
numbers unchanged. So the same screen adjusts one attack's face for a debuff and
not the other two, and the two it does not adjust are exactly the two that carry
Spend. I confirmed the same split later in the reverse direction: under
**Strength 3**, Soloist's went 6 → 9 and Freminet 6 → 9, while Curtain Rise
stayed 7/13, Tidal stayed 8/12 and Grand Entrance stayed 10/20.

**The decision this turn was a sequencing one and it was a good one.** I wanted
Curtain Rise's Spend 3 and Tidal's Spend 2 in the same turn, which is 5 off a bar
that starts at 3. So I played **Standing Ovation first** to top the bar up, then
Curtain Rise, then Tidal Flourish+. *Rejected:* leading with the attacks, which
would have emptied the Usher on the first Spend and left the second one
riderless. That is a real, kit-specific decision about an invisible resource, and
I liked it. I would have liked it more if I could see the resource.

Enemy 33 → 15. Took 4, of which 7 was eaten by the bar (11 incoming, 0 Block).

**Turn 3.** Freminet + Soloist's (12) and **Stage Presence over Standing
Ovation**: the incoming was exactly 6 and 6 Block covers it exactly, which
*preserves the Fanfare bar* for a later Spend, whereas soaking the 6 into the bar
would have burned fuel. That is a genuinely nice trade the kit produces and it
only exists because the bar is both armour and ammunition.

**Turn 4.** Enemy at 3. One Soloist's. No decision — the fight was decided in
turns 2 and 3.

Also on turn 4 the header read **`Fanfare: 2`** having read `3` the turn before,
with the accompanying line explaining a 20% fade. So the header's Fanfare is a
*third* thing again: not the performer bar (which was demonstrably 3 in fight 1
while this read 0), but a buff that grows when I lose HP and decays 20% a turn.

**Rewards:** Scene Change+ / Ebb and Flow+ / Shared Billing+. Took **Shared
Billing+** — cost 0 and "Gain 1 Energy", so it is energy-positive, and its Hydro
gave me a second element to pair with Freminet's Cryo on purpose rather than by
accident. Rejected Ebb and Flow+ ("Spend 1 Encore. Gain 4 Encore") because I had
still never seen Encore be anything but 0. Rejected Scene Change+ ("Rotate the
cast: the front performer moves to the back seat") because with one performer on
stage — which is all I had ever knowingly had — it does nothing, and I had no way
to check how many I had.

## Fight 4 — Calcified Cultist 39/39 and Damp Cultist 52/52

Both opened on Empower, so turn 1 was free of incoming damage. I spent it on the
stage rather than on damage or block, which was the decision of the fight.

**Turn 1.** **Shared Billing+** (free, refunded its energy, Hydro on a random
body), then **Salon Début**. The Damp Cultist went 52 → 47 and **both** cultists
gained Hydro Aura 2. So the summon again dealt damage and applied an aura that
its face does not mention, and again nothing named the performer that arrived.

A new buff appeared on me at the same moment: **`Limelight 25 — Spotlighted
Companion cards gain 25% this turn.`** That is the glossary finally defining
Limelight — but it defines it in terms of "Spotlighted", which is still not
defined anywhere, so the definition does not close.

Then Soloist's ×2 into A and Regal Bearing. *Rejected:* using the free turn to
block, or to dump 18 into the big cultist. Rejected because a free turn is the
only turn where building the stage costs nothing, and the stage had already shown
me it was worth ~11 damage a turn.

**Turn 2.** Both cultists revealed **Ritual 2** and **Ritual 5** — 2 and 5
Strength gained *every turn*. And my hand was, for the third time, **all skills**:
Standing Ovation, Stage Presence ×2, Bis!+, Lynette. Against an enemy that gets
permanently stronger every turn I could not attack at all.

Played Bis!+ (3 Block), Standing Ovation (bank), Stage Presence, Lynette. Took 0.
*Rejected:* pure block. I gave up 6 Block to bank 5 Fanfare specifically because
Ritual told me the fight was going to be long and expiring Block does not
compound. That was the right call for a reason the screen printed.

**Turn 3.** Finally a full hand: Freminet, Curtain Rise, Tidal Flourish+.
Freminet's face showed the Frozen preview against the Hydro auras. Played
**Freminet on B** (Frozen: halves its next action, and the next Attack to hit it
Shatters for 6), then **Curtain Rise on A** (Spend 3 → 13), then **Tidal
Flourish+** (Spend 2 → 12 to all, which is also the Attack that collects the
Shatter on B).

A died exactly. B went 37 → 13. And I took **0**, from 17 incoming, with 0 Block
— the whole 17 went into the Fanfare bank I had built on turn 2. That is the kit
working, and it is the best turn I played.

*Rejected:* blocking, and hitting A with everything. The sequencing — Freeze
first, then land the AoE last so it collects the Shatter — was a real decision
and the reaction preview on the card face made it readable.

**Turn 4.** B at 13, Strength 10, and I could reach exactly 12 with two
Soloist's. **One short.** So I played **Bis!+ first, specifically because I did
not know what it would do** — and it dealt **5 damage and applied Hydro**. Earlier
in this same run, the same card had given me **3 Block**. The card had changed
what it does because a different performer was in the front seat, and no screen
at any point told me who was in the front seat or that it had changed.

That is the finding of the round. The card was, at that moment, either 3 Block or
5 damage, the difference decided whether I had lethal, and the only way to find
out was to play it and look.

B died. Fight won at 66/78, again losing **nothing**.

**Rewards:** Interposition / An Invitation / Macaron Break — three skills. I
**skipped**. My deck was 8 skills to 4 attacks and had handed me an all-skill hand
three times in four fights; thinning was worth more than a fourth block card.

## Fight 6 (Elite) — Phantasmal Gardener ×4, 31/28/27/29

Every body carried **Skittish 6 — "The first time Phantasmal Gardener is hit each
turn, it gains 6 Block."** 115 HP across four bodies, all four gaining Strength
from rotating Empower intents. This fight killed me and I think it was the right
fight to have taken; the deck was the problem, not the choice.

**Turn 1.** **Command Performance** gave me **Bennett — Fantastic Voyage** ("If
you are above 70% HP, gain 3 Strength. Otherwise, gain 10 Block. Exhaust") and
**Sucrose — Wind Spirit Creation** (free, Swirl, draw 1). I was at 64/78 = 82%,
so I played Bennett **immediately, on turn one, specifically because the
condition was going to stop being true** — in a four-body elite my HP was only
going one direction. Strength 3, permanent.

*Rejected:* holding Bennett for the 10 Block later. Rejected because 3 Strength
across a fight this long is worth far more than 10 Block once, and because the
card told me plainly that waiting would change which half I got. **That is a good
card.** It made me read my own HP bar as a resource.

Then, with the last energy, **Standing Ovation over Stage Presence** — bank 5 in
the bar rather than 6 expiring Block, for the same long-fight reason as before.
Took 4 of 15.

**Turn 2.** Focus-fired C: **Grand Entrance** (Spend 5) + **Freminet** + Bis!+.
*Rejected:* Tidal Flourish+ AoE. Rejected because Skittish taxes each body 6 on
the first hit, so spreading damage pays that tax four times and concentrating
pays it once. That is a real, readable decision and I am confident it was right.

But the arithmetic did not come out. C went 27 → 11, i.e. **16 damage landed**
from Grand Entrance's Spend-5 20 plus Strength 3 (23) and Freminet's 9. And
**Bis!+ produced nothing at all** — no Block, no damage, no message, no line under
"What reacted this turn". I believe the Spend 5 had Bowed the performer away and
Bis!+ then had no lead performer to act, but the screen printed nothing to say
so. A card that silently does nothing is worse than a card that refuses.

**Turn 3 is where the run was lost, and the kit told me exactly why.** With the
stage empty I took the full **17 to the face** (60 → 43), no buffer at all. The
Fanfare buff jumped to 16 in the same beat, since it is "generated by losing HP" —
so the meter went up precisely because the thing that was supposed to protect me
had gone.

So I spent the whole turn rebuilding: Shared Billing+ (free), **Salon Début**,
Lynette, Regal Bearing. *Rejected:* Tidal Flourish+ for ~5 chip per body.
Rejected because chip into Skittish is worth nothing and I had just been shown,
in one turn, that an empty stage costs 15 HP.

**Turn 4 proved it.** Same size of incoming, stage rebuilt: **43 → 41. Two
damage.** One turn without a stage cost 17; the next turn with one cost 2. If
there is a single number in this record that says what the kit is about, that is
it.

**Turn 5.** Killed C with two Soloist's. **Turn 6.** Tidal Flourish+ with Spend 2
landed **15 on every body with no Skittish reduction at all** (31→16, 28→13,
29→14, exactly 15 each) — which flatly contradicts the Grand Entrance result two
turns earlier, where the block visibly ate part of the hit. Same buff, same
fight, two different behaviours, and **enemy Block is never printed**, so I could
only ever infer it by subtracting.

**Turn 7.** B at 13. Skittish meant two Soloist's (9+9) could not kill it: the
first lands, the body gains 6, the second lands 3. So I deliberately sequenced
**Soloist's → Bis!+ → Soloist's**, using Bis!+ as a throwaway hit to burn the 6
Block off before the real second hit. That is a genuinely good decision and the
kit produced it. It also **failed by one point** — B finished on 1 HP — because I
could not know what Bis!+ was going to be worth.

**Turn 8, the last.** 11 HP, 29 incoming across three bodies, no attack in hand
but Grand Entrance. Shared Billing+ (free energy) → Grand Entrance to kill the
1-HP body → Salon Début to put a body back on stage → Lynette for 5 Block. It was
not enough and I did not expect it to be; it was the best line available. HP 0,
floor 9.

**Judgement on the Elite:** it had more real decisions in it than the previous
five fights combined — focus-vs-spread against Skittish, Bennett's HP threshold,
the Bis!+ block-burning sequence, and above all the recurring stage-or-damage
question. It also contained every legibility failure in this record, and two of
them (Bis!+'s identity, and the Skittish arithmetic) directly cost me the kills
that would have won it.

## What I could not read, or that contradicted itself

**1. Two different things are called Fanfare, and the header shows the wrong one.**
The relic reads *"Start each combat with the Gentilhomme Usher in the front seat
at **3 Fanfare**"*. The combat header read **`Fanfare: 0`** for the entire first
fight, while the Usher demonstrably held 3 — I proved it by spending 3 and
collecting the Bow. The header's Fanfare is a *different* quantity, described in
its own line as *"Generated by losing HP, and by spending, absorbing or paying
out Encore — and by Center Stage plays. Cards read it; nothing spends it. It
fades by 20%..."*. Meanwhile the glossary on the same screen defines Fanfare as
*"A performer's own bar. Attacks hit your Block, then the lead performer's
Fanfare, then you."* One word, two resources, one of them shown and the other —
the one every card actually spends — never shown at all.

**2. Nothing ever prints the stage.** Not once, in 141 actions, did any screen
tell me: who is on stage, how many performers there are, which is lead and which
is back, or what any bar holds. I had five cards that read the stage —
Bis!+, Curtain Rise, Tidal Flourish+, Grand Entrance, Standing Ovation, Salon
Début, Scene Change+ — and the glossary carefully distinguishes *lead performer*
("the one attacks reach and the only one that regains 1 Fanfare each turn") from
*back performer* ("the reserve: nothing hits it"). Those are precise, meaningful
rules about a board I was never shown.

**3. The sharpest case: Bis!+ is three different cards.** Face: *"The lead
performer performs its act now."* It gave me **3 Block** (fight 2), **5 damage +
Hydro** (fight 4), and **nothing whatsoever, twice** (the Elite). Same card, same
run, no printed difference. On the turn in fight 4 where I was one damage short
of lethal, this was the whole game.

**4. Salon Début does things its face does not say.** Face: *"Summon a random
performer who is not on stage."* Observed, twice: 5 damage to an enemy **and** a
Hydro aura applied. The aura then changed what my other cards did.

**5. A meter with no rule.** `Furina Burst: 5/70 — the game's data feed carries
this meter's amount and its maximum, and no rule for how it is spent.` It first
appeared mid-fight-2 with no announcement, and over the run I watched it read
5, then 10, then 5 again, then 10. I never learned what fed it or what it was
for. The bridge is at least honest that it cannot say — but the meter is on the
screen either way.

**6. Debuff and buff maths are applied to some card faces and not others,
consistently along one line.** Under Weak, Soloist's printed 4 (adjusted) while
Curtain Rise printed 7/13 and Tidal Flourish+ printed 8/12 (unadjusted). Under
Strength 3, Soloist's and Freminet printed 9 (adjusted) while Curtain Rise,
Tidal Flourish+ and Grand Entrance printed base. **Every card that carries Spend
shows an unmodified number.** Those are exactly the cards whose numbers are
biggest and whose decisions are tightest.

**7. Skittish behaved two different ways.** Grand Entrance's hit was visibly
reduced by the 6; Tidal Flourish+ landed its full 15 on three bodies with no
reduction. Enemy Block is never printed, so there is no way to reconcile these
from the screen.

**8. Encore is a whole subsystem I never touched.** `Encore: 0` printed on every
combat screen of the run, and its glossary entry — *"After Block it absorbs

## The kit, after 6 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

The kit has one genuinely excellent decision and it recurs: **the Fanfare bar is
simultaneously my armour and my ammunition, and Spend converts one into the
other.** Every Spend card asks "do you want 6 more damage now, or do you want to
still have a body between you and the next attack?" I made that trade knowingly
at least six times, and the Elite proved the stakes with two adjacent turns:
**empty stage, 17 damage taken; stage rebuilt, 2 damage taken.** That is a real,
character-specific, legible-in-consequence-if-not-on-screen decision, and no
other character I can think of makes it in quite that shape.

Made *on the turn*: the Weak-vs-Block trade in fight 1; Freeze-then-Shatter
sequencing in fights 1 and 4 (the reaction previews made this readable and it was
a pleasure); focus-fire-vs-AoE against Skittish; and the Bis!+-as-block-burner
sequence in the Elite.

Made *earlier in the fight*: delaying the Corpse Slug kill by two turns so the
Ravenous stun would land on the turn both slugs were swinging — the fight's whole
plan, printed on the enemy, paid off exactly. And playing Standing Ovation
*before* two Spend cards so the bar could fund both.

Made *at the draft*: taking Bennett on turn one while above 70% HP; skipping the
all-skill reward on floor 6; buying Grand Entrance because my deck's shape, not
its power, was wrong.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

Automatic: every turn where my hand was all skills, which happened **three times
in the first four fights**. On those turns there is no decision — only which four
of five block cards to play. Stage Presence in particular is a card with no
decision attached to it in either direction.

Also automatic, and this is the more interesting version: **the finishing turns.**
Fights 1, 3 and 5 all ended with "the enemy is at N, play the attack that does
more than N." I do not count fights 1 and 5 against the kit, because those were
set up by earlier plays. Fight 3's last turn genuinely was dead.

Never worth playing: **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint** was, in practice, "gain 5
Block" — its Swirl mattered exactly once and only because everything already had
the same aura. **Sucrose — Wind Spirit Creation** was a free draw with a
decorative keyword. And the entire **Encore** half of the card pool was
unplayable to me for the whole run.

**(c) What I could not understand.** The eleven items above. If I had to keep
one: **I could not see the board my character plays on.** Furina's cards are
about a stage with seats and bars, and the stage is not on the screen.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Stage Presence.** "Gain 6 Block", no interaction with the
performer system at all, in a kit whose entire identity is the performer system.
It was the card I paid 75 gold to remove and I would remove the second one too.

Happiest to draw: **Tidal Flourish+.** It killed two rats in one turn on floor 8
and it hit for 15 across three bodies in the Elite, and — crucially — its Spend 2
is a *small enough* bite that I could take it without losing the stage, which
made it the one Spend card I never had to agonise over. Honourable mention to
**Bennett — Fantastic Voyage**, which is the single best-designed card I saw:
one line, a threshold on my own HP bar, and it made me choose a turn.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes — but partly the wrong kind.** There was a real decision in it: Regal
Bearing's 3 Block + Weak versus Stage Presence's 6 Block against an 11-damage
intent, which is a genuine trade and I made it on printed numbers.

But my *first* play of the run was a decision about **whether the screen was
lying to me** — the relic said the Usher had 3 Fanfare and the header said 0, and
I led with Curtain Rise to find out which. That is not a game decision, it is a
diagnostic. A new player would have played Curtain Rise not knowing the Spend
would fire, watched a performer they were never told they had leave the stage,
and had no idea any of it had happened except that a 4 appeared in the Block box.

## The five debrief questions

**1. On turn one of fight one, which line, and why.**

**Curtain Rise, then Regal Bearing, then Soloist's Solicitation.** Curtain Rise
first, and deliberately, because the relic and the header disagreed about whether
I had a performer and I could only settle it by spending. It resolved as 13
damage plus a 4-Block Bow, so the Usher had been there all along. Regal Bearing
over Stage Presence because 1 Weak turned the incoming 11 into 8, which made
3 Block strictly better than 6, and freed the third energy for Soloist's 6
damage. I ended the turn having dealt 19 and taken 2.

I want to be plain about the shape of that: **the interesting part of my first
turn was reverse-engineering the UI, and the merely competent part was the actual
card decision.**

**2. Did you ever spend a performer at 1 on purpose, and did that feel like a
play or a loophole.**

I could not have known if I did, and that is the honest answer — **the bar is
never printed, so I never once knew what a performer was at when I spent from
it.** I inferred emptiness only afterwards, from a Bow appearing (4 Block, fight
1 turn 1; 4 Block, fight 5 turn 1) or from a Bis!+ that silently did nothing
(Elite, twice).

What I *did* do on purpose, repeatedly, was spend a performer I knew was **short**
— relying on the printed promise *"It fires in full even if the bar is short; an
emptied performer takes a Bow."* The clearest case was fight 5 turn 1: Tidal
Flourish+ Spend 2 followed by Grand Entrance Spend 5, off a bar that had started
the fight at 3. I knew the second one could not be paid for. I played it anyway,
took the full 20, and collected the Usher's 4 Block as the Bow — which was
exactly the Block I needed to cover the surviving rat's 6.

**That felt like a play, and a good one** — the best-feeling sequence of my run.
It reads as a designed release valve rather than an exploit, for three reasons:
the card face states it plainly, the Bow is a *reward* the departing performer
hands you rather than a punishment dodged, and it costs you the thing that was
protecting you. When I did it in fight 5 the fight was ending and the cost was
zero, which is why it felt clean. When I did the same thing in the Elite, the
cost arrived the very next turn as 17 damage to the face. Same play, opposite
feeling. **That is a mechanic working.**

The one thing that keeps it from being fully a play: I was choosing to overdraw
an account whose balance is not printed anywhere.

**3. Did you want a second performer on stage, and what for.**

**Yes, badly, and for two distinct reasons that I could feel separately.**

First, **as a reserve.** The glossary is explicit — *"back performer: the back
seat, and the reserve: nothing hits it"* — and I lived the consequence: on the
one Elite turn when my stage was empty I took 17 to the face, and on the next
turn with a body back on it I took 2. A second performer means the lead can be
spent to zero and Bow without leaving me naked, which is precisely the trade that
killed me. Every Spend I made was really a bet that I would not need the armour
before I could rebuild it, and a back performer is how you stop making that bet.

Second, **as ammunition.** Standing Ovation reads *"Raise 5 Fanfare on the **back**
performer"* — the kit's own refuelling card is written to load the reserve, not
the lead. With one performer the card's whole clause collapses into "with one
performer on stage, that is the lead" and the design intent evaporates. And the
three Bows are different effects (Usher: 4 Block; Chevalmarin: Hydro on all;
Crabaletta: 8 damage), which only becomes a *choice* if you can pick which one is
in front.

I did play Salon Début three times, and at least twice a second performer must
actually have arrived — the Bis!+ that dealt 5 Hydro damage proves someone other
than the Usher was leading. **But I could never confirm I had two, never choose
who led, and never knew who Standing Ovation was loading.** Scene Change+ was
offered to me as a reward — "Rotate the cast: the front performer moves to the
back seat" — and I turned it down purely because I could not tell whether I had
anyone to rotate.

**4. Which performer did you protect, and could you say why in one sentence.**

**The Usher, by default and without ever deciding to** — it starts in the front
seat every combat by relic, and the front seat is the only one anything reaches,
so "protecting" it just meant not spending it.

The one-sentence why: *I kept the Usher alive because its 4-Block Bow was the
cheapest insurance I had against the turn after a big Spend* — which is a real
reason, but notice it is a reason to protect **whoever is in front**, not a
reason to protect **the Usher specifically**.

And that is the honest answer to the question underneath the question: **I never
protected a performer, I protected a seat.** I could not name who was standing in
it after the first Salon Début, so no attachment to a particular one was
available to me. When Chevalmarin turned out to be leading in fight 4, I found
out by playing Bis!+ and reading the damage number.

**5. Did the three read as three, or as one Osty three times.**

**Neither, and that is the finding: they did not read as anything.** I finished a
six-fight run without ever having seen a performer's name on a game screen.

Everything I know about the three, I know from **one glossary line** that the
bridge printed on combat screens:

> **Bow** — A departure effect, earned by Spend only. Usher: 4 Block.
> Chevalmarin: Hydro on all. Crabaletta: 8 damage.

That line is genuinely good design on paper — three clearly differentiated
identities, a defender, an elementalist and a striker, each with a distinct exit.
**Block, aura, damage** is three, not one thing three times. And the two I met
behaved differently in play: the Usher's act was 3 Block, Chevalmarin's was 5
Hydro damage. I never met Crabaletta, or if I did I could not tell.

But that differentiation only ever reached me **as arithmetic after the fact.**
I never chose between them, never saw which was on stage, never saw a name, never
saw a bar, and never made a decision that turned on which of the three I had.
The design is three; the screen is zero. So I cannot tell you whether they read
as three — I can tell you that Bis!+, the card whose entire text is *"the lead
performer performs its act now"*, was to me a card that did 3 Block or 5 damage or
nothing, at random, and that this is what the roster of three felt like from the
seat.

If the three are meant to read as three, the fix is not more differentiation.
They are already differentiated. **The fix is printing the stage.**
