# Furina — stage round 3 — lane 1, natural run

- **Run type:** NATURAL — nothing granted.

  a floor number and says so explicitly ("This is the run's own floor number --

  roughly 25–30 accepted acts, so there was no room for an eighth combat and I

  Applause; Command Performance; Commanding Gaze; Gentilhomme Usher; Ousia

## Fight 1 — Corpse Slug (1) [A] 26/26 and Corpse Slug (2) [B] 25/25

Both wore *Ravenous 4 — "When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it,
becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength."* A intended 3×2, B intended 8.

**Turn 1.** Opened with **Salon Début** (summon a random performer not on
stage), then **Curtain Rise** on B, then **Freminet — Pers, Deploy!** on B.

Curtain Rise reads *"Choose one: Deal 7 damage | Spend 3: deal 13 instead."*
The Spend was the turn's real decision and the fight's only interesting one:
Usher stood at exactly 3 Fanfare, so Spend 3 would have emptied him, taking 13
instead of 7 and collecting his Bow (4 Block) but ending my only performer's
existence on turn one. **I rejected the Spend** because the printed rule —
*"Attacks hit your Block, then the lead performer's Fanfare, then you"* — told
me the 3 on Usher's bar was 3 points of my own HP, and 14 damage was inbound.
Trading a turn-one shield for 6 extra damage on a 51-HP board looked bad.

I also rejected splitting the damage across both slugs: with Ravenous on the
board, spread damage kills nothing and a kill hands the survivor +4 Strength,
so I wanted one body dead fast and the other one's Ravenous trigger to come
with its Stun.

*A note on the interface, not the kit:* Curtain Rise's mode choice does not
appear on the card. Playing the card opens a separate chooser screen listing
"Deal 7 damage" and "Spend 3: deal 13 instead" as two 0-cost skills. That is
clear enough once seen, but it costs an extra accepted action every time, and
on an empty stage the chooser still opens with **one** row in it, which is a
modal dialogue for a non-decision.

**Turn 2 — the finding of the fight.** The turn opened with this on the stage
panel:

> - The stage is empty. A **Spend** rider cannot fire at all, so those cards play at their base number.
> - **Usher** performed: Furina gains 3 Block.
> - **Crabaletta** performed: 5 to Corpse Slug (2).
> - **Usher** left the stage: emptied by a hit, so no Bow.
> - **Crabaletta** left the stage: emptied by a hit, so no Bow.

Fourteen points of chip damage from two floor-2 slugs erased **both**
performers in a single enemy turn, gave me nothing in return ("no Bow" twice),
and I still took 3 to the face. I had spent one of my three cards that turn
building that stage. The stage did not survive one round of act-1 trash.

That also produced my first flat contradiction. Crabaletta was in the **back**
seat, and the same page's glossary says of the back performer: *"The back seat,
the reserve: no single attack reaches it."* It was emptied by a hit anyway.

Turn 2 itself: I played **Command Performance**, which handed me Freminet —
Shattering Pressure and **Kaedehara Kazuha — Kazuha Slash** (*"Deal 10 damage
and Swirl ALL enemies. Exhaust."*). I aimed Kazuha at B (7 HP) to kill it and
eat the Ravenous stun on a free turn; the bridge answered *"Its `on \"B\"` was
ignored: this card does its own aiming."* Rejected: holding the Kazuha for a
bigger target — with 14 a turn coming in and no stage left, removing a body now
was worth more than 3 wasted damage. I finished with Regal Bearing on A for a
Weak that, judging by the next turn's intent, did not stick.

**Turn 3.** Two Soloist's Solicitation and a Curtain Rise for 19 into a 16-HP
slug. No rejected alternative: three attacks, one target, lethal. A dead turn,
and I am saying so — but the fight's shape was set two turns earlier, and it
was set badly.

**Verdict on fight 1:** one real decision (Spend or not, turn 1), one draft
decision (which rare), and a turn-2 lesson that reframed the whole run.

## Fight 2 — Seapunk [A] 45/45

Intended 11.

**Turn 1 — the answer to fight 1.** **Commanding Gaze** (2 Block, 1 Weak to
ALL) + **Stage Presence** (6 Block) + **Salon Début**. Rejected: Curtain Rise
for damage. The reasoning was explicit — Weak takes 11 down to about 8, 2+6
block plus Usher's 3 covers it, and therefore **the bar survives the turn**.
This is the first time the kit's central knob became legible to me: Fanfare is
only a resource if the enemy never reaches it, so the block cards are not
defence, they are *bar protection*.

It worked. Next turn: Usher at **4** (3, untouched, +1 regen) and Chevalmarin
on the back seat.

**Turn 2 — the best turn of the run.** Freminet on Seapunk consumed its Hydro
aura (Chevalmarin's own end-of-turn Hydro, note) for **Frozen**; the card face
had told me in advance: *"Reaction preview: Frozen — Hydro meets Cryo."* Then
Curtain Rise in **Spend 3** mode off a bar of 4. Real decision, and the
opposite of turn 1 of fight 1: **spending 3 from a bar of 4 leaves 1, and a
performer that is not emptied does not leave.** Damage landed 6 + 13 + 6
Shatter = 25, Seapunk 43 → 18, Usher still on stage. Rejected: Rising Applause,
because it raises the **back** performer and I had no card that can spend the
back seat's bar; that card was dead in hand for the third time.

Third energy went to Lynette — Enigmatic Feint for 5 Block, chosen over Regal
Bearing purely because 3+5 = 8 exactly covers a 2×4 = 8 attack with no rounding
question, where Weak's 25% on four small hits could round either way. Full
block, Usher survived, +1 regen.

**Turn 3.** Two Soloists into a Seapunk that had switched to Empower+Defend.
Third energy went to Rising Applause on the back Chevalmarin (1 → 6) purely to
find out what the card does, since nothing else that turn mattered — and this
is where I learned the card's shape: it built a 6-point bar on a performer no
card of mine could spend.

**Turn 4 — the plan cashing in.** Seapunk at 2 HP behind 7 Block. Curtain Rise
in Spend mode: 13 through 7 Block, kill; Usher's bar hit exactly 0, so *"Usher
left the stage: emptied by a Spend, so it takes a Bow"* — 4 Block. The obvious
lethal, but it was set up two turns earlier by the block cards that kept the
bar alive. **Fight 2 was won at zero HP cost.**

## Fight 3 — Toadpole (1) [A] 24/24, Toadpole (2) [B] 21/21

**Turn 1 — the loophole.** I played **Rising Applause** with only Usher on
stage. Its rider says *"Raise 5 Fanfare on the back performer"* and its
glossary says *"With one performer on stage, that is the lead."* Usher went 3 →
8. Then two attacks into B.

This is the run's clearest bit of play and also, honestly, a loophole: a card
built to charge the reserve becomes a straight 5-point shield on the front
seat *provided the stage is exactly one deep*. Rejected: Curtain Rise Spend,
which would have cashed the bar rather than banking it. Result: 7 damage came
in, 3 was blocked, 4 hit an 8-bar, Usher ended the turn at 5. **The kit's most
satisfying single moment in the whole run.**

**Turn 2.** Hand was five skills. Salon Début + Gentilhomme Usher (Usher 5 → 8)
+ Stage Presence. Block 9 against a 9 attack, exactly. Rejected: playing the
Explosive Ampoule to kill B at 9 HP — I was taking zero damage a turn and
wanted the potion for the elite (which is exactly where it was spent).
Toadpole A had picked up Thorns 2 by then, which is a real reason to prefer the
performers' chip damage to my own attacks.

**Turn 3.** Soloist to kill B (7 HP, one point of Thorns paid), Stage Presence
to cover A's 7, third energy to Salon Début. Rejected: Commanding Gaze, since
I was already blocking the whole attack and Weak would have bought nothing.
This is where I first had **three seats filled** — *"lead: Usher 9 · middle:
Crabaletta 1 · back: Chevalmarin 1"* — and the difference was visible
immediately: 3 Block + 5 to one body + 2 to all with Hydro, every turn, for
free.

**Turn 4.** A at 17 with a Hydro aura and a Buff intent, i.e. a free turn.
Freminet for Frozen, Curtain Rise Spend 3 off a bar of 9 for 13 + 6 Shatter.
Dead. **Fight 3 also cost zero HP.**

## Fight 4 — Living Fog [A] 80/80

The fight that showed the kit's structural weakness.

**Turn 1.** Rising Applause on a lone Usher again (3 → 8), then two attacks.
Same trick, same result, zero damage taken.

**Turn 2 — Smoggy.** The Fog applied *"Smoggy 1 — You can only play 1 Skill per
turn."* Furina's deck at that point was nine skills to six attacks. **From
here to the end of the fight I wasted an energy nearly every turn**, because
the only legal plays were one skill and whatever attacks I happened to hold.
This is the sharpest thing the run found: the stage costs cards, and the cards
that build it are all skills, so a single common debuff turns the character's
whole engine off. I spent the fight playing Ousia Surge (4 damage — its face
prints the lead's live Fanfare, so it was 4) and a lone Salon Début, and
watched the third energy evaporate.

Decision each turn was therefore reduced to: *which one skill*. Turn 2:
Salon Début over Stage Presence — a performer is permanent, block is not, and
the Fog's 5 was small enough that the bar could eat it. Turn 3: Stage Presence,
after Curtain Rise (base mode) killed the 7-HP Gas Bomb — I rejected Spend mode
outright because 13 into a 7-HP minion wastes 6 damage *and* the bar. Turn 4:
Salon Début again, filling the third seat. Turn 5: killed the second Gas Bomb
with Ousia Surge + Freminet rather than freezing the Fog, because a Death Blow
minion is 8 guaranteed damage and the Fog's Frozen halving is forfeited the
moment you Shatter it. Turn 6: Gentilhomme Usher to bank the bar to 8.

**Turn 7.** Fog at 15, bar at 8: Soloist 6 then Curtain Rise Spend 3 for 13.
Dead, and *"Minions abandon combat without their leader"* removed the third Gas
Bomb for free. **Zero HP lost across a 7-round fight against an 80-HP monster**
— but it took seven rounds, and the reason it took seven rounds is that Smoggy
turned a three-energy turn into a two-energy turn.

## Fight 5 — Skulking Colony [A] 75/75 (elite)

*Hardened Shell 20 of 20 left this turn — "Skulking Colony cannot lose more
than 20 HP each turn."* It opened hitting for 14.

**Turn 1.** Dexterity Potion (+2 to Block from cards, does not decay) — chosen
because the elite is a long fight, my kit gains block three separate ways, and
a permanent +2 on every one of them compounds. Then Salon Début + Stage
Presence (which then printed **8**, so Dexterity is folded into the card face)
+ Curtain Rise base 7. I rejected the Spend, correctly this time and for a
reason I got wrong: I thought 3+8 = 11 covered 14. It doesn't, and it also
turned out that **Dexterity does not apply to a performer's Block** — Usher's
act still printed 3, never 5. Three damage got through and emptied Usher's bar
of 3. *"Usher left the stage: emptied by a hit, so no Bow."*

**Turn 2.** Chevalmarin led at 2. Gentilhomme Usher (summon) + Regal Bearing
(5 Block, 1 Weak) + Soloist. Block 8 against a weakened 14 — 2 through, which
emptied Chevalmarin's 2-point bar exactly and cost me 1 HP. **The pattern is
now unmistakable: a bar under about 6 is not a shield, it is a formality.**

**Turn 3.** Freminet → Frozen, Soloist → Shatter (18 damage), Stage Presence
for full block. Rejected Rising Applause (bank 5 on the bar) in favour of
Stage Presence (8 expiring block), on the grounds that the bar was going to be
eaten either way and the block was bigger this turn. Zero damage taken.

**Turn 4 — the loophole the debrief asks about.** Colony at 40, Usher's bar at
**3**, hand held Grand Entrance: *"Choose one: Deal 10 damage | Spend 5: deal
20 instead."* The Spend glossary says the quiet part out loud: *"Pays the lead
performer, **fires in full even if the bar is short**, and an emptied performer
takes a Bow."* So I spent **5 from a bar of 3**, got the whole 20 (exactly the
Hardened Shell cap, so the shell read *"0 of 20 left this turn"* afterwards),
**and** collected the Bow: *"Usher left the stage: emptied by a Spend, so it
takes a Bow. Usher took a Bow: Furina gains 4 Block."* Colony 40 → 20 in one
2-cost card. I finished with Regal Bearing and ate 4.

**Turn 5.** Explosive Ampoule (10, and potions ignore Thorns-style riders) +
Soloist + Curtain Rise = 23 into a 20-HP body under a 20 cap. Elite dead at
**64/78**, for one potion, one Dexterity Potion, and 5 HP.

I skipped the card reward (Surintendante Chevalmarin / Bis! / Duet) — all three
were skills, and after the Smoggy fight adding a tenth skill to a deck that
already could not spend its energy was the wrong shape. **Rejecting a whole
reward screen on deck-shape grounds was itself one of the run's real
decisions.**

## The kit, after 7 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Three kinds, and they are genuinely different from each other:

1. **On the turn — "spend the bar or keep it."** Every Spend card asks the
   same question and it never got boring, because the answer moved. Fight 1
   turn 1: keep (the bar was my only shield against 14). Fight 2 turn 2:
   spend 3 of 4 (the *remainder* is what matters — a performer at 1 is still a
   performer). Fight 2 turn 4: spend all of it (the Bow pays out only when the
   bar hits zero, so the last spend of a fight is strictly better than the
   others). Fight 5 turn 4: spend 5 from a bar of 3 (the rider fires in full;
   see (b) on whether that is a play).
2. **Earlier in the fight — "protect the bar or use the energy."** This is the
   decision the character is actually built on and I did not see it until
   fight 1 ended with both performers wiped. Block cards in this kit are not
   survival, they are *bar maintenance*; Stage Presence exists so that Curtain
   Rise can be played next turn. That converted every block card into a bet
   about a future Spend, which is a much better decision than "do I want 6
   Block."
3. **At the draft — "attacks or engine."** Twice I turned down whole reward
   screens (the elite's three skills; a straight skip) and twice I took an
   attack over a stage card. Smoggy made that a live axis: a deck of nine
   skills has no answer to "you may play one skill this turn," and the kit's
   whole identity is printed on skills.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Automatic:** Stage Presence. Six (later eight) block, no rider, no
  interaction with the stage at all. I played it eight or nine times and never
  once thought about it. Commanding Gaze and Regal Bearing at least ask "does
  Weak change the arithmetic," which Stage Presence never does.
- **Automatic in a worse way:** the whole first turn of a fight, when Salon
  Solitaire has already put Usher on stage at 3 and every hand wants to raise
  him. The relic hands you the opening and the opening plays itself.
- **Never worth playing: Rising Applause, as printed.** *"Raise 5 Fanfare on
  the back performer."* No card in my deck could spend the back seat's bar,
  and the back seat is (allegedly) unhittable, so charging it is charging a
  battery with no terminals. The card was live **only** through its own
  exception clause — *"With one performer on stage, that is the lead"* — i.e.
  it was good exactly when the stage was in its most degenerate state, and
  dead on an empty stage and on a full one. I played it four times: three
  times as a lead-raise loophole and once, in fight 2, as a genuine back-seat
  raise, where it did nothing for the rest of the fight.
- **Ousia Surge came close.** *"Deal 0 damage, the lead performer's Fanfare."*
  On the four occasions I drew it, it printed 5, 4, 3 and 0. A 1-cost attack
  whose real range is 2–5 is not a payoff card, it is a Strike that sometimes
  misses.
- **The Bow is nearly a dead mechanic.** *"A departure effect a Spend earns and
  a hit does not."* Across seven fights a performer left the stage **nine**
  times and the Bow fired **three** of them. Six times the screen printed
  *"emptied by a hit, so no Bow."* The design gives the enemy the power to deny
  your payoff by doing the one thing enemies always do.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

- **The back seat is not safe.** Glossary: *"back performer — The back seat,
  the reserve: **no single attack reaches it**."* Fight 1, round 2, same page:
  *"**Crabaletta** left the stage: emptied by a hit, so no Bow"* — Crabaletta
  was in the back seat. Either the rule is wrong or something other than "a
  single attack" is chewing through reserves, and no screen said which.
- **The Companion glossary contradicts itself between screens, in the same
  fight.** Fight 1, round 1: *"On Furina's stage playing one performs the front
  member, then sends it to the back; an empty stage performs nobody. The member
  picks its own enemy at random, never the card's target."* Fight 1, round 2:
  *"**It does nothing to your stage: no performer acts, rotates or leaves for
  one.** Chevalmarin's Hydro is the touchpoint."* Those are opposite claims
  about the same keyword, one round apart. Both texts then reappeared on later
  screens, alternating. I never worked out which is true.
- **"Deal 0 damage."** Both Let the People Rejoice (*"Deal 0 damage to ALL
  enemies"*) and Ousia Surge (*"Deal 0 damage, the lead performer's Fanfare"*)
  print a literal zero outside combat. In combat Ousia Surge updates to the
  live number, so the mechanism is legible once you have seen it move — but
  the version you read at the **reward screen**, which is where you decide
  whether to put it in your deck, is the one that says 0. I turned down a rare
  because of this on floor 1.
- **The Raise glossary contradicts the Gentilhomme Usher card.** Card:
  *"Summon Usher. If he is already on stage, Raise 3 on him instead."*
  Keyword, printed directly beneath it: *"Raise — Adds Fanfare to the **back**
  performer."* Usher is the front seat by relic. The two lines cannot both be
  right, and only playing it told me the card wins.
- **Kazuha Slash's damage.** Face: *"Deal 10 damage and Swirl ALL enemies."*
  I aimed it at B (7 HP); the bridge said the aim was ignored, A dropped by
  exactly 10, **and B died as well**. Whether the 10 hit both bodies, or the
  Swirl killed B, or something else, the card face gives no way to know.
- **The mode chooser does not apply Weak, the card face does.** Fight 6, with
  Weak 2 on me, Grand Entrance's face read *"Choose one: Deal 7 damage | Spend
  5: deal 15 instead"* and the chooser it opened read *"Deal 10 damage"* /
  *"Spend 5: deal 20 instead."* Two numbers for the same effect, one click
  apart.
- **Dexterity and performers.** *"Dexterity — Increases Block gained from cards
  by 2."* Stage Presence went 6 → 8. Usher's end-of-turn act stayed at 3, every
  turn. Consistent with the word "cards," but it means the buff you buy to hold
  the stage together does not touch the stage.
- **Two potion/reward screens miscounted themselves.** Power Potion: *"Choose 1
  of 3 random Power cards"* — two were listed. And the Neow card screen printed
  *"Say `confirm` after `choose` to take it"* directly above *"Confirm is not
  available"*; `choose` alone resolved it and my `confirm` was refused.
- **A hand I could not read.** Fight 7, round 5, the hand printed:
  > - **Rising Applause** — cost 1, skill
  > - **Rising Applause** — cost 1, skill
  >     Raise 5 Fanfare on the back performer.

  Two rows, one body of text, and no `(1)`/`(2)` numbering, which every other
  duplicate on every other screen got. I could not tell whether I held one
  Rising Applause or two, and that is why my end-of-run deck list above is
  flagged as a reconstruction.
- **The Endless Conveyor destroys its own alternatives.** The room opened with
  "Grab Clam Roll (40 gold, heal 10)" and "Observe the Chef (upgrade a random
