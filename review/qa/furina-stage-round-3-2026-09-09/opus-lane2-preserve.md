## Fight 1 — Corpse Slug (1) 26/26 [A] and Corpse Slug (2) 25/25 [B]

Both carried *"Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it,
becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength."* That buff is the fight: killing one
feeds the other.

**Turn 1** (Usher lead at 3 from the relic; incoming 3×2 + 8 = 14).
Played Warm Reception → Gentilhomme Usher → Freminet — Pers, Deploy! on B.
The decision was **what Fanfare is for**, and it was made on the turn: Usher's bar
is simultaneously my damage scaler and a shield that eats hits before my face does.
I chose to pump it to 9 and hit with the leftover energy.
**Rejected:** Stage Presence (6 Block, flat) — Fanfare seemed strictly better,
being uncapped, carrying over turns, and feeding Rejoice. **That was wrong, and
turn 1 ended the argument** (below). Also rejected: Let the People Rejoice, which
by then printed "Deal 6 damage" — spending a 6-bar for 6 AoE that killed nothing
and exhausted my only rare.

**What the screen said afterwards, and it is the finding of the run:**

> **Usher** performed: Furina gains 3 Block.
> **Usher** left the stage: **emptied by a hit, so no Bow.**

Nine Fanfare, two cards and two energy, absorbed 9 of 14 damage and paid nothing
back. The keyword prints the rule plainly — *"Bow — A departure effect a Spend
earns and a hit does not"* — but reading it and being taught it are different, and
the teaching cost me the stage for the next four rounds.

**Turns 2–4** were the consequence, and they were **turns with no decision at all**.
With the stage empty, Ousia Surge printed "Deal 0 damage", Rising Applause and
Pneuma Refrain and Scene Change had nobody to act on, and Let the People Rejoice
printed 0. On turn 2 I spent 1 energy on **Ousia Surge on an empty stage to see
what would happen: it dealt 0 and the game let me do it** (A stayed at 26/26). On
turn 3 I did the same with **Rising Applause on an empty stage: accepted, 1 energy
gone, "The stage is empty" still printed on the very next screen**. Turn 4 my whole
hand was Salon Début / Lynette / Warm Reception / Gentilhomme Usher / Scene Change —
five skills, zero damage.

**Turn 4** had one real decision, made on the turn: with Usher (lead, 1) and
Crabaletta (back, 4) both about to be hit for 14, I could rotate with the free
Scene Change and let Crabaletta die 3 HP cheaper, or keep Crabaletta and eat 3 more.
I kept Crabaletta because my hands had no damage and she deals 5 a turn.
**Both died anyway** — 3×3 and 8 are separate hits, so the second one walked
straight through to the "safe" back seat:

> **Usher** left the stage: emptied by a hit, so no Bow.
> **Crabaletta** left the stage: emptied by a hit, so no Bow.

**Turns 5–8.** The one good decision here was made at the *draft* and paid off
across turns: I focused Corpse Slug (2) down, then used Freminet's 6 to kill it at
1 HP so that Ravenous fired on my terms — A went Stunned (it skipped a whole turn)
and I spent that free turn hitting it. The final turn's Freminet-for-lethal was an
obvious button, but it was the plan two turns earlier arriving, not a dead turn.

**Won at 42/78.** Reward: 17 gold, Explosive Ampoule, and I took **The Witness
Stand** (Vulnerable + draw) over a second Ousia Surge and Commanding Gaze —
specifically because Ousia Surge had just proved it is a blank when the stage is
down, which was my whole problem.

**Turn 4 — the fight ended on a plan laid two turns earlier.** Seapunk at 18 HP + 7

**Won at 42/78 having taken zero damage.** Took **Gentilhomme Usher** (a second

## Screens I could not read, or that contradicted themselves

Quoting the printed text in each case.

1. **`Let the People Rejoice` never says what its damage number is.** It prints
   *"Spend all Fanfare on stage. **Deal 0 damage to ALL enemies.** Every performer
   takes a Bow, then returns at 1."* The number silently tracked the Fanfare on
   stage — 0 when empty, 3 at Usher 3, 6 at Usher 6, 7 at 7, 9 at 9 — but the face
   never says so, and I only worked it out by watching it change. **Then it broke
   the pattern:** fight 7, round 7, stage line **`lead: Usher 12`**, no Weak on me,
   and the card printed **"Deal 2 damage to ALL enemies."** I have no reading of the
   card that produces 2 from a 12-bar.

2. **`Ousia Surge` and `Pneuma Refrain` print "Deal 0 damage" / "Gain 0 Block"** and
   are playable for full price on an empty stage, doing literally nothing. I tested
   it: A stayed at 26/26. Same for **`Rising Applause`** — accepted, 1 energy gone,
   and the next screen still read *"The stage is empty."* The game prints the reason
   (*"The stage is empty. A Spend rider cannot fire at all"*) on the stage line but
   never on the card, and never refuses the play.

3. **The `Raise` keyword contradicts the card it is printed on.**
   `Gentilhomme Usher` reads *"Summon Usher. If he is already on stage, **Raise 3 on
   him** instead"* and glosses *"**Raise** — Adds Fanfare to the **back**
   performer."* With Usher leading and Crabaletta in the back, it raised **Usher, in
   the lead seat**. One of those two lines is wrong.

4. **Modal choosers print base numbers while the hand prints real ones.** Hand:
   *"Choose one: Deal 5 damage | Spend 3: deal 9 instead"*. Chooser, same card, same
   instant: *"Deal 7 damage / Spend 3: deal 13 instead."* Asking by the hand's name
   was refused. The damage dealt was **9** — the hand was right, the chooser was
   wrong. Same for Grand Entrance (hand 7/15, chooser 10/20, dealt 15).

5. **A "Choose one" with one row.** With an empty stage, Curtain Rise's chooser
   printed only *"Deal 7 damage"* — a forced choice that costs an extra action and
   presents no choice. It never says *why* the Spend row is missing; the Spend
   keyword's *"On an empty stage the Spend mode is not offered at all"* appears only
   on some screens.

6. **The chooser tells you to `confirm`, then refuses `confirm`.** Every chooser
   prints *"Choosing here arms a pick; it does not close the screen. Say `confirm`
   after `choose` to take it"* immediately above *"Confirm is not available."*
   Following the instruction gave *"there is nothing waiting to be confirmed."*

7. **`choose` intermittently has to be issued twice.** On Curtain Rise (fight 4),
   Grand Entrance (elite turn 4), Curtain Rise (fight 6 turn 6) and Curtain Rise
   (fight 7 turn 8), the first `choose` returned `ok` and the chooser stayed open,
   refusing the next two commands with *"a card chooser is open and has to be
   answered first."* Repeating the identical `choose` worked. Other times the first
   one closed it. It cost me ~5 actions and two of the three refusals I am allowed.

8. **`Crabaletta` applies Hydro that nothing on screen accounts for.** The keyword
   reads *"Crabaletta deals 5 to a random enemy"* — no element — while Chevalmarin's
   clause explicitly says *"and applies Hydro."* Yet after Crabaletta-only turns the
   target wore *"Hydro Aura 1."* The Elemental Reaction gloss states flatly:
   *"An element comes from a CARD that prints one and from nothing else: a potion, a
   relic or an enemy applies none unless its own face says so."* A performer's act
   is applying an element its face does not print.

9. **The middle seat is undocumented.** `lead performer` and `back performer` each
   have a full gloss; when three performers were on stage the line read
   *"middle: Chevalmarin 1"* and **no screen ever explains what the middle seat does,
   whether attacks reach it, or where a Raise lands with three bodies up.**

10. **"no single attack reaches the back" is true and useless.** Twice a multi-hit
    intent (3×3, and 3×2 + 8) killed the lead and then the back performer in the same
    enemy turn. The rule is technically kept and the promise it makes to a reader is
    not.

11. **`Weak` is not shown on the enemy.** I played *"Regal Bearing — Gain 2 Block.
    Apply 1 Weak"* on Corpse Slug (1) and its line still printed only *"Ravenous 4"*.
    Vulnerable *does* show. I could not tell whether Weak had landed.

12. **`Power Potion` promised three and offered two.** *"Choose 1 of 3 random Power
    cards"*; the chooser listed Courtroom Drama and Quick Change only.

13. **The `(1)`/`(2)` disclaimer is honest and still unusable.** *"…it is re-counted
    on every screen, so `(1)` names a different copy once one of them leaves your
    hand."* Playing `Soloist's Solicitation (1)` twice in a row worked — because the
    second copy had been renumbered to (1) — which is correct and deeply
    counter-intuitive.

14. **No seed and no ascension anywhere.** Neither appears on any screen the bridge
    prints, so a blind seat cannot report either.

## The kit, after 7 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

- **Spend now or hold the bar** — *on the turn*, and the best recurring decision in
  the kit. Fanfare is a shield **and** an ammo pool **and** a damage scaler for Ousia
  Surge, and the three uses are mutually exclusive. The rule I ended up playing by:
  *if the incoming hit will break the bar anyway, Spend it — a hit pays nothing and a
  Spend pays a Bow; if it will not, hold it, because Ousia Surge reads the bar
  without consuming it.* Both halves came up (elite turn 1 vs. fight 7 turn 1) and
  they pointed opposite ways. That is a real trade.
- **Whose Bow to cash** — *on the turn*, via the free Scene Change. Usher's Bow is 4
  Block, Chevalmarin's is Hydro on all, Crabaletta's is 8 damage. Rotating a
  1-Fanfare Crabaletta into the lead and Spending *her* turned a 13-damage card into
  a 21-damage turn while preserving Usher. Scene Change costing 0 is what makes this
  a decision rather than a tax.
- **Which body to protect** — *on the turn*. Raise only ever targets the **back**
  seat, hits only ever reach the **lead**, so where a bar sits is a choice about
  whether it is ammunition or armour.
- **Second body vs. more shield** — *on the turn* (fight 3 turn 2, fight 4 turn 1). A
  performer costs 1 energy and pays 2–5 damage every turn it survives; the same
  energy as +3 Fanfare, which pays once.
- **At the draft:** taking a second Gentilhomme Usher and Chevalmarin over more
  payoff cards, and later *skipping* an all-skill reward outright, were the picks
  that shaped whether my turns had anything in them at all.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Automatic:** every turn with the stage down. That was **five full hands** across
  the run with zero attacks and 3–4 dead skills — fight 1 turns 2/3/7, fight 3 turn
  3, fight 4 turns 5/6, elite turn 3, fight 6 turn 4, fight 7 turn 4. On those turns
  I did not choose, I found the one card that did anything and pressed it.
- **Never worth playing:** **`Let the People Rejoice`**, both copies, all run. It is

  Surge`** and **`Pneuma Refrain`** are the same card twice over: brilliant with a
  fat bar, literal blanks without, and they blank at exactly the moments you are
  losing. **`Scene Change`** is dead unless there are two performers, which was maybe

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

The full list is above. The three that actually cost me: Let the People Rejoice's

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Let the People Rejoice** — my Neow rare, and the only card in the

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a genuinely good one** — the best first turn the kit could have offered.
Three energy, two Corpse Slugs telegraphing 14 total, and a hand holding a Raise, a

tells you Fanfare doubles as Block (*"Attacks hit your Block, then the lead
performer's Fanfare, then you"*). I chose Fanfare, and I chose wrong, and the wrongness
was legible in one line the moment the turn resolved. A first turn that poses a
question, lets you answer it badly, and then explains the answer is a good first turn.

## The five debrief questions

**1. On turn one of fight one, which line, and why.**

Warm Reception → Gentilhomme Usher → Freminet — Pers, Deploy! on Corpse Slug (2).
Reasoning as I had it at the time: the relic starts Usher at 3, `Raise` pumps him,
and the Fanfare gloss says attacks hit Block, *then* the lead's Fanfare, *then* me —
so 9 Fanfare looked like 9 Block that carries over turns, is uncapped, and doubles as
ammunition for Let the People Rejoice and Ousia Surge. Stage Presence's flat 6 Block
looked strictly worse. Freminet went on B rather than A because B was the 8-damage
intent and A's Ravenous made me want to kill the *weaker* slug first so the survivor's
+4 Strength arrived as late as possible.
**It was the wrong line, and the screen said so in one sentence:** *"Usher left the
stage: emptied by a hit, so no Bow."* Nine Fanfare bought 9 damage of mitigation once
and paid nothing back, where 9 Fanfare Spent would have bought 13 damage *and* 4
Block. I would now play Stage Presence and Freminet and Soloist's, keep Usher at 3
as a disposable Bow, and Spend him the moment a Spend card appeared.

**2. When I chose NOT to spend on a Spend card, what was I keeping the bar for; and
when I spent from a small bar, was it a play or a loophole.**

*Not spending* — I held the bar for exactly two things, and both are real:
**Ousia Surge and Pneuma Refrain, which read the bar without consuming it.** Ousia
Surge at 1 energy for 7–9 damage every turn is better than any single Spend as long
as the bar survives, and Pneuma Refrain converts the same number into Block that a
hit cannot eat for free. So the bar was being kept as a *renewable* number rather
than a one-shot. The clearest instance is fight 7 turn 1: the enemy's whole move was
"debuff + 5 Status cards", nothing was going to break the bar, so Curtain Rise went
off at base 7 and Usher stayed at 6 to be re-read next turn. The mirror instance is
elite turn 1, where the incoming 14 was going to erase a 3-bar Usher whatever I did,
so refusing to Spend would have forfeited the Bow for nothing.

*Spending from a small bar* — **it is a loophole, and I used it deliberately and
repeatedly because it is far and away the strongest thing in the kit.** The Spend
gloss says it outright: *"Pays the lead performer, fires in full **even if the bar is
short**, and an emptied performer takes a Bow."* So:

> summon a body for 1 energy → it arrives at **1 Fanfare** →
> `Curtain Rise: Spend 3` fires **13** → the body is emptied → it **takes a Bow**.

The "cost" of 3 is not a cost at all; a 1-Fanfare body pays it. Worse (better), the
under-payment is *rewarded* — emptying is exactly what triggers the Bow, so paying
too little is how you collect the bonus. I killed the last Toadpole with it, killed
the elite with `Grand Entrance: Spend 5` off a 2-Fanfare Chevalmarin for the full 20,
killed Sneaky Gremlin with it off a 2-bar Usher, and won fight 7 with it. Two energy
for 20 damage plus a departure effect, available on demand from an empty stage, is
not a play the bar is meant to gate — it makes the entire Raise half of the kit
(Rising Applause, Warm Reception, the Gentilhomme Usher raise mode) optional. The
honest version — build a real bar, then spend it — is *strictly worse*, because a
big bar Spent costs you a big Ousia Surge and a big Pneuma Refrain, while a 1-bar
Spent costs you nothing.

**3. Did I want a second performer on stage, and what for.**

**Yes, badly, and for two distinct reasons that pull in opposite directions.**

First, **free damage**. Every performer acts at the end of my turn from any seat.
Two bodies is 5–7 damage and 3 Block a turn that costs no energy and no cards. Elite
turn 3 with three of them up was the highest-output turn of the run and I played no
attack at all.

Second, and more important, **a second performer is a spare Bow and a spare lead**.
With one body, the lead is the only thing that can be Spent, the only thing that can
be hit, and the only thing Ousia Surge can read — so one enemy hit takes all three
functions away at once and my hand turns to bricks. With two, Scene Change (free)
lets me choose which one to cash and which one survives, which is where the kit's
best decisions live.

The pull the other way: **every new body arrives at 1 Fanfare, and a 1-Fanfare body
is free for the enemy to kill.** Twice a multi-hit intent wiped a freshly-built
two-body stage in a single enemy turn and paid me no Bows. So I wanted a second
performer constantly and could only safely *keep* one against single-hit enemies.

**4. Which performer did I protect, and could I say why in one sentence.**

**Usher**, and yes: *he is the only performer who gives Block, so he is the only one
whose survival makes the next turn cheaper rather than just stronger.*

Longer, because the choice was not always his: Usher's act (3 Block a turn) and his
Bow (4 Block) are the only defensive numbers the cast produces, and my HP was the
resource I ran out of, not damage. Crabaletta I treated as **ammunition rather than
an asset** — her Bow is 8 damage, so the correct thing to do with her is to rotate
her into the lead and Spend her, not to protect her. Chevalmarin I protected least;
her act is 2 damage and her Bow is an aura, and neither kept me alive. The one time
I chose to protect Crabaletta instead (fight 1 turn 4, declining a free Scene Change
to save 3 HP) I was choosing damage over defence with a hand that had none, and both
performers died anyway.

**5. Did the three read as three, or as one Osty three times.**

**Mostly as one performer with three skins — and the reason is structural, not
cosmetic.**

They *are* written as three distinct things, and on paper the distinctions are sharp:
Usher gives 3 Block / Bows for 4 Block; Chevalmarin deals 2 to every enemy and applies
Hydro / Bows for Hydro on all; Crabaletta deals 5 to a random enemy / Bows for 8
damage. Block, AoE-plus-element, and single-target burst is a clean three-way split,
and there was exactly one turn — elite turn 3, all three seats filled — where I felt
a *cast*: three different lines in the log, 3 Block and 7 damage arriving from three
directions for no energy.

But for the other ~95% of the run they collapsed into one, for four reasons:

- **Only one of them can matter at a time.** The lead is the only seat that can be
  Spent, the only seat that is hit, and the only seat Ousia Surge and Curtain Rise
  and Grand Entrance read. Whoever is in front *is* "the performer"; the others are
  a queue.
- **The generic cards do not know who is on stage.** Salon Début summons "a random
  performer", Rising Applause raises "the back performer", Pneuma Refrain reads "the
  back performer", Scene Change rotates "the cast". Nothing in that vocabulary asks
  me *which* of the three, so I stopped thinking about which.
- **Fanfare is one undifferentiated number on all three.** A bar of 5 on Crabaletta
  and a bar of 5 on Usher behave identically for shielding, for Spending and for
  Ousia Surge; the identity only reappears at the instant of the Bow.
- **They died too fast for their differences to accumulate.** A performer that lives
  one turn contributes one act. The difference between 3 Block and 5 damage over one
  turn is not enough to make them feel like different characters.

The moments they *did* read as three were exactly the moments the Bows differed and I
could choose between them — Scene Change into a Crabaletta Spend for 8 damage instead
of an Usher Spend for 4 Block. **The cast is three when I get to choose whose exit I
am buying, and one when I do not.**
