### The six extra cards

I was told six extra cards were placed in my deck before the run opened. **The
game never marked which six.** The floor-3 removal screen printed the whole deck
at 19 cards, and nothing on it distinguished a planted card from a starter:
no tag, no ordering, no "added" note. So I cannot name the six with any
confidence — I can only give the full deck as printed and say which of it looked
like it was not base kit.

Full deck as the floor-3 "Choose a card to Remove" screen printed it (19):

> Injury · Freminet — Pers, Deploy! [Cryo] · Soloist's Solicitation (×2) ·
> Lynette — Enigmatic Feint · Stage Presence (×2) · Regal Bearing · Curtain Rise ·
> Salon Début · Rising Applause · Understudy (×2) · Surintendante Chevalmarin ·
> Mademoiselle Crabaletta · Tidal Flourish · Grand Entrance · Let the People
> Rejoice · Ousia Surge

Of those, **Let the People Rejoice** and **Injury** I know came from my own Neow
pick (Hefty Tablet), and **Ousia Surge** was not in the deck at floor 1 — I drew
it as a fight-1 card reward. My guess at the planted six, offered as a guess and
nothing more, is the six that do not look like a starter's spine: **Freminet —
Pers, Deploy!**, **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint**, **Understudy ×2**, **Salon
Début**, **Rising Applause** — every one of them a summon, a Raise, or a
Companion, i.e. exactly the cards that turn the stage on. If that guess is
right, the run I played was a stage-rich one and my read of "the stage is the
engine" is partly a read of the plant, not of the base kit. **A seat cannot tell
these apart from the screen, and the record should carry that caveat.**

Deck at the end (25): the 19 above, minus Injury (removed at the Dark Door on
floor 3), with Tidal Flourish now **Tidal Flourish+** (the floor-5 event's random
upgrade), plus Ousia Surge, Interposition ×2, a second Surintendante Chevalmarin,
a second Grand Entrance, Bis!, Warm Reception, Crashing Waves.

### Neow pick

**Hefty Tablet** — "Choose 1 of 3 Rare cards to add to your Deck. Add 1 Injury to
your Deck." I took it over Kaleidoscope (two card rewards from *other*
characters) and New Leaf (transform 1 at random) because the only one of the
three that would show me this kit's own top end was the one that dealt me three
of its rares, and a single unplayable curse is a price a `?`-room card removal
can undo — which is exactly what happened two floors later.

From the three rares (Singer of Many Waters, heal 6 and exhaust; Let the People
Rejoice; Command Performance, add 2 random Uncommon Companions) I took **Let the
People Rejoice**, because it was the one card on the screen that named three
mechanics at once — Spend, Fanfare, Bow — and I wanted the card that would teach
me the kit fastest. It turned out to be the best card in my deck.

## Fight 1 — Corpse Slug (1) 26 HP + Corpse Slug (2) 25 HP

Both carried *Ravenous 4*: "When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it,
becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength."

**Turn 1** (energy 3; Usher lead at 3 Fanfare; incoming 3×2 and 8 = 14).
Played **Mademoiselle Crabaletta** (a second body, into the back seat where "no
single attack reaches it"), then **Tidal Flourish** in its **Spend 2** mode for 9
to all instead of 5, then **Stage Presence** for 6 Block.

The rejected alternative was Tidal Flourish's plain 5-to-all, and the reason I
rejected it is the whole shape of this kit: 14 incoming against 3 Block from
Usher's act meant **11 would arrive at Usher's 3-point bar and empty it whatever
I did**. The bar was already spent by the enemy's plan; spending it myself cost
2 points of a shield that was going to be gone anyway and bought 4 damage on each
of two bodies. I also rejected playing Freminet for 6 instead of Stage Presence —
6 damage now against 6 HP now, and I wanted Usher alive as a wall.

Outcome: 9 to each; I took 4. The screen then told me exactly what I had traded:
"**Usher** left the stage: emptied by a hit, so no Bow." That is the sentence
that made the kit legible to me — a bar taken by a hit is wasted, a bar spent by
me pays out.

**Turn 2** (slugs at 17 and 16; A intends 8, B intends a debuff).
Played **Soloist's Solicitation ×2 into B** for 12, killing it at 11 HP, then
**Surintendante Chevalmarin**.

This was the fight's real decision and it was made on Ravenous, not on the kit:
killing B fed it to A, which *Stunned* A and skipped its 8-damage attack, at the
price of handing A four Strength it would never live to use. I rejected spreading
the damage (kill nothing, take 8) and I rejected Stage Presence (block I would
not need if the kill landed). The gamble was that Stun would fire; it did, and A
sat there at 17 with Strength 4 and "Intent: Stunned".

**Turn 3** (A at 10 after the performers' acts, intending 12).
Played **Grand Entrance** in its plain **Deal 10** mode for exact lethal.
Rejected the **Spend 5** mode: 20 into a 10-HP body is 10 damage thrown away, and
it would have emptied Crabaletta for a Bow that nothing needed. This turn had no
decision in it worth the name — but the *fight* did, on turn 2, and the turn-1
Spend is what made the turn-3 number reachable.

Won at 58/78. Rewards: 17 gold, Explosive Ampoule, and a card — I took **Ousia
Surge** ("Deal 0 damage, the lead performer's Fanfare") over The Witness Stand
(Vulnerable + draw) and Commanding Gaze (2 Block, Weak all), because Ousia Surge
is the one card that makes the size of a bar matter without spending it, and that
tension — the shield is also the weapon — is the thing I wanted to test.

## Fight 2 — Seapunk 45 HP

**Turn 1** (Usher lead 3; incoming 11).
Played **Mademoiselle Crabaletta**, then **Surintendante Chevalmarin** — and this
is where the stage got its third seat: the page came back reading "middle:
Crabaletta 1 · back: Chevalmarin 1". **There is a middle seat and no screen in
the run ever defined it.** The glossary defines "lead performer" and "back
performer" and stops. I inferred the middle is safe from single-target hits like
the back is, but I inferred it; I was never told.

Then **Tidal Flourish, Spend 2** for 9, on the same reasoning as fight 1: 11
incoming against 3 Block would eat Usher's whole bar regardless.

**Turn 2** (Seapunk at 29, intending 2×4; Crabaletta lead at 2, Chevalmarin back).
Played **Grand Entrance, Spend 5** — out of a **2**-point bar.

This is the loophole, and it is enormous. The keyword says it plainly: "Pays the
lead performer, **fires in full even if the bar is short**, and an emptied
performer takes a Bow." So a 2-point bar bought the full 20 damage *and* triggered
Crabaletta's Bow for 8 more. 28 damage out of two Fanfare. Seapunk went 29 → 1,
and **Soloist's Solicitation** finished it.

I rejected the plain 10 mode without much thought once I had read that clause;
what I want on the record is that the decision felt less like a play than like
finding the seam in the rules. See debrief 2.

Won at 51/78, untouched that turn. Card reward: **Interposition** ("Gain 5 Block |
Spend 2: gain 10 instead") over a second Gentilhomme Usher summon and An
Invitation, because I wanted to see whether the short-bar seam paid on defence
too.

## Fight 3 — Toadpole (1) 24 HP + Toadpole (2) 21 HP

**Turn 1** (A empowering, B attacking for 7; Usher lead 3; incoming 7).
Played **Curtain Rise, Spend 3**, emptying Usher exactly — 13 damage into A *and*
Usher's Bow, "Furina gains 4 Block".

The reason is the fight-1 lesson applied deliberately: 7 incoming against 0 Block
in hand would take 7, and Usher's 3-point bar would be **emptied by the hit for
nothing**. Spending it first converted a bar that was doomed into 6 extra damage
and 4 Block. Rejected: the plain 7, which keeps a wall that was about to fall
over anyway.

Then **Understudy** (0 cost, "Summon a random performer who is not on stage" —
it brought Usher straight back), **Mademoiselle Crabaletta**, **Soloist's
Solicitation** into A.

**Turn 2** (A at 5 with *Thorns 2* and 3×3 incoming, B at 16 empowering).
Played **Grand Entrance, Spend 5** into **B**, out of Usher's 2-point bar: 20
damage killed the 16-HP body outright. Then **Surintendante Chevalmarin**, and
ended the turn knowing the performers' acts (Crabaletta 5, Chevalmarin 2) would
finish A's remaining 5 before it ever swung.

Rejected: pointing the 20 at A, the 5-HP body — the Spend's number is fixed and
overkill is pure waste, so the target choice, not the mode choice, was the
decision. Also rejected attacking A directly at all, because of Thorns 2: every
card hit pays 2 back, and the performers' end-of-turn acts appear not to be card
hits.

Won taking **zero** damage. Rewards: 14 gold, Dexterity Potion, and a card — I
took a **second Surintendante Chevalmarin** over Final Bow ("The lead performer
takes a Bow and leaves. Gain 0 Block, its Fanfare") and a second Ousia Surge,
because the Chevalmarin card never blanks: it summons her, or, if she is already
out, it Raises 3.

## Fight 4 — Living Fog 80 HP

**Turn 1** (Fog telegraphing 8 damage *and* a card debuff; the page warned it
"makes no claim about which of them the enemy will perform").
Played **Salon Début**, **Soloist's Solicitation**, **Freminet — Pers, Deploy!**
for 12 damage and a third body. Rejected Ousia Surge (3 damage off a 3-bar, worse
than a plain 6-damage attack) and Regal Bearing.

Only the debuff half of the intent resolved: I took no damage and got **Smoggy 1
— "You can only play 1 Skill per turn."** In a deck where every summon and every
Raise is a Skill, that debuff shuts the stage down. It did not shut down the
attacks, which is what made the rest of the fight playable.

**Turn 2** (Fog at 66, telegraphing 5 and a summon; Smoggy live).
Played **Understudy** — 0 cost, and a Skill, so it *was* my one Skill for the
turn, which is the cheapest possible way to pay Smoggy's tax — then **Curtain
Rise** and **Grand Entrance** both in their **plain** modes, for 17.

This is the turn where I did **not** spend, and it is the sharpest keep-decision
of the run. Incoming was 5. Block 3 from Usher's act left 2 arriving at Usher's
3-point bar — **the bar would survive**. The moment the incoming number is
smaller than Block plus bar, the bar stops being doomed and starts being an
asset, and paying it out costs a real shield instead of a spent one. I rejected
the double-Spend line (33 damage instead of 17) because it would have emptied the
stage on the turn the Fog telegraphed a summon, and my only answer to adds was
the performers' AoE.

**Turn 3** (Fog at 42, plus a **Gas Bomb** minion at 7 HP with a Death Blow for 8).
Played **Tidal Flourish+** plain 8-to-all, killing the Bomb before it detonated
and hitting the Fog for the same 8; then Soloist; then **Rising Applause**, my
one Skill, "Raise 5 Fanfare on the back performer", banking Crabaletta to 6 in a
seat nothing could reach.

**Here the screen contradicted itself**, and it decided something. My hand
printed:

> **Tidal Flourish+** (upgraded) — cost 1, attack
> Choose one: Deal 8 damage to ALL enemies | Spend 2: deal 12 instead.

The mode chooser that opened when I played it printed:

> - **Deal 5 damage to ALL enemies** — cost 0, skill
> - **Spend 2: deal 9 instead** — cost 0, skill

Those are the *unupgraded* numbers, and the chooser is the screen you are
actually choosing on. The Fog went 42 → 34, so the **8** was real and the
chooser's **5** was a lie. I had already decided on the hand's numbers; had I
been deciding on the chooser's, I would have mis-costed the Bomb kill.

**Turn 4** (Fog at 21; Chevalmarin lead 2, Crabaletta back 6, total 8 on stage).
Played **Let the People Rejoice**: "Spend all Fanfare on stage. Deal 8 damage to
ALL enemies. Every performer takes a Bow, then returns at 1."

Eight Fanfare on the stage, eight damage printed, and the log paid out both Bows —
"**Chevalmarin** took a Bow: Hydro on every enemy", "**Crabaletta** took a Bow: 8
to Living Fog" — and then put both of them straight back at 1. This is the card
Rising Applause was played for two turns earlier: 16 damage and the stage still
standing. The performers' acts finished the Fog before it could act. That is a
plan paying off, not a dead turn.

Won at 48/78. Card reward: a second **Grand Entrance** over a Gentilhomme Usher
summon and An Invitation.

## Fight 5 (Elite) — Skulking Colony 75 HP

*Hardened Shell 20 of 20 left this turn — "Skulking Colony cannot lose more than
20 HP each turn."* This monster is built to punish exactly the thing my deck does
best, and it made the elite the most interesting fight of the run.

**Turn 1** (incoming 14; Usher lead 3).
Played **Surintendante Chevalmarin**, then **Grand Entrance, Spend 5** — 20
damage out of a 3-point bar, which is **precisely the shell's cap, from one card,
for two energy**, plus Usher's Bow for 4 Block.

Rejected: Curtain Rise plus Tidal Flourish, which also reaches 20 but burns two
cards to get there and leaves the cap hit anyway; and rejected Regal Bearing's
Weak, which I wanted and could not afford. Against a per-turn damage ceiling the
Spend seam stops being a loophole and becomes the correct, efficient line — you
are buying *exactly* the cap for the smallest possible outlay. I liked this turn.

Took 9. The log then printed something I could not read: "**Chevalmarin**
performed: **nothing landed**." Her act is 2 damage to every enemy plus Hydro.
The Colony's shell had already absorbed its 20 for the turn, so the 2 was eaten —
fine — but the enemy came back next turn wearing *Hydro Aura 1*, so something
plainly did land. "Nothing landed" is wrong on its face.

**Turn 2** (HP 39; stage empty; Colony at 55).
Used the **Dexterity Potion** first (+2 to every Block from a card, permanent for
the fight — the right moment for it is the start of a long block fight, not the
end), then **Salon Début** (it brought Crabaletta), **Stage Presence** for 8, and
**Soloist's Solicitation**. Rejected Lynette's 5 Block + Swirl, because Swirl
needs an aura on a body I want copied *from*, and against one enemy it does
nothing at all.

**Turn 3** (HP 34; stage empty again — Crabaletta was emptied by a hit).
**Surintendante Chevalmarin**, **Stage Presence** (8), **Soloist's Solicitation**.
Rejected **Ousia Surge**, which on an empty stage prints "Deal 0 damage", and even
after the summon would have read 1. Rejected **Interposition, Spend 2**: 12 Block
instead of 7, but paid out of the 1-point bar of the performer I had just spent an
energy to put on the board, who would then leave immediately. Paying a
just-summoned body straight back out is the seam turning against you.

This is the stretch where the kit stopped working, and it is worth naming: the
Colony hits for 14–18 in one lump, every performer arrives at **1 Fanfare**, and
so **every body I summoned died to the very next hit before its bar could grow**.
Three turns in a row the log read "left the stage: emptied by a hit, so no Bow."
With no bar, Ousia Surge is blank, every Spend mode is a wet firework, and the
deck degenerates into 6-damage Soloists and 8-point blocks. The kit's whole
payoff layer is gated behind a resource that a big single hitter deletes on
arrival.

**Turn 4** (HP 34; Colony 36, intending 9×2).
The turn that fixed it. Played **Understudy**, **Understudy** (both 0 cost — they
brought Usher and Crabaletta), **Mademoiselle Crabaletta** (already on stage, so
it Raised 3 instead), **Rising Applause** (Raise 5) — Crabaletta ended the turn at
**9 Fanfare, in the back seat, where nothing could touch her** — and then
**Freminet — Pers, Deploy!**, whose Cryo met the Hydro the Colony was wearing:
"**Frozen** on **Skulking Colony**, off Freminet — Pers, Deploy!". Its 9×2 became
4×2, and the shell dropped from 20 to 14.

That is the answer to the previous three turns: **the back seat is the only place
a bar survives**, and stacking one there is how you get a Spend payload against a
big hitter. Rejected: any attack card, all of which would have been individually
larger than Freminet's 6 and none of which would have halved the incoming.

**Turn 5** (HP 33; Colony 25, intending 16; Crabaletta now lead at 5).
Played **Grand Entrance, plain 10**, and **Regal Bearing** (5 Block, 1 Weak).
Rejected **Grand Entrance, Spend 5**: 20 + Crabaletta's 8-damage Bow is 28 into a
**20-cap**, i.e. eight points on the floor, in exchange for my only performer and
my only shield at 33 HP. The cap inverted the seam — this was the one turn where
the short-bar Spend was clearly *wrong*, and it was the most satisfying read of
the run. Weak took the 16 down and I ended the turn on 31 HP with the Colony at 10.

**Turn 6:** plain Grand Entrance for exact lethal.

Won at 31/78. Rewards: 43 gold, Power Potion, **Beating Remnant** ("You cannot
lose more than 20 HP in a single turn"), and a card — I took **Bis!** ("The lead
performer performs its act now") over a third Chevalmarin and Duet, because it is
the only card in the run that pays you for *which* performer is in front.

## Fight 6 — Gremlin Merc 49 HP (→ Sneaky Gremlin 13, Fat Gremlin 17)

**Turn 1** (HP 31; incoming 7×2).
**Understudy**, **Surintendante Chevalmarin** (already summoned by the Understudy,
so it Raised 3 — Chevalmarin ended at **4 in the back seat**), **Lynette —
Enigmatic Feint** for 5 Block, **Soloist's Solicitation**. Deliberately
front-loaded the bank into the safe seat this time, having learned the elite's
lesson. Rejected the turn-1 Grand Entrance Spend that had opened fights 2, 3 and
5: at 31 HP I could not afford to hand my body count away for tempo.

**Turn 2** (Merc at 41; Chevalmarin lead at 5).
Played **Understudy**, **Stage Presence** (6 Block), **Grand Entrance, plain 10**.

The rejected alternative is the interesting one. Chevalmarin sat on **exactly 5**,
which is exactly Grand Entrance's Spend cost — the only "full price" Spend the
run ever offered me. I turned it down. 20 damage instead of 10 would have cost my
only performer, my only shield, and my 5-point bank, at 28 HP against a 41-HP
enemy. **The one time the game let me pay full price, full price was too
expensive** — which is a fair summary of what is wrong with the pricing. I also
rejected **Bis!**, whose text I had bought the card for: with Chevalmarin leading,
Bis! buys her act, which is 2 damage. With Crabaletta leading it buys 5, with
Usher 3 Block. It is a card whose value is set entirely by a seat I only sometimes
control.

**Turn 3** (Merc at 29; Chevalmarin lead 3, Usher back 1; I was wearing Weak 2).
The double-seam turn: **Curtain Rise, Spend 3** (emptying Chevalmarin — Bow: Hydro
on all), which promoted Usher to lead at 1, then **Tidal Flourish+, Spend 2** out
of that **1**-point bar (emptying Usher — Bow: 4 Block), then **Mademoiselle
Crabaletta** for a fresh body. Two full-price payloads out of four Fanfare, plus
two Bows. The Merc went 29 → 6.

I want to be honest that this turn was not a decision so much as an exploit
rehearsal: once you know the rider fires in full from a short bar, and that
emptying earns a Bow, chaining Spends down through your seats is simply the most
damage available and there is nothing to weigh.

**Turn 4** (Merc at 6). **Freminet** (Cryo onto its Hydro → Frozen), **Chevalmarin**,
**Stage Presence**; the performers finished it — and the Merc's *Surprise 1* buff
turned out to mean it splits into two gremlins on death.

**Turn 5.** Fat Gremlin carried *Heist 60* — "When killed, returns all the stolen
Gold" — and was intending to **Escape** that turn. **Grand Entrance, Spend 5** out
of Chevalmarin's 2-point bar killed the 17-HP body and banked 60 gold; **Soloist**
plus the **Explosive Ampoule** cleared the 13-HP Sneaky Gremlin so the fight ended
without another swing. Rejected saving the potion: at 25/78 with a boss ahead, a
common potion for a clean exit is cheap.

Won at 25/78. Card reward: a second **Interposition** over a third Grand Entrance
(three 2-cost bombs in a 23-card deck is a clog) and Stage Combat.

## Fight 8 — Calcified Cultist 41 HP + Damp Cultist 51 HP

Both carried **Ritual** (+2 and **+5** Strength at the end of every one of their
turns), so the fight had a clock: their damage compounds and mine does not.

**Turn 1** (both buffing, no damage incoming).
**Mademoiselle Crabaletta**, **Warm Reception** (Raise 3 into the back seat plus a
draw), **Soloist's Solicitation**. Banking on a free turn, again.

**Turn 2** (incoming 10; hand was five cards and **every one of them a Skill**).
**Understudy**, **Surintendante Chevalmarin** (Raise 3), **Stage Presence**,
**Interposition — plain 5 Block**.

The rejected alternative here is the best keep-decision of the run.
Interposition's Spend 2 gives 10 Block instead of 5, and Usher was leading on 5
Fanfare. I refused, because I was assembling a Let the People Rejoice turn and
every point I spent on defence was a point off that card's damage. **Fanfare
became a currency with two competing sinks, and that is the first time in eight
fights the resource felt like a resource rather than a seam.** I ended the turn
with 13 Fanfare spread across three seats and took zero damage.

**Turn 3** (Calcified 34, Damp 38 and growing; energy 4).
**Tidal Flourish+, Spend 2** for 12 to both, then **Grand Entrance, Spend 5** out
of Usher's remaining 3 (Bow: 4 Block) for 20 into the Damp Cultist, then
**Soloist's Solicitation** to finish it. Killed the 51-HP body outright and left
Crabaletta and Chevalmarin standing on 4 apiece.

Rejected: pointing both Grand Entrances at one body (I only had energy for one),
and rejected spending Crabaletta's bar as well — the +5-Strength body was dead,
so the clock had stopped and there was no longer anything to buy with the rest of
the bank.

**Turn 4** (Calcified at 15; hand again **all Skills**, no attack in it).
**Salon Début**, **Rising Applause**, **Stage Presence**. A turn with no real
choice in it, forced by the draw: with no attack in hand the only question was
which blocks to play, and the answer was all of them. Worth recording that this
deck draws all-Skill hands often — this was the second in one fight — because
every summon, every Raise and every block is a Skill and only about a third of
the deck is an attack.

## The kit, after 8 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Three kinds, and only one of them lives on the turn.

*At the draft:* the Neow choice between three rares was real (a heal, a
board-wipe-plus-reset, and a Companion generator are genuinely different decks),
and so was every card reward that offered a Raise against a payload — Rising
Applause and Warm Reception are cards you take because you have decided the back
seat is where you live. The single most consequential decision I made all run was
taking **Let the People Rejoice** at Neow, and the second was taking
**Interposition** so that Fanfare had a defensive sink to compete with its
offensive one.

*Earlier in the fight:* the real decisions are **where to put Fanfare and
whether to protect it**. Fight 4's turn 3 (Rising Applause banking Crabaletta to
6 in the back seat) is the decision that made fight 4's turn 4 a 16-damage
Rejoice. Elite turn 4 (two Understudies, a Raise 3 and a Raise 5, all into the
back seat, plus Freminet for Frozen) is the decision that turned a losing elite
around. Neither of those turns did much when I played it; both of them decided a
later turn. That is a good shape, and it is the kit's best feature.

*On the turn:* the honest answer is that the plain-versus-Spend choice is usually
**not** a decision, for the reason in (b), and it becomes one only in three
specific situations, all of which I met: (i) when the incoming number is smaller
than Block-plus-bar, so the bar will survive and spending it costs a real shield
— fight 4 turn 2, fight 7 turns 1 and 2; (ii) when the enemy has a per-turn damage
cap, so overkill is visibly wasted — the elite, where I refused a 28-damage line
because the shell only permitted 20; (iii) when two Spend cards in the same hand
want the same bar — fight 8 turn 2, refusing Interposition's 10 Block to keep 5
Fanfare for Rejoice. Those three turns were excellent. They were three turns out
of about thirty.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

**The Spend rider is automatic, and the clause that makes it automatic is
printed right on it:** "*fires in full even if the bar is short, and an emptied
performer takes a Bow*." Since the payload does not scale with what you pay, and
since emptying is *rewarded* with a Bow, the arithmetic is nearly always: Spend.
A 2-point bar buys the whole 20 of Grand Entrance and adds Crabaletta's 8-damage
Bow — 28 damage for two Fanfare. I did this in fights 2, 3, 5, 6 and 8 and it was
correct every time except once. Worse, the default state of the bar is *doomed*:
a lead performer's Fanfare is what the enemy's attack eats after Block, so on most
turns the bar is going to be emptied by a hit **for nothing** ("emptied by a hit,
so no Bow") unless I empty it myself for full value. When the resource's baseline
outcome is total loss, "should I spend it" is not a question. The kit needs the
short-bar Spend to cost something — scale the payload, or deny the Bow when the
bar could not cover the price — or the whole Fanfare economy is a formality
between a summon and a payout.

**Never worth playing: Ousia Surge.** "Deal 0 damage, the lead performer's
Fanfare." It printed 3, 2, 5, 3 and 2 in the hands I held it, i.e. it is a 1-cost
attack for less than a Soloist's Solicitation, *and* it wants the exact resource
that everything else in the deck wants to spend, *and* it wants it on the lead —
the one seat the enemy is actively emptying. I never played it once. Ousia Surge
is the card that should reward a big bar and it cannot, because a big bar only
survives in the back seat and Ousia Surge reads the front.

**Bis!** ("The lead performer performs its act now") I drafted and never played,
for the adjacent reason: its value is 2, 3 or 5 depending on who happens to be in
front, and I control who is in front only by killing off the one there now.

**Regal Bearing** and **Lynette** are fine but generic; Lynette's Swirl was dead
in six of the eight fights because there was one enemy or no aura.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

The list is in the section above; the four that actually cost me something were
the mode chooser printing unupgraded numbers, the `confirm` instruction that is
false on the screen that prints it, the undefined **middle seat**, and the
**Raise** keyword saying "back performer" while the cards that carry it raise
whoever they name. Conceptually, the one thing I never resolved is **who acts,
and for how much**: the glossary gives fixed numbers ("Usher gives you 3 Block,
Chevalmarin deals 2 to every enemy, Crabaletta deals 5") and the log printed a 4
for Chevalmarin once. If those numbers scale with something, nothing on screen
says with what.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Ousia Surge**, for the reasons in (b) — a payoff card pointed at
the one seat where the resource cannot accumulate.

Happiest to draw: **Let the People Rejoice**. It is the only card in the deck
that spends the *whole* stage and gives it back — "Every performer takes a Bow,
**then returns at 1**" — so it converts the bank into damage *and* both Bows
*and* keeps the board. Playing it in fights 4 and 7 felt like the kit's actual
idea. **Grand Entrance** did more damage more often and I was less happy to see
it, because I knew before I read the board what I was going to do with it.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

Yes — a real one, and a good one, though not the one the kit probably intends.
Two Corpse Slugs at 26 and 25, 14 damage incoming, three energy, and a lead
performer sitting on a 3-point bar. The choice was Tidal Flourish's plain 5-to-all
against its Spend 2 for 9, and it turned on a fact I had to work out from the
Fanfare keyword rather than being told: 14 incoming against 3 Block meant the bar
was going to be destroyed by the enemy whatever I did, so spending 2 of it cost
nothing real. I also had to choose between a second body (Crabaletta) and a
6-damage attack for the same energy, which is a genuine tempo-versus-engine
question on turn one.

So: yes, but note *why* it was a decision. It was a decision because I did not yet
know the rule. By fight 3 the same turn-1 configuration had a known answer and I
played it in eight seconds.

## The five debrief questions

**1. On turn one of fight one, which line, and why.**

Mademoiselle Crabaletta → Tidal Flourish in **Spend 2** mode (9 to all) → Stage
Presence. Crabaletta first because a second body is the only thing on turn one
that pays every subsequent turn, and because the back seat is out of reach of
single-target attacks, so the summon is also 1 point of banked Fanfare that
cannot be taken. Tidal Flourish in Spend mode because both enemies were AoE-worthy
and, critically, because 14 incoming against 3 Block meant Usher's 3-point bar was
going to be emptied by the enemy anyway — I was spending a shield that had already
been sold. Stage Presence over Freminet's 6 damage because keeping Usher alive one
more turn is worth more than 6 damage in a fight I expected to last four turns.
The line was not optimal — Usher died anyway — but the reasoning it was built on
turned out to be the correct model of the kit.

**2. When you chose NOT to spend on a Spend card, what were you keeping the bar
for; and when you spent from a small bar, was it a play or a loophole.**

I kept the bar on exactly three occasions and for three different reasons.

*To keep a shield that would actually survive.* Fight 4, turn 2: incoming 5, Block
3, Usher on 3. Block-plus-bar exceeded the incoming, so the bar was **not** doomed
— it was a wall that would still be standing next turn, and Usher regains 1 a turn
as lead, so keeping it was compounding. I played Curtain Rise and Grand Entrance
in plain mode and gave up 16 damage for it. Same reasoning, fight 7 turns 1 and 2.

*To keep a board against a summoner.* The same fight-4 turn: the Living Fog was
telegraphing "Summon", and the double-Spend line would have emptied my stage the
turn before adds arrived. I was keeping the bar in order to keep the *bodies* — a
Spend does not just cost Fanfare, it removes the performer, and the performer is
3 Block or 5 damage every turn thereafter.

*To keep it for a bigger sink.* Fight 8, turn 2: Interposition offered 10 Block
instead of 5 for 2 Fanfare, and I refused because I was assembling a Let the
People Rejoice turn and every point spent on Block was a point off that card's
damage and off Crabaletta's Bow. This is the only time in the run that Fanfare
behaved like a currency with competing uses, and it is the only time refusing a
Spend felt like a decision rather than caution.

And once I kept it because I could not afford the *body*: fight 6, turn 2,
Chevalmarin sitting on exactly 5 with Grand Entrance in hand — the one full-price
Spend the run offered — and at 28 HP against a 41-HP enemy the 10 extra damage was
not worth my only performer and my only shield.

**Spending from a small bar was a loophole, not a play,** and I want to be exact
about why. The rider prints "*fires in full even if the bar is short*", so the
payload is completely decoupled from the payment; and it prints "*an emptied
performer takes a Bow*", so emptying is not a cost but a **second reward**. Put
together: paying 5 out of a 2-point bar bought Grand Entrance's full 20 damage
*plus* Crabaletta's 8-damage Bow — 28 damage for 2 Fanfare, versus 20 damage for 5
Fanfare if I had waited and paid properly. **The kit charges you less for being
poorer and hands you a bonus for going bankrupt.** After I noticed it in fight 2
there was nothing left to weigh on those turns; I was executing, not deciding, and
fight 6's turn 3 — chaining a Spend 3 through Chevalmarin and then a Spend 2
through the 1-point bar it promoted — was pure seam-running.

The exception, and it is a genuinely good one, was the **elite**. *Hardened Shell*
capped the Colony's loss at 20 a turn, which put a ceiling on the payload and
therefore restored a price to overkill: on turn 1 the 20-damage Spend out of a
3-point bar was *exactly* the cap for two energy, which felt brilliant; on turn 5
the same button would have thrown 8 damage into the ceiling and cost me my last
performer, and I refused it. A damage cap is the only thing in eight fights that
made the Spend decision hard, which suggests the fix is on the card, not the
monster.

**3. Did you want a second performer on stage, and what for.**

Yes, immediately and in every fight — I summoned a second body on turn 1 of six of
the eight fights, and the two exceptions were hands with no summon in them.

For four distinct things, and they are worth separating because only the first is
obvious:

*For the act.* Every performer acts at the end of every turn from any seat.
Crabaletta's 5 and Chevalmarin's 2-plus-Hydro are free damage that costs no
energy and no card once the body is down, and in fights 3 and 4 the performers'
acts, not my cards, landed the killing blow.

*For the seat.* The back seat is the only place in the game where Fanfare is safe
— "no single attack reaches it" — so a second performer is not just a second act,
it is **a vault**. Everything the kit wants to do at scale (Let the People
Rejoice, a real Ousia Surge, a Spend you actually paid for) requires a bar that
has survived more than one enemy turn, and the front seat cannot provide one. The
elite taught me this the hard way over three turns of "left the stage: emptied by
a hit, so no Bow", and then round 4 — two Understudies plus a Raise 3 plus a Raise
5, all funnelling into the back seat — was the turn that won it.

*For the Bow.* Each performer's Bow is a different effect (Usher 4 Block,
Crabaletta 8 damage, Chevalmarin Hydro on all), so a second body is a second
payout attached to a Spend I was going to make anyway. Crabaletta's 8 is worth
roughly a whole card.

*For depth.* When the lead dies the back seat leads. A second performer means the
next big hit lands on Fanfare instead of on me. Chevalmarin's 5-point bar ate five
damage in fight 8 that would otherwise have been HP.

What I wanted and rarely got was a second performer **with a bar on it**. A body
summoned at 1 Fanfare is an act and a speed bump; a body Raised to 6 in the back
is the deck's whole payoff engine. The gap between those two things is one Rising
Applause, and it is far too large.

**4. Which performer did you protect, and could you say why in one sentence.**

**Crabaletta**, and yes: her act is the biggest (5 a turn), her Bow is by far the
best (8 damage, against Usher's 4 Block and Chevalmarin's Hydro), so a Fanfare
point parked on Crabaletta is worth more than the same point anywhere else — which
is why every Raise I played went onto her when she was in the back, and why the
elite turned around on the turn I stacked her to 9 where nothing could touch her.

The honest footnote is that "protect" mostly meant "put her in the back and never
promote her", and I only ever controlled which seat she was in by choosing the
order I summoned people in. I could not move a performer, and the one card that
would have let me trade seats deliberately (Final Bow, "the lead performer takes a
Bow and leaves") I passed on because I read it as a Fanfare-cashing card and did
not spot until much later that its real use is **seat management** — killing off a
lead I do not want in front so that the one I have banked steps up. That is a
subtle card and its text does not advertise the thing it is best at.

**5. Did the three read as three, or as one Osty three times.**

**As three, clearly, and it is the strongest thing in the kit.** They differ on
all three axes that matter and the differences interacted with my decisions
constantly:

- *Act:* Usher gives Block, Chevalmarin does AoE damage plus an element,
  Crabaletta does single-target damage at random. That is a defender, a
  board-clearer and a striker.
- *Bow:* 4 Block / Hydro on all / 8 damage — three different reasons to want a
  Spend to empty *that* performer rather than another. Fight 3's turn 1 I spent
  Usher **for** the Block; fight 4's Rejoice I valued for **Crabaletta's** 8.
- *Element:* only Chevalmarin paints Hydro, which is what my Cryo Companion needed
  to make Frozen, which is what halved the elite's damage. She is the setup piece
  and the other two are not.

They also came with the right amount of friction: Salon Solitaire always opens
with **Usher**, so the tank is my default lead and the striker has to be summoned;
Understudy and Salon Début summon "a random performer who is not on stage", so who
I get is partly luck; and because a Spend always pays the *lead*, the question
"which one do I want emptied" has a real answer that changes turn to turn.

Two caveats. First, the differentiation is invisible until you have watched a full
turn cycle — the seat names, the acts and the Bows are spread across four separate
keyword entries and one of them (the middle seat) is not written down at all.
Second, **Chevalmarin's printed 2 is too small to matter next to Crabaletta's 5**,
so in practice she reads as "the Hydro one" and I valued her almost entirely for
the element and the Bow. The three are three; two of them are close to equal and
the third is a support piece whose damage number could be deleted without my
noticing.
