## Fight 1 — Sludge Spinner [A], 39 HP

**Pre-turn (Gambling Chip mulligan).** The screen was `Choose any number of cards
to replace` and it showed Defend ×4 and Slack Water. **It does not show the
enemy.** I was mulliganing blind into a lineup I could not see. I replaced three
Defends. *Rejected: keeping the hand* — four Defends and one 1-cost attack cannot
use three energy on anything.

A refusal here, and it is a finding: after selecting `Defend (2)` and
`Defend (3)`, I asked for `Defend (4)` and got
`nothing here is called 'Defend (4)'. What is on the screen: Defend (1),
Defend (2), Slack Water.` Selected cards *leave the list* and the survivors
renumber, so a name that was correct when the screen was drawn is wrong one act
later. I re-asked as `Defend (2)` and it worked. One refusal, not three.

**Turn 1** (HP 64, energy 3). Played **Slack Water on A** (4 damage, apply 1
Weak; the Casket fired for 2, so 6), then **Kurage's Oath onto the Bake-Kurage**
as a Plan, then **Strike on A** (6). Enemy 39 → 27, exactly 4+2+6, and its intent
fell from 8 to 6 in front of me — Weak visibly repriced the incoming attack on
the intent line, which is the clearest single piece of feedback the kit gave me
all round. *Rejected: Slack Water + Strike + Strike (16 now instead of 12 now and
7 later).* I took the Plan because Kurage's Oath converts 3 damage into 7 by
waiting one turn, and 3 damage to one enemy is not worth a card.

**Turn 2** (HP 58; Weak 1 on me). The Plan resolved at the top of the turn and
the screen printed it: `Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 7 — the 7 is damage. /
Sludge Spinner lost 7 HP`. Enemy carried `Hydro Aura 1`. Hand was Defend ×2,
Strike ×3, and **my Strikes printed "Deal 4 damage"** — the faces had already
subtracted my own Weak. Played **Strike, Strike, Defend**. *Rejected: three
Strikes* (12 now, but it does not change the kill turn and costs 5 more HP);
*rejected: two Defends* (leaves 16 to kill on turn 3 with an unknown draw).
This turn had no Plan card in hand and the Bake-Kurage panel said so —
`No Plan card in hand: the jellyfish waits.` The turn was pure vanilla Spire.

**Turn 3** (HP 52; Sparkling Rouge gave +1/+1). Enemy at 12. **Strike, Strike**
(7 each) for the kill. *No alternative rejected — this was an arithmetic check.*
But it was the turn-1 Plan that put it in range, so I count it as the plan
paying off rather than as a dead turn.

**Plans written:** T1 Kurage's Oath. T2 none. T3 none.
**Verdict:** two real decisions (T1 plan-vs-tempo, T2 damage-vs-block), one
lethal check. Fine for a first fight.

## Fight 2 — Toadpole (1) [A] 21 HP, Toadpole (2) [B] 25 HP

**Mulligan.** Defend ×3, Strike ×2. I replaced two Defends hoping for a Plan
card and **drew four Strikes and a Defend** — I mulliganed a bad hand into a
worse one and the jellyfish sat empty for the whole first turn. Again, blind to
the lineup.

**Turn 1** (HP 52). Played **Strike ×3 into A**, the 21-HP body. *Rejected:
focusing B, the one actually attacking for 7.* I took A because it was the
smaller body and the one buffing, and a buffing body left alive compounds. It
gained Thorns 2 for its trouble. No Plan card in hand: no decision the kit owns.

**Turn 2** (HP 45; A at 3 with Thorns 2 and intent 3x3, B at 25 buffing). Played
**Riptide** (9 to ALL — killed A, took B to 16, I ate 2 thorns) then
**Kurage's Oath onto the Bake-Kurage** (Plan, 7 to ALL next turn). *Rejected:
planning Riptide for 13 instead of playing it for 9* — that delivers 13 to B by
turn 3 versus 9+7=16, and it leaves A alive to land 9 damage on me. The
best line was visible on the screen because B's intent said Buff, so killing A
meant a turn where nothing could hit me. That was the fight's real decision and
it was made on the turn, off the intent lines.

**Turn 3** (HP 43; Strength 1). Plan landed for 7 (`Toadpole (2) lost 7 HP`), B
at 9 with Thorns 2 and intent 3x3. **Strike, Strike** (7 each) killed it.
*Rejected: Slack Water first for the Weak* — two hits is the thorns floor either
way and 14 ≥ 9, so the Weak would have bought nothing.

**Ask (3) data point.** On this turn, with Strength 1 up, **Kurage's Oath printed
"Deal 4 damage to ALL enemies. Plan: Deal 7 damage to ALL enemies."** Its own
line had moved 3 → 4 with my Strength; its Plan line had not moved off 7. The
Plan I had written the turn before, with 0 Strength, also delivered exactly 7.
So at this point the two numbers on the face were consistent with the glossary's
"no damage term of yours does [count]", and each delivered its own printed
number. **Feint** behaved the same way later: "Deal 7 damage. Plan: Deal 10
damage" with Strength 1, having printed 6/10 with none.

**Plans written:** T1 none. T2 Kurage's Oath. T3 none.
**Verdict:** one strong on-turn decision (kill A into a no-damage turn), one
draft-shaped decision (Riptide was the fight), one turn that presented nothing.

## Fight 3 — Corpse Slug (1) [A] 25 HP, Corpse Slug (2) [B] 26 HP

Both printed `Ravenous 4 (buff) — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately
eats it, becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength.` That text is the fight, and it
is legible: it tells you a kill is half a gift.

**Mulligan.** Riptide, Slack Water, Strike, Defend ×2 — I kept all five. The one
time the mulligan screen had nothing to fix.

**Turn 1** (HP 39). **Riptide** (9 to both) then **Slack Water on A** (4 + Weak,
Casket 2 → 15 off A total). A 25→10, B 26→17, A's intent 8→6. *Rejected: planning
Riptide for 13.* At 39 HP against 51 enemy HP I wanted bodies down, not a
promise. I misplayed the leftovers — I had two Defends in hand and no energy, and
took 6 I could have blocked.

**Turn 2** (HP 33, Frail 2 so Defends printed 3). A at 10 with intent Debuff, B
at 17 with intent 3x2. **Strike, Strike** killed A; B ate it and went **Stunned
+4 Strength**, so I took **zero damage** that turn. Then **Kurage's Oath onto the
Bake-Kurage**. *Rejected: playing Oath for 3 to all now* — 3 now versus 7 later,
into a turn I already knew would be free because the stun was printed on the
relic text before I committed. This was the best decision of the round and the
screen gave me everything I needed to make it.

**Turn 3** (HP 33). Plan landed 7 (`Corpse Slug (2) lost 7 HP`), B at 10 with
Strength 4 and intent 7x2. **Strike, Strike** (7 each) for the kill. *Rejected:
Defend + one Strike* — 14 ≥ 10, and killing dodges 14 damage.

**Plans written:** T1 none. T2 Kurage's Oath. T3 none.
**Verdict:** the whole fight was one decision — kill A on turn 2 and buy a free
turn — and the kit's Plan slotted into the free turn perfectly. The best fight of
the round.

## Fight 4 — Punch Construct [A], 55 HP, Artifact 1

**Mulligan.** Seven cards (Bag of Preparation). Strike ×4, Feint, Defend ×2. I
replaced two Strikes and drew Riptide, Vanguard and **Clumsy** — the curse the
event gave me. It printed `CANNOT BE PLAYED: has unplayable keyword`, which is
the clearest refusal message the bridge produced.

**Turn 1** (HP 33). Enemy intent was `Defensive (Defend)`, so damage now lands
clean and damage next turn does not. **Strike, Strike, Feint** — 18. *Rejected:
Riptide + Strike (15) and rejected planning anything.* Three 1-cost attacks beat
a 2-cost AoE against a single body, and against a telegraphed Block the correct
answer is to dump now. That is a genuine decision the kit created: **the Plan
mechanic is bad into an incoming Block and good into a live Block**, and the
intent line tells you which you are in.

**Turn 2** (HP 33; enemy 37/55 with Block 10 and Artifact 1). I spent
**Vanguard** (0 cost) as an experiment: would Artifact eat the Vulnerable, and
would the Casket still fire? The screen after: **Artifact gone, no Vulnerable,
HP unchanged at 37.** So a debuff negated by Artifact is not "applied" and the
Casket stays silent. Consistent with the relic's wording, but it took an
experiment to learn — no screen says it. **Vanguard also exhausted on the normal
play** (exhaust pile 1 → 2), which the face does not make clear: it prints
`Apply 1 Vulnerable. Plan: Apply 1 Vulnerable and 1 Weak. Exhaust.` and that
trailing Exhaust reads as if it belongs to the Plan clause.

Then **Slack Water on A** (Weak; its 5x2 fell to 3x2), **Kurage's Oath onto the
Bake-Kurage**, **Defend**. *Rejected: Strike into the 10 Block* (6 damage into 10
block is nothing) *and rejected planning Slack Water* (Weak next turn is worth
less than Weak before the attack lands). I took 1 damage.

**Turn 3** (HP 32). The plan **dodged the block**: it resolved at the top of my
turn, after the enemy's own turn had wiped its Block, and landed a clean 7
(37 → 30). That is the mechanic's real edge and nothing on the screen states it.
Enemy intent 14. **Slack Water** (Weak; 14 → 10), **Strike**, **Defend**. Took 6.
*Rejected: two Strikes + Defend* — the Weak was worth more than 2 damage.

**Turn 4** (HP 26). Enemy 16 and back on `Defensive (Defend)`. **Feint (7),
Strike (7), Kurage's Oath (4)** = 18 for the kill. *Rejected: planning any of
them* — the block intent again decided it, in the opposite direction from turn 3.

**Plans written:** T1 none. T2 Kurage's Oath. T3 none. T4 none.
**Verdict:** the best-decided fight in the round, and all of it came from the
enemy's Block/Attack alternation rather than from the kit. Four turns, four
different answers.

## Fight 5 (Elite) — Skulking Colony [A], 75 HP

`Hardened Shell 20 of 20 left this turn (buff) — Skulking Colony cannot lose more
than 20 HP each turn.` A per-turn damage cap, and it makes the Plan mechanic
structurally correct: damage above the cap is thrown away, but damage moved into
*next* turn lands under a fresh cap. That is the best argument for the kit's core
idea that anything in the round produced, and I found it by reading an enemy, not
a card.

**Mulligan.** Vanguard, Battle Plan, Riptide, Strike ×2, Defend ×2. Kept all.

**Turn 1** (HP 50; enemy intent 14). **Vanguard onto the Bake-Kurage**,
**Battle Plan onto the Bake-Kurage**, **Riptide** (9). *Rejected: playing
Vanguard now for the immediate Vulnerable* — Vulnerable expires at the end of the
enemy's turn, so playing it now buys a multiplier on this turn's cards only,
while planning it buys Vulnerable **and** Weak at the top of next turn where it
multiplies a whole turn's worth. *Also rejected: Defend ×2 for 10 block* — 0
damage against 75 HP is a losing race. I took the full 14.

**Turn 2** — the round's densest screen. The carry-out block printed:

```
Bake-Kurage: Vanguard, 1 — the 1 is Vulnerable. Inside the same beat:
  Tamakushi Casket 3 on Skulking Colony, Tamakushi Casket 3 on Skulking Colony.
  Skulking Colony lost 6 HP
Bake-Kurage: Battle Plan, 1 — the 1 is Energy.
  no enemy lost HP
```

Three things at once: (a) a Plan the jellyfish carries out **does** count as "you
apply a debuff" for the Casket; (b) it fired **twice**, once per debuff, for
Vulnerable and Weak; (c) each hit was **3, not 2**, because the Vulnerable it had
just applied was already on the body when the second half of the same beat
resolved. Energy read `4/3` and my hand was two cards fatter. Everything the two
Plans promised, arrived.

**And then the finding I care most about.** With `Vulnerable 1` on the enemy,
**Kurage's Oath printed "Deal 3 damage to ALL enemies. Plan: Deal 10 damage to
ALL enemies."** Its own line still read the base 3 — it does *not* fold the
target's Vulnerable into the number it shows — while its Plan line had jumped
7 → 10, which is 7 × 1.5 rounded down. So the same face showed two numbers
computed under two different rules: one ignoring the target's Vulnerable, one
pricing it in. I planned it on the strength of the printed 10.

Played **Kurage's Oath onto the Bake-Kurage** (printed Plan: 10), **Strike**
(6 × 1.5 = 9), **Slack Water** (Weak, refreshing it), **Defend**. The cap held
exactly: the Casket's 6 plus my 14 came to 20, and the enemy stopped losing HP at
`14 of 20 left this turn` → 0. I took 0 damage. *Rejected: a second Strike
instead of Slack Water* — identical damage after the cap, but Slack Water renews
the Weak that was blanking the incoming attack.

**Turn 3 — where the screen and the outcome disagreed.** The Plan I wrote while
its face read **10** carried out for **7**:
`Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 7 — the 7 is damage. / Skulking Colony lost 7 HP.`
The Vulnerable had expired at the end of the enemy's turn, in between the writing
and the carrying-out. So the number printed on a Plan line is **a live preview of
the board as it stands when you are looking at it, not a commitment about the
turn on which the Plan actually resolves** — and for a mechanic whose entire
premise is "commit now, collect next turn", that is the one number that most
needs to mean what it says. I made a real decision (spend an energy for 10) and
was paid 7. Nothing on the screen warned me; the Plan glossary's "Enemy
Vulnerable counts" is true at write-time and true at carry-out-time, but they are
different boards.

Rest of turn 3 (HP 31, enemy 39, cap 13 left after the plan): **Strike**,
**Slack Water**, **Defend**. Capped out at 13 and took 0. *Rejected: planning
Feint for 10* — it would have cost me the Defend and 6 HP to move damage into a
turn I did not yet need.

**Turn 4** (HP 31, enemy 26, cap 20, intent 6x2). **Riptide** (10), **Defend**
(6). Took 6. *Rejected: Riptide + Kurage's Oath for 14* — 14 could not kill 26
either, so the extra 4 damage cost 6 HP for nothing; *rejected: Riptide + Battle
Plan planned*, which would have made next turn's kill near-certain at the price
of 6 more HP. At 31 HP I bought the HP. This was a real, close call.

**Turn 5** (HP 25, enemy 16). **Strike ×3** (7 each) for the kill. *No
alternative — two Strikes is 14 and does not get there.*

**Plans written:** T1 Vanguard + Battle Plan (2). T2 Kurage's Oath (1). T3 none.
T4 none. T5 none.
**Verdict:** the fight the kit was built for. The damage cap made planning
correct rather than optional, and the turn-1 double-Plan (Vulnerable+Weak+energy+
draw arriving together) was the only moment in five fights where the Bake-Kurage
felt like an engine instead of a slower Strike.

## The extra logging asks

**(1) Plans per turn.** Recorded in each fight above. The totals: 6 Plans written
across 17 combat turns, and **11 of the 17 turns had no Plan written at all**.
Of those 11, at least 5 were turns where I held no Plan card and the panel said
so (`No Plan card in hand: the jellyfish waits.`). The rest were turns where
planning was wrong — into a telegraphed Block, or when the body was in kill
range.

**(2) First turn of every fight, on the jellyfish.** Before I had played
anything, every fight's first battle screen carried an identical three-part
statement: a buff line, `Bake Kurage 1 (buff) — Enemies cannot target it. Lasts
all combat. Play a Plan card on it: it carries out the Plan at the start of your
next turn.`; a `## The Bake-Kurage` section giving the targeting rule ("A Plan
with one target hits the front enemy and never a Minion… A Plan whose card says
ALL hits every living enemy, Minions included"), the Hydro-hit rule, and a state
line — `Nothing is planned. The morning is empty.` on a truly empty board, and
`No Plan card in hand: the jellyfish waits.` once it could tell my hand had none.
**Did I know which cards could be planned?** Yes, immediately and every time: a
plannable card prints a `Plan:` clause in its own body, and Strike/Defend/Clumsy
do not. That part of the kit is completely legible and I never once had to guess.
What I did **not** know from the first screen was that a Plan resolving at the
top of my turn lands *after* an enemy's Block has expired — I had to discover
that in fight 4 — nor that the Plan number is a live preview rather than a quote.

**(3) Cards showing two different numbers for their own line and their Plan
line.** Three cards did, and the rule is not the same in both directions:

| Card | Face at the time | What each delivered |
|---|---|---|
| Kurage's Oath, Strength 1, no Vulnerable | `Deal 4 damage to ALL. Plan: Deal 7 to ALL.` | Own line dealt 4. Plan carried out for exactly **7** — my Strength did not reach it. |
| Feint, Strength 1 | `Deal 7 damage. Plan: Deal 10 damage.` | Own line dealt 7 (base 6 + 1). Plan line never moved off 10 under Strength. |
| **Kurage's Oath, enemy Vulnerable 1** | **`Deal 3 damage to ALL. Plan: Deal 10 to ALL.`** | The own line ignored the target's Vulnerable (still base 3); the Plan line had multiplied it in (7 × 1.5 = 10). **When it carried out one turn later, with the Vulnerable expired, it delivered 7.** |

So: my own Strength moves the own line and never the Plan line, exactly as the
glossary says. The *target's* Vulnerable does the opposite — it moves the Plan
line and not the own line — and because the Plan line is computed against the
board in front of me rather than the board the Plan will land on, it is the one
number on the card that can lie. Vanguard is a fourth, softer case: its
`Exhaust` reads as part of the Plan clause and is not.

## The kit, after 5 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and where they were made.**

- *Plan-versus-play, on the turn, priced by the enemy's intent.* This is the
  kit's decision and it is a good one. Fight 4 gave me the same card two turns
  running with opposite answers: into a telegraphed `Defensive (Defend)` I dumped
  everything immediately, because a Plan would land into fresh Block; into a
  standing Block I planned, because the carry-out resolves after that Block has
  expired. Fight 5's damage cap gave a third reading — planning is how you spend
  energy that this turn's cap would otherwise throw away.
- *Which body to kill, on the turn, off printed enemy text.* Fight 3 turn 2 was
  the round's best moment: `Ravenous 4` told me killing A would Stun B, so I
  killed A and bought a free turn, then used the free turn to write a Plan. The
  kit's Plan and the enemy's rule combined into one decision.
- *At the draft: Riptide, and Vanguard.* Riptide decided fights 2 and 3 by
  itself. Vanguard's Plan line — Vulnerable **and** Weak, at 0 cost, arriving at
  the top of a turn where it multiplies everything — is where the Bake-Kurage
  stopped being a delayed Strike and started being an engine, and I only found
  that because I planned it instead of playing it.
- *Turn 5 of the elite, and turn 4 of fight 4: how much HP a marginal 4 damage is
  worth.* Real, close, and mine to get wrong.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

Eleven of seventeen turns were Strike/Defend allocation with no Plan involved,
and those turns are indistinguishable from base Spire. **Kurage's Oath played
normally** — 3 damage to all, 4 with Strength — was never worth a card; I played
it face-up exactly once, as the third card of a lethal. Its Plan line is a
different and much better card. **Defend** is Defend. The **Gambling Chip
mulligan** felt automatic in the wrong way: it does not show you the enemies, so
"replace the Defends, hope for a Plan card" is the only policy available, and in
fight 2 it turned a mediocre hand into four Strikes and a dead jellyfish.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **The Plan number is a preview, not a quote.** Kurage's Oath's face said
   `Plan: Deal 10` when I paid for it and delivered 7 when it resolved, because
   the target's Vulnerable expired in between. For a mechanic sold on committing
   a turn early, the committed number moving is the sharpest contradiction in the
   kit. I would either freeze the number at write-time or print the base and the
   modifier separately.
2. **The same face computes its two numbers under two different rules.** Own line
   folds in *my* Strength and ignores the *target's* Vulnerable; Plan line does
   the reverse. Nothing on the card or in the glossary says so; I worked it out
   from `Deal 3 … Plan: Deal 10` on one screen.
3. **The Casket's silence under Artifact.** Vanguard's Vulnerable was eaten by
   `Artifact 1` and the relic's 2 Hydro simply did not happen. Defensible, but
   invisible — no line anywhere said the debuff had been negated; the Artifact
   stack just vanished and the HP did not move.
4. **Vanguard's trailing `Exhaust`** reads as part of the Plan clause and is not.
5. **The Elemental Reaction glossary is enormous and, for this run, entirely
   theoretical.** It defines Vaporize, Electro-Charged, Swirl and gestures at six
   reactions, and one screen was honest enough to say `NO REACTION IS REACHABLE
   HERE: Hydro is the only element this screen can supply.` I played five fights
   with a Hydro-only deck and never triggered a single reaction. It is a large
   amount of printed rules for a system the base kit cannot reach on its own; it
   only becomes real if you draft a Companion of another element, which is a
   fourth-slot lottery.
6. Two of four cards in the first reward were `Companion`-gated
   (`The General's Banner`, `Rally`) and the fourth, `Fischl — Nightrider`, had
   its own glossary tell me two of its three clauses can never fire in this run
   (`Hexerei … on this face it is decoration`; `Oz … the clause never fires`).
   That is an honest bridge and a strange reward screen.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Kurage's Oath, played face-up.** Three damage to all enemies for
a card and an energy is below the floor even in a two-body fight; its only real
use is as Plan fodder, which means the printed card and the card I actually play
are two different things. Happiest to draw: **Vanguard**, once I understood it —
0 cost, and its Plan line hands the whole next turn Vulnerable, Weak, and (via
the Casket, twice, at Vulnerable-boosted 3 apiece) six free damage. **Riptide**
is the honest runner-up and won two fights outright.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

Yes, and a real one. Turn 1 of fight 1 I held Slack Water and Kurage's Oath, both
plannable, with three energy and a 39-HP body in front of me, and the choice was
whether to convert Kurage's Oath's 3 into 7 by giving up a turn of tempo. I
rejected Slack Water + Strike + Strike (16 now) for Slack Water + Strike + Plan
(12 now, 7 later). That is exactly the decision the kit is supposed to be about,
available on the opening turn, with everything I needed to make it printed on the
screen. The caveat is that it was available because the mulligan happened to
leave me both Plan cards; fight 2's opening turn had none and offered nothing.
