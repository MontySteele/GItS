# Kokomi — blind seat, lane 2, round 17, natural run

## Identity

- **Model / seat:** Claude Opus, blind TESTER seat (KLEEMOD-KOKOMI), lane 2.
- **Seed:** `FNT3MWHUPCJT`. **Ascension:** 3. **Character:** Kokomi (the screen
  never named her; I inferred the kit from the Bake-Kurage and Hydro).
- **Act:** 1. **Boss the map named:** Lagavulin Matriarch. **Not reached** — I
  stopped 8 floors short of it.
- **Actions accepted:** 120 of 120.
- **Termination:** action budget. The bridge refused nothing at the end; I
  spent the last four acts on a deliberate final turn and stopped mid-fight 5
  (Haunted Ship, round 3, my turn about to start). Wall clock was nowhere near
  5400 s.
- **HP trajectory:** 64/80 (start, A3) → 51 after fight 1 → 48 after fight 2 →
  63 (Meal Ticket, shop) → 80 (rest) → 63 through the elite → 78 (Meal Ticket,
  shop-as-Unknown) → 80 (Meal Ticket, second shop) → 77 (Shinobu's self-damage,
  fight 4) → **77/80 at the stop**.
- **Gold at the stop:** 40.
- **Potions:** Swift Potion (1 of 3 slots). Weak Potion was drawn as a reward
  and spent on the elite.
- **Relics:** Tamakushi Casket (start each combat with the Bake-Kurage; applying
  a debuff makes it hit for 2 Hydro), Large Capsule, Meal Ticket (heal 15 on
  entering a shop), Sturdy Clamp (up to 10 Block persists across turns), Tiny
  Mailbox (2 potions on Rest), Anchor (start each combat with 10 Block).
- **Deck at the stop (19):** Strike ×5, Defend ×5, Kurage's Oath, Slack Water,
  Flank, Ambush, Vanguard, Undertow, Riptide, Ripple, Gorou — Juuga: Forward
  Unto Victory, Shinobu — Sanctifying Ring. (Stack, the Trash Heap's random
  card, was removed at the first shop.)

**Neow pick:** Large Capsule — two relics is the largest immediate swing on
offer, and one Strike plus one Defend is a small price on a starting deck that
is mostly Strike and Defend anyway. It paid: Meal Ticket and Sturdy Clamp.
Fishing Rod was too slow for one act; Scroll Boxes adds cards to a deck I
already wanted to thin.

## Fight 1 — Toadpole 24 HP, Toadpole 22 HP

**Turn 1.** Hand: Kurage's Oath, Strike ×2, Defend ×2. I played Kurage's Oath
onto the Bake-Kurage as a Plan, then both Strikes into Toadpole (2).
**Rejected:** playing Oath now for "Deal 3 damage to ALL enemies" instead of
planning it for 7. The Plan line costs the same 1 energy and pays more than
twice as much one turn later, and this was clearly a multi-turn fight, so
paying now for later was the whole decision. Also rejected: a Defend instead of
the second Strike — the printed incoming was only 7.

**Turn 2.** The morning log read: *"Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 7 — the 7 is
damage. Toadpole (1) lost 7 HP / Toadpole (2) lost 7 HP."* Toadpole (2) was on
3 HP; Toadpole (1) had picked up **Thorns 2** off its Empower and was swinging
3×3. I Struck Toadpole (2) dead, Struck Toadpole (1), and Defended.
**Rejected:** three Strikes. Thorns charges 2 per attack, so the third Strike
would have cost me 2 to deal 6 into a 17 HP body while the printed 9 incoming
went unblocked. The thorns turned "hit it more" into a priced question, which
is the first real turn-level decision of the run.

**Turn 3.** Toadpole 11 HP, attacking for 7. I played Slack Water on it (4
damage, 1 Weak — the Weak set off Tamakushi Casket for 2 more, so 6 total),
planned Kurage's Oath, and Defended. **Rejected:** Slack Water's Plan line
("Apply 1 Weak to ALL enemies") — there was only one enemy left, so the
immediate half was strictly better, and the Weak plus 5 Block took the printed
7 down to nothing. The fight ended on the Kurage's carry-out; the tool printed
it as *"Toadpole lost 5 HP, and died"* on the reward screen, which was a good
touch since that beat never reaches a battle screen.

**Judgement:** the fight had two decisions and both were mine — plan-vs-now on
turn 1, and pay-thorns-or-block on turn 2. Neither was hard, but neither was
automatic.

**Card reward:** took **Gorou — Juuga: Forward Unto Victory**; nearly took
**Treatise**. Gorou is 18 damage over three turns for one energy, and — the
reason I actually wanted it — it was the only second element on offer, and
every reaction rule the screens keep printing is unreachable without one.

## Fight 2 — Corpse Slug 25 HP, Corpse Slug 26 HP (both Ravenous 4)

**Turn 1.** Hand: Strike ×2, Defend ×3 — no kit card at all. I Struck Corpse
Slug (1) twice and Defended. **Rejected:** nothing worth naming. This turn
presented no decision: with two Strikes, three Defends and one 6-damage intent,
the only question was which slug to point at, and *Ravenous 4* ("when an enemy
dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it, becoming Stunned and gaining 4
Strength") made that answerable without thought — focus one so the eat lands
once, later, on my terms.

**Turn 2.** Frail 2 had been applied; Defend's face had rewritten itself from 5
Block to 3, which I liked. Slug (1) at 13, Slug (2) at 26 swinging 8. I played
Kurage's Oath **now** (3 to ALL) and then both Strikes to kill Slug (1).
**Rejected:** planning Oath. Planning it would have left Slug (1) alive on 1 HP
— 13 minus 12 — and the whole turn was built to get the kill inside this turn,
because the eat *stuns* the survivor. It worked exactly as printed: Slug (2)
came up **Stunned, Strength 4**, and I took zero that round. That is the best
decision I made all act, and it was legible entirely off the enemy's own buff
text.

**Turn 3.** Slug at 23 with Strength 4, swinging 12. I played Slack Water (4 +
Weak, plus 2 from the Casket), Gorou, and a Defend. **Rejected:** a second
Defend over Gorou. Gorou's 18-over-3-turns beats 3 Block on a 23 HP body, and I
specifically wanted to see a Geo hit land on a Hydro aura.

**Turn 4.** Slug on 11. Two Strikes killed it.

**Where the screen and the outcome disagreed.** Two places in this fight.

1. **The reaction that wasn't announced.** Gorou's end-of-turn 6 Geo landed on
   an enemy printed as wearing *Hydro Aura 2*. Next screen: the aura was gone
   and the slug had lost exactly 6. By the rules block on that very screen a
   reaction *must* have happened — "the aura is CONSUMED to trigger the
   reaction". Nothing named it, nothing showed an effect, and the only evidence
   it occurred at all was an aura row that stopped being printed. From the
   seat, "my Geo hit ate my own Hydro aura for nothing" is what it looked like.
2. **Arithmetic I could not reconcile.** On turn 3 I had 3 printed Block
   against a printed 9-damage attack and lost 2 HP (50 → 48), not 6. I have no
   account of the missing 4.

**Card reward:** took **Flank** (8 for 1, and its Plan hits every attacker);
nearly took **Exposed Flank**. **Passed: Sayu — Yoohoo Art: Fuuin Dash**
(Anemo, Swirl) — the first reaction card I turned down for want of a second
element I could apply reliably. Swirl copies an aura onto everyone, which is
worth nothing when the only follow-up element in my deck is one Geo card.

## Fight 3 — Terror Eel, 140 HP (Elite). Shriek 70: stunned the first time its HP reaches 70

**Turn 1.** Hand: Strike, Defend, Ambush, Kurage's Oath, Shinobu — Sanctifying
Ring. I played Shinobu (lose 3 HP; 3 turns of 5 Electro to ALL and 5 Block),
then planned **both** Ambush (12) and Kurage's Oath (7) onto the jellyfish.
**Rejected:** Defend, and playing Ambush's immediate 5 instead of its Plan's
12. Against a 140 HP body with a printed 16 incoming, eating 11 to bank 19 for
next turn was plainly right, and I wanted Shinobu's Electro to land *before*
the Hydro carry-outs so the two elements would meet. **This is the draft
decision paying off,** not a turn decision: the fight was shaped at the shop,
where I bought Shinobu specifically to own a second element.

**Turn 2 — the reaction, and how badly the screen hid it.** The carry-out log
read:

> Bake-Kurage: Ambush, 12 — the 12 is damage. **Inside the same beat: Tamakushi
> Casket 2.** / Terror Eel lost 14 HP

and the eel was now wearing **Poison 4** and *Hydro Aura 1*. Nothing on any
screen said "Electro-Charged", or said where Poison 4 came from. Reading it
cold, this is the trap the rules block itself warns about: the Hydro carry-out
consumed the Electro aura, the reaction's own debuff set off the Casket, and
the Casket's Hydro hit re-applied a Hydro aura inside the same beat, so the
aura never *appeared* to go anywhere. The single word "Inside the same beat:
Tamakushi Casket 2" is the only trace, and I only decoded it because I had been
told to expect exactly this shape.

I then played Vanguard (free, 1 Vulnerable, and the debuff fired the Casket),
Slack Water, Flank, and Gorou. **Rejected:** planning Vanguard for
Vulnerable-plus-Weak next turn. Vulnerable *now* multiplied everything else in
the turn, and the Plan text says outright "Enemy Vulnerable counts" — so
front-loading it was the turn's real decision, and a good one. Rejected also:
Strike over Gorou, for the same reason as fight 2.

**A printed contradiction.** The glossary box says *Vulnerable — takes 50% more
damage from every hit it takes, **a Skill's damage too***. The power on the
enemy's own body says *Vulnerable 1 — Receive 50% more damage **from Attacks***.
The numbers say the glossary is right: the Casket's Skill-triggered 2 became 3
under Vulnerable. The body text is wrong and it is the text a player actually
reads.

**Turn 3.** 90 → 70, which is exactly the Shriek line. Hand: Strike, Defend ×3,
Ripple — no kit card again. I planned Ripple (0 cost; Plan: 1 Energy and 4
Block), Struck, and burned the last two energy on Defends. **Rejected:**
holding the Defends. With Sturdy Clamp keeping up to 10 Block across turns,
Block is not wasted on a debuff turn, so this was a small real choice rather
than a dead one. **A second unexplained number:** I began the turn on Block 9
having only ever been granted Shinobu's 5. My best guess is that Gorou's Geo
meeting the Hydro aura produced a Crystallize shield — which would make the
*second* reaction of the fight whose entire visible output was an unlabelled
number in my Block field.

**Turn 4.** The eel's "Strategic (Debuff)" turned out to be **Vulnerable 99 —
receive 50% more damage for 99 turns**, and its next intent was 24 (36 on me).
I used the Weak Potion, then Strike, Strike, Defend, Defend for 24 Block.
**Rejected:** saving the potion. 3 Weak for 3 turns against a 24-damage
attacker while I was permanently Vulnerable was the moment; and the potion's
Weak was itself a debuff, so the Casket hit for 2, that Hydro hit landed on the
Electro aura, and **Poison went from 2 to 6.** A potion doing a chunk of its
work through two relic-and-reaction bounces is the most interesting thing that
happened all act, and again nothing named it.

**Turns 5–6.** Slack Water plus two Strikes took it to 1; a Strike finished it.
Rejected on turn 5: Defends — the eel was one turn from dead and Block was
already 6.

**Judgement:** the best fight of the round by a distance. Real decisions at
three levels — the shop pick that gave me Electro, the turn-1 double-Plan, and
the turn-2 Vulnerable-first ordering — and the Shriek threshold gave the whole
middle of the fight a number to aim at. Cost: 17 HP on an elite at A3.

**Reward:** took **Undertow** (7, or 10 into a debuffed enemy — my deck applies
debuffs constantly); nearly took **Shinobu — Grass Ring of Sanctification**.

## Fight 4 — Sludge Spinner, 38 HP

**Turn 1.** Anchor gave 10 starting Block. Hand: Shinobu, Strike ×3, Defend. I
played Shinobu and two Strikes. **Rejected:** Defend — Anchor's 10 already
covered the printed 8, so the block card was the dead card, not the attack.

**Turn 2 — the first time the game explained a reaction to me.** Flank's face
now carried a line it had never carried before:

> *Reaction preview: Electro-Charged* — Hydro meets Electro: the reacted enemy
> loses 4 HP at the start of its turn, 1 less each turn.

So the previews **do** exist — they print on a card in hand when the enemy is
currently wearing a reacting aura. They did not print for the Kurage's
carry-outs or for the end-of-turn companion ticks, which is where three of my
four reactions actually happened. My attack faces had also rewritten themselves
downward for my own Weak (Ambush 5 → 3, Flank 8 → 6), which is genuinely good.

I played Vanguard (free Vulnerable; the Casket's answering Hydro ate the Electro
aura and applied the reaction), then Flank, then Ambush. **Rejected:** planning
Ambush for 12 instead of playing its 3. The enemy was on 21 with a 7-damage
Electro tick and 9 Block-adjusted damage already coming; killing it this turn
beat banking 12 for a turn that would not exist. Rejected also: Ripple's Plan —
no point buying energy for a turn after the fight ends.

The end-of-turn Electro finished it.

**One more naming inconsistency:** the preview calls it *Electro-Charged*; what
lands on the body is a debuff printed as **Poison**. Same effect, two names, and
only one of them appears on the enemy.

**Reward: skipped.** Feigned Retreat, Sea-Salt Prayer, Stolen Chapter, and
**Shinobu — Grass Ring of Sanctification** (companion — passed). At 19 cards
with ten of them Strike and Defend, another one-energy filler card was worth
less to me than the draws it would displace. Recording the skip as a judgement:
nothing in that pack was a power card.

## Fight 5 — Haunted Ship, 63 HP (partial; the action cap fell mid-fight)

**Turn 1.** Hand: Defend ×2, Strike ×2, Ripple — the third opening hand in five
fights with no kit card in it. I planned Ripple (0 cost, +1 Energy and 4 Block
next turn), Struck twice, Defended. **Rejected:** playing Ripple's 2 Block now.
A 0-cost card whose Plan line is energy-positive is free to bank, and this was
the only interesting thing in the hand.

**Turn 2.** The ship's Status-card intent had landed (my piles went from 20
cards to 25). I played Vanguard for the immediate Vulnerable, Riptide for its
now-half (printed face "Deal 6 damage to ALL enemies", already reduced from 9
by my Weak 3), and planned Ambush for 12. **Rejected:** planning Riptide for 13
to ALL — against one enemy on 51 HP with my budget about to run out, a bird in
the hand.

**Where the screen and the outcome disagreed, again.** The ship was on 51 at
the start of that turn and on 36 at the start of the next. The carry-out log
credits Ambush with 12 of that. That leaves **3 HP** for Vanguard's
Casket trigger (printed 2, and 3 under the Vulnerable Vanguard had just
applied) plus Riptide's printed 6. I expected somewhere between 11 and 15 and
got 15 total including the Ambush — meaning Riptide's six-damage face
apparently landed for something close to nothing. I had no actions left to
probe it, so I am reporting the numbers rather than a diagnosis: **51 → 36
across a turn whose printed faces totalled 20 or more.**

Stopped here at 120/120, on my turn, round 3, ship at 36/63.

## The kit, after 5 fights (4 completed)

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and where each was made.**

- **Now-or-Plan, on the turn.** This is the kit's engine and it is a good one.
  Every Plan card prints two faces at the same price, one worse and immediate,
  one better and delayed, and the fight state decides which. I chose "now" over
  "Plan" three times and "Plan" over "now" five times, and each time I could
  say why in one sentence: Oath planned on fight 1 turn 1 because the fight had
  turns left; Oath played immediately on fight 2 turn 2 because 3 damage now
  was the difference between killing a Corpse Slug this turn and next; Ambush
  planned against the elite because 12 beats 5 when you can afford the wait;
  Ambush played bare against the Sludge Spinner because there was no next turn.
  That is a real decision that keeps being re-asked with different answers.
- **Ordering inside the turn, on the turn.** Vanguard-first on elite turn 2 was
  worth about 8 damage over Vanguard-last, because Vulnerable multiplies
  everything after it including the Casket's Skill damage. The kit rewards
  sequencing and the screens give you enough to work it out.
- **The element you bought, at the draft.** The single largest decision of the
  run was buying Shinobu — Sanctifying Ring for 74 gold. Without a second
  element, every reaction rule the game prints is inert text; with one, the
  elite fight ran on a poison engine I never had to spend a card on. Gorou was
  the same call at the first card reward.
- **Kill-order against Ravenous, earlier in the fight.** Choosing to line up a
  kill *inside* one turn so the survivor's eat-and-stun landed when I wanted it
  was a decision made a turn ahead.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Strike and Defend.** Ten of my nineteen cards. Three of five opening hands
  were entirely Strike/Defend/Ripple and presented, honestly, nothing — I
  pointed the Strikes at the only sensible target and spent the leftovers on
  Block. The kit's decisions live in about eight cards, and the starting deck
  dilutes them past the point where a turn reliably has one.
- **Defend specifically** felt worse than it should, right up until I noticed
  Sturdy Clamp and Anchor were quietly making Block a persistent resource. That
  relic pair is doing a lot of work that the Defend card itself never hints at.
- **Ripple's non-Plan half** (Gain 2 Block) is never the reason you play it.
  That is fine — the card is a Plan card wearing a 0-cost coat.
- Nothing in the kit was a card I actively did not want. The *starter* cards
  were.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **Reactions are invisible where they matter.** Four reactions happened to
   me. Exactly one was announced, and only as a *preview on a card in my hand*.
   The three that fired from a Bake-Kurage carry-out, a relic trigger and an
   end-of-turn companion tick produced: an aura silently disappearing (Geo into
   Hydro, fight 2, no visible effect whatsoever); a **Poison 4** debuff
   appearing on an elite with no stated cause; and — I think — about 4 Block
   arriving in my own Block field from nowhere. The rules block *warns* that
   this is going to happen, in a paragraph in all-caps, which is an admission
   rather than a fix. A player who has not read that paragraph three times will
   conclude reactions do not work.
2. **Two names for one thing.** The preview says **Electro-Charged**; the
   debuff that lands on the enemy is called **Poison**. Nothing connects them.
3. **Vulnerable's own body text is wrong.** Glossary: "50% more damage from
   every hit it takes, a Skill's damage too." The power on the enemy: "Receive
   50% more damage from Attacks." The behaviour follows the glossary. The text
   a player is most likely to read is the wrong one.
4. **Three numeric outcomes I could not reconcile with the printed faces** — a
   9-damage attack against 3 Block costing me 2 HP; a Block total of 9 when 5
   was all I had been granted; and a turn of printed faces totalling 20+ that
   moved an enemy 15. Two of the three were in my favour, which is how I
   noticed them at all.
5. **"Hexerei"**, printed in a shop glossary on a Kokomi run: *"A Companion card
   that prints the word, and Klee herself. Some are Klee's own, some are not.
   Cards of hers pay when you play one."* I could not extract a rule from that
   sentence, and it names a character who is not in this run.
6. **The Trash Heap** gave me "a random forgotten card from the past" and never
   told me which card. I found out it was **Stack** two rooms later, at a
   removal screen.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- Never wanted: **Defend**, and the run's low point was a five-card hand of
  Strike ×2 and Defend ×3 against an elite.
- Happiest to draw: **Vanguard.** Zero cost, exhausts so it never clogs, and it
  does four things at once — applies Vulnerable that multiplies the rest of the
  turn, fires the Casket for free damage, and that Casket hit is a Hydro hit
  that sets off whatever reaction is waiting. It is the card that made turns
  feel like the kit rather than like Ironclad. **Shinobu — Sanctifying Ring** is
  the close second and the reason the elite was winnable.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a good one.** The opening hand held Kurage's Oath, whose face prints
"Deal 3 damage to ALL enemies" and "Plan: Deal 7 damage to ALL enemies" at the
same cost, next to a jellyfish the screen explains in three lines. Turn one,
card one, I had to decide whether the fight had a next turn in it. That is the
right first question for this kit to ask, and it asked it immediately.
