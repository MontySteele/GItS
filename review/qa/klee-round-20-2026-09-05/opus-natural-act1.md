## Fight 1 — Nibbit, 43 HP

Opened at Spark 1 with no Bomb having gone off. Nothing on the combat screen
explained why I had a Spark; I did not learn the reason ("Start each combat
with 1") until a card reward two fights later expanded the keyword.

- **Turn 1** — Strike, Strike, Defend. *Rejected:* double Defend for 10 Block
  (taking 2 instead of 7). Rejected because 5 HP is cheap at 62/62 and cutting
  a 43-HP body down a turn faster removes a whole 12-damage swing. Ka-pow! held
  under Retain rather than spent bare for 4.
- **Turn 2** — Jumpy Dumpty (Bomb 8), Ka-pow! (Set off), Strike, Defend.
  *Rejected:* letting the Bomb grow to 12 and setting off next turn. Rejected on
  printed text: Nibbit's intent showed "Defensive (Defend)" alongside its attack,
  and Set off says **"Block stops them"** — a grown Bomb would have been eaten by
  the shield. This is the first turn of the run that felt like Klee.
- **Turn 3** — Strike, Strike, Jumpy Dumpty (second Bomb 8). *Rejected:* Defend.
  Rejected because the intent read "Empower (Buff)" — no incoming damage, so
  Block was dead value and planting was strictly better.
- **Turn 4** — Ka-pow! for lethal: Bomb 12 + 4 into a 3-HP body.

**Where screen and outcome agreed/disagreed.** Every number landed as printed
(12 damage on turn 2 = Bomb 8 + Ka-pow! 4). The one thing I misread was the
Bomb status line: after Jumpy Dumpty's bomb went off it printed
*"Mine 3 (buff) — … **Bombs here: 3, including 1 Mine**"*, and I read "Bombs
here: 3" as *three bombs*. It is the total bomb **value**. I only worked that
out on the next fight when the same field printed "Bombs here: 9 / 12" against
"Bomb 21". Recording it because I was genuinely confused for a fight and a half
by a line I would call ambiguous, not wrong.

**Judging the fight, not the turns:** turn 4's lethal was obvious, but it was
the turn-3 plant paying off, not a dead turn. The fight's real decision was
turn 2 — detonate small now, or grow into a shield — and it was decided by
reading the enemy's intent against the Set off keyword.

## Fight 2 — Shrinker Beetle, 38 HP

The Beetle applied **Shrink −1**: "While Shrinker Beetle is alive, your Attacks
deal 30% less damage." Strike printed as 4 and Ka-pow! as 2 in hand — the hand
re-prints the reduced numbers, which was excellent and meant I never had to do
the arithmetic myself.

- **Turn 1** — Pop! (Bomb 5), Jumpy Dumpty (Bomb 8), Strike, Strike.
  *Rejected:* Toric Toughness / Defend. Rejected because the intent read
  "Strategic (DebuffStrong)" — no damage incoming, so blocking was waste.
- **Turn 2** — Ka-pow!, then Strike for the kill. *Rejected:* holding a turn to
  grow 21 → 29. Rejected because 23 damage already left the body at 3 and a
  fourth turn only invites the debuff to keep stacking.

**The measurement this fight was worth.** Bomb keyword says *"Not an Attack:
only Vulnerable and a cap move it."* Under Shrink −1 the body went **26 → 3 =
23 damage**, which is Bomb 21 landing **in full** plus Ka-pow!'s own hit reduced
4 → 2. The printed rule is exactly true, and the split between "the Bomb" and
"the card that sets it off" is legible in the numbers. Spark went 1 → 3, matching
"in 2 hits for 2 Sparks". This was the most convincing single moment in the run:
a non-obvious rule, printed plainly, and true to the digit.

Took **zero damage** this fight.

| 2 | 3 | yes — Bombs 9 and 12 (+2) |

## Fight 3 — Fuzzy Wurm Crawler, 55 HP

- **Turn 1** — Strike, Strike, Defend; **Ka-pow! retained**. *Rejected:* playing
  Ka-pow! bare for 4 damage. Rejected because it is my only Set off and Retain
  exists precisely to hold it until Bombs are down. Learned here that Retain
  stacks on top of the normal draw — I had a 6-card hand next turn.
- **Turn 2** — Pop!, Jumpy Dumpty, Strike, Defend. *Rejected:* setting off the
  13 immediately. Rejected on arithmetic the screen gives you: two Bombs grow
  +8 a turn, which beats a 6-damage Strike, and the intent was "Empower" so
  there was no punishment for waiting. The enemy came out of that Empower with
  **Strength 7**, so the wait was not free — but the Bombs outgrew it.
- **Turn 3** — **Barbara — Front Row Seat**, then Ka-pow!, then Strike, Defend.
  *Rejected:* Ka-pow! before Barbara. Rejected because Set off says *"the first
  takes the aura"*, so the ordering is the whole combo — Hydro first, then the
  oldest Bomb Vaporizes.

**The combo, measured.** Barbara printed 5 Block, Front Row Seat 3, a Companion
Spark (1 → 2) and Hydro Aura 2. Then Ka-pow! took the body **37 → 8 = 29
damage**, which is exactly (9 × 1.5 = 13) + 12 + 4. Block went 5 → 11 from the
two detonations. Sparks 2 → 4. **I predicted all four of those numbers off the
printed keywords before playing the card, and all four were right.** That is the
strongest thing I can say about this kit's legibility.

**One place the screen under-reports itself:** the Bomb line still advertised
"Set off here deals 21 Pyro damage" while a Hydro aura was up and the true
figure was 25 (13 + 12). The preview does not fold in a pending Elemental
Reaction. I could compute it, but only because I had read the Vaporize keyword.

Enemy turn: the Mine 3 finished the 2-HP body, "which lands in full unless the
Mine kills" — it killed, so the 11-damage hit never landed. Zero damage again.

## Fight 4 — Twig Slime (M) 27 [A], Leaf Slime (M) 35 [B], Twig Slime (S) 7 [C], Leaf Slime (S) 11 [D]

80 HP across four bodies, mostly "give you a Status card" intents. This was the
hardest fight to read and the one that cost me 23 HP.

- **Turn 1** — Pop! (Bomb 5 on B), Jumpy Dumpty (Bomb 8 on B), Strike on C,
  Defend. *Rejected:* spreading Bombs across bodies. Rejected because a Bomb
  damages only its carrier, so stacking on the biggest body (35) wastes none —
  and "Kills move it on" means a stack is not lost if the carrier dies anyway.
- **Turn 2** — Ka-pow! on B (Bomb 21 + 4 = 25), Powder Charge on A, Strike,
  Strike on B for the kill, Defend. *Rejected:* growing another turn. Rejected
  because all four bodies flipped to Attack intents totalling 26 and I was the
  one on a clock.
- **Turn 3** — Slimed, Slimed (both "Draw 1 card. Exhaust."), then Barbara on A.
  *Rejected:* Strike on D for a guaranteed 6. Rejected in favour of putting a
  Hydro aura on A to set up a Vaporize on its growing Bomb. **This was a
  mistake** — see below.
- **Turn 4** — Powder Charge (0 energy) on A, Jumpy Dumpty on A, Slimed, Strike
  on D. *Rejected:* Toric Toughness. **I then hit a refusal** (below).
- **Turn 5** — Ka-pow! on A: **Bomb 40** (18/10/12) into a 21-HP body.

**Two rules confirmed the hard way, both in my favour.**
1. A took 6 damage on turn 2's enemy phase, not the 3 I expected from one Mine.
   The extra 3 is B's Mine: B died carrying it and **"Kills move it on"** moved
   it to a survivor. I worked the mechanism out from the arithmetic afterwards,
   and it is a genuinely good rule.
2. On turn 5, Bomb 40 went off as 18 + 10 killing A, and the **third bomb (12)
   transferred to D as a live charge** rather than being lost. Sparks went 7 → 9
   (two detonations, not three), which is consistent — the transferred bomb
   never went off.

**Where the plan was wrong, plainly.** I put Hydro on A on turn 3 to set up a
Vaporize, and the aura **expired unused** because Ka-pow! is one card in a
twelve-card deck and did not arrive until turn 5. The aura runs on a 2-turn
timer; my Set off runs on a shuffle. Those two clocks do not line up, and I lost
the whole 1.5× to it. That is the sharpest design tension I met all run, and it
is what made me draft Fwoosh! and Pocket Match later.

**Refusal (1, and it was mine).** I asked `play "Strike" on "D"` and got:
*"'Strike' cannot be played right now: you do not have enough energy."* I had
miscounted — Powder Charge costs 0 **energy** (1 Spark), but Jumpy Dumpty,
Slimed and the first Strike had already spent all 3. The refusal named the exact
reason and listed the working forms; no complaint about the tool, the error was
mine. D survived at 2 HP as a result.

**A false alarm I want on the record.** Mid-fight I believed Jumpy Dumpty's
"place a Mine 3 on **ALL** enemies" had put Mines on only 3 of the 4 bodies, and
I nearly wrote it up as a defect. It was **my own shell filter** (an `awk` I was
using to strip a duplicated block) dropping alternate lines. A clean re-read
showed the Mine on all four. The card is correct; I was wrong, and I would have
filed a bug on my own tooling.

**A rendering defect that is real.** On several combat screens the entire
"## The other side" block printed **twice** — heading, every enemy, every intent
and status line, and the trailing italic note, all duplicated for a single set
of bodies. It is **intermittent**: it happened on fight 3 turn 3 and fight 4
turn 1, and did not happen on the very next observe of the same fight. It never
changed what I could do, but it is what sent me chasing the false Mine alarm.

**Deck pollution as the real clock.** These bodies gave me 5+ Status cards. This
version of Slimed is "cost 1, Draw 1 card. Exhaust." — a cycler, not a brick, so
it was mild, but it did eat energy I wanted for Bombs.

## Fight 5 — Snapping Jaxfruit 33 [A], Flyconid 47 [B]

80 HP, and I came in at 31/62. A scaled Strength +2 every turn off a repeating
Empower; B hit for 11.

- **Turn 1** — Pop! (free Bomb on B), Toric Toughness, Defend. *Rejected:*
  Barbara for the Hydro. Rejected because at 31 HP against 14 incoming I wanted
  the better block rate (Toric is 15 Block over 3 turns for 2 energy) before I
  wanted a combo. Took 4.
- **Turn 2 — the best decision the kit gave me all run.** I held **1 Spark and
  two Spark-priced cards** (Powder Charge and Fwoosh!) and could seemingly play
  only one. I played **Fwoosh!** first and watched the Spark counter: spent 1,
  refunded 1 by Pounding Surprise off the detonation, **net zero** — so I could
  then play Powder Charge as well. B took exactly 15 (Bomb 9 + 6). *Rejected:*
  Powder Charge first, which would have been the same cards in the wrong order
  and left me unable to verify the refund. Then Strike, Defend, and I **retained
  Ka-pow!** rather than popping the fresh Bomb 6 for 10 — holding converts it to
  a Bomb 10 next turn at zero cost, which is strictly better while I survive.
- **Turn 3** — Ka-pow! (Bomb 10 + 4), Strike, Strike: **exactly 26 into a 26-HP
  body**, killing B. *Rejected:* Toric + Defends to survive the 18 incoming.
  Rejected because killing B removed 11 of that 18 permanently, which beats
  blocking it once. Then Jumpy Dumpty with the last energy, eating 2 damage on
  purpose to start the engine on A.
- **Turn 4** — Pop!, Barbara (Hydro on A), Defend, Defend. Under **Frail 2** the
  three Block cards gave 3 each = 9 against a 9-damage hit: **took 0**.
  *Rejected:* attacking at all. Rejected because A was at full 33 with rising
  Strength and I was at 22; the Bombs were doing the work in the background.
- **Turn 5 — the payoff.** A carried **Bomb 25 (16/9)** under a Hydro Aura and I
  held both Spark cards with exactly 2 Sparks, **neither costing energy**. I
  played Powder Charge first as insurance — with Vaporize the kill was 39, but
  *without* it 16+9+6 = 31 against 33 HP would have been **two short**. Then
  Fwoosh! for the kill. *Rejected:* Fwoosh! alone, which was the line I nearly
  took and which would have failed by 2 if I had misread the aura.

This fight is the one I would point at to say the kit works: the Spark economy,
the Retain-and-grow tempo call, an exact-lethal arithmetic kill, a Frail-adjusted
block sum, and a two-card Spark finish, in five turns.

## The kit, after 5 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

- **Detonate now vs. let it grow** — the spine of the kit, and it was live in
  every fight. Bombs grow +4 each per turn, so waiting is compound interest;
  what prices the wait is the enemy's intent. *On the turn*, and decided by
  reading printed text: fight 1 turn 2 I detonated early **because** the Beetle
  telegraphed Block and Set off says "Block stops them"; fight 3 turn 2 I waited
  **because** the intent was Empower and nothing could punish me. Same question,
  opposite answers, both legible off the screen. This is the kit's best idea.
- **1 Spark, two Spark-priced cards** (fight 5 turn 2). *On the turn.* Traded
  off Bomb placement against detonation — and resolved into a genuine discovery,
  that Pounding Surprise makes a Set off Spark-neutral so the "choice" was
  actually a sequencing puzzle. Best single moment of the run.
- **Ordering Barbara before the Set off** (fight 3 turn 3). *On the turn.*
  "The first takes the aura" means the sequence is the whole combo; playing the
  same two cards backwards loses 1.5× on the biggest Bomb.
- **Retain Ka-pow! or spend it bare** (fights 1 and 3, turn 1 both times).
  *On the turn.* Traded 4 immediate damage against having the only Set off in
  hand when the Bombs are worth setting off.
- **Which body to stack, and which to kill** (fight 4). *Earlier in the fight.*
  "Kills move it on" means a stack is never wasted, which changes where you
  aim — I stacked the 35-HP body and let the transfer clean up.
- **The draft itself.** Powder Charge and Fwoosh! did not add a card so much as
  turn a dead counter into a resource; every turn of fight 5 was different in
  kind from every turn of fight 1 because of two draft picks. *At the draft.*

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Defend.** 5 Block for 1 energy, played almost every turn, and never once a
  decision — it was what I did with leftover energy. It competes directly with
  the Bomb engine for the same energy and always loses on interest, so playing
  it felt like admitting the turn had nothing in it.
- **Strike.** 6 damage of filler. It only ever mattered as arithmetic — twice it
  was the exact difference between lethal and not (26 into B, 12 into D) — but I
  never *chose* to play a Strike, I played it because the energy was there.
- **Pop!** is auto-include rather than a decision: at 0 cost you always play it,
  there is no turn where you would not.
- **Ka-pow! when a big Bomb is up** is obvious, but per the brief that is a plan
  paying off, not a dead turn — the decision was made two turns earlier when I
  chose to plant rather than block.
- **Toric Toughness** got played twice in five fights and both times it was a
  concession, not a plan.

The honest shape of it: the Klee cards nearly all presented decisions, and the
**starter Strikes and Defends presented none**. Roughly half my plays this run
were on cards with no Klee content in them.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that seemed to contradict its own text.**

- **"Bombs here: 3, including 1 Mine"** reads as a count and is a **value**. I
  misread it for a fight and a half until "Bomb 21 … Bombs here: 9 / 12"
  disambiguated it. When there is one Bomb the field is genuinely ambiguous.
- **The Bomb preview does not price a pending reaction.** With a Hydro aura up
  and Bomb 25 on the body it still printed "Set off here deals 25 Pyro damage";
  the real number was 39. I had to compute the Vaporize myself, and in fight 5 the
  difference between those two numbers was the difference between lethal and
  dying two short.
- **Where Spark comes from is not on the combat screen.** I opened fight 1 with
  "Spark 1" and no explanation; "Start each combat with 1" appears only inside a
  *card's* keyword expansion, which I first saw at the fight-1 reward.
- **The Spark line disappears at 0** instead of printing 0, so "do I have a
  Spark" is answered by a line's absence.
- **Toric Toughness's cost was never printed on the event that offered it.** The
  Wood Carvings screen showed the text but not the 2, and I picked it believing
  it was a 1-cost upgrade of a Defend.
- **The duplicated "## The other side" block** (intermittent, described in
  fight 4). Real, harmless to play, but it is what led me to chase a false defect.
- **The long Elemental Reaction paragraph** about a reaction being hidden when a
  relic re-applies the aura "inside the same beat" — I read it three times and
  never established whether it could apply to me. It is the one piece of text on
  these screens I could not turn into a decision.

Nothing **contradicted** its own printed text. Every number I checked — and I
checked the ones that mattered — came out exactly as the keywords predicted,
including the two non-obvious ones (Shrink not touching Bomb damage; the oldest
Bomb taking the aura for 1.5×). That is a strong result.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted: Defend.** Not because it is weak but because drawing it told me
  the turn was going to be filler. (The literal worst card I held was Slimed, but
  that is the slimes' card, not Klee's — and at "Draw 1, Exhaust" it was kinder
  than I expected.)
- **Happiest to draw: Fwoosh!** It costs no energy, so it never competed with
  anything; it is Spark-neutral, so it never really cost the Spark either; and
  it turned a counter I had been ignoring for three fights into the thing that
  killed the last two bodies of the run. **Ka-pow! landing on a turn when a Bomb
  had grown fat** is the close second and the more spectacular feeling — Bomb 40
  off a 0-cost card is the single best moment the kit gave me.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Not really — and I want to be exact about it.** My opening hand was Strike,
Strike, Ka-pow!, Defend, Defend against a 43-HP body telegraphing 12. There was
no Bomb placer in it, so the kit's entire mechanic was unavailable on turn 1.
What I actually decided was (i) 12 damage versus 10 Block, which is a generic
deckbuilder tempo question with no Klee content, and (ii) whether to spend
Ka-pow! bare for 4 or hold it under Retain. The second one is a *small* Klee
decision, and it is real — but it is a decision about not using the mechanic yet.

The first turn that presented a Klee decision was **fight 1, turn 2**, when
Jumpy Dumpty arrived and the Beetle telegraphed Block, and it was a good one.
So: the kit takes one turn to start. Whether that is a problem depends on
whether a starting hand is guaranteed a placer — with two placers in a nine-card
deck, mine was not.

**Overall, blind:** the Bomb/Set off/Spark loop is legible from the printed text
alone — I predicted its numbers correctly before playing cards, repeatedly,
including the reaction multiplier — and the decisions it produces are real and
recur with different answers. The weaknesses I felt were (1) the starter Strikes
and Defends contribute nothing and occupy about half the deck early, (2) with one
Set off card the aura timer and the shuffle are on clocks that do not line up,
and (3) Sparks were an inert counter for three fights until the pool happened to
offer a sink. Points 2 and 3 both *fixed themselves through the draft*, which I
would call the pool doing its job rather than a defect.

## Non-blindness declaration

**Repo files read: none.**

`blindplay act`), all with `GITS_LANE=2`. I never used lane 1. I did not run
`harness state`, `scenario`, `staged_turn`, `soak`, or any other understudy
subcommand, and I did not launch, deploy, or tear down anything.

  ended up not writing any scratch notes into it; it is empty.
- `mkdir -p review/qa/klee-round-20-2026-09-05` — created the record directory.
- `echo ok` — appended to the two `mkdir`s to confirm they succeeded.
- Shell filters applied **only to the stdout of `blindplay observe`**, to re-read

  enemies" reading described in fight 4; I caught it with a clean re-read and
  have recorded both the error and the correction above.
- `cd` into the working directory as part of each command.

No file in the repository was opened, and no card sheet, C# source, doc, packet,
review material, or other seat's record was read at any point.
