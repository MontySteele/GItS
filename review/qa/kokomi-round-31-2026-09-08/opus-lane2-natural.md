## Fight 1 — Fuzzy Wurm Crawler, 57 HP

Opening hand: Strike (1), Strike (2), Kurage's Oath, Defend (1), Defend (2).

**Turn 1** (enemy intends Attack 4). Wrote all three attacks as Plans on the
Bake-Kurage: Strike, Strike, Kurage's Oath. **Rejected:** playing them for
6/6/3 = 15 now. The printed faces make the trade explicit — Strike is "Deal 6
damage. Plan: Deal 8 damage", Oath is "Deal 3 damage to ALL enemies. Plan: Deal
7". Planning converts 15 into 23 at the cost of one turn's delay, and the enemy
was telegraphing 4, so the delay cost 4 HP. That is the whole decision and it is
a real one.

**Turn 2** (enemy at 34, intends Empower — no damage coming). Played **Slack
Water** now (4 damage + 1 Weak, and the relic's answering 2, so 6), then wrote
both Strikes as Plans. **Rejected:** writing Slack Water's Dusk Plan instead. Its
Dusk line only spreads Weak to ALL, and there was one enemy, so the played half
was strictly bigger. Also rejected planning War Council's 7 over Slack Water's 6
— I wanted the Weak *on the board before* the Empower resolved.

**Turn 3** (enemy at 12, Strength 7, intends Attack 11). Played Defend, Defend,
Strike. **Rejected:** Strike + Oath + Defend, which would have left it on 3 and
me taking 6. Two Defends and one Strike left it on 6 and me taking 1. With no
lethal available, the cheapest line was the one that ate the fewest HP; that
comparison was the decision.

**Turn 4** (enemy at 6). Played Strike. **No alternative rejected** — 6 damage
into 6 HP. This was the turn-3 plan paying out, not a decision.

Judged across the fight: three decisions (the plan/play conversion, the Weak
timing, the block-versus-damage split), one payoff turn.

## Fight 2 — Shrinker Beetle, 38 HP

Opening hand: Slack Water, Kurage's Oath, Defend, Strike (1), Strike (2).

**Turn 1** (intends "Strategic (DebuffStrong)"). Wrote Strike, Strike, Kurage's
Oath as Plans. **Rejected:** playing them. The Bake-Kurage panel prints "Every
planned HIT is the jellyfish's, so Shrink or Weak on you never cuts it, before
or after you write it" — so writing before an incoming damage debuff dodges it
entirely. That is the best-read line of text in the kit: it made a debuff
telegraph into a *reason to plan*, and it was right.

**Turn 2.** The debuff landed as "Shrink -1 … you deal 30% less damage", and my
23 planned damage arrived at full value — 8, 8, 7, exactly as written. Meanwhile
**Flank's printed face changed from "Deal 8 damage" to "Deal 5 damage"** in my
hand: the card sheet folds Shrink into the number it shows me. That is very good
legibility and it made the next decision easy. Wrote **Flank** and **War
Council** as Plans (8 + 5, and the relic's 2 on the Weak = 15, lethal into 15
HP), played Defend. **Rejected:** playing Flank for its Shrunk 5 and War Council
for 2 — 7 now versus 15 next turn, into an enemy that could not kill me first.

**Turn 3** never happened: the carry-out killed it, and the bridge printed the
kill on the reward screen under "The Bake-Kurage's last carry-out", which is a
nice piece of plumbing — a plan that ends a fight would otherwise be invisible.

Across the fight: the whole thing was one decision, made on turn 1, off one line
of rules text. It paid.

## Fight 3 — Nibbit, 44 HP

Opening hand: Defend, Defend, Strike, Defend, Second Wave.

**Turn 1** (intends Attack 12). Wrote **Second Wave** then **Strike** as Plans,
played Defend. Second Wave's Plan reads "The next Plan carried out with this one
is carried out twice", so Strike's 8 becomes 16. **Rejected:** Strike now (6)
plus two Defends, which takes 2 damage instead of 7. I paid 5 HP for +10 damage.
The carry-out log confirmed it exactly: `Strike, 8` twice.

**Turn 2** (enemy at 28, intent now "Attack 6 … **and also** Defensive (Defend)").
Played Strike, Strike, War Council **now** — nothing planned. **Rejected:**
planning them, and this is the second good rules line: "A Plan is carried out
before you play anything, so it lands in whatever Block the enemy is still
standing in from its own turn — you cannot strip that Block first." A Defend
telegraph is therefore a *reason not to plan*. That is a genuine turn-level
decision and it is legible from the screen.

**Turn 3** (enemy at 14 behind 5 Block, all Slippery gone). Played **Shinobu —
Sanctifying Ring** (lose 3 HP; for 3 turns, end of turn, 5 Electro to ALL and 5
Block), Flank, Strike. **Rejected:** three attacks for 17 into 5 Block. I took
Shinobu because it was the only Electro I had and the glossary kept insisting no
reaction was reachable — I wanted to see one. The end-of-turn Electro finished
the fight.

## Fight 4 — Twig Slime (M) 28, Leaf Slime (M) 34, Leaf Slime (S) 12, Twig Slime (S) 8

Opening hand: Defend (1), Sango Isshin, Strike, Defend (2), Defend (3).

**Turn 1** (three status-card intents, one Attack 4). Wrote Strike as a Plan,
played two Defends. **Rejected:** playing **Sango Isshin** for its floor value.
Its face reads "Deal 8 damage. If the Bake-Kurage carried out a Plan this turn,
deal a quarter of your Max HP to ALL enemies instead" — on turn 1 no Plan has
carried out, so it was 8 to one body instead of 20 to four. Deliberately holding
it and writing a Plan to *arm* it next turn is the single best decision this kit
offered me all run, and it is entirely readable off two card faces.

**Turn 2.** Sango did not come back to hand, and this is where the run nearly
ended. Hand was Kurage's Oath, Strike, Flank, Slack Water, Treatise — **no block
at all** — against 11 + 8 + 3 + 4 = 26 incoming. Played **Flank** to kill the
8-HP Twig Slime, **Kurage's Oath** for 3 to all, and wrote **Slack Water's Dusk
Plan** ("Dusk Plan: Apply 1 Weak to ALL enemies"), which resolves at the end of
*this* turn, before the enemies act: four Weaks, four relic pings, and ~25% off
every incoming hit. **Rejected:** killing the 12-HP Leaf Slime instead of the
8-HP Twig (it hits for 3, the Twig for 4), and rejected holding Slack Water to
play it at one body for 6. The Dusk half exists precisely for this turn and it
was clearly the right half; it still cost me 16 HP, 43 → 27. The finding here is
not the kit's decision quality — it is that a hand with three Strikes and no
Defend has no defensive line at all except Weak.

**Turn 3** (all three survivors on status-card intents; free turn). Played
Shinobu, wrote War Council and Strike as Plans. **Rejected:** playing War
Council now for 4 total relic damage instead of writing it for 5-to-all + Weak +
relic. With nothing incoming, delay was free, so the bigger halves win
automatically — which is the shape of most "free" turns in this kit.

**Turn 4** (one Leaf Slime on 3 HP, carrying Poison 3 that would kill it at its
own turn start). Played Strike. **No alternative** — a dead turn, and it was
dead because the *previous* turn had already won the fight.

Where the screen and the outcome disagreed, first instance: the Leaf Slime was
wearing **Poison 3** that I had never applied, on a screen whose glossary said
"NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE" and whose "What reacted this turn" block said
"Nothing reacted this turn." Shinobu's Electro had met a Hydro aura at the end
of my previous turn. I could not tell, from the screen in front of me, where the
Poison came from.

## Fight 5 — Inklet ×3 (15, 14, 16), each with Slippery 1

Opening hand: Slack Water, Strike, Defend, Treatise, Defend.

**Turn 1.** "Slippery 1 — The next time Inklet loses HP, it only loses 1 HP
instead." Played **Treatise**, wrote **Slack Water's Dusk Plan**, wrote Strike
as a Plan. **Rejected:** Defend, and rejected playing Slack Water at one body.
The reasoning is a chain of three printed facts: Slippery eats one *instance* of
HP loss regardless of size; the relic deals 2 damage "whenever you apply a debuff
to an enemy"; the Dusk half applies Weak to ALL. So one 1-cost card, written on
the jellyfish, stripped all three Slipperies and Weakened all three bodies at the
end of my own turn. The carry-out proved it — each Inklet lost exactly 1 HP. That
is the most satisfying thing I did in the run and it came entirely off card text.

**Turn 2** (a Plan had carried out this turn). Played **Sango Isshin** alone:
20 damage to ALL. All three Inklets died at once. **No alternative rejected on
the turn** — but the decision that won it was made on turn 1, by writing a Plan
purely to arm a card that was still in the draw pile. I count this as a fight
with one decision, made a turn early, and it is the good kind.

## Fight 6 — Snapping Jaxfruit 32 + Slithering Strangler 54

Opening hand: Song of Pearls, Strike, Treatise, Sango Isshin, Defend.

**Turn 1** (3 damage + an Empower; a Debuff from the other). Played **Song of
Pearls** and **Treatise** (both "Once per turn, when the Bake-Kurage carries out
a Plan…"), wrote Strike as a Plan. **Rejected:** Sango for 8. Same arming logic
as fight 4, plus both powers are dead cards unless I plan every turn, so the plan
was mandatory to make them live. The debuff that landed was "Constrict 3 — While
the Slithering Strangler is alive, at the end of your turn, take 3 damage",
which put a clock on the fight.

**Turn 2** (17 incoming + 3 Constrict). Wrote **Second Wave**, then **War
Council** as the doubled Plan, played Defend. **Rejected:** doubling **Strike**
instead. This was the sharpest arithmetic decision of the run: doubled Strike is
16 into one body; doubled War Council is (5 damage + 1 Weak + the relic's 2) ×2
× two bodies = 28 spread, plus 2 Weak on each. The log next turn read exactly
that — two `War Council, 5` beats, each with two `Tamakushi Casket 2` pings,
7 HP off each body twice. **Rejected also:** War Council played now for its 4,
which would have blunted the incoming 17 but thrown away the doubling.

**Turn 3.** Played Kurage's Oath (3 to both), **Flank** to finish the Jaxfruit
at 10, and **Shinobu**. **Rejected:** two Defends. With the Jaxfruit dead the
incoming halved, so killing was cheaper than blocking — the usual comparison,
but here the 10-HP body and the 8-damage card lined up exactly, which is the
draft paying off rather than the turn being clever.

**Turn 4** (Strangler at 26, telegraphing "Attack 5 **and also** Defensive
(Defend)", Constrict now 6). Wrote **Ripple** as a Plan (cost 0 — "Plan: Gain 1
Energy and 4 Block") and played three Strikes for 18. **Rejected:** planning the
Strikes, for the Block reason again; **rejected:** playing Ripple for its 2
Block, because written it is strictly better *and* it counts as a carry-out, so
it turns on Song of Pearls and Treatise for free. A 0-cost card whose Plan half
refunds the energy is the cleanest engine piece in the deck.

The kill landed on the carry-out; the reward screen printed it.

## Fight 7 (elite) — Phrog Parasite 62, "Infested 4 — Upon dying, summons… something"

Opening hand: Shinobu, Flank, Strike, Defend, Second Wave.

**Turn 1** (intends 3 status cards). Wrote **Second Wave** + **Strike** (doubled
to 16), played Shinobu. **Rejected: writing Flank.** Flank's Plan reads "Deal 8
damage to each enemy that intended to attack **when you wrote this**" — the
Parasite was telegraphing status cards, not an attack, so its planned half would
have hit nothing. Catching that off the card face, against a single enemy where
the played half is a flat 8, is a real decision and a good piece of design: the
same card is worth 16 or 0 depending on a telegraph.

**Turn 2** (Parasite at 39, Poison 4 on it, "Attack 4 × 4"). Played **Sango
Isshin** — a Plan had carried out, so 21 to all — and **War Council** for the
Weak. **Rejected:** Defend for 5 Block. Weak on a 4×4 attack is worth about the
same block and also deals 2 and lasts, so the block card was the weaker half.

**Turn 3** (Parasite at 1 HP, Poison 7 on it, Shinobu's ring still live). Wrote
**Ripple**, **Strike**, **Strike** as Plans and played Defend — **spending no
energy on the kill at all**, because the ring's end-of-turn 5 Electro would kill
it before its turn, and the Plans would then land on whatever it summoned.
**Rejected:** killing it with a Strike and planning less. This is the turn I am
proudest of and it is only legible because the ring's timing ("At the end of your
turn") and the Plan's timing ("carried out before you play anything") are both
printed.

**Turn 4** — four **Wrigglers** (18/19/20/21). Wrote **Second Wave** + **Kurage's
Oath** (doubled: 7 to ALL, twice), played Strike to finish the 2-HP one, played
Treatise. **Rejected:** doubling Strike (16 into one body versus 14 into each of
three). The log paid it out exactly: two `Kurage's Oath, 7` beats, three bodies
each time.

**Turn 5.** Killed one Wriggler with a Strike, chipped the second, then played
**War Council** so the relic's 2-damage pings would finish it — 1 HP left after
a Strike, killed by a relic ping the Weak triggered. **Rejected:** Defend. That
line left exactly one enemy alive, on a non-attacking intent, so I took zero
damage. Using a debuff card as *lethal* because of the relic is a genuine second
use for a card I had been treating as a soft-support piece.

**Turn 6.** One Wriggler at 4. Played Flank. **No alternative** — dead turn.

Elite cleared at 35/85 without ever being seriously threatened after turn 1.

**Turn 2.** Played Slack Water, Strike, Strike, Flank: four cards, **five**

**Turn 3** — the fight's real turn. Vantom telegraphed **"Attack 28" plus 3

stack), **Defend** at 10, and **Shinobu** for another 5 Block at end of turn.
Total 20 Block. **Rejected:** attacking at all this turn, and rejected the Weak
Potion (Weak is a flat 25%, so a second source adds nothing). Took **3 damage**

lining up, and the screens gave me every one of them.

**Turn 4** (Empower, nothing incoming). Played **Song of Pearls**, wrote **Tide
Wall** and **Strike** as Plans. Tide Wall reads "Plan: Gain 3 Block for each Plan

**Turn 5** (9 incoming, 9 Block already banked from the carry-outs). Wrote
Strike, Strike, **Flank** as Plans — Flank's plan half was live this time because

**Turn 6.** A Plan had carried out, so **Sango Isshin** for 21, plus War Council
for the Weak, plus a free written Ripple. Poison from the Electro reactions was

**Turn 7** (28 incoming again, me at 42). Played Slack Water for the Weak, Defend,
Second Wave *as a played block card* rather than as a doubler, and one Strike.
**Rejected:** the greedy line of doubling War Council for ~14 more damage and
taking 9 instead of 5. At 42 HP against a 28 telegraph I took the survival line.
This was a real, uncomfortable choice, and it is the one place the kit made me
choose between its engine and my HP bar.

**Turn 8.** Free turn (Empower). Wrote Strike as a Plan and played Flank + Oath.
**Rejected:** planning Flank — it was telegraphing Empower, so the plan half was
worth nothing. Third time that read mattered.

**Turn 9** (boss at 13, Poison 6). Hand: Tide Wall, Ripple, Defend, Defend and
**two unplayable Wounds** — no damage in it at all. Wrote Ripple and Tide Wall,

**Turn 10.** Boss at 7 after poison. Two Strikes. **No decision** — the payoff.

## The kit, after 9 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Four kinds, and three of them are good.

1. **Write or play, judged against the enemy's telegraph — on the turn.** This is
   the kit's spine and it is genuinely live, because the *same* telegraph
   argues both ways depending on what it says. A Defend telegraph says play now
   ("a Plan … lands in whatever Block the enemy is still standing in … you cannot
   strip that Block first" — fight 3 turn 2, fight 6 turn 4). A damage-debuff
   telegraph says write now ("Shrink or Weak on you never cuts it" — fight 2
   turn 1). An attack telegraph makes Flank's plan half worth 8 and an Empower
   telegraph makes it worth 0 (fight 7 turn 1 against fight 8 turn 5). A free
   turn makes writing automatic. That is four distinct readings off one
   mechanic, and I made all four from printed text alone.
2. **Arming a card that is not in your hand yet — earlier in the fight.** Sango
   Isshin ("If the Bake-Kurage carried out a Plan this turn, deal a quarter of
   your Max HP to ALL enemies instead") means a Plan written on turn 1 is worth
   13 extra damage on turn 2 *even if the Plan itself is a Strike*. Fight 5 was
   won by a turn-1 Slack Water written for a reason that had nothing to do with
   Slack Water. Best thing in the kit.
3. **What to double — on the turn.** Second Wave forces an explicit comparison
   every time it appears: 16 into one body, or 28 spread over two, or 42 over
   three (fight 6 turn 2, fight 7 turn 4). Clean, arithmetical, never the same
   answer twice.
4. **Engine versus HP — at the draft and on the turn.** Song of Pearls, Treatise
   and Tide Wall are all worth nothing unless I plan every turn, which is exactly
   the thing an incoming Defend telegraph tells me not to do. Boss turn 7 was a
   real choice between the engine and my HP bar. This is the one that only
   showed up under pressure, and it should show up more.

At the draft: taking Sango Isshin over Riptide, Song of Pearls over Salt Line,
and Tide Wall over Mika all shaped fights I had not seen yet, and all three
mattered. Skipping a reward once was also a decision the run rewarded.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Free turns are automatic.** Whenever the enemy telegraphed Empower or status
  cards, "write everything, biggest halves" was correct with no thought at all.
  That is maybe a third of the turns in this run.
- **Defend.** In nine fights I do not think Defend ever beat the alternative on a
  turn where I had one. It lost to War Council (Weak ≈ 4 damage prevented *plus*
  2 dealt *plus* it lasts), to killing the small body, and to Tide Wall. It was
  only ever right when my hand had nothing else, which is not the card being
  good, it is the hand being bad.
- **Sango Isshin on turn 1** is a dead card by construction, every fight, and I
  learned to hold it. That is fine once you know it and confusing before.
- **Kurage's Oath against a single enemy** is a strictly worse Strike (3 versus 6
  played, 7 versus 8 written) and I only played it as filler.
- **Second Thoughts** (offered at Neow, not taken): "Cancel your last Plan" —
  I never once wanted a plan back, because the whole fight tells you the
  telegraph before you write. I would want to know what that card is for.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

**One real contradiction, printed on the same screen as its own refutation.**
Every combat screen carries this glossary entry:

> **Elemental Reaction** — … **NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE:** Hydro is the only
> element this screen can supply, and Hydro meeting a Hydro aura refreshes it
> rather than reacting.

It kept saying that after I put **Shinobu — Sanctifying Ring** ("deal 5 **Electro**
damage to ALL enemies") into my deck. On round 4 of the elite fight, the same
screen printed *both* "NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE" *and* a full
**Electro-Charged** entry, while the enemy stood there wearing Poison 4 that the
reaction had applied. The claim is false whenever an Electro card is in the deck,
and the screen that prints it can be the screen that prints the reaction it says
is unreachable.

**Second, smaller: the "What reacted this turn" block never once reported a
reaction**, in a run where Electro-Charged fired at least six times and stacked
Poison to 9 on the boss. It always said "Nothing reacted this turn. A reaction
that happened would be listed here by name." Twice I found Poison on an enemy I
had never poisoned, and no screen I could reach told me where it came from — I
worked it out by inference. If reactions fire at end of turn and the block only
covers start-of-turn, then the block is reporting on a window in which reactions
cannot happen, and it is functionally always empty. Poison did more damage to the
boss than most of my cards and I never saw it announced.

**Third, mild:** "Twig Slime **(M)**" and "Leaf Slime **(S)**" against a Plan rule
written entirely in terms of "Minion" left me genuinely unsure, mid-fight,
whether my single-target Plans could hit those bodies. They could — the "(M)" is
a size — but I guessed.

**Fourth:** Tide Wall's "3 Block for each Plan carried out with it" paid 6 once
and 9 once, and I still cannot tell you from the text whether Second Wave's
doubled carry-out counts as one Plan or two.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted: Defend.** Five Block, no Plan line, no interaction with the
  jellyfish, and therefore no interaction with Song of Pearls, Treatise, Tide
  Wall, Sango or Ripple. In a deck whose every other card gets *bigger* by being
  written on the Bake-Kurage, the card that cannot be written is dead weight, and
  drawing three of them (boss turn 9) produced the only turn in the run with no
  choice in it at all.
- **Happiest to draw: Sango Isshin.** "Deal 8 damage. If the Bake-Kurage carried
  out a Plan this turn, deal a quarter of your Max HP to ALL enemies instead."
  It turns the plan engine into a payoff, it deleted a three-enemy fight in one
  card, and its condition is one I control a turn in advance. **Runner-up:
  Ripple** — a 0-cost card whose Plan half refunds its own energy *and* counts as
  a carry-out for two powers is the most elegant thing in the deck.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a good one.** Opening hand Strike/Strike/Oath/Defend/Defend against a
57-HP enemy telegraphing 4. Every card in hand printed two halves with different
numbers ("Deal 6 damage. **Plan:** Deal 8 damage"), and the enemy's small
telegraph priced the delay at exactly 4 HP. I converted 15 damage now into 23
damage next turn and paid 4 HP for it. That is a legible, quantified, genuinely
optional trade on the first turn a new player ever sees, and the fact that the
answer flips later in the same fight (turn 3, when the enemy had Strength 7 and
was telegraphing 11) is what makes it a mechanic rather than a rule of thumb.
