## Fight 1 — Corpse Slug (1) [A] 27 HP, Corpse Slug (2) [B] 26 HP

Both carried `Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it, becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength.`

**Turn 1.** Wrote Kurage's Oath on the Bake-Kurage, then Battle Plan on it, then Strike on B.
*Rejected:* playing Oath's face (3 to ALL) now. Only 6 damage was incoming, so the turn was cheap to spend; the plan line more than doubles it (7 to ALL). Writing the second card was also a deliberate test of whether the jellyfish holds one plan or several — the screen answered immediately: `Plan 2 (buff) — Carries out 2 Plans at the start of your next turn, in order`, with both titles listed in order. That is a clean piece of teaching.

**Turn 2** (carry-outs: Oath 7 to both, Battle Plan +1 energy and +2 cards). Slack Water on B (4 damage, 1 Weak; the Casket added 2 for the debuff — 13→7, exactly as printed), Strike, Strike → B dead; Strike on A with the leftover energy.
*Rejected:* blocking. Killing B was worth more than any block, because Ravenous made A eat the corpse and become **Stunned** — I bought a whole free turn at the price of A gaining 4 Strength. That was the fight's real decision, and it was made on the turn.

**Turn 3.** Defend, Defend, Strike.
*Rejected:* writing Oath as a plan for 7. A was at 14 and would die on turn 4 either way, so 6 more blocked HP beat 4 more damage. The fight was already decided.

**Turn 4.** Strike, Strike — kill. **No rejected alternative: no decision.** This was turn 3's arithmetic paying out.

## Fight 3 — Seapunk [A] 45 HP

**Turn 1.** Battle Plan → Bake-Kurage, Strike, Defend.
*Rejected:* Strike + Strike + Defend (12 damage now). 45 HP is a long fight, so I paid 6 damage of tempo for a turn-2 of 4 energy and 7 cards. This is the same "spend a turn to buy a turn" trade the whole kit is built on, and it is the one I most enjoyed making.

**Turn 2** (carry-out: +1 energy, +2 cards). Bennett — Passion Overload (0), then Strike: **10 damage** (39→29) and the Seapunk came up wearing `Pyro Aura 2`. Then Slack Water into that Pyro: **Vaporize**, `4 × 1.5 = 6`, plus the Casket's 2 for the Weak = 8 (29→21). Then Oath → Bake-Kurage and Strike.
*Rejected:* Oath's face for 3.

*Where the screen and the outcome disagreed:* after the Vaporize the Seapunk printed **`Hydro Aura 2`**, not "no aura" — so on the aura row the reaction looks as if it never happened. The Elemental Reaction tooltip had already announced this in capitals: the reaction's own debuff (Slack Water's Weak) set off the Tamakushi Casket, whose Hydro hit re-applied the aura inside the same beat. The tooltip was right and the damage number proved it, but I still had to stop and re-read to believe the board. A kit that has to shout a caveat in capitals to keep its own state screen honest is telling you something about that state screen.

**Turn 3** (carry-out: Oath 7 → 15→8). Bennett (0), then Ambush. **Ambush dealt 5, not 9, and Bennett's buff stayed on my bar** as `Passion Overload 4 (buff)`. Ambush is printed `cost 1, skill`, so it is not an Attack — the types are consistent, but a card whose entire text is "Deal 5 damage" not counting as an Attack is a trap the face does not flag. Then Kurage's Oath for exact lethal (3 into 3).
*Rejected:* writing Ambush as a plan for 12. The Seapunk was at 8 and its intent read `Empower (Buff)` **and also** `Defensive (Defend)` — waiting a turn would have let it block, so killing now beat planning. The intent screen made that call for me, which is a point in its favour.

## Fight 4 — Fossil Stalker [A] 52 HP

**Turn 1.** Weak Potion on A first, *then* Defend, Defend, Strike.

## Fight 5 (ELITE) — Phantasmal Gardener ×4: [A] 30, [B] 31, [C] 26, [D] 29 (116 HP total)

All four carried `Skittish 6 — The first time Phantasmal Gardener is hit each turn, it gains 6 Block.` I came in at 50/80 and left at 24.

**Turn 1.** Oath → Bake-Kurage, Ambush → Bake-Kurage, Defend.
*Rejected:* Strikes now. I did not want to trip Skittish before the plans landed, and 15 incoming against 5 block was going to hurt either way, so I front-loaded the jellyfish.

**Turn 2 — the finding.** The carry-outs read:

```
Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 7 — the 7 is damage.
  Phantasmal Gardener (1) lost 7 HP   … (2) (3) (4) each lost 7 HP
Bake-Kurage: Ambush, 12 — the 12 is damage.
  Phantasmal Gardener (1) lost 12 HP
```

A went 30 → 23 → **11**. **Skittish never fired on either Bake-Kurage hit** — the second plan hit landed in full against a body that had already been hit that turn. Two turns later I hit D with a plain Strike and the board immediately printed `HP 9/29, Block 6`, so Skittish plainly works against cards. So a Plan carry-out does not register as a *hit* for an enemy's on-being-hit trigger. Nothing on any screen says so. That is a large, hidden advantage for planning into blockers, and I only found it by accident.

**Turn 2 play.** Bennett (0) + Strike into A's Hydro aura = Vaporize for 15, killing A at 11 — A was the one on the `Empower (Buff)` intent that had already handed D `Strength 2`, so it was the right body. Then War Council → Bake-Kurage, Slack Water on B.
*Rejected:* War Council's face (Weak to ALL now, 2 Casket damage each, 25% off the turn's incoming). I chose the plan because its carry-out is 5+2 per body **and still applies the Weak** — same defensive effect, one turn late, for four times the damage.

**Turn 3** (carry-out: 7 to each of three, Weak to all). The Weak collapsed the turn's incoming from ~17 to **6**: `0×3`, a buff, and a 6. Battle Plan → Bake-Kurage, Undertow on C, Defend.
*Note on a face that changes:* Undertow prints `Deal 7 damage, already including 3 if the enemy has a debuff` and it read **10** aimed at a Weak'd Gardener. The face doing the conditional arithmetic for me, on the target I was aiming at, is the single best piece of screen design in the kit.
*Rejected:* spending the turn on damage without the Battle Plan. 6 incoming was the safest turn in the fight to invest in.

**Turn 4 — the best turn of the run.** Enemies B 13, C 2, D 15, and 16 incoming against 24 HP. Strike on D first (this is where I confirmed Skittish: `HP 9/29, Block 6`), then **Kurage's Oath bare** — 3 to all: it killed C outright and chewed D's block from 6 to 3 — then Strike (3 through the block) and Strike (6) to kill D. Everything that intended to attack was dead; the survivor was on a buff intent, so I took **zero** damage.
*Rejected:* leading with Oath. The order mattered: Strike first put block on D that Oath's AoE then partly ate, and reversing it would have left D alive at 6 and me down 9 HP. This was the one turn where sequencing three cheap cards against one enemy keyword was the whole game, and I liked it a lot.

**Turn 5.** War Council → Bake-Kurage, Defend, Defend (10 block vs a 7 intent, zero taken).
*Rejected:* War Council's face. Same trade as turn 2, same answer.

**Turn 6.** Carry-out 7 (10→3), Strike kills. No decision.

## The kit, after 5 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

- **Face or plan, on nearly every turn.** Ambush is 5 now or 12 next turn; Kurage's Oath is 3-to-all now or 7-to-all next; War Council is Weak-now or 5-and-Weak next; Battle Plan is draw-1 now or +1 energy and draw-2 next. The trade is always the same shape — a turn of tempo, paid in HP because the enemy swings in between — and it stayed interesting because the *right* answer moved with the board: I planned when the incoming was small and the enemy was healthy, and I stopped planning the moment a body was in kill range or the intent screen said it was about to Block. **On the turn**, every time.
- **Fight 4, turn 1: the Weak Potion.** Made *before* any card, by reading one enemy keyword (Suck) against one number (my maximum block). It converted a leaky turn into a sealed one and set the whole fight's shape.
- **Fight 4, turn 2: the flip from blocking to racing**, forced by noticing that 5 block could never cover 9 damage, so Suck was firing whatever I did. That is a decision the fight's own rule made available, and the kit's plan cards were what made racing viable.
- **Fight 1, turn 2: killing B to trigger A's Ravenous**, buying a stunned turn at the price of +4 Strength on the survivor.
- **The elite, turn 4: sequencing Strike → Oath → Strike → Strike** so that the AoE ate the block the first Strike had created. Three cheap cards, one enemy keyword, and a real ordering puzzle.
- **At the draft: taking Bennett over Deep Current.** That one pick is what made Vaporize reachable, and it changed a 6-damage Strike into a 15-damage one for a 0-cost card. The best turns of the run were all downstream of that pick.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Defend.** Five block, no plan line, nothing to do with the jellyfish. It was what I played when I had a spare energy and nothing better, on maybe eight turns. It never presented a choice, only an amount.
- **Strike, except as a Bennett carrier.** Six damage, no plan line. Its real job in this deck was to be the Attack that Bennett's +4 and Pyro ride, and outside that it was filler.
- **Battle Plan's face** (draw 1 for 1) is strictly worse than its plan line (+1 energy and draw 2 for 1). That is not a decision, it is a dominated option printed next to the good one.
- **Kurage's Oath's face.** I played it bare exactly once, and only because 3-to-all was exact lethal on one body while chewing another's block. Otherwise the plan line is more than double for the same cost.
- **The last two turns of the elite** were automatic — one enemy, on a buff intent, at 10 HP and then 3.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **Skittish did not fire on Bake-Kurage plan carry-outs, but did fire on my cards.** Same enemy, same fight, two different rules, and nothing printed anywhere says a planned hit is not a *hit*. Either a defect or a very large undocumented property of Plans.
2. **Ambush is a "skill" whose entire text is "Deal 5 damage."** Bennett buffs "your next Attack", so Bennett's buff sat unspent on my bar while Ambush hit. The card types are internally consistent, but the faces give a blind reader no warning that a damage card can fail to be an Attack.
3. **The Vaporize that left a Hydro aura on the board.** The one place the screen and the outcome disagreed — and the tooltip predicted the disagreement in capitals before it happened. The reaction was real; the state row lied about it; only the HP number settled it.
4. **`Well Laid`** (offered, not taken): "Deal 2 damage, already including 3 for each Plan carried out this morning." With no carry-outs that sentence reads as a contradiction. I worked out that the 2 is a live number that would read 8 after two carry-outs, but it cost me a re-read — and "this morning" is doing quiet work that is never defined.
5. Minor: the Bake-Kurage panel calls an empty plan list "The morning is empty," and `Well Laid` says "this morning" — a fight is apparently a morning. It is charming and it is also the only place a time-word carries rules weight.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Defend**. It is the only card in the deck that neither talks to the jellyfish nor to the elements, and drawing three of them (elite turn 3) was the flattest hand of the run.

Happiest to draw: **War Council**. It is the one card that pays on both axes at once — a planned 5-plus-Casket-2 into every body *and* a Weak on every body that halved the turn's incoming. Its carry-out on elite turn 3 dropped the four Gardeners' combined intent from about 17 to 6 while dealing 21, and it is why I finished that fight at 24 rather than at 0. Runner-up: **Bennett**, purely for the 15-damage Strikes.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a real one.** Two Corpse Slugs, only 6 damage incoming, three energy: spend it all on faces (Strike, Strike, Defend — 12 damage now) or write Kurage's Oath and Battle Plan on the jellyfish and cash 7-to-all plus a whole extra energy and two cards at the start of turn 2. I also did not know whether the jellyfish could hold more than one plan, so writing both was partly a test — and the screen answered on the spot with `Plan 2 (buff) — Carries out 2 Plans at the start of your next turn, in order`, listing both titles in written order. A first turn that is simultaneously a genuine tempo decision and a legible lesson in the character's core rule is about as good an opening as a blind seat can be given.
