## Fight 1 — Seapunk 45/45

**Turn 1** (3 energy, Encore 2, hand: Ethereal Spotlight, An Invitation,
Soloist's Solicitation ×2, Regal Bearing, Stage Presence; enemy intent 11).

Played **An Invitation** (0) first purely to see what a Companion card is — it
gave *Diona — Icy Paws* (1: gain 5 Block, apply Cryo to a random enemy) with
the rider *"No member on stage: performs nobody. Deploy a member first, or this
card is only its own line."* That rider is the clearest single line in the kit
and it told me the whole shape of the character in one read.

Then **Regal Bearing** (3 Block + 1 Weak) → **Diona** (5 Block) → **Soloist's
Solicitation** (6 damage). *Rejected:* double Soloist for 12 damage, which
would have cost ~4 HP and my entire Encore pool. Encore was printed as both a
damage sponge and the fuel a member spends, and with 2 of it I would not spend
it on a first turn to learn nothing.

**Where the screen and the outcome disagreed:** the Weakened intent printed as
**8**, I held **8 Block**, and I still lost **1 Encore** over the turn (2 → 1)
with HP unchanged. So something landed for 9 while the screen said 8. Probably
11 × 0.75 = 8.25 displayed floored and dealt rounded, but the screen gave me no
way to plan around it.

**Turn 2** (Encore 1, Fanfare 0). Hand had Salon Début, Chevreuse — Interdiction
Fire (Pyro, *Reaction preview: Melt — 1.75x*), Aria of Recompense, Charlotte,
Stage Presence.

Played **Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire** first *specifically* so its Pyro would
Melt the Cryo aura Diona had left last turn — a two-turn plan, and the payoff
was clean: 12 damage off a printed 7. Then **Aria of Recompense** (+5 Encore),
then **Salon Début** (deploy Mademoiselle Crabaletta, which performed at once
for 6 Hydro paying 1 Encore). 18 damage, 45 → 21.

*Rejected:* Chevreuse + Aria + Stage Presence, which would have blocked the
whole incoming 8 and kept 6 Encore but dealt only 12. I chose to let the Encore
pool eat the hit (it absorbed 5 of 8, I took 3 HP) in exchange for a deployed
member. That is a real trade and I felt it: **Encore is simultaneously my shield
and my engine fuel, and every turn asks which one it is going to be.** That is
the best thing in this kit.

**Turn 3.** Enemy at 21 and turning to Buff+Defend. Played **Chevreuse** (10,
Vaporize on the Hydro aura Crabaletta had left) → **Salon Début** (a second
Crabaletta, both dry at 3/4 for 4 each) → **Charlotte — First-Person Shutter**
(performs Crabaletta again) for the kill. 21 → 3 → 0. *Rejected:* blocking and
grinding, which would have let a buffing enemy compound.

Companion cards played: **3** (Diona, Chevreuse, Charlotte). Turns the stage
held no member: **2 of 3** (rounds 1 and 2 up to the moment Salon Début
resolved).

**Reward:** took **Lynette — Bogglecat Box** (1: draw 2, and it performs the
front member). Nearly took *Ebb and Flow* (spend 1 Encore, gain 3, draw 1) — I
had just watched Encore run dry, but a Companion that draws two cards *and*
performs a member for free is two cards' worth of rate.

## Fight 2 — Toadpole (1) 25/25, Toadpole (2) 23/23

Opened from an "Unknown" node that turned out to be a fight.

**Turn 1.** **An Invitation** → Diona again. Played **Diona** to see where the
random Cryo landed (Toadpole 1), then **Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire** on that
one for the Melt (12), then **Soloist's Solicitation** on the same target.
*Rejected:* splitting damage across both toadpoles — I concentrated on the one
wearing the aura because the Melt made it the cheap target, which is the draft's
elemental hook doing its job. Diona's 5 Block + 2 Encore ate the whole 7.

**Turn 2.** No damage cards in hand at all; the whole turn was **Aria**
(+5 Encore) → **Salon Début** (deploy) → **Charlotte**. Both Crabaletta
performances picked **Toadpole (2)** at random, leaving the 7-HP Toadpole (1)
alive. *This is the sharpest complaint I have:* I had exactly one job — 7 more
damage into a specific body — and the kit's own damage refused to aim. The
printed rule is honest ("The member picks its own enemy at random, never the
card's target") but honest is not the same as playable.

**Turn 3 — the best turn of the run, and the plan was laid the turn before.**
Played **Diona** first: her Cryo hit Toadpole (2)'s Hydro aura (left by
Crabaletta the previous turn) and **Froze** it — its 3×3 became 1×3, and the
screen printed *"The first Attack to hit it Shatters for 6 unblockable damage."*
That let me kill an 11-HP enemy with a 6-damage card: **Soloist's Solicitation**
on Toadpole (2) = 6 + 6 Shatter = 12. And killing it first meant the *random*
member target was now forced: **Salon Début** deployed, Crabaletta had only
Toadpole (1) to pick, and its 4 finished the 3-HP body. Fight over on the turn.

That sequence — aura laid two turns earlier, reaction, Shatter, then killing one
enemy specifically to remove the randomness from my own member — is the most fun
I had, and every step of it was legible off the screen.

Companion cards played: **3** (Diona, Chevreuse, Charlotte) + Diona again in
turn 3 = **4**. Turns the stage held no member: **2 of 3**.

**Reward:** took **Curtain Rises** (1: deal 6, deploy Gentilhomme Usher).
Nearly took *Chevreuse — Vanguard's Valor*. I took the deployer because my whole
problem in two fights had been that Salon Début is a 1-of and every Companion
card is half-dead without a body on stage. (See (c): this pick backfired in a
way the card does not warn about.)

## Fight 3 — Sludge Spinner 38/38

**Turn 1.** **An Invitation** (drew a second Lynette) → **Aria** (+5 Encore) →
**Ethereal Spotlight** (0 energy, **2 Encore**) to light the Companions → **Salon
Début** (Crabaletta 6) → **Charlotte** (Crabaletta again, 6; her Block printed
**4 → 6** under the Spotlight). 12 damage, 6 Block, 0 taken.

*Rejected:* not lighting at all and banking the Encore as a sponge. Against a
38-HP body I judged a combat-long +50% worth 2 Encore, and Charlotte's printed
number visibly moving 4 → 6 confirmed it immediately. The Spotlight is the one
piece of the kit whose payment and payoff are both legible on the same screen.

**Turn 2.** I had picked up **Weak** from the Spinner's debuff. Played
**Chevreuse** (printed 7 under Weak, *Reaction preview: Vaporize 1.5x*) then
**Lynette** (draw 2 + a second Crabaletta performance). 26 → 8.

**Where the screen and the outcome disagreed — the one I would actually file.**
Crabaletta's two performances were logged at 6 and 4 (the 4 marked *dry*), so
Chevreuse dealt **8**. The card printed **7** with a **1.5x Vaporize preview**
sitting under it. 7 × 1.5 = 10 or 11, not 8. 8 is exactly what you get if the
Vaporize multiplier never applied (7 base × 1.5 Spotlight × 0.75 Weak = 7.875).
In fight 1, with no Spotlight and no Weak, the same card's Melt and Vaporize
previews *did* compose correctly (12 and 10 dealt off a printed 7). So the
preview and the printed number stopped agreeing the moment two other multipliers
were in play, and I had no way from the screen to tell which one dropped.

Third energy went to **Stage Presence** rather than **Curtain Rises+** (4 damage
+ a body). *Rejected* because the Spinner dies next turn either way and 3 HP
was worth more than tempo I did not need.

**Turn 3.** Chevreuse now printed **10** with Weak gone — so the Spotlight *does*
move the printed damage; it just did not compose with the reaction. One card,
lethal.

Companion cards played: **3** (Charlotte, Chevreuse, Lynette). Turns the stage
held no member: **0** — Salon Début was in the opening hand and the stage was
occupied from turn 1 on.

**Reward:** took **Chevreuse — Vanguard's Valor** (0: next Attack +3, +3 more
if an Elemental Reaction triggered this turn — and, being a Companion, it
performs a member for free). Nearly took *Mademoiselle Crabaletta* (a third way
onto the stage). A 0-cost card that performs a member is the cheapest damage in

## Fight 4 — Calcified Cultist 38/38 + Seapunk 44/44

82 HP across two bodies, and the Cultist carried **Ritual 2** (+2 Strength every
turn). **I took zero damage across all eight rounds of this fight.**

**Turn 1.** Opening hand was Ethereal Spotlight, Stage Presence ×2, Aria,
Charlotte, **Clumsy** — no damage, no deployer, one dead card. Played **Aria**
(Encore 7) → **Ethereal Spotlight** (Encore 5) → **Charlotte** (6 Block, +6 next
turn) → **Stage Presence** (6). 12 Block against 11. *Rejected:* holding the
Spotlight to keep the Encore as a sponge — against 82 enemy HP I wanted the
combat-long buff and I had the Encore to spare exactly once.

**Turn 2.** **Chevreuse** (10) + **Soloist+** (9) into the Cultist, 38 → 19,
then **Regal Bearing** for 3 Block and Weak *on the Cultist specifically* so its
9 became 6. Block 9 vs 14 total incoming, Encore ate the last 5. Zero HP.
*Rejected:* deploying Crabaletta for the engine, which would have cost me ~12 HP
this turn; against an enemy whose damage grows +2 a turn, killing it beats
building.

**Turn 3.** **An Invitation** → *Thoma — Blazing Barrier* (7 Block, +3 next
turn). Played **Aria** (Encore 5) *before* everything so nothing performed dry
→ **Chevreuse — Vanguard's Valor** (0) → **Curtain Rises+** (9 + 3 = **12**,
deploying the Usher, who performed 3 Block) → **Thoma** (7 Block + the Usher
again). 13 Block against 11. Cultist 19 → 7.

*Rejected:* Lynette for card draw — with 0 Encore at the start of the turn the
members would have performed dry and I'd have had no energy for what I drew.
The ordering decision here (Aria first so every performance is paid) is a real,
repeatable skill the kit teaches.

**Turn 4.** **Soloist+** killed the Cultist at 7, then **Regal Bearing** Weak'd
the Seapunk and **Stage Presence** covered the rest. Zero HP again.

**Turn 5.** **Chevreuse** (10) + **Soloist+** (9) into the Seapunk, and
**Charlotte** for 6 Block + an Usher performance. 44 → 25, zero taken.

**Turn 6 — the turn where the kit's numbers stopped being readable.** The
Seapunk was Defending+Buffing (no attack), so I spent the free turn on damage:
**Lynette** (draw 2 — got Vanguard's Valor and a Soloist) → **Salon Début**
(deploy Crabaletta, whose Hydro met the Pyro aura) → **Chevreuse — Vanguard's
Valor** → **Curtain Rises+**. 25 → 4.

The Salon log for that one turn read:

> - **the Usher** gave you 3 Block.
> - **Crabaletta** hit Seapunk for 4 Hydro, and left no aura on it (dry: it could not pay its Encore, so it acted at three-quarters).
> - **the Usher** gave you 2 Block (dry: …).
> - **the Usher** gave you 3 Block (dry: …).

Three things in four lines that I could not reconcile from the screen:

1. I played **Salon Début**, which the card says *"Deploy Mademoiselle
   Crabaletta"* and the glossary says *"a Deploy performs the member it fields
   at once."* The log's **first** line is the **Usher** performing and taking my
   last Encore — so Crabaletta, the member the card actually fielded, went
   **dry** and performed at 3/4. Something performed the front member off a
   Deploy, and no printed line says it should.
2. **"hit Seapunk for 4 Hydro, and left no aura on it"** — *left no aura* is the
   glossary's own signature for a reaction having consumed the aura. So the
   Vaporize fired, and the damage was 4 (= 6 × 0.75 dry) with **no 1.5x
   anywhere in it**. Same shape as the fight-3 anomaly.
3. Two "dry" Usher performances printed **2** and **3** for the same member on
   the same turn. I eventually worked out that Fanfare crossed 10 mid-turn and
   *"Member numbers gain +1 per 10 Fanfare"* kicked in between them — but that
   took me three screens of arithmetic to reconstruct, and the log does not say
   it.

**Turn 7.** Seapunk at 4 with 7 Block and hitting for 13. I wanted to kill it
and could not, because of the rotation: *"playing one performs the front member,
then sends it to the back."* My stage was Usher/Crabaletta/Usher, so only the
**first** Companion card of the turn reached Crabaletta and every one after it
performed an Usher for Block. **Curtain Rises — an Attack card — had quietly
filled my stage with a Block member that demotes every subsequent Companion play
from 7 damage to 3 Block.** Nothing on Curtain Rises warns about this; I drafted
it as "6 damage plus a body" and it turned out to be "6 damage plus a tax on my
other cards." That is the single worst-explained interaction in the kit and I
walked straight into it.

So I played **Aria** (Fanfare ≥ 3, so **10 Encore**) → Valor → Charlotte →
Thoma for 21 Block and stripped the enemy's 7 Block with the one Crabaletta
performance I was allowed.

**Turn 8.** Stage was FULL, and the Salon text now read *"the next deploy Evokes
the front member first."* I played **Salon Début** rather than the lethal
Chevreuse in hand, specifically to see an **Evoke** (14 Hydro) — it killed the
Seapunk. Both were lethal, so this cost nothing, and it is the only Evoke I got
to see all run.

Companion cards played across the fight: **9** (Charlotte ×3, Chevreuse —
Interdiction ×2, Thoma ×2, Valor ×2 — plus Lynette = 10 by the strict count of
"a card titled Name — Title"). Turns the stage held no member: **2 of 8** (rounds
1 and 2).

**Reward:** took **Fischl — Nightrider** (1: Hexerei, deal 7 Electro, performs a
member). Nearly took *Dramatic Entrance*. See (c) — the card is printed in a
language this run does not speak.

**Turn 5.** **Soloist's Solicitation+** for the kill. 39/78 at the end.

Companion cards played: **4** (Charlotte ×2, Valor, Fischl). Turns the stage

## The kit, after 5 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

- **Encore: sponge or fuel.** Made *on the turn*, every turn, and it is the
  spine of the character. Encore sits after Block and eats damage before HP,
  *and* a member spends 1 of it to perform at full instead of at three-quarters,
  *and* the Ethereal Spotlight costs 2 of it. Fight 1 turn 2 was the cleanest
  version: spend Encore to deploy Crabaletta and take 3 HP, or hold it and deal
  6 less. I have not played a Spire character where one number is asked for by
  three different systems at once, and it makes almost every turn a small budget
  problem rather than a sequencing puzzle.
- **Play Aria *before* the Companion cards.** Made *on the turn*, and it is the
  kind of small ordering skill a good kit teaches quietly: an unpaid member
  performs at 3/4, so the +Encore card goes first. Fight 4 turn 3 is where I
  finally internalised it.
- **Laying an aura one turn to react to it the next.** Made *earlier in the
  fight*. Fight 1: Diona's Cryo on turn 1 so Chevreuse could Melt for 12 on
  turn 2. Fight 2: Crabaletta's Hydro so Diona's Cryo could Freeze, and the
  Frozen Shatter let a 6-damage card kill an 11-HP enemy. This is the best
  designed thing here and it is entirely legible from the printed reaction
  previews.
- **Killing one enemy specifically to aim the other one's damage.** Made *on the
  turn*, fight 2 turn 3, and it only exists because member targeting is random —
  a decision manufactured by a frustration. I enjoyed it anyway.
- **Deploy now or kill now.** Made *on the turn*, fight 4 turn 2, against an
  enemy with Ritual. The Salon is an investment and investments are bad against
  a clock; the kit lets you feel that.
- **The elite's damage cap.** Made *on the turn*: once "Hardened Shell 0 of 20
  left" printed, attacking was worthless and the right play was to convert
  energy into Block. Good, unusual, and entirely the enemy's doing rather than
  the kit's.
- **At the draft:** taking Curtain Rises over Vanguard's Valor after two fights
  of empty stages was a real read of my own deck's failure mode. It was also
  wrong, for a reason the card never printed — see (c).

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Turn 1 of a fight with no Salon Début is not a decision.** It is: play
  An Invitation because it is free, play the Block card, end turn. Six of my 22
  turns had an empty stage and in every one of them the Companion cards
  collapsed to their own printed line, which is a 4-block skill or a 7-damage
  attack. The character's whole identity is switched off until a 1-of card shows
  up.
- **Ethereal Spotlight is a decision exactly once per combat and a dead card
  every other turn.** The relic hands me one every single turn and after it is
  lit it prints *"CANNOT BE PLAYED: the Spotlight is already on your Companion
  cards."* That is 5–7 draws per fight of a card whose only job is to be
  ignored. It never cost me anything, but it is a card slot in my hand that
  never asks me a question.
- **Stage Presence** (gain 6 Block, no rider, no Companion, no element) is the
  most boring card in the deck and I played it four times without thinking once.
  **Regal Bearing** is a strictly-more-interesting version of the same card
  (3 Block and a Weak that lets you block a bigger number with less) and it
  cost me no more thought.
- **Soloist's Solicitation** is a vanilla 6-damage strike. It did real work
  (it is what actually killed the elite) but it never presented a choice.
- **Never seemed worth playing:** Clumsy, obviously, but it is a curse. Of my
  real cards, **nothing was unplayable-bad** — but Lynette's *draw 2* was
  frequently worthless because I had no energy left to use what she gave me. A
  1-cost draw-2 in a 3-energy deck where every other card costs 1 mostly draws
  cards you will discard.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **The reaction multiplier does not show up in the damage, twice.** Fight 3
   turn 2: Chevreuse printed **7**, previewed **Vaporize 1.5x**, dealt **8**.
   Fight 4 turn 6: Crabaletta's performance printed the reaction in its own log
   line — *"hit Seapunk for 4 Hydro, and **left no aura on it**"*, which is the
   glossary's exact phrasing for a reaction consuming the aura — and the damage
   was **4**, which is base 6 at the dry three-quarters rate with **no 1.5x
   anywhere**. In fight 1, with no Spotlight and no Weak in play, Melt and
   Vaporize composed correctly (12 and 10 off a printed 7). So the previews are
   right in the simple case and silently wrong once a second multiplier is on
   the board, and there is nothing on the screen that lets a player tell which
   case they are in. **This is the finding I would most want checked.**
2. **A Deploy performed the wrong member.** Fight 4 turn 6: I played Salon
   Début, whose card text is *"Deploy Mademoiselle Crabaletta"* and whose
   glossary says *"a Deploy performs the member it fields at once."* The log's
   first line was **the Usher** performing and consuming my last Encore, and
   Crabaletta — the member the card fielded — then performed **dry**. If a
   Deploy also performs the front member, no card says so; if it does not, the
   log is lying about the order.
3. **Curtain Rises poisons your own Salon and never says so.** It is printed as
   an Attack: *"Deal 6 damage. Deploy Gentilhomme Usher."* What it actually does
   is put a **Block** member on a 3-slot stage that every Companion card then
   rotates through, so a stage of Usher/Crabaletta/Usher means only the first
   Companion card each turn reaches your damage member and the rest give you 3
   Block. In fight 4 turn 7 this is precisely why I could not kill a 4-HP enemy
   with three Companion cards in hand. The rotation rule *is* printed ("performs
   the front member, then sends it to the back") — but nothing connects it to
   the drafting decision, and the card that causes the problem reads like a
   damage card.
4. **Fischl — Nightrider is printed in another character's language.** It says
   *"**Hexerei** — A Companion card that prints the word, and **Klee** herself.
   Some are Klee's own, some are not. **Cards of hers pay when you play one.**"*
   and *"**Oz** — Fischl's raven, out while you hold the Power *Oz, at Your
   Side*."* In a Furina run I have no Klee cards, no way to obtain that Power,
   and no idea what "pay" means or what it would cost me. I drafted it anyway
   and it behaved as a plain 7-damage Electro Companion — but I took that pick
   blind in the literal sense, and half its rules text was noise.
5. **Electro-Charged is previewed under one name and lands under another.** The
   preview read *"Electro-Charged — the reacted enemy loses 4 HP at the start of
   its turn, 1 less each turn"*; what appeared on the elite's body was
   **`Poison 3`**. Same effect, two names, and Poison is a word with a lot of
   existing baggage in this game.
6. **"Lasts this combat" vs "lasts until the Spotlight moves."** Ethereal
   Spotlight's own rider says *"Lighting your Companion cards lasts this
   combat"*; the buff it creates says *"Guest Cast — … Lasts until the Spotlight
   moves."* Another card (Curtain Cue, seen in a reward) says *"Playing Ethereal
   Spotlight moves it."* Those three lines can be reconciled, but only by
   holding all three in your head at once, and the relic's *"it does nothing
   once your Companion cards are lit"* is doing a lot of quiet work to stop the
   contradiction from ever biting.
7. **Small one:** the Weak rounding in fight 1 turn 1 (printed intent 8, held 8
   Block, still lost 1 Encore).
8. **Bridge-side, not kit-side:** several observe screens printed `## Your hand`
   and `## The other side` twice, with card bodies duplicated line-for-line. It
   never changed what I could do, but it made two screens genuinely hard to
   read.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted:** **Ethereal Spotlight**, after the one turn I lit it. The
  relic deals me a fresh unplayable copy every single turn for the rest of the
  combat. (Discounting Clumsy, which is a curse and is supposed to be miserable.)
- **Happiest to draw:** **Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire.** It is an attack, it
  is a Companion so it performs a member for free, it carries Pyro so it turns
  every aura on the board into a bonus, and it printed a **Reaction preview**
  line that told me on the card face exactly what it was about to do. It is the
  only card in the deck that is doing all three of the kit's jobs at once.
  Honourable mention to **Chevreuse — Vanguard's Valor** simply for costing 0
  and still performing a member.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, but a thin one, and only because I chose to make it thin.** The real
choice on turn 1 was *do I spend 3 energy on 12 damage and eat 11, or on 8 Block
and deal 6* — a stock Spire opening question, not a Furina question. Everything
that makes this character interesting (Encore's triple duty, the stage, the
auras) was either unavailable or invisible: I had 2 Encore and nothing to spend
it on, no member and no way to field one, and no aura on the board. The one
genuinely Furina-flavoured act available to me was playing An Invitation to
manufacture a Companion card out of nothing, and the card I got could not
perform anybody. **The kit's first real decision arrived on turn 2 of fight 1**,
when Salon Début and Aria showed up together and Encore suddenly had three
claimants.
