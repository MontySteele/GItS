## Fight 1 — Seapunk 44/44

Starting kit as first printed: HP 62, Energy 3, **Spark 1**, relic *Pounding
Surprise*. Hand: Strike, Jumpy Dumpty, Defend ×3. Draw pile 6.

**Turn 1** (enemy intent: Attack 11).
Played **Jumpy Dumpty** (*Place a Bomb 8. When it goes off, place a Mine 3 on ALL
enemies*), then **Defend**, **Defend**.
*Rejected:* Strike + 2 Defends. The Bomb text says it "grows 4 a turn, goes off
only when Set off", so a bomb planted on turn 1 is worth strictly more than 6
Strike damage as long as I eventually draw a Set off — and I had no idea yet
whether I owned one. The trade was 6 immediate damage against an unknown-sized
delayed payoff. Result: took 1 (11 vs 10 Block).

**Turn 2.** Bomb read **12** — the growth is real and it is printed on the enemy,
which is the single clearest thing on the screen. Drew **Ka-pow!** (0 cost,
*Retain. Set off. Deal 4*).
Played **Dodoco Cover** (Bomb 4 → the pile), then **Ka-pow!** on Seapunk, then
**Strike**, **Strike**.
*Rejected:* holding Ka-pow! another turn to let 12 become 16. Ka-pow! Retains, so
holding costs nothing but tempo — but the ordering trick was worth more: playing
Dodoco Cover *before* the detonation folded its Bomb 4 into the same Set off.
That sequencing (place, then pop) is the first genuinely interesting thing the
kit asked me.
Screen agreed with outcome: 44 → 24, exactly 12 + 4 (bombs) + 4 (Ka-pow!), and
Spark went 1 → 3, one per bomb popped, exactly as *Pounding Surprise* prints.
Jumpy Dumpty's rider fired and put **Mine 3** on Seapunk.

**Turn 3.** Enemy at 9 (24 − 12 Strikes − 3 from the Mine, which had gone off on
its own before the enemy's hit, as the *Mine* keyword says). Intent: Empower +
Defend.
Played **Strike**, **Strike** for the kill.
*Rejected:* replanting bombs. It intended to Block, so the bombs would have been
racing a shield; 12 raw damage into 9 HP was flatly enough.

Won on turn 3, 58/62. Rewards: 19 gold, Fysh Oil, and a card — took **Powder
Charge** (*cost 1 Spark: Place a Bomb 6*) over Mine Toss, Dodoco Cover #2 and
Bennett — Passion Overload, because a card whose price is a Spark rather than
Energy is free tempo and *Pounding Surprise* prints Sparks back every time a
bomb goes off. That felt like finding the engine.

## Fight 2 — Sludge Spinner 37/37

**Turn 1** (intent: Attack 8 + Debuff). Hand: Defend ×3, Ka-pow!, Jumpy Dumpty.
Played **Jumpy Dumpty**, **Defend**, **Defend**; held Ka-pow! (Retain).
*Rejected:* Jumpy Dumpty + Ka-pow! for 12 immediate. Retain means holding the
detonator is free, and I wanted to find out how big the pile gets. Took 0.

**Turn 2.** Bomb 12. I was **Weak 1** — and the hand printed **Strike — Deal 4**
and **Ka-pow! — Deal 3**, i.e. the card faces re-price themselves under the
debuff. That is excellent: no mental arithmetic required.
Played **Powder Charge** (Spark, Bomb 6), **Dodoco Cover** (Bomb 4, 5 Block),
**Defend**, **Strike**.
*Rejected:* the full dump — Powder Charge + Dodoco + Ka-pow! + 2 Strikes, which I
worked out to 32 damage and would have left the Spinner on 4. Holding instead
put the bombs at 22 with a printed promise of 34 next turn, against an enemy on
33. I traded five points of immediate damage for a guaranteed one-shot. **This
was the best decision the kit gave me all round** and it was legible entirely
from printed numbers.

**Turn 3.** The enemy status read exactly what I'd projected:
`Bomb 34 (buff) — Set off here deals 34 Pyro damage. Bombs here: 16 / 10 / 8` —
each of the three bombs had grown 4, not the pile as a whole. Enemy on 33.
Played **Ka-pow!**. 34 killed it outright.
*Rejected:* nothing. There was no alternative worth naming; the turn was a
cash-out, and cashing out is the payoff for the turn-2 decision, not an absence
of one.

Won on turn 3, 58/62, **zero damage taken**. Rewards: 18 gold, Energy Potion
(belt full, declined), card — took **Bang Bang!** (*cost 2 Sparks: Set off. Deal

By this point the deck's real bottleneck was obvious: I had bomb mass and exactly
**one** detonator. Bang Bang! is a second detonator that costs no Energy.

## Fight 3 — Toadpole (1) 24/24, Toadpole (2) 23/23

**Turn 1** (T1 buffs, T2 attacks 7). Played **Powder Charge** and **Jumpy
Dumpty** both onto Toadpole (1), then **Defend**, **Defend**.
*Rejected:* splitting the bombs one per Toadpole. Bombs are single-target and
Set off only reaches "the target's Bombs", so splitting halves every detonation.
The counter-argument — that a split covers both enemies — lost to the *Bomb*
keyword's last clause, "Kills move it on", which promised that overkill spreads
by itself. Took 0.

**Turn 2.** T1 on 24 with Bomb 22 and **Thorns 2**.
Played **Ka-pow!** on Toadpole (1): 22 bomb damage put it on 2, the 4-damage
attack finished it, and I ate 2 from Thorns. Then **Strike ×3** into Toadpole
(2).
*Rejected:* waiting one more turn for Bomb 30 on a 24-HP body — pure waste; and
striking T1 to death instead of Ka-pow-ing it, which would have wasted the
bombs.
**The interaction that made this fight:** T1 died carrying an unexploded Mine 3
(Jumpy Dumpty's rider had put one on *both* toads), and the survivor's status
then read `Mine 6 … Bombs here: 3 / 3, including 2 Mines`. The dead toad's mine
walked over to the live one. "Kills move it on" is a real rule and it is the
kit's AoE.

**Turn 3.** Toadpole on 5, carrying **Mine 14** (7 / 7) and intending to attack.
Played **nothing**. Ended the turn.
*Rejected:* Strike for the kill (6 into 5 HP). It would have worked, but it would
also have eaten 2 from Thorns 2 — and the *Mine* keyword says a Mine "goes off
before its enemy's hit, which lands in full unless the Mine kills". 14 into 5 HP
kills, so the correct play was to do nothing and let the trap do it. **A turn
where the right move is to play no cards, and where the screen tells you exactly
why, is a good turn.**

Won, 56/62. Reward card: **Sizzle** (see fight 4 rewards below — the order of the
reward screens is as recorded; Sizzle came after fight 4).

## Fight 4 — Corpse Slug (1) 27/27, Corpse Slug (2) 26/26, Corpse Slug (3) 25/25

All three carried `Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats
it, becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength.` 78 HP across three bodies, 14
incoming on turn 1. This was the hardest read of the round and the best fight.

**Turn 1.** Played **Pop!** (Bomb 5) and **Dodoco Cover** (Bomb 4) both onto
**Corpse Slug (3)**, the *smallest* at 25, then **Strike** into it as well, then
**Defend**.
*Rejected:* bombing the biggest slug, and spreading. The whole plan came from
reading Ravenous as a *gift*: it stuns the survivors. So the goal was not "kill
the dangerous one" but "kill **something** as early as possible", which points
at the smallest body. Took 4, and picked up **Frail 2**.

**Turn 2.** Slug 3 on 19 with Bomb 17.
Played **Powder Charge** (Bomb 6) onto **Slug 1** — deliberately *not* onto the
one I was about to blow up — then **Ka-pow!** on Slug 3.
*Rejected:* putting Powder Charge's bomb on Slug 3 too. Adding it would have
made the detonation 23 into a 19-HP body, i.e. four points thrown away, and
would have left nothing seeded for the next detonation. Seeding elsewhere is
the difference between one big turn and a chain.
Slug 3 died. Both survivors went **Stunned** and **Strength 4** — exactly as
printed. Spark went to 2, which is precisely Bang Bang!'s price, and the hand
face flipped from `CANNOT BE PLAYED: you have 1 Spark, and this costs 2` to
playable inside the same turn. **This is the kit's best moment: the detonation
pays for the next detonator.**
Then **Bang Bang!** on Slug 1 (set off its Bomb 6, 8 damage, new Bomb 4) and
**Strike**. Slug 1 → 7. Note the whole chain — Powder Charge, Ka-pow!, Bang
Bang! — cost **zero Energy**; I still had all 3 for Strike and Defends.

**Turn 3.** Slug 1 on 7 with Bomb 8, intending 12. No Set off in hand.
Played **Jumpy Dumpty** onto **Slug 1** (bombs there now 16), **Powder Charge**
onto Slug 2, then **Strike ×2** to kill Slug 1 with attacks.
*Rejected:* holding the strikes and blocking. Killing by Strike deliberately
*wasted* nothing, because the 16 bomb damage on the corpse walked over: Slug 2's
status then read `Bomb 22 — Bombs here: 6 / 8 / 8`. I killed a slug with 12
points of Strike and moved 16 points of bomb onto the last one, and stunned it
into the bargain. Took 0.

**Turn 4.** Slug 2 on 26, `Bomb 34 (10 / 12 / 12)`.
Played **Ka-pow!**. 34 killed it.
*Rejected:* nothing again — cash-out turn.

Won on turn 4, 52/62. Reward card: **Sizzle** (*1 energy: Set off. Deal 6.*) over
Pocket Fireworks, Witches' Circle and Arlecchino, because a third detonator was
still what the deck wanted more than 9 raw damage.

## The kit, after 6 fights

### (a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off

Four kinds, and three of them are good:

1. **Pop now or grow one more turn.** This is the kit's spine and it is a real,
   arithmetic-legible trade every single turn: the enemy prints
   `Bomb 34 — Bombs here: 16 / 10 / 8, growing each turn`, so I can compute both
   branches exactly. Fight 2 turn 2 was the round's best decision — five points
   of damage now against a guaranteed one-shot next turn — and I could only make
   it because every input was printed. The counter-pressure (enemy intents,
   summons, an escaping gremlin) is what stops "always wait" from being right.
2. **Where to plant.** Bombs are single-target, but `Kills move it on` means
   overkill spreads. So the multi-enemy question is never "spread the bombs", it
   is "which body do I want to blow *through*". Fight 4 turn 3 — kill a slug with
   Strikes so its 16 points of bomb walk onto the last one — is the most
   satisfying thing I did all round, and it is a decision the printed keyword
   taught me rather than one I had to be told.
3. **Ordering inside a turn.** Plant-then-pop folds new bombs into the same Set
   off; Careful Now must be played between the plant and the pop to see a big
   bomb; a seeded bomb on the *other* enemy converts one detonation into a chain.
   These are cheap to learn and they reward attention.
4. **Which Skill to spend under Smoggy** (only 1 Skill/turn) — a debuff that bites
   this kit specifically, and made Dodoco Cover's two-jobs-in-one card the right
   answer. Good friction.

The Spark economy is the quiet star. Pounding Surprise pays 1 Spark per bomb
popped, and Powder Charge / Bang Bang! are priced in Sparks rather than Energy,
so a detonation literally funds the next placement *and* the next detonator in
the same turn while leaving all 3 Energy for Strikes and Defends. In fight 4
turn 2 I played three kit cards for zero Energy. And the bridge is scrupulous
about the distinction — every Spark card prints "Its 1 Spark is a price, not an
Energy cost", and an unaffordable card prints
`CANNOT BE PLAYED: you have 1 Spark, and this costs 2` right on its face.

### (b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing

- **Cash-out turns are automatic, and that's fine** — the pop turn after a good
  hold has no alternative worth naming, because the decision was made the turn
  before. I'd not call that a defect.
- **Strike and Defend are the dead weight.** Eight of sixteen cards, and fight 6
  turn 1 dealt me all-generic: three Defends and two Strikes, a turn with
  literally no kit in it, resolved by a coin-flip between 6 damage and 5 Block.
  The bomb cards are so much better that every Strike drawn alongside one is a
  small disappointment. (I never got to a rest site or shop, so I never got to
  thin them — that's the map's fault, not the kit's.)
- **Defend specifically** was the card I most often played out of having spare
  Energy rather than intent, and Careful Now (Block = largest Bomb, up to 10)
  showed how much better a defence card feels when it is priced off the kit's own
  engine.
- **Nothing in the kit itself felt unplayable.** Even Pop! at 0 cost earned its
  slot every time.

### (c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text

- **The invisible Mine (fight 6, turn 2).** Jumpy Dumpty's rider prints "When it
  goes off, place a Mine 3 on ALL enemies". After Sizzle popped that bomb, the
  Gremlin Merc's status block printed a Pyro Aura and no Mine — yet the Merc lost
  exactly 3 HP (21 → 18) before its next hit, which is precisely a Mine 3 going
  off. The same rider printed a visible `Mine 3 (buff)` in fights 1 and 3. So the
  effect happened and the screen didn't show it. **This is the one place the
  screen and the outcome disagreed.**
- **`Hexerei`** is defined as "A Companion card that prints the word, and Klee
  herself. Some are Klee's own, some are not. Cards of hers pay when you play
  one." I read that four times across three reward screens and still could not
  tell you (i) whether any card *I* owned was Hexerei, or (ii) what "pay" means —
  pay what, to whom? Witches' Circle and Coven Errand both key off it and I
  skipped both partly because I could not price them. **This is the least legible
  text in the kit by a distance.**
- **`Elemental Reaction`.** The keyword block is enormous — a full paragraph
  including a warning about a case where "no screen ever shows it gone and the
  reaction looks as though it did not happen" — and then ends with **"NO REACTION
  IS REACHABLE HERE: Pyro is the only element this screen can supply."** Every
  screen for six fights printed that paragraph, and it was never once actionable.
  Meanwhile Sizzle and Perfect Timing both have riders conditional on a reaction
  I had no way to cause. Either the wall of text should shrink to its last
  sentence when no second element is on the board, or the reaction-conditional
  cards should be flagged as inert.
- **`Surprise 1 — Something is off about this creature...`** is deliberate
  mystery and I have no complaint; it meant "splits on death" and finding that
  out by killing it was fine.
- The bridge printed **`## The other side` and the whole enemy block twice,
  verbatim**, on five separate observes (fights 3, 4, 5, 6). Cosmetic, but on the
  three-Corpse-Slug screen it doubled six enemies into what looked like a
  different fight for a moment. Worth a fix.

### (d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw

- **Never wanted: Defend.** 5 Block, no interaction with anything the kit does,
  and four copies of it. Under Frail it fell to 3 and felt like a blank.
- **Happiest to draw: Ka-pow!** — 0 cost, **Retain**, Set off, 4 damage. Retain
  is what makes the hold-and-grow decision possible at all: because it never
  leaves my hand, waiting costs nothing but tempo, so every turn I get to ask
  "is the pile big enough yet?" rather than "will I still have a detonator?".
  Every good turn I had was built on it. **Bang Bang!** is a close second for
  the moment its face flips from *CANNOT BE PLAYED* to playable in the middle of
  my own turn because the bombs I just popped paid for it.

### (e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?

**Yes, and a real one.** The opening hand was Strike, Jumpy Dumpty, Defend ×3
against an 11-damage intent, with 3 Energy. The choice was Jumpy Dumpty + 2
Defends versus Strike + 2 Defends: plant an 8 that grows 4 a turn and pays off on
an unknown future turn, or take 6 damage now. Two things made it a decision
rather than a formality: the Bomb keyword prints "goes off **only** when Set
off", so on turn 1 I did not yet know whether I owned *any* Set off card and the
bomb might have been a dead investment; and the block maths was tight enough
(10 vs 11) that the third card genuinely had to choose a side. I took the bomb,
ate 1 damage, and found Ka-pow! on turn 2 — but the uncertainty was honest at
the time.

The caveat: it was a decision about a *card*, not yet about the kit's engine. The
first time the kit itself asked me something was fight 1 turn 2 (plant Dodoco
Cover *before* Ka-pow! so it folds into the same detonation), and the first
genuinely hard one was fight 2 turn 2 (hold at 22 for a guaranteed 34). So:
decision on turn 1, engine on turn 2, real tension on the fourth turn of the run.
That is a fast enough ramp.

## Non-blindness declaration

- Commands run in the game: only `GITS_LANE=2 python -m understudy.blindplay
  observe` and `GITS_LANE=2 python -m understudy.blindplay act "<command>"`. No
  `harness state`, no `scenario`, no `staged_turn`, no `soak`, no other
  understudy subcommand.
- Other Bash-tool calls, all of them scratch or output-shaping:
  1. `mkdir -p` plus an `echo` into a notes file under the session scratchpad
     (`…/scratchpad/klee-r14/notes.md`) — one call, at the start.
  2. `sed -n '<ranges>p'` and `grep -vE '<pattern>'` piped onto `observe` output,
     used throughout, purely to re-read one block of a screen I had already been
     shown in full and to suppress the repeated keyword glossary.
  3. `>/dev/null` on `act` calls whose JSON echo I did not need.
  4. `&&` chaining of several `act` calls in one Bash invocation.
- Tools used: **Bash** (as above) and **Write** (once, this file). No Read, no
  Grep, no Glob, no Agent, no web access.
- **Repo files read: none.**
