## Fight 1 — Nibbit [A] 45/45

**Turn 1** (HP 64, energy 3; hand Slack Water, Strike ×2, Breakwater, Feint; Nibbit intends 12).
Played Slack Water on A, then both Strikes. **Rejected:** writing Slack Water's Plan
("Apply 1 Weak to ALL enemies") on the jellyfish — the Weak would have arrived at the start of
my *next* turn, i.e. after the 12 had already landed, whereas face-up it cut the intent to 9 on
the spot. Also rejected Breakwater's Dusk Plan (6 Block), because at 64 HP a 12 was not worth an
energy. The screen and the outcome agreed exactly: 45 → 27 is 4 + 2 (the Casket answering the
Weak) + 6 + 6, and the intent icon rewrote itself from 12 to 9. **A real decision, made on the
turn:** tempo against investment, settled by the enemy's printed intent.

**Turn 2** (HP 55; hand Scout Ahead, Defend ×2, Kurage's Oath, Read the Field; Nibbit intends 6
and Block). Scout Ahead first (drew Night Watch), then Kurage's Oath face-up, then Night Watch
on the Bake-Kurage. **Rejected:** two Defends for 10 Block, five more than the turn could use;
and rejected planning Kurage's Oath, because the printed line *"A Plan is carried out before you
play anything, so it lands in whatever Block the enemy is still standing in"* told me a planned
hit would eat the Block it was about to gain, while a face-up hit lands before it. Took 0
damage: Night Watch's Dusk carry-out gave 4 Block and 1 Weak before the enemy acted, exactly as
its text says. **Decision on the turn, and the Dusk text is what made it.**

**Turn 3** (Nibbit 22/45 with Block 5, intent Empower — a safe turn; hand Defend ×2, Strike ×2,
Battle Plan). Wrote Battle Plan on the jellyfish, then both Strikes. **Rejected:** Battle Plan
face-up, which draws 1 now; the Plan draws 2 and adds +4 to the next face-up Attack next turn,
and with the enemy spending its turn buffing I had the tempo to spare. **Decision on the turn,
payoff a turn away.**

**Turn 4** (Nibbit 15/45, Strength 2, intends 14). The Plan carried out and both attacks
re-printed themselves — Strike as 10, Feint as *"Deal 9 damage. If a Plan was carried out this
turn, deal 14 damage instead."* Feint for 14, Strike for 6, dead. **Rejected:** the reverse
order, which totals the same 20. **This turn had no decision of its own — it was turn 3's plan
paying off, and that is what it should look like.** One legibility wrinkle: with the +4 rider
live, *all three* Strikes in hand printed 10, though the rider says "the next Attack", singular.
After Feint spent it the remaining Strike re-printed as 6. The face is honest after the fact but
over-promises before it.

independently of what the cards do.

**Turn 1** (Effigy asleep — a completely free turn). Wrote Kurage's Oath, wrote Feigned Retreat,

**Turn 3** (intends 23). Night Watch (Dusk Block + Weak), Ambush as a Plan (12), Flank last for
the Slow stacks. **Rejected:** Read the Field's 5 Block, traded for Ambush's planned 12 — 5 HP

**Turn 4** (Effigy 66; I hold Opening Gambit ×2, Slack Water, Defend, Battle Plan). One Gambit

*Small numeric disagreement:* on turn 2 a 10-damage Strike played third (two cards before it)

## The kit, after 9 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

- **Write a Plan or spend the card now** — the kit's spine, and a real question on nearly every
  turn, because the jellyfish charges you now and pays you next turn. Three printed things
  decided it, in this order of weight: the enemy's **intent number** (a 4 buys investment, a 23
  does not); the enemy's **Block intent** (the page tells you a carry-out lands in the Block the
  enemy is standing in, so you go face-up into a body about to block); and **its remaining HP
  against what your hand can do right now**. Made on the turn, nearly every turn.
- **Opening Gambit written, then a damage Plan written after it** — fight 5 turn 2 (24 to each of
  two bodies) and boss rounds 4 and 8 (42 to a single body, twice). **The best thing in the kit.**
  It is genuinely constructed: the doubling clause, the ALL clause, the Vulnerable landing inside
  the same packet, the Casket answering that Vulnerable, and the "carries them out in the order
  written" rule all have to be held at once, and all of them are printed. **Decided at the draft,
  cashed on the turn.**
- **Card ordering** — against Slow at the elite, and Kyouka-before-an-Attack everywhere. The
  faces re-print live, so you can see the ordering pay before you commit. Made on the turn.
- **Vanguard at 0 cost** — a draft decision that changed what every later turn was worth. A free
  Vulnerable that also pings 2 through the Casket, and it refreshes across a turn boundary.
- **Which body to kill** — the Leaf Slime before the Strangler, the Eye before Fogmog, the
  Follower before the Priest. Made on the turn, off **intent text**, not off HP bars.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Defend.** In nine fights I do not think a Defend ever changed an outcome. Twice I played one
  *only* to stack Slow; once I played one knowing it was pure waste, because a status-card intent
  and a debuff intent left the third energy with nothing at all to buy. **Night Watch and
  Breakwater — Dusk Plans that resolve before enemies act — dominate plain Block so completely
  that Defend is a dead slot.**
- **Breakwater** specifically: it is Night Watch minus the Weak plus 2 Block, and the Weak is
  worth more than 2 Block every time — it cuts the intent *and* fires the Casket for 2. I only
  played Breakwater after I had already played Night Watch.
- **Read the Field.** I never once made a decision off what its filter showed me, and its 5 Block
  competes for the same dead slot as Defend.
- **Flank's Plan** on any board where the enemies are not all attacking, which is most boards.
- **Scout Ahead's Plan.** I never wrote it: it costs a slot a *damage* Plan wants, and it only
  pays if I then write more Plans on top of it.

with everything needed to answer it printed on the cards in hand. **That is a strong first turn.**

## Debrief

### SAFE turns holding three or more Plan-capable cards

I count a card as Plan-capable only if its face carries a `Plan:` or `Dusk Plan:` clause.
**Feint does not** — it references Plans but has no Plan line — so it is excluded throughout.
There were **seven** such turns.

**1. Fight 3 (Fuzzy Wurm Crawler), turn 1.** Incoming 4, uncovered, at 51/80 — no lethal, no
threshold, the intent trivially absorbed. Plan-capable held: Slack Water, Battle Plan, Flank (3).
- *Wrote:* Battle Plan, Flank.
- *Played face-up:* Strike.
- *What competed with writing a third Plan:* **Strike, and it bought 6 immediate damage.** Slack
  Water's Plan (Weak to ALL) was the third Plan on offer and it lost to a vanilla attack, because
  against a single body a planned Weak is worth 2 Casket damage and a shaved intent, and the
  intent was 4. **Something other than Block competed, and it was a plain Strike.**

**2. Fight 4 (Leaf Slime + Slithering Strangler), turn 3.** Both intents non-damaging; Constrict 3
the only certain loss. Plan-capable held: Flank, Scout Ahead, Slack Water (3).
- *Wrote:* nothing.
- *Played face-up:* Scout Ahead, Kamisato Ayato — Kyouka, Flank.
- *What competed:* **Kyouka, and it bought +4 on every Attack for four turns.** It won outright —
  61 enemy HP left and a per-turn bleed. Separately, **two of the three Plans were unwritable
  rather than outbid**: Flank's Plan pays only against enemies that intended to attack and neither
  did, and Slack Water's Weak is worthless against two non-attacking intents. The board disabled
  two of three Plans before I chose anything.

**3. Fight 6 (elite, Bygone Effigy), turn 1.** Enemy asleep — nothing incoming at all.
Plan-capable held: Kurage's Oath, Breakwater, Feigned Retreat (3).
- *Wrote:* Kurage's Oath, Feigned Retreat.
- *Played face-up:* Strike.
- *What competed:* **Strike, and it bought 7 damage** (a 6 lifted to 7 by the two Slow stacks the
  two written Plans had just laid down). Breakwater was the third Plan and it lost **to Block
  being useless**, not to Block competing — its Dusk clause is pure Block and there was nothing to
  stop. So yes, something other than Block competed, and it was a Strike whose value the ordering
  had raised.

**4. Fight 7 (Fogmog + Eye with Teeth), turn 2.** Incoming 8 against 4 Block already standing, at
50/80 — no lethal, no threshold, the intent nearly covered. Plan-capable held: Ambush, Flank,
Night Watch, Scout Ahead (4).
- *Wrote:* Ambush (12 damage), Night Watch (Dusk Block + Weak).
- *Played face-up:* Flank.
- *What competed:* **Flank face-up, and it bought the removal of the Eye with Teeth — three status
  cards that would otherwise have entered my deck.** Flank's own Plan was the alternative and lost
  to its face-up mode, because a Plan arrives a turn late and the status cards were arriving now.
  Scout Ahead's Plan bought nothing worth an energy.

**5. Fight 8 (three Inklets), turn 1.** Incoming 12 total at 58/80 — no lethal, no threshold.
Plan-capable held: Scout Ahead, Flank, Vanguard, Vanguard (4).
- *Wrote:* Vanguard (2) then Flank, in that order, so the planned Vulnerable would land before the
  planned damage.
- *Played face-up:* Vanguard (1) on A, and Defend.
- *What competed:* **nothing did, for the Plans — Vanguard costs 0, so writing one and playing the
  other were never in competition.** The only card that competed for real energy was Defend, and
  it bought 5 Block. **This is the one turn in the run where the honest answer is "nothing other
  than Block competed."**

**6. Boss round 4.** Both Kin bodies intended Empower — nothing incoming whatsoever, at 35/80.
Plan-capable held: Scout Ahead, Opening Gambit ×2, Flank, Riptide (5) — the richest Plan hand of
the run.
- *Wrote:* Opening Gambit, then Riptide.
- *Played face-up:* Flank, for 16.
- *What competed with writing a third Plan:* **Flank face-up, and it bought the kill on Kin
  Follower A.** It was carrying +4 from Kyouka and +4 from the carried-out Battle Plan, so its
  face read 16 against a 52 HP body that the Plan packet would finish. **A second competitor was
  Riptide's own face-up mode:** it printed **17 to ALL enemies** and I turned it down in favour of
  its written 13, because written it gets doubled by Opening Gambit and then lifted 50% by the
  Vulnerable that same packet applies — 39, versus 17. That is the clearest case in the run of the
  Plan side simply winning on arithmetic the cards print for you.

**7. Boss round 8.** The Priest intended Empower — nothing incoming, at 17/80. Plan-capable held:
Riptide, Night Watch, Battle Plan, Opening Gambit (4).
- *Wrote:* Opening Gambit, then Riptide (the identical packet as round 4).
- *Played face-up:* nothing — the two Plans consumed all 3 energy.
- *What competed:* **nothing did.** Night Watch's Dusk Block had no incoming damage to stop and
  Battle Plan's draw was worth less than the 42 the packet was about to deliver. This is the
  second and last turn where the answer is "nothing".

**The pattern across the seven.** On **five** of them something other than Block competed with
writing another Plan, and on **four of those five it was a face-up attack whose damage was needed
sooner than a carry-out could arrive** (Strike, Strike, Flank, Flank); the fifth was **Kyouka**, a
multi-turn damage buff. **Block never once won a slot on a safe turn** — not in nine fights. What
actually stopped me writing more Plans was usually not competition at all but **the Plan being
disabled by the board**: Flank's attacker clause with no attackers, Slack Water's Weak against
non-attacking intents, Breakwater's and Night Watch's Block against nothing incoming. On a safe
turn, roughly half the Plan lines in a typical hand are dead letters.

### Turns where I played an Attack while a "next Attack" rider was showing

There were **five**. The rider in every case was **Battle Plan**'s carried-out clause, *"the next
Attack you play face-up this turn deals 4 additional damage."*

1. **Fight 1, round 4 — Feint (printed 14). Planned around it: yes.** I wrote Battle Plan on
   round 3 specifically so round 4 would satisfy both halves of Feint's condition. **What it
   added:** +4 on top of Feint's plan-carried-out mode, 5 → 14, which with a 6-damage Strike
   behind it was exactly lethal on a 15 HP body. The rider is what made it a one-turn kill.
2. **Fight 3, round 2 — Feint (printed 14). Planned around it: yes**, written the turn before for
   exactly this. **What it added:** +4, and the same packet's carry-out is what turned Feint's 5
   into 10. 43 → 29 out of one card.
3. **Fight 5, round 2 — Slack Water (printed 8 = 4 + 4). Planned around it: partly.** I wrote
   Battle Plan on round 1 for the two draws, not the rider; when the rider arrived I aimed it at
   the biggest Attack in hand. **What it added:** +4 damage, and because Slack Water also applies
   Weak it cut the incoming 14 to 10 in the same beat.
4. **Fight 7, round 2 — Flank (printed 12 = 8 + 4) into a 6 HP Eye with Teeth. Planned around it:
   no — this is the rider mis-spent, and I knew it as I played it. What it added: nothing.** Six
   of the twelve damage was thrown away and the +4 in particular bought exactly zero, because the
   target's entire HP bar was 6. I did it anyway because killing the Eye stopped three status
   cards and nothing else in hand could reach it. **A rider that prints on every Attack in hand
   and applies only to the first one is easy to waste like this.**
5. **Boss round 4 — Flank (printed 16 = 8 + 4 Kyouka + 4 rider). Planned around it: yes**, and
   this is the cleanest case of the five. I wrote Battle Plan on round 3 knowing round 4 would be
   safe, and the rider is what took Flank from 12 to 16 — enough, with the Plan packet, to remove
   Kin Follower A from the fight in a single turn. **What it added:** +4 that converted a
   two-turn kill into a one-turn one. Note also that I deliberately kept the rider *off* Riptide
   (which printed 17 with it) by writing Riptide as a Plan instead, so the rider fell to Flank —
   the ordering of face-up-versus-written decided which attack got it.

### A card that counts Plans against a fixed number

**I drafted none, so there is no count of mine to check against a face.** I want to be precise
about why, because two cards look at first as though they qualify:

- **Well Laid** (offered in a fight-3 card reward, **not taken**): *"2, plus 3 for each Plan the
  Bake-Kurage carried out at the start of this turn."* That is per-Plan scaling, not a threshold —
  there is no fixed number to reach, so there is nothing to count against.
- **Scout Ahead** (one of my granted six, played five or six times, **its Plan never once
  written**): *"Draw 1 card for each later Plan carried out with this one."* Again per-Plan, not a
  fixed number.

The nearest thing to a fixed count I met was **Opening Gambit**'s *"the next Plan carried out with
this one deals double damage"* — a count of exactly **one** — and I checked that against the face
four times, three of which I can show numbers for:

- **Fight 5, round 2:** wrote Opening Gambit, then Kurage's Oath (7 to ALL). My count said 7 → 14
  doubled → 21 with Vulnerable → ~23 with the Casket's answer. **Observed: 24 to each of two
  bodies.** Agreed.
- **Boss round 4:** wrote Opening Gambit, then Riptide (13 to ALL). My count said 13 → 26 doubled
  → 39 with Vulnerable, plus a Casket ping. **Observed: the Priest went 171 → 129, i.e. 42.**
  Agreed.
- **Boss round 8:** the identical packet. My count said 42 again. **Observed: 71 → 29, i.e. 42.**
  Agreed, to the point.
- **Elite round 4 and fight 7 round 3:** I held Opening Gambit with **zero** damage Plans available
  to be the "next Plan", and the clause paid nothing at all.

So my count and the face agreed every time the card had a partner. The card simply never warns you
that its second sentence needs one — and on two of five occasions it did not have one.
