# Blind seat record — KLEEMOD-KOKOMI, lane 1, round 14

- **Run seed:** `075DQ6YYQQAY`. **Ascension:** 2.
- **Character:** never named on any screen I saw. The kit is the jellyfish kit —
  it opens with a **Bake-Kurage** on the field, a starting relic **Tamakushi
  Casket**, and cards named *Kurage's Oath*, *Nereid's Ascension*, *Slack
  Water*. Every element it supplies itself is Hydro. I am recording it as the
  Kokomi kit because the brief names it, not because a screen did.

- **Actions accepted:** 120 of 120.
- **Termination reason:** the action budget. The lane's 120th `act` was

  22 → 15 (low point, after fight 5 turn 1) → Rest 39 → Rest 63 → **49/80 at
  the stop**.
- **Gold:** 99 at Neow; 231 walking into the shop; spent 229 (Festive Popper
  153, Lisa — Lightning Rose 76); **2 gold** at the stop.
- **Potions held:** Energy Potion, Swift Potion (2 of 3 slots). Neither was
  ever used — no turn got tight enough to want them, which is itself a note.
- **Deck at the end** (assembled from the map screen's last feed, floor 7, plus
  everything I picked up after it — see the finding below about that feed going

- **Relics at the end:** Tamakushi Casket (starting), Arcane Scroll (Neow),
  Bowler Hat (chest), Festive Popper (shop).

**Neow pick: Arcane Scroll** — "Obtain a random Rare Card." I took it over the
two-colorless choice because a rare from *this* kit is the fastest way for a
blind seat to see what the kit's ceiling looks like, and over Neow's Bones
because a curse muddies a first read of the deck.

**It did not do what it printed.** Arcane Scroll arrived as a *relic* sitting
in my relic list reading "Upon pickup, obtain a random Rare Card to add to your
Deck", and no rare card ever appeared. The deck the map printed after fight 1
was 11 cards — Strike ×4, Defend ×4, Kurage's Oath, Nereid's Ascension, Slack
Water — which is the starter list with nothing added, and fight 1's own piles
confirmed it (5 in hand + 6 in draw = 11). So my Neow pick bought me a relic
whose text describes a one-off effect that never fired. That is the single
clearest defect of the round.

## Fight 1 — Seapunk 44/44

**Turn 1** (HP 64, energy 3). Hand: Kurage's Oath, Defend ×2, Strike, Nereid's
Ascension. I played **Nereid's Ascension on the Bake-Kurage**, then **Kurage's
Oath on the Bake-Kurage**, then ended.

The screen had told me a Plan is "carried out at the start of your next turn"
but said nothing about whether the jellyfish holds *more than one*. Ascension's
own line ("Plan: for 2 turns, the Bake-Kurage carries out every Plan twice")
only pays off if it can sit alongside another Plan, so I spent the turn finding
out. It can: the buff bar changed to `Plan 2 — Carries out 2 Plans at the start
of your next turn, in order`, and the Bake-Kurage block listed them numbered
1. Nereid's Ascension, 2. Kurage's Oath. That is a genuinely good screen — it
answered the question I had without my having to guess.

*Rejected:* Strike + Defend + Defend (6 damage, 10 block against an 11-damage
attack). I turned it down because it makes no progress on a 44-HP body and
because I wanted the Plan question answered on the cheapest fight in the act.
Cost: I took the full 11.

**Turn 2** (HP 53). The carry-out printed:

> Bake-Kurage: Nereid's Ascension — no enemy lost HP
> Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 7 — the 7 is damage. → Seapunk lost 7 HP
> Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 7 — the 7 is damage. → Seapunk lost 7 HP

So Ascension resolves *first* and the Plan behind it in the queue is already
doubled on the same beat. That ordering is load-bearing and the screen showed
it plainly.

I played **Slack Water on Seapunk**, then **Strike**, **Strike**. *Rejected:*
three Strikes, which is the same 18 damage — Slack Water (4) plus the Casket's
2 Hydro for the Weak it applies is exactly 6, so the two lines tie on damage
and the Slack Water line also drops the enemy's 2×4 to 1×4. Free mitigation,
identical damage. That is a good card: the "which of these two 18s" question
had a real answer.

**Turn 3.** Hand was Defend ×3 + Strike ×2 against a 12-HP enemy. 6 + 6 = 12.
**No alternative existed.** No decision.

Reward: 13 gold, and a card choice of Ambush / Exposed Flank / Feint / **Shinobu
— Thundergrust**. I took Shinobu: 8 damage for 1 is above the Strike rate, and
it was the only Electro on the screen — the glossary had just told me at length
that no reaction is reachable while Hydro is the only element I can supply, so
the pick that makes the kit's headline mechanic reachable was obvious.

## Fight 2 — Corpse Slug (1) 25/25, Corpse Slug (2) 26/26

Both wore `Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it,
becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength`.

**Turn 1** (HP 49). **Shinobu — Thundergrust on Slug 1**, **Kurage's Oath on
the Bake-Kurage**, **Strike on Slug 1**. The line was chosen for the reaction:
Shinobu leaves an Electro aura, and next turn's planned Hydro hit should
consume it. *Rejected:* Kurage's Oath played now (3 to ALL) plus two Strikes —
more spread damage but no reaction, and Ravenous means spread damage is
actively bad, since bringing both slugs low at once hands the survivor a free
+4 Strength.

**Turn 2** (HP 41 — Slug 2 applied Frail instead of attacking). The carry-out
read:

> Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 7 — the 7 is damage. Inside the same beat:
> Tamakushi Casket 2. → Corpse Slug (1) lost 9 HP, Corpse Slug (2) lost 7 HP

That is the reaction working exactly as the glossary's long "hidden reaction"
paragraph promised: the Hydro plan consumed Slug 1's Electro aura, the
Electro-Charged debuff landed, the Casket fired 2 Hydro off that debuff, and
the Hydro aura re-applied inside the same beat so the screen never showed it
gone. Slug 1 came out at 2/25 wearing 4 stacks of a decay debuff.

**Named "Poison 4", not "Electro-Charged".** The glossary on that same screen
defines Electro-Charged; the body wears `Poison 4 (debuff) — At the start of its
turn, loses 4 HP, then reduce Poison by 1`. The *numbers* match the
Electro-Charged text; the *name* does not. I spent a real moment wondering
where poison had come from.

I played **Slack Water on Slug 2**, **Strike on Slug 2**, **Defend**, and
deliberately left Slug 1 alive at 2 HP. *Rejected:* killing Slug 1 with any
one card. The reason is the whole decision of the fight — Slug 1 would die at
the start of its own turn to the reaction tick *before it acted*, which makes
Slug 2 eat it and become Stunned before *its* turn. Spending a card to kill
Slug 1 myself would have bought the same Stun for a card I did not need to
spend. It worked: 41 → 41, no damage taken, Slug 2 stunned at 19 HP with the
+4 Strength it can no longer use that turn.

This was the best turn of the round. It was a real read, it came entirely off
printed text (Ravenous, Poison's tick timing, Stun), and it paid.

**Turn 3.** Slug 2 at 7. Two Strikes. No decision.

Reward: 20 gold and a card choice of Sea-Salt Prayer / Read the Field /
Cleansing Wave / **Gorou — Juuga: Forward Unto Victory**. Gorou is 18 damage
across 3 turns for 1 energy; nothing else on the screen was close. Note that
Cleansing Wave is strictly better than Read the Field on the same screen (same
cost, same 5/10 block, plus "Remove one of your debuffs") — a dominated pair
offered side by side.

## Fight 3 — Toadpole (1) 23/23, Toadpole (2) 21/21

**Turn 1** (HP 41). Hand: Nereid's Ascension, Defend ×3, Strike. I played
**Ascension on the Bake-Kurage** and **Strike on Toadpole 2**. *Rejected:*
three Defends — 15 block against a printed 7-damage attack is obvious waste.
The real question was whether to spend 2 energy on a doubling buff with **no
Plan card in hand to double**, and the printed "Lasts for 2 turns" is what made
me do it: the buff outlives the turn it resolves on, so a Plan drawn next turn
still gets doubled. That reasoning turned out to be correct but irrelevant — I
never drew a Plan card worth writing while it was up, and the 2 energy bought
nothing at all in this fight. Ascension is a card that reads like a decision
and, on a 5-card hand with one Plan card in the deck, mostly is not one.

**Turn 2** (HP 34, below half). **Slack Water on Toadpole 2** (4 + Casket 2 =
exactly enough to set up), **Shinobu — Thundergrust on Toadpole 2** (13, because
below half), **Gorou**. Toadpole 2 died. *Rejected:* Shinobu + Strike on the
same target, which also kills it but wastes 4 damage; Slack Water first is 2
damage cheaper to the same end. *Also rejected:* leaving Toadpole 2 at 2 HP for
Gorou's end-of-turn tick to finish — Gorou picks "a random enemy", and a coin
flip on whether the buffing enemy gets another turn is not a trade I wanted at
34 HP.

**Turn 3.** One Toadpole at 17, Gorou ticking 6 at end of turn. Strike, Strike,
Defend. *Rejected:* a third attack for the outright kill — with a single enemy
left, Gorou's "random" target is deterministic, so the Defend was free
insurance in case it was not. Barely a decision.

Reward: 15 gold and Moon / Rally / Salt Line / **Sayu — Naptime**. I took Sayu:
0-cost 4 block is efficient every single draw, where Salt Line's 8 is once.
Sayu's rider — "draw 2 next turn if you play no Attacks this turn" — is
anti-synergy with everything else in the deck and I never once satisfied it in
the four fights I held the card.

## Fight 4 — Living Fog 80/80

**Turn 1** (HP 25). **Sayu — Naptime** (free 4 block), **Shinobu — Thundergrust**
(13, below half), **Kurage's Oath on the Bake-Kurage**, **Gorou**. *Rejected:*
Strike for 6 now instead of writing the Oath Plan for 7 later — I chose the Plan
because the Electro aura Shinobu leaves should let next turn's planned Hydro hit
react, the way it had in fight 2.

**It did not react.** The carry-out printed:

> Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 7 — the 7 is damage. → Living Fog lost 7 HP

Flat 7. No Casket line, no debuff on the body, and the Fog was afterwards
wearing `Hydro Aura 1`. The arithmetic is exact — 80 − 13 (Shinobu) − 6 (Gorou)
− 7 (Oath) = 54, which is what the screen showed — so nothing extra happened. I
cannot tell from the screens whether the Electro aura had quietly expired
between my playing Shinobu and the plan resolving, or whether the reaction
simply did not fire; the aura line is only ever printed for the aura currently
worn, so an aura that lapses leaves no trace. **This is the thing I could least
understand in the round**, and it is the same two-card sequence that reacted in
fight 2.

**The Fog's printed attack never landed, twice.** Round 1 its intent read
"Attack for 8" *and also* "Malicious (CardDebuff)". I had 4 block. I took 0
damage and gained the Smoggy affliction. Round 2 its intent read "Attack for 5"
*and also* "Summon". I had 0 block. I took 0 damage and it summoned a Gas Bomb.
Both times the screen printed a damage number that did not happen. I saw the
same shape again on the Gremlin Merc in fight 5 and on the Terror Eel in the
elite: **every enemy turn where the intent listed an attack number *and* a
second intent, the attack did not land.** I planned two turns of blocking
around numbers that were never going to arrive.

**Turn 2** (HP 25, Smoggy: only 1 Skill per turn). **Slack Water**, **Deep
Current**, **Strike**, all on the Fog — 18 plus Gorou's 6. *Rejected:* Defend +
two attacks. At 25 HP with 54 enemy HP left and a summon coming, blocking 5 of
a 5-damage hit loses the race; I took the aggressive line and (by accident of
the intent bug above) paid nothing for it.

**Turn 3.** The Gas Bomb was a 7-HP minion with `Death Blow — it will attack
you for 8 damage before being destroyed`, alongside the Fog's 8. 16 incoming at
25 HP. **Strike, Strike on the Gas Bomb** (12 into 7 — deliberate overkill,
because the alternative was a coin flip) and **Defend**. *Rejected:* Strike the
Bomb once to 1 HP and let Gorou's random 6 finish it, freeing a card for the
Fog. Half the time that leaves an 8-damage death blow live at 25 HP. Not at
that HP. *Also rejected:* Nereid's Ascension — 2 energy of setup in a fight I
was losing on tempo. This was a real decision and the printed Death Blow text
is what made it legible.

**Turn 4.** Fog at 24, me at 22 and below half. Shinobu 13 + Slack Water (4 +
Casket 2) + Strike 6 = 25. Exact lethal, and the Casket's 2 was load-bearing in
getting there. Satisfying, but forced.

Reward: 18 gold, Energy Potion, and Change of Plans / Stolen Chapter / Ambush /
**Shinobu — Sanctifying Ring**. The Ring — 1 energy, lose 3 HP, then 3 turns of
5 Electro to ALL *and* 5 Block — is far and away the strongest card I was
offered all round, and at 22 HP the block half decided it.

## Fight 5 — Gremlin Merc 47/47 (Surprise, Thievery 20)

**Turn 1** (HP 22, 14 incoming). **Sanctifying Ring**, **Shinobu —
Thundergrust** (13), **Defend**. *Rejected:* Ring + two Defends, which is 15
block against 14 and takes literally nothing — I turned down the free turn
because 5 damage a turn against 47 HP is nine turns of standing there, and I do
not have nine turns at 22 HP. That is a good decision to be handed: the Ring
gives you both halves of the trade in one card and then makes you choose how
much of the block half to actually keep.

**Turn 2** (HP 15). **Sayu** (4 block), **Kurage's Oath played now** (3 to all),
**Strike**, **Gorou**. The Merc was wearing an Electro aura from Shinobu, and I
half-expected the Hydro Oath to react. It did not, and **here the printed text
does explain it**: Kurage's Oath carries the rider *"Hydro on the carry-out —
Its own hit applies no aura; the Bake-Kurage carries out the Plan as a Hydro
hit, which does."* So the card is titled `[Hydro]` and its own hit is not Hydro
at all. That is legible only if you read a four-line rider under a card you have
played a dozen times, and it directly contradicts the tag in the title.

**Turn 3** (HP 15, Weak 2 on me). Merc at 9. I played **Deep Current** first
specifically to test the reaction against the same Electro aura with a card
whose rider says it *does* apply its element. It fired: 9 → 3, i.e. 4 (Deep
Current, reduced by my Weak — the screen helpfully prints the reduced number on
the card face) + 2 Casket, and `Poison 4` landed. So the rule is consistent
after all *between those two cards*; Kurage's Oath is the exception by its own
text. Then **Strike** for the kill.

The Merc's `Surprise 1 — Something is off about this creature...` resolved into
two Stunned gremlins, one of them holding 40 stolen gold. **Slack Water on the
Fat Gremlin** (3 + Casket 2 = 5) — *rejected:* Strike for 4, which is less, and
which is the second time in the round that Slack Water's debuff-plus-relic beats
a raw Strike. Ring + Gorou finished the Fat Gremlin; **Deep Current** finished
the Sneaky one (4 + reaction 2 ≥ 5).

Reward: 20 gold + 40 gold stolen back + Swift Potion + a second **Deep Current**.
I took the duplicate over Chevreuse's 10-to-all-for-2 because 6-to-all-for-1 is
the better rate and Deep Current is the card that actually detonates my own
Electro auras.

## Elite (in progress at the stop) — Terror Eel 140/140

`Shriek 70 — The first time Terror Eel's HP reaches 70 or below, it becomes
Stunned.`

**Turn 1** (HP 63; Festive Popper had already taken it to 131). **Lisa —
Lightning Rose**, **Gorou — Juuga**, **Shinobu — Sanctifying Ring**: all three
end-of-turn engines in one turn, 3 energy, 3 HP. *Rejected:* Defend +
Thundergrust + one engine, the tempo line. Against a 140-HP body with a
16-damage attack, three stacked engines is 16 printed damage a turn plus 5
block a turn, and the Eel gives you the turns to set it up.

The turn resolved for **28**, not the 16 the three cards print. The extra 12 is
the Casket chaining off the Vulnerable and the Electro-Charged debuffs — every
debuff applied is 2 Hydro, which reacts with the Electro aura the same engines
keep laying down, which is another debuff, which is another 2. I could not
predict this number from the cards, and I could not read it off the screen
afterwards either; I only know it happened because 131 − 103 = 28.

**Turn 2.** **Deep Current**, **Strike+** (9), **Defend**. *Rejected:* Nereid's
Ascension for the doubling, which does nothing when nothing in hand is a Plan.
The round resolved for **54 damage** — 103 → 49 — and the `Poison` stack on the
Eel had climbed from 3 to 10.

**Turn 3** was my 120th action (`end turn`). The engines ticked again: Eel at
**11/140**, `Poison 13`, and it will die to its own tick before acting. I also
picked up `Vulnerable 99 (debuff) — Receive 50% more damage from Attacks for 99
turns` from the Eel's debuff intent, which is either a very long-lived debuff or
a placeholder duration.

**The Electro-Charged decay is going the wrong way.** The glossary says the
reacted enemy "loses 4 HP at the start of its turn, **1 less each turn**". The
Eel's stack went 3 → 10 → 13 across three turns while it was ticking. Whatever
that power is, it stacks upward and the printed rule says it decays. Two
different fights, same mismatch.

I stopped here on the budget, with the elite effectively won and not collected.

## The kit, after 5 fights (and 3 turns of a 6th)

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Three shapes, and all three are good:

1. **Write it on the jellyfish, or play it now.** Kurage's Oath is 3-to-all now
   or 7-to-all a turn later; Ambush is 5 now or 12 later. That is a clean tempo
   loan, it is priced steeply enough to be a real question, and the answer
   changed with my HP and with what the enemy's intent said. Fight 4 turn 1 was
   exactly this question and I got it wrong for a reason I still cannot see.
2. **Which enemy dies, and when.** Fight 2's Ravenous slugs was the best turn of
   the round: the right play was to *not* kill something, and every fact I
   needed was on the screen. Fight 4's Gas Bomb was the same shape under time
   pressure.
3. **How much of the Ring's block to keep.** Sanctifying Ring hands you damage
   and block in one card and then the rest of the turn is deciding how much of
   the block half to convert back into tempo. That is a good card.

The relic underneath all of it — Tamakushi Casket, 2 Hydro per debuff applied —
is quietly the best-designed thing here. It is why Slack Water beats a Strike
twice over, it is why Weak and Vulnerable are damage and not just mitigation,
and it is what turns the reaction system from a curiosity into a chain.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Nereid's Ascension is the dud.** 2 energy, exhausts, and doubles Plans — in
  a deck with two Plan cards. I played it twice. Once it doubled a 7 into 14 and
  once it did nothing at all, and both times it cost me the turn's tempo. It is
  a card that reads like the kit's payoff and behaves like a tax, because the
  deck has no Plan density to double.
- **Every kill turn.** Turn 3 of fight 1, turn 3 of fight 2, turn 3 of fight 3
  — hand of Defends and Strikes, enemy at exactly-in-range, nothing to decide.
  Three of my sixteen combat turns had no rejected alternative at all.
- **Sayu — Naptime's rider.** "Draw 2 if you play no Attacks this turn" never
  once fired in four fights, because this deck plays attacks. It is a 0-cost
  4-block card with a dead line printed on it.
- **Defend.** Standard, and against the enemies I fought — whose printed attack
  numbers kept not landing — I could rarely tell whether blocking had done
  anything.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

Five things, in order of how much they cost me:

1. **Arcane Scroll never gave the rare card it promises.** Taken at Neow,
   present in the relic list all run, deck stayed at 11 starter cards.
2. **Printed attack numbers on dual intents never land.** Living Fog "Attack 8
   + CardDebuff" → 0 damage. Living Fog "Attack 5 + Summon" → 0 damage. Gremlin
   Merc "6×2 + Debuff" → 0 damage through 9 block. Terror Eel "3×3 + Empower" →
   0 damage. Four for four. I blocked around numbers that were never coming.
3. **Electro-Charged prints as "Poison", and stacks upward.** The glossary
   defines Electro-Charged and says it decays 1 per turn; the body wears a
   debuff called Poison whose stack went 3 → 10 → 13 while the engines ran.
   Name wrong, direction wrong.
4. **A [Hydro] card whose own hit is not Hydro.** Kurage's Oath carries the tag
   in its title and a rider four lines down saying its own hit applies no aura.
   I built a turn on the tag before I found the rider.
5. **The same sequence reacted in fight 2 and did not in fight 4.**
   Shinobu-then-planned-Oath produced Electro-Charged + a Casket proc on a
   Corpse Slug and a flat 7 on the Living Fog. If the difference is aura
   expiry, no screen said so — an aura that has lapsed prints as nothing at all,
   which is indistinguishable from a reaction that failed.

Smaller: the deck panel on the map stopped updating after floor 7 and printed
its own disclaimer about it, so for the last five floors I was tracking my own
deck by hand; and the Light Door never told me which two cards it upgraded — I
found Strike+ by drawing it.

Also, the **Companion** glossary entry talks about "Furina's stage" and a front
member being performed and sent to the back. Nothing on any screen in this run
had a stage or a member order, and the Companion cards I played (Gorou, Sayu,
Shinobu ×2, Lisa) all just resolved. That entry appears to be describing a
different character's kit.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Nereid's Ascension.** Two energy to double Plans I do not have.

Happiest: **Shinobu — Sanctifying Ring.** 1 energy and 3 HP for 15 damage to
everything and 15 block across three turns, and it is the card that turned a
22-HP death march into a won elite. **Slack Water** deserves an honourable
mention for being the card that made me think hardest per energy spent, purely
because of the Casket.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

Yes, and a good one. Five cards, 3 energy, one enemy telegraphing 11. The
choice was between a clean defensive turn (Strike + 2 Defends), a value turn
(plan the Oath, hold up 10 block), and the greedy setup I took (Ascension +
Oath, take the 11 on the chin to find out whether the jellyfish holds two
Plans). Three distinguishable lines with different costs on turn one is more
than most decks manage. The kit's opening question — *when do I stop playing
cards and start writing them down* — is asked immediately and asked well.

The honest counterweight: that question is asked well and then not asked often
enough. Across 16 combat turns I wrote a Plan on 5 of them, because the deck
holds two Plan cards and a starter pile of vanilla Strikes and Defends. The
best turns of this round were all jellyfish turns. Most turns were not
jellyfish turns.

## Non-blindness declaration

Commands run outside the two allowed ones:

- `mkdir -p <scratchpad>` and one `echo ... >> notes.md` into the scratchpad
  directory, to hold the Arcane Scroll finding while I kept playing.
- `GITS_LANE=1 python -m understudy.blindplay observe` piped through `sed -n`
  and `grep -E` on many calls, purely to re-read one block of the screen the
  bridge had already printed to me (hand, enemy block, buff list). No other
  understudy subcommand was ever run — no `harness state`, no `scenario`, no
  `staged_turn`, no `soak`, no `session`.
- Several `act` calls were chained with `&&` and had their JSON receipt sent to
  `/dev/null` to keep the transcript readable; the acts themselves are ordinary
  allowed commands and all 120 are counted by the bridge.

Tools used: **Bash** (for everything above and for every `observe`/`act`), and
**Write**, once, for this file.

**Repo files read: none.**
