## Fight 1 — Seapunk [A], 46 HP

**Turn 1 (the turn-one play):** Jumpy Dumpty on A (Bomb 8) → Ka-pow! (set off:
8 + 4 = 12, and the Bomb's rider dropped a Mine 3) → Strike → Strike. 24
damage, 46 → 22.
*Rejected:* holding Ka-pow! (it prints Retain) to let the Bomb grow to 12 and
detonate next turn for 16. I rejected it because 24 now beats 18 now + 4 later,
and because setting off immediately seeds the Mine, which is free damage on the
enemy's own turn. Also rejected Defend (5 Block against an 11) — at 62/62 the
race is worth more than five points of prevention. **This turn had a real
decision, and it was on the turn.**

**Turn 2:** Strike, Strike, Defend. 46 → 19 (the Mine had already gone off for
3 during the enemy's turn; I took the full 11 anyway, which matches the printed
"the enemy's hit lands in full unless the Mine kills").
*Rejected:* nothing meaningful — the hand was three Defends and two Strikes,
with no Klee card in it. **This turn presented no decision.** It was a vanilla
Ironclad turn wearing a Klee sticker.

**Turn 3:** enemy at 7, telegraphing Empower + Defensive (Block). Jumpy Dumpty
(Bomb 8) → Ka-pow! (8 + 4 = 12), lethal before it could block.
*Rejected:* Ka-pow! alone (4, not lethal) and two Defends (would have let it
block and drag the fight). This is the plan paying off, not a dead turn: the
draft-level shape (placer + a free set-off that Retains) is what made an
otherwise dead 7-HP turn into an exact kill.

**Spark at the end of the fight:** ~4. **Spent on: nothing.** Fireworks Show
was in the deck the whole fight and was never castable at the moment it would
have mattered.

**Bomb sat with nothing to set it off:** never this fight — Ka-pow! is 0 cost

## Fight 2 — Sludge Spinner [A], 39 HP

**Opening hand:** Jumpy Dumpty, Tinder Toss, Defend, Chain Fuse, Pocket
Fireworks. Energy 3, Spark 1. Intent: Attack 8 + a Debuff.

**Turn 1 (the turn-one play):** Jumpy Dumpty (Bomb 8) → Chain Fuse (Bomb 14) →
Tinder Toss (1 Spark, 0 energy) → Pocket Fireworks. 39 → 5, i.e. **34 damage on
turn one.**
*Rejected:* Defend, and rejected banking the Bomb another turn. The reason the
turn was interesting is that Chain Fuse is a *multiplier on a thing that only
exists if you spent an earlier card placing it*, so the ordering (place, grow,
then set off, then face damage) is a genuine sequencing puzzle, and the first
time I ran it the numbers taught me something: 39 → 14 was 25 damage, which is
Bomb 14 + 4 + **Mine 3** + 4. **Tinder Toss sets off twice, and the second
set-off caught the Mine that the first set-off had just created.** Nothing on
the card face told me that would happen; the arithmetic did. That is the single
most satisfying thing I learned all run.

**Turn 2:** Fish-Flavored Bait (Bomb 4) → Ka-pow! (4 + 3), lethal at 5 HP.
*Rejected:* Strike + Ka-pow!, which was also exactly lethal (7 ≥ 5) — I took the
Bait line for the margin. **A decision, but a trivial one.**

**Where the screen and the outcome agreed, notably:** I was carrying Weak 1
this turn and the card faces had *re-printed themselves* — Strike read "Deal 4
damage" instead of 6, Ka-pow! read 3 instead of 4, Fish-Flavored Bait read 3
instead of the 4 it was bought as. That is excellent: the debuff was applied to
the number I was reading, not left for me to compute.

**Spark at the end:** 2 (start 1, −1 Tinder Toss, +2 from two Bombs going off).
**Spent on:** Tinder Toss ×1.

**Bomb sat with nothing to set it off:** never.

## The kit, after 4 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

- *On the turn, elite turn 2:* **detonate 32 now, or bank to 40 and detonate
  through a Vulnerable next turn.** This is the kit's signature decision and it
  is a good one, because the Bomb is a visible, growing number that the screen
  prints for me (`Bomb 32 ... Bomb sizes here: 18 / 14`), and the cost of
  waiting is a specific telegraphed enemy hit. Banking traded ~11 HP for ~30
  damage and a Stun.
- *On the turn, fight 4 turn 2:* **which single Skill to play under Smoggy.**
  Chain Fuse vs Dodoco Cover was a 24-point swing and decided whether the enemy
  lived. Enemy debuffs that restrict card types interact unusually well with
  this kit because its fuel and its payoff sit in different card types.
- *On the turn, elite turn 5:* **spend the Strength Potion on an exactly-lethal
  count, or trust the arithmetic.** Trading a consumable for certainty against
  a lethal counter-hit.
- *Earlier in the fight:* every detonation turn was really decided one or two
  turns before it, by how much fuel I had chosen to stack. The kit's decisions
  live at a longer range than a normal deck's, which I liked.
- *At the draft:* **Big Badda Boom over Alice's Recipe** decided fight 4 (a
  doubler on a number I already knew how to make large, versus a slow engine).
  **Pop! over a second Fish-Flavored Bait** — free fuel over priced fuel —
  changed what a 3-energy turn could contain. And **Fish-Flavored Bait in the
  shop over everything else** was the choice that stopped my set-off cards
  from being blanks.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- The 4 Strikes and 4 Defends are automatic and they are a big share of every
  hand. Fight 1 turn 2 was literally *Strike, Strike, Defend* with no thought
  in it. Every hand that came up starter-heavy was a non-turn.
- **Fireworks Show was never worth playing and I never played it, in four
  fights.** It was my Neow pick. It costs 2 Sparks to set off ALL enemies —
  but I fought one enemy in three of four fights, and Tinder Toss sets off for
  **one** Spark *and* adds 8 face damage, and Ka-pow! sets off for **zero** of
  anything. Fireworks Show has no niche in Act 1 that another card does not
  cover more cheaply. It printed `CANNOT BE PLAYED` at me four separate times
  (twice for "no enemy is holding a Bomb", twice for "you have 1 Spark, and
  this costs 2").
- **The Spark economy did not bind.** I ended fights with 4, 5, 2 and ~3 Sparks
  unspent. Pounding Surprise pays a Spark per Bomb going off, and my whole plan
  is to make Bombs go off, so Sparks arrive faster than the Spark-priced cards
  can spend them. The consequence is that a "Spark price" mostly reads as
  **"free"**, not as a cost — which is why Tinder Toss and Dig In are such
  obvious picks and why the 2-Spark cards feel *mispriced upward* rather than
  expensive. The one turn where Sparks genuinely bound was fight 4 turn 1,
  where I held Fireworks Show at 1 Spark and could not cast it.
- Mercury Hourglass and the Tea Set's +2 energy both did nothing visible: on
  turn 1 of fight 4 I ran out of *cards* with a spare energy in hand.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

- **"Set off a random enemy and deal 4 damage to it, twice"** — I could not
  tell from the face whether "twice" repeats the whole clause (set off + 4) or
  only the damage. It repeats the whole clause, and against one enemy the
  second set-off catches Mines the first one just created. I only learned this
  from arithmetic (39 → 14 is 25, which only decomposes as 14 + 4 + 3 + 4).
  Worth printing as two lines.
- **Fireworks Show's `CANNOT BE PLAYED: no enemy is holding a Bomb` vs Ka-pow!'s
  `No Bomb on the field: this card is only its own line`** — the same board
  state produces a hard refusal for one card and a soft note for the other. It
  is correct (one card is *only* a set-off), but the asymmetry took a moment.
- **Hexerei**, seen once on Razor — Lightning Fang: *"A Companion card that
  prints the word, and Klee herself. Only the ones marked Klee's own pay: 1
  Spark a play, up to 3."* I read this four times and still cannot say what it
  costs me or what "up to 3" caps. I did not take the card, partly for that
  reason.
- **The Mine's timing.** The Mine reads as though it protects you ("goes off
  before its enemy's hit"), but the same sentence says the hit "lands in full
  unless the Mine kills", so in practice it is just chip damage on the enemy's
  turn. I expected some mitigation the first time and got none. That is the
  text being honest and my reading being hopeful, but the sentence invites the
  hopeful reading.
- **What worked extremely well:** the enemy's Bomb readout
  (`Bomb 40 ... Bomb sizes here: 22 / 18, growing each turn, and dropping Mine 3
  on ALL enemies when they go off. None goes off by itself.`) is the single
  best piece of text in the kit. And card faces re-print themselves under Weak
  (Strike showing "Deal 4 damage") so I never had to compute a debuff by hand.
  The `CANNOT BE PLAYED:` lines saved me at least two wasted actions.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted:** Fireworks Show (drawn repeatedly, played zero times — see
  (b)). Runner-up, Defend: 5 Block is not a real answer to a 16 or a 22, and
  the kit's actual defence turned out to be killing things a turn earlier.
- **Happiest to draw:** **Chain Fuse.** It is the card that makes the Bomb
  number *mine* rather than the clock's — the difference between a stack that
  grows 4 a turn on its own and one I can push to lethal on the turn I choose.
  Big Badda Boom is the bigger card, but Chain Fuse is the one whose arrival in
  hand actually changed what I did.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a genuine one.** The opening hand was Jumpy Dumpty, Strike, Ka-pow!,
Strike, Defend, with three energy against a telegraphed 11. Jumpy Dumpty places
a Bomb 8; Ka-pow! costs **0** and sets it off; Ka-pow! also **Retains**, so
holding it costs nothing. That is exactly the shape of a real choice — detonate
now for 24 total, or hold the free Retaining set-off and detonate a 12-point
Bomb next turn — and the answer was not obvious before I did the arithmetic.
The kit's core loop is legible from the first hand: one card makes a number,
another card cashes it, and the number grows if you wait. That is a very good
turn one. The problem is not the first turn; it is that fight 1 turn 2 was
Strike/Strike/Defend, because the starter cards outnumber the Klee cards.

### Refusals encountered (both are findings)

1. `confirm` at the Room Full of Cheese two-card selection → *"there is nothing
   waiting to be confirmed."* The selection screen had printed **"Confirm is not
   available."** while nothing was picked, and then auto-resolved the moment the
   second card was toggled, so the confirm step I was told to expect never
   existed. The identical screen at the Smith **did** require `confirm` and
   printed **"Confirm is available."** once a card was picked. Same verb, two
   different contracts, one of them undiscoverable until you trip on it.
2. `play "Tinder Toss" on "A"` → *"'Tinder Toss' is played on you, not on an
   enemy, so it takes no `on \"A\"`."* Correct and clearly worded — the card
   picks its own random target — but the card face says "Set off a **random
   enemy**", which reads as targeting something, and it sits in a deck where
   every other set-off card is aimed. The refusal message told me the working
   form, so it cost one action and nothing else.
