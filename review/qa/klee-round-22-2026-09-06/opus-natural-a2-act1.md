## Fight 1 — Sludge Spinner [A], 38 HP

**Opening hand:** Jumpy Dumpty, Defend ×2, Strike ×2. Spark 1 at the bell.

**Turn 1** (intent: Attack 8 *and also* Debuff). Played **Jumpy Dumpty on A**
("Innate. Place a Bomb 8. When it goes off, place a Mine 3 on ALL enemies"),
then **Strike, Strike**.
*Rejected:* Jumpy + Strike + Defend. With 38 HP opposite and 8 incoming, 6 extra
damage beat 5 Block. This was a decision, but a shallow one — the only question
was how to spend two leftover energy, because Jumpy Dumpty is Innate and costs
1, so turn one plays itself.

**Turn 2.** Bomb had grown 8 → 12 ("grows 4 a turn"). Drew **Ka-pow!** — "cost 0,
attack. Retain. Set off. Deal 3 damage." Played **Ka-pow! on A** (12 + 3 = 15,
A: 26 → 11, Spark 1 → 2, and the Jumpy rider dropped **Mine 3** on A), then
**Strike, Strike** (A → 3), then **Defend**.
*Rejected:* holding Ka-pow (it Retains) to let the Bomb reach 16. I turned it
down because A at 26 was already inside a two-source kill and the Mine's
"goes off before this enemy's hit" would close the last 3.
It did: A died to its own Mine on its turn. **Fight over, 2 turns, 8 HP lost.**

- **Spark at the end:** 2 on screen after Ka-pow (+1 more owed from the Mine
  that killed A, which I never saw because combat ended). **Spent on:** nothing —
  I owned no Spark-priced card yet.
- **Bomb with no way to set it off:** none.

**Verdict:** the fight's one real decision was made on turn 2 (spend the Bomb at
12 or bank it), and the kill came from a rider I had placed a turn earlier. That
is a plan paying off, not a dead turn.

## Fight 2 — Toadpole (1) [A] 22 HP, Toadpole (2) [B] 21 HP

Red Mask opened with Weak 1 on both.

**Opening hand:** Jumpy Dumpty, Defend ×2, Strike ×2 — **identical to Fight 1.**

**Turn 1.** **Jumpy Dumpty on A**, then **Strike, Strike on A** (A → 10).
*Rejected:* splitting the Strikes onto B, and bombing B instead. I bombed the
higher-HP body because a Bomb is the only burst I have and B was already inside
Strike range. No Defends: B's whole telegraph was 5 damage.

**Turn 2** — the best turn of the round, and the one where the kit's parts
visibly interlocked. A had Thorns 2 and 10 HP under a Bomb 12; B was untouched
at 21.
1. **Ka-pow! on A** — Set off 12, A dies. The rider dropped **Mine 3 on B**
   (the printed line is "place a Mine 3 on ALL enemies"), Spark 1 → 2.
   Set off is explicitly *not* an Attack, so A's Thorns 2 never fired on the
   12 — a real, printed, non-obvious edge I used deliberately.
2. **Bang Bang! on B** — "cost 2 Sparks … Set off. Deal 8 damage. Place a Bomb 4."
   The 2 Sparks I had were *made by step 1*. Set off ate B's Mine 3, the 8
   landed, B: 21 → 10, Spark back to 1 from the Mine.
3. **Strike, Strike on B** → dead. **Fight over, 2 turns, 5 HP lost.**

*Rejected:* Ka-pow on A then Strikes into B without Bang Bang — that leaves B
at 9 and gives it a turn. The chain only existed because the first detonation
paid for the second card, which is the most interesting thing the kit did all
round.

- **Spark at the end:** 1. **Spent on:** Bang Bang! (2), refunded 1 by the Mine.
- **Bomb with no way to set it off:** none.

## Fight 3 — Corpse Slug (1) [A] 27 HP, Corpse Slug (2) [B] 25 HP

Both printed **Ravenous 4** — "When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats
it, becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength." That text reframed the whole
fight: killing one is a cost, and the Bomb line "if this enemy dies with it
still on, it moves to a survivor" is the counter-play.

**Opening hand:** Jumpy Dumpty, Defend ×3, Strike ×1 — Jumpy again, as always.

**Turn 1.** **Jumpy Dumpty on A**, **Strike A**, **Defend**.
*Rejected:* second Defend over the Strike; the telegraph was a Debuff plus 2×2,
so Block was near-worthless and I wanted A inside bomb range.

**Turn 2 — a bomb sat with no way to set it off.** A carried **Bomb 16** and my
hand was Defend ×2, Strike ×3. No Ka-pow, no Bang Bang, no Quick Fuse.
*What I did instead:* three Strikes into B (25 → 7), which also tripped
Ornamental Fan for 4 Block. That was the best available line but it was not a
decision — with no detonator, the hand had exactly one shape.

**Turn 3 — the round's best draft payoff.** A: 21 HP, Bomb 16. B: 7 HP.
I needed 21 and Bomb 16 + Ka-pow 4 = 20, one short.
1. **Dodoco Cover on A** (the card I had drafted two rewards earlier: "Place a
   Bomb 4. Gain 5 Block") — A now carried 16 *and* 4.
2. **Ka-pow! on A** — Set off fires "oldest first": 16, then 4, then the card's
   own 4. A died exactly. The rider dropped Mines on both bodies, and A's own
   Mine *moved to B* on death, so B ended holding **Mine 6 (3 / 3, 2 Mines)**.
   Spark 1 → 3.
3. B ate A: Stunned, Strength 4. **Bang Bang! on B** — set off Mine 6, then 8
   damage, B dead from 7. **Fight over, 3 turns, 10 HP lost.**

*Rejected:* the obvious line, Ka-pow now for 16+4 = 20 leaving A at 1 and handing
the survivor a free +4-Strength turn. The 1-damage gap was closed by a card I
drafted, not by a card I drew — that is the draft making the turn, and it is the
clearest "real choice" of the round.

- **Spark at the end:** 3 before the killing card. **Spent on:** Bang Bang! (2).
- **Bomb with no way to set it off:** turn 2 (Bomb 16, five vanilla cards).

## Fight 4 — Sewer Clam [A], 56 HP, Block 8, Plating 8

The hard fight, and the one that shows where the kit's floor is. Plating: "At
the end of your turn, gain 8 Block. Plating is reduced by 1 at the start of your
turn." Set off's own text says **"Block stops them"** — so an armoured enemy is
a hard counter to the entire Bomb engine.

**Opening hand:** Jumpy Dumpty, Defend ×2, Strike ×2. **Turn one:** Jumpy Dumpty
on A, then **Defend, Defend**.
*Rejected:* two Strikes. 12 raw into 8 Block nets 4; the Bomb grows 4 a turn for
free. Declining to attack because the target's armour regenerates is a genuine
turn-one decision, and it was the first one the round gave me that Jumpy Dumpty
did not make for me.

**Turn 2 — a bomb sat with no way to set it off, and this is the sharper case.**
A carried Bomb 12. My hand *held* a detonator, **Bang Bang!**, and the screen
printed `CANNOT BE PLAYED: you have 1 Spark, and this costs 2`. Sparks come from
detonations; a fight in which I have not yet detonated cannot afford the card
that detonates. That loop is the single most frustrating thing in the kit.
*What I did instead:* Strike ×2 into 8 Block for 4 net (intent was Empower, so
Block was dead weight) and ended with a spare energy.

**Turn 3.** A: 52 HP, Block 7, Bomb 16, Strength 4, telegraphing 14. I held
Strike, Ka-pow!, Quick Fuse (1 Spark), Defend.
Ordered **Strike → Ka-pow! → Quick Fuse → Defend**: the Strike chews Block 7 down
to 1 so the Bomb is not eaten by armour, Ka-pow sets off 16 (15 through) + 4, the
rider drops Mine 3, Quick Fuse grows it 3 and sets it off for 6 on a bare body.
52 → 27. Then Defend; took 9.
*Rejected:* Quick Fuse first (bomb 16 → 19 but 7 of it eaten by Block). I checked
both orders and they conserve to the same total, which is worth saying plainly:
**against Block the ordering does not matter, only the total.** The decision that
does matter is Strike-before-Set-off, and the screen tells you so.

**Turn 4** (intent Empower, so no Block needed): **Dodoco Cover** (Bomb 4 + 5
Block) then **Strike ×2** into Block 6 for 6 net. Planting while it buffs.

**Turn 5 — the kill, and the clearest decision the kit produced.** A: 21 HP,
Block 5, Bomb 8, Strength 8, telegraphing 18 at my 30 HP.
**Jumpy Dumpty on A** (a *second* Bomb 8 on the same body — 8 and 8), then
**Ka-pow!**: Set off resolved oldest-first, 8 into Block 5 (3 through) then 8
in full, then the card's 4 → A: 21 → **6, exactly as I had counted**.
Then **Strike** → dead. **Fight over, 5 turns, 23 HP lost.**
*Rejected:* Ka-pow on the single Bomb 8 and Defending. Stacking a second placer
so the first hit eats the armour and the second lands clean is the fight's real
answer, and I had to work it out from the printed order of "oldest first" plus
"Block stops them". Nothing on the screen told me to do it.

- **Spark at the end:** 4 after the double detonation. **Spent on:** Quick Fuse (1)
  on turn 3. Bang Bang! was in hand on turn 2 and unaffordable.
- **Bombs with no way to set them off:** turn 2 (Bomb 12, detonator in hand but
  Spark-locked).

## Fight 5 — Haunted Ship [A], 63 HP

Telegraph: Debuff *and* "give you 5 Status cards". Ended up with Weak 3 on me
and Dazed in the deck.

**Opening hand:** Jumpy Dumpty, Strike, Dodoco Cover, Defend ×2.
**Turn one:** Jumpy Dumpty on A, **Dodoco Cover on A**, **Strike**. Two bombs
planted (8 and 4) on turn one, which is the shape I had been drafting toward.
*Rejected:* Defends — the whole telegraph was Debuff plus Status.

**Turn 2 — a bomb sat with no way to set it off** (Bomb 20, split 12 / 8, hand
was Clumsy, Strike, Explosives Workshop+, Defend ×2). *Instead:* played
**Explosives Workshop+** ("your Bombs grow by 2 more") and both Defends, taking
3 of 13. *Rejected:* the Weak-reduced 4-damage Strike; 4 into 57 HP is noise
next to +4 growth a turn compounding across two bombs.

**Turn 3 — the number the kit exists to produce.** Bomb **32** (18 / 14). The
Workshop was visibly working: the screen's own line, "Bomb sizes here: 18 / 14",
against 12 / 8 the turn before.
**Ka-pow!** → 32 through a bare body, +3, ship 57 → 22, Spark 1 → 3, Mine 3
dropped. **Bang Bang!** (paid for by that detonation) → Mine 3 + 6 → 13, and it
replanted a Bomb 4. **Strike ×3** (Weak: 4 each) → **1 HP.** Ornamental Fan gave
4 Block off the five attacks; took 8.
*Rejected, and I worked it properly:* holding Ka-pow (it Retains) one more turn
for a 44-point Bomb. I counted both lines out — 56 damage now leaving 1 HP, or
12 now and a 47-point kill next turn — and they take the **same** amount of
damage and the same number of turns. I popped now because Block is the Bomb's
only counter and banking 44 into an unknown intent risks losing all of it to one
armour move. Landing on exactly 1 HP was luck, not misplay; the counting was right.

**Turn 4:** Ka-pow!, 0 energy, sets off the replanted Bomb 10. One card, no
alternative — but it is the two-turn plan closing, not a dead turn.
**Fight over, 4 turns, 11 HP lost.**

- **Spark at the end:** 2 (3 after the big detonation, minus Bang Bang's 2, plus 1).
  **Spent on:** Bang Bang! (2).
- **Bombs with no way to set them off:** turn 2 (Bomb 20).

*Rejected:* Explosives Workshop+ in the third slot. At 19/62 HP against a 10-damage

**Turn 2.** Bomb 20 (12 / 8), 47 HP, 12 incoming, me at 19.
**Ka-pow!** (20 + 4, Spark 1 → 3, Mine 3 dropped) → **Quick Fuse** (Mine 3 + 3,

*Rejected:* banking again. At 19 HP the bank is not affordable; the enemy's HP
bar is the only thing that keeps me alive.

**Turn 3.** A at 5. Weak 2 was on me, so **Strike printed "Deal 4 damage"** — the
screen showed me the reduced number honestly, and 4 < 5 denied lethal by one.
Strike + Defend ×2, took 0. *No alternative worth the name: the turn was decided
by a debuff, not by me.*

**Turn 4.** Killed A with a Strike; the Surprise fired and A split into B and C,
both Stunned. With no incoming damage I went for value:
**Bang Bang! on C** (no bomb on the field, so its printed fallback "this card is
only its own line" applied — 6 damage, plant Bomb 4), then **Barbara — Front Row
Seat on C**, then **Strike on C**.
Barbara is the first non-Pyro card I drafted and the reaction machinery worked
exactly as printed but *silently*: Bang Bang left a Pyro aura, Barbara "applies
Hydro twice" so the first Hydro consumed the Pyro aura for an Elemental Reaction
and the second re-applied Hydro. The only evidence was the Spark ledger —
Spark 3, minus Bang Bang's 2, plus 1 (Companion) plus 1 (**"1 more if it
triggered an Elemental Reaction"**) = 3. Nothing on the screen said "Vaporize".

**A screen-versus-outcome disagreement worth reporting.** After Barbara, C's
bomb line read: *"**Bomb 6** (buff) — Set off here deals 6 Pyro damage. **Bomb
sizes here: 4**, growing each turn."* Six and four on the same line. My reading
is that 4 is the charge and 6 is what it would deal *because* the Pyro hit would
Vaporize the Hydro aura for ×1.5 — which is genuinely helpful forecasting, but
the page never says so, and two different numbers for one bomb in one sentence
is the single most confusing thing I read all round.

**Turn 5 — a bomb sat with no way to set it off** (C carried Bomb 12, hand was
Strike, Dodoco Cover, Defend ×3). C intended to **Escape** with my gold.
*Instead:* **Strike killed C** (5 HP) — and the Bomb line's promise held: the
charge "moved to a survivor", so B inherited it. **Dodoco Cover on B** planted a
second, **Defend** covered the 9 incoming. This is the best thing the Bomb rules
do: the charge is not wasted by killing its host.

**Turn 6.** B carried **Bomb 20** (12 / 8) at 13 HP. **Ka-pow!** for 0 energy,
set off, dead. **Fight over, 6 turns, 3 HP lost across the whole fight.**

- **Spark at the end:** 3. **Spent on:** Bang Bang! (2) and Quick Fuse (1).
- **Bombs with no way to set them off:** turn 5 (Bomb 12).

## The kit, after 6 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and where they were made.**

- *At the draft, cashed on the turn:* Fight 3 turn 3. Dodoco Cover — taken two
  rewards earlier as "a Defend that also plants" — supplied the one extra Bomb 4
  that turned a 20-damage detonation into a 24-damage exact kill, which in turn
  denied the survivor a free Ravenous turn. The turn was decided in the reward
  screen.
- *On the turn:* Fight 4 turn 5. Stacking a second Jumpy Dumpty Bomb on a body
  that already had one, so the first charge is eaten by armour and the second
  lands clean. The trade was tempo (a whole energy on a card that deals nothing
  now) against the Plating tax. Nothing prompted it; I had to read "oldest first"
  and "Block stops them" together.
- *On the turn:* Fight 4 turn 1 and Fight 6 turn 1 — declining to attack. Against
  regenerating armour, and again at 19 HP, the right play was to let the Bomb
  grow for free and buy Block. A Bomb that grows 4 (or 6) a turn while I do
  nothing is the most interesting thing about the kit, because it makes *not
  attacking* a live option.
- *Earlier in the fight:* Fight 5 turn 3, spend 32 or bank for 44. I counted both
  lines to the same HP and the same turn count, and broke the tie on risk (Block
  is the Bomb's only counter). A decision where the arithmetic ties and the
  reasoning is about risk is a good decision.
- *The Spark chain,* Fight 2 turn 2 and Fight 6 turn 2: detonate, get paid, spend
  the payment on a second detonation in the same turn. This is the kit's signature
  and it is genuinely satisfying.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Turn one is automatic in five of six fights.** Jumpy Dumpty is Innate and
  costs 1, and it is the only way to start the engine, so it is played first,
  every fight, without thought. The remaining 2 energy went to Strikes or Defends
  purely off the telegraph. Only Fight 4 and Fight 6 had a turn one worth
  thinking about, and both times the thought was "how much do I *not* attack".
- **Strike and Defend are filler and it shows.** With 10 of 16 cards vanilla, most
  turns are: play the one Klee card I drew, then dump basics. Every "rejected
  alternative" on those turns was Strike-versus-Defend off the intent, which is
  Ironclad-grade decision-making, not Klee's.
- **Ka-pow! is never a choice, only a timing.** Cost 0 and Retain mean it is
  always playable and never competes for energy; the only question is *when*, and
  in four of six fights the answer was "the moment the bomb is big enough".
- **Nothing was unworth playing**, but Bang Bang! was *unplayable* twice while
  sitting in my hand, which is worse than useless — see (c).

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **The Spark loop is circular and the screen never warns you.** "Start each
   combat with 1"; Bang Bang! costs 2; Sparks come from Bombs going off. So in
   any fight where I have not yet detonated, my Spark-priced detonator is dead
   in hand. Fight 4 turn 2 printed it flatly: `CANNOT BE PLAYED: you have 1
   Spark, and this costs 2`, while a Bomb 12 sat on the enemy. The rule is stated
   correctly everywhere; it is the *consequence* that is invisible until it bites.
2. **"Bomb 6 … Bomb sizes here: 4"** (Fight 6 turn 4). Two numbers for one bomb
   in one sentence. I believe the 6 is the Vaporize-adjusted forecast against a
   Hydro aura, but I inferred that from a Spark counter, not from any printed
   word.
3. **Elemental Reactions happen invisibly.** Barbara triggered one — the Spark
   ledger proves it (+1 companion, +1 reaction) — and no screen ever said a
   reaction occurred, what it was called, or what it did. The glossary warns at
   length that a reaction can hide itself, which is honest, but the effect is
   that a whole subsystem ran and I could only detect it by arithmetic.
4. **Plating and the Bomb.** "Block stops them" makes armour a hard counter to
   the character's entire damage plan. That is legible and I beat it, but it is
   worth naming: against Plating 8, a 32-point bomb is a 24-point bomb, and the
   fix (spend Strikes first) makes the Bomb deck play like a Strike deck for a turn.
5. **Ornamental Fan at 5 attacks** gave 4 Block, same as 3. "Every time you play
   3 Attacks" reads as though it should have paid twice. Relic, not kit, but it
   is a printed text I could not reconcile with the outcome.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted: Defend.** Five copies, 5 Block, and in four of six fights I
  spent whole turns playing it into enemies that were buffing or debuffing rather
  than attacking. It is the card that makes turns feel automatic.
- **Happiest to draw: Ka-pow!** Cost 0, Retain, and it converts however much
  patience I have banked into damage on the turn I choose. Drawing it means the
  turn has a decision in it. Honourable mention to **Dodoco Cover**, the card
  that made Fight 3's exact kill possible and the only Klee card I drafted that
  I was pleased to see in *every* opening hand.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Barely — and I would say no.** Jumpy Dumpty is Innate, costs 1, and is the
only card that starts the engine, so it plays itself. What was left was two
energy and a choice between two Strikes and a Defend against a telegraphed 8 —
a decision, but one any starter deck in this game offers. The kit's own first
real decision arrived on **turn 2 of fight 1** (spend a Bomb 12 now or let it
reach 16), and its first *interesting* one on **turn 2 of fight 2** (detonate to
pay for a second detonation). Compare Fight 4 and Fight 6, where turn one was
genuinely live because declining to attack was correct — the kit can produce a
real opening turn, but only when the enemy supplies armour or my HP supplies
pressure, never off its own opening hand.

**Two structural notes for whoever reads this.** First, the dead-bomb turn is
the kit's recurring failure state: **five turns across six fights** had a live
Bomb on the board and nothing in hand that could set it off (F3 t2, F4 t2, F5 t2,
F6 t5, plus F4 t2's Spark-locked variant). Those turns are 100% filler. The
draft can fix it — I took three detonators by floor 5 and the rate fell — but the
starting deck cannot. Second, the reward screens were consistently well judged:
every one of the six offered at least one card that changed how a fight would
play, and the three "grow / merge / move your Bombs" cards I kept declining
(Chain Fuse, Careful Arrangement, Sorry Jean) suggest a whole manipulation
archetype I never got to see.
