## Fight 1 — Sludge Spinner (39 HP), in the tester's own words

1. **Turn by turn.** Opening hand was three copies of Duck and Cover, Fish-Flavored Bait and Kaboom!. Bait said "Deal 5 damage. Place a Bomb 5" and Kaboom! opened with the word "Set off", which I read as a detonator, so I played Bait then Kaboom! and got exactly 16 damage — the 5, the bomb's 5, and Kaboom!'s 6. That confirmed the whole engine on turn one. Last energy went to Duck and Cover, taking 3 of an 8-damage hit. Turn 2 I was Weak, drew Pop! and Ka-pow!, placed a bomb and set it off, and only got 7 out of an expected 11 — Weak was cutting the bomb, not just the attack. Turn 3 the Spinner was at 7; Pop! then Ka-pow! killed it for zero energy.
2. **The line I did not take.** After reading "Grows at the start of your turn; never goes off by itself" I seriously considered sitting on the bomb and playing two Duck and Covers to eat the 8 for free, to learn the growth rate. I gave up a turn of tempo information for a turn of damage, and it nagged at me — that tooltip is an explicit invitation to hold, and I never once accepted it in five fights because every enemy died faster than the bomb grew.
3. **What would have changed my line.** A bigger intent number. At 8 incoming against 62 HP the block was almost irrelevant; if the Spinner had opened at 15 I would have blocked twice and let the bomb ripen, which is the game the card text is advertising.
4. **Automatic and dead.** Bait-then-Kaboom! became automatic by the second play of it. Duck and Cover was never a decision — it was what I did with leftover energy. Ka-pow! was dead on turn 2 until a detonation handed me a Spark.
5. **How the plan changed.** It didn't, much. I came in guessing "Set off" meant something and turn one proved it. The only update was learning Weak applies to bomb damage.
6. **Confusing.** The Bomb tooltip's "Grows at the start of your turn" gives no growth number, so "should I hold this?" is unanswerable on turn one. I only learned the rate (+2/turn) by accident in fight 5 when a bomb survived a round.

## Fight 2 — Corpse Slug ×2 (27, 25), in the tester's own words

1. **Turn by turn.** Two slugs with "Ravenous 4 — when an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it, becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength." I had just picked up Kujou Sara, whose Electro was the first non-Pyro thing I'd owned, so I played her into the 25 HP slug and followed with Kaboom!'s Pyro. That did 18 to the target, splashed 6 onto the other slug, and applied Weak — far more than the 12 I'd priced. Turn 2 I bombed the 21 HP slug, set it off for 15, killed it with the now-live Ka-pow!, watched the survivor eat the corpse exactly as printed (Stunned, Strength 4), and Kaboom!'d it down before the Strength mattered.
2. **The line I did not take.** Killing the small slug first. It would have stunned the big one and denied its 8-damage swing, but it hands +4 Strength to the enemy with more HP. I chose to kill the *big* one instead and leave the 7 HP survivor holding a Strength buff it would never get to use. Giving up the stun timing was the cost; it turned out not to matter because both died the same turn.
3. **What would have changed my line.** If the slugs had been closer in HP, or if Stunned had lasted more than one turn, the eat-order becomes a real puzzle. As printed, Ravenous is a drawback I got to choose the timing of, which is nearly free.
4. **Automatic and dead.** Sara became automatic the moment I owned her — she is the only card in my deck that isn't Pyro, so she's the only card that turns a reaction on. Ka-pow! was dead in hand at the start of turn 2 with a hostile "CANNOT BE PLAYED: BlockedByCardLogic" and became free damage three plays later.
5. **How the plan changed.** Completely. I went in planning to block a combined 14 and grind; the reaction did so much damage that I switched to racing and never blocked again in this fight.
6. **Confusing.** Nothing told me what Pyro-into-Electro *does* before I did it. I bought Sara on the theory that a second element must matter, played her on faith, and inferred "Overloaded = 6 splash + Weak" from HP arithmetic. A meter labelled **Burst: 5** also appeared out of nowhere and the render openly said it carries "no maximum, and no rule for how it is spent." It never moved again and no card I saw mentioned Burst.

## Fight 3 — Seapunk (44 HP), in the tester's own words

1. **Turn by turn.** One turn. Sara for Strength and an Electro aura, Pop! for a bomb, Bait for damage plus a second bomb and the Pyro that popped the reaction (17 so far), then Kaboom! to set off both bombs for 22, then Ka-pow! — free, because the detonations had paid me two Sparks — for the last 9. Forty-four HP and I never ended a turn.
2. **The line I did not take.** Holding the two bombs a turn to watch them grow, and eating one 11-damage hit to find out the rate. I gave up the only clean experiment I had left, and I keep not running it because racing keeps working.
3. **What would have changed my line.** If Seapunk had 70 HP I'd have had to block and would have learned the growth number. Nothing in a 44 HP single-target fight punishes the all-in.
4. **Automatic and dead.** By this fight the entire sequence — Sara, bomb, bomb, set off, Ka-pow! — was automatic. I was not making decisions, I was executing a known combo in a fixed order. Duck and Cover was dead: I never drew it and never wanted it.
5. **How the plan changed.** It didn't. This was the first fight I played entirely from a script formed in the previous two.
6. **Confusing.** The Bomb power read "Set off deals 10 total Pyro damage here (2 Bombs, 0 of them Mines)" and then dealt 14, because my Strength 2 is added to *each* bomb and the tooltip's total silently omits it. That is the one number on screen I actively could not trust, and it's the number the whole deck is built around.

## Fight 4 — Fossil Stalker (51 HP), in the tester's own words

1. **Turn by turn.** "Suck 3 — whenever Fossil Stalker deals unblocked attack damage, it gains 3 Strength," 12 incoming, and not one block card in hand. So I raced: Sara, Bait, Kaboom!, Ka-pow! for exactly 41, leaving it on 10. I ended turn with nothing left, ate the 12 (it became a 9 after the reaction's Weak), watched Suck tick it to Strength 3, and killed it next turn with two Kabooms.
2. **The line I did not take.** Fire Potion for the last 10. Twenty damage would have closed the fight a turn early, taken zero damage and denied the Suck stack. I kept it, banking on an act-one boss I never reached — which, in hindsight, is a worse trade than it looked, and I only got away with it because the Stalker was already effectively dead.
3. **What would have changed my line.** A single Duck and Cover in the opening hand. Suck only triggers on *unblocked* damage, so five points of block would have shut the whole mechanic off. Drawing zero of my four block cards is what made this the scariest fight of the run, and it was still not close.
4. **Automatic and dead.** Same four-card script, now genuinely automatic. Sparks 'n' Splash was dead the whole fight — it printed "CANNOT BE PLAYED: EnergyCostTooHigh" while I had spent everything on the combo, and a 2-cost power that pays off at end of turn has no place in a deck that kills things on turn one.
5. **How the plan changed.** I started intending to block against a Strength-snowballer and abandoned that within one card, because the hand had no block in it and 41 damage was available.
6. **Confusing.** The Energy Potion reads "Gain [ironclad energy icon][ironclad energy icon]" — a raw asset placeholder, naming a different character. I could guess it meant two energy but the screen never said so.

## Fight 5 — Calcified Cultist (40) + Seapunk (45), in the tester's own words

1. **Turn by turn.** No Sara in the opening hand, so no reaction. I burst Seapunk for 23 with Bait/Kaboom!/Ka-pow! and, for the first time all run, actually built a block wall — Run Away! paid 7 because a bomb had gone off, plus Duck and Cover for 5, which ate the full 11 and I took nothing. Turn 2 the Cultist showed "Ritual 2 — at the end of its turn, gains 2 Strength," so I spent the Fire Potion to delete Seapunk outright, put a fresh bomb on the Cultist and blocked. Turn 3 Sara came back upgraded, I opened with Bennett for +3 Strength, Sara's Electro popped the Cultist's Pyro aura for the Overloaded, stacked a second bomb with Pop!, and Sizzle set off both for the kill.
2. **The line I did not take.** Ignoring Seapunk and racing the Cultist down before Ritual could compound. That gives up the safe blocked turn, and against a stacking-Strength enemy it might genuinely have been correct — but with no Sara in hand on turn one I didn't have the burst to make it.
3. **What would have changed my line.** Sara in the opening hand. She is the difference between a 23-damage turn and a 35-damage turn, and which of my five cards she is decides the entire fight. Also: if Ritual had been on the *attacking* enemy instead of the one standing still for two turns, I'd have had to make a real choice.
4. **Automatic and dead.** Run Away! was the best card I drew all run — free, and 7 block is more than the 1-cost Duck and Cover gives — and it was automatic on sight. Duck and Cover became dead weight the moment I owned Run Away!; four copies of a strictly worse card is the deck's real problem. Sparks 'n' Splash was never castable, again.
5. **How the plan changed.** This was the only fight where the plan genuinely moved: I opened defensive because the draw forced it, spent a potion I'd been hoarding once Ritual made the clock real, and finished on the biggest single turn of the run.
6. **Confusing.** The upgraded Sara suddenly printed a "*Reaction preview: Overloaded* — deals 6 splash damage to all enemies and applies 1 Weak" which is precisely the rule I'd spent three fights reverse-engineering from HP totals. I could not tell whether the preview appeared because the card was upgraded or because the target happened to be holding an opposing aura at that moment. Either way, base Sara never showed it when it would have mattered most.

## The run, in the tester's own words

1. **What the character seems to be about.** Planting explosives on enemies and choosing when to trigger them. Almost every card is either a bomb *placer* (Pop!, Bait, Jumpy Dumpty) or a *detonator* (Kaboom!, Ka-pow!, Sizzle, Fwoosh!, Quick Fuse), with a relic that pays a Spark for every detonation and a Spark-priced card that spends it back. Layered on top is an elemental system: everything the character owns is Pyro, so reactions only happen if you draft an off-colour Companion, and the moment I did the damage roughly doubled. The advertised fantasy is delayed, escalating explosions; the fantasy I actually played was a same-turn burst combo.
2. **The recurring tension.** Exactly one, and the game never made me resolve it: bombs grow if you leave them, but nothing lived long enough to be worth waiting for. Every turn the choice was "detonate now for a known number, or hold for an unknown larger number against an enemy that will be dead anyway," and the answer was always detonate. The tension is printed on the cards but not present in the fights.
3. **What carried and what never mattered.** Carried: Fish-Flavored Bait and Pop! (bombs are the damage), Kaboom! (three copies, always live), Kujou Sara (the only non-Pyro card I owned, and single-handedly the reaction engine), Ka-pow! (repeatedly free because detonations refund Sparks), and Run Away! late. Never mattered: Duck and Cover — four copies of 5 block for 1 energy, which I played almost exclusively as an energy sink and which Run Away! strictly outclasses at 0 cost; Jumpy Dumpty, which I drew twice and never once had the 2 energy for; and Sparks 'n' Splash (proto), a 2-cost power I drafted, never cast, and which showed "EnergyCostTooHigh" every time it appeared.
4. **When it became repetitive.** Fight 3, hard. From then on every fight reduced to the identical five-card script: Sara for Strength and an off-element aura, Pop!/Bait to lay bombs, a detonator to set them off, then Ka-pow! with the Spark the detonation just paid me. I was not choosing, I was checking whether the pieces were in hand. Fight 5 was interesting only because Sara *wasn't* in hand on turn one, which is a bad sign — the variance, not the decisions, supplied the interest.
5. **What I would draft differently.** I would cut Duck and Cover on sight and take every Run Away! offered, and I'd take a second off-element Companion (I passed Dahlia twice) because one Sara is a single point of failure for the deck's best mechanic. I would not take Sparks 'n' Splash again — a 2-cost end-of-turn payoff is unplayable in fights that end on turn one or two. And I'd stop hoarding the Fire Potion; I carried 20 free damage past two fights that wanted it and finished the run still holding potions.

## Defects and oddities

- **The shop is completely unusable — the single worst thing I hit.** Both shops printed fourteen priced items whose every name was `**(unnamed)** — 25 gold`, and every purchase attempt was refused with `nothing here is called '(unnamed)'. What is on the screen: (nothing)`. So the screen renders fourteen items while the command resolver simultaneously reports the screen is empty. I tried the bare name and the numbered form `(unnamed) (1)`; both failed identically, at two separate shops. I finished the run with **164 gold I could never spend**, having walked past roughly 28 purchasable items.
- **The treasure room gave me nothing to name.** The whole screen was `# An open chest` with no items listed, yet it offered `choose "<relic>"`. With no relic name printed there was no command to issue and I had to `proceed`. I still do not know whether I was given a relic — my relic list never changed from the two I started with.
- **Raw internal error enums are shown to the player.** Ka-pow! printed `CANNOT BE PLAYED: BlockedByCardLogic` whenever I had 0 Spark, and Sparks 'n' Splash printed `CANNOT BE PLAYED: EnergyCostTooHigh`. The second is at least guessable; "BlockedByCardLogic" tells a player nothing, and the actual reason (you have no Spark) is printed nowhere.
- **An unlocalised asset placeholder in potion text**, which also names the wrong character: `**Energy Potion** — Gain [ironclad energy icon][ironclad energy icon].`
- **The Bomb tooltip's damage number is wrong whenever you have Strength**, which is always. It printed `Set off deals 10 total Pyro damage here (2 Bombs, 0 of them Mines)` and the detonation dealt 14, because Strength 2 is added per-bomb and the "total" ignores it. This is the central number of the whole character and it is the one I learned not to trust.
- **A meter appeared that nothing explains and nothing uses.** After my first elemental reaction: `Burst: 5 — the game's data feed carries this meter's amount only: no maximum, and no rule for how it is spent`. It stayed at 5 for the remaining four fights, no card I ever saw referenced Burst, and I finished the run with no idea what it was.
- **A development tag is in a shipped card name:** `**Sparks 'n' Splash (proto)** — cost 2, power`. It appeared as a normal card reward and went into my deck with "(proto)" in its title.
- **An unexplained cost discrepancy on a reward card.** Flame Dance printed `The cost printed on this card is 2; it is showing 1 here.` with no indication of why, whether it was permanent, or which number I'd actually pay.
- **Enchantments render inconsistently.** In battle, hands carry the disclaimer `The game's data feed does not report a card's enchantment, so if one of them is enchanted, this page cannot show which` — but the rest-site upgrade list showed it fine: `*Sharp* — Increases damage on this card by 2`. So the data exists; the battle screen just doesn't get it. This actively changed my play: I deliberately enchanted a card I owned only one copy of, purely to dodge the ambiguity.
- **`confirm` is offered when it does nothing, and the screen auto-advances anyway.** The Gorge card-select listed `confirm` under "What you can say" while its body said `Confirm is not available.`; after my second pick the screen silently advanced on its own, and my `confirm` then failed with `there is nothing waiting to be confirmed`. (Harmless — both cards were correctly added.)
- **The map offered no choices at all for most of act one.** Six consecutive rooms presented exactly one node (`Where you can go next: - **Monster (path 1)**`). The first time I was given two options was the twelfth node. Whatever the intent, as a player I made no routing decisions for the entire first half of the act.
- **Minor:** on an event screen whose only option is Proceed, `choose "Proceed"` works but the bare `proceed` verb is the one the grammar advertises — the operator warned me about this, but a player reading only the screen would hit it.
