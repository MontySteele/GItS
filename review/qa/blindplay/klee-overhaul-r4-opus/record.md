## The run, in the tester's own words

1. Klee is a delayed-damage character. Almost nothing she plays hurts anything the moment it lands; instead she plants numbered charges that sit on enemies and grow by 3 every turn, and the whole deck reduces to the question of when to cash them. The detonators are the only cards that convert stored charge into damage, and they pop the entire stack on one target at once, so the natural shape of play is to pile several bombs onto one enemy and set them all off together. Underneath that runs a second economy: every bomb that goes off pays a Spark, and Sparks buy cards that cost no energy at all — block, and more bombs. So detonating is not just damage, it is income; it is what switches on the free half of the deck. Mines are the third piece and the most characterful: a bomb that fires when its enemy attacks, so the enemy pays for it. Twice in this run an enemy killed itself walking into one, which was the most satisfying thing that happened.

2. Bank or cash, over and over. A bomb left alone is worth 3 more damage every turn, so waiting is always nominally correct — but detonators do not stay in your hand between turns, so waiting is a bet on redrawing the one card that can cash the stack. I lost that bet three times: fight 1 round 3, and twice in a row in fight 5, where I ended the session holding a Bomb 43 that I had spent two turns building and could not light. Underneath that sits an energy squeeze: the payoff card costs 2 of 3, so the turn that places bombs usually cannot also detonate them, which is exactly why the Spark-priced cards feel so good and why I drafted toward them.

3. Ka-pow! defined it, simply by being the cheap detonator — it was in nearly every turn's plan and it is the card the whole deck is waiting to draw. Big Badda Boom defined the ceiling; paying the bomb twice turned a 39 HP enemy into a one-turn kill. Jumpy Dumpty defined the texture, because the Mine it leaves behind after detonating is damage the enemy inflicts on itself. And Doubt, the Neow curse, defined more than it looks like it should: its Weak shaves 25% off bombs and Mines as well as attacks, and it personally cost me two kills I had counted on.

4. By the fourth fight the turn had a fixed shape — place every bomb I could, point a detonator at the pile, spend leftover Sparks on Dig In, block with the remainder. The genuinely interesting decisions were nearly all in fights 1 and 2, while I was still working out whether banking was correct. Once the answer settled into "cash now, because you will not redraw the detonator," the sequencing mostly wrote itself. The block cards never presented a decision at all; Duck and Cover was simply whatever energy was left over.

5. I would avoid Duck and Cover and anything shaped like it. 5 block for a full energy never competes with a card that adds 6 to a bomb, and it was dead or filler in four of five fights. I would avoid Flame Dance, whose clause "Set off each enemy that has a non-Pyro aura" is actively turned off by my own deck, since nearly every Klee attack applies Pyro — that one looked like a trap for exactly the deck it is printed in. And I would not take a second 2-cost payoff card; on 3 energy, fitting one Big Badda Boom alongside the placement cards is already the hard part. What I would actively hunt instead is more Spark-priced detonators — Quick Fuse and Fwoosh!, which I passed on — because they cost no energy and they fix the one failure that actually threatened this run: a huge stack and nothing to light it with.

## Defects and oddities

- **Reward entries cannot be addressed by the name they print, and the numbered form is refused too.** The Neow reward screen printed `**Relic**` with `Golden Pearl` beneath it. `choose "Golden Pearl"` was refused: *"nothing here is called 'Golden Pearl'. What is on the screen: Relic"*. The documented disambiguator also failed — `choose "Relic (1)"` was refused with *"nothing here is called 'Relic (1)'"* — even though the grammar says two things printing the same name are numbered in printed order. Only the bare `choose "Relic"` worked. Identical problem after fight 4, where two rewards printed `12 Gold` and `40 Gold (stolen back)`: `choose "Gold (1)"` was refused and only bare `choose "Gold"` worked, taking them one at a time.
- **`play` gives a factually wrong refusal while a combat card-chooser is open.** After `use potion "Touch of Insanity"` the screen was "Choose a card to make free." Posting `play "Big Badda Boom" on "Sewer Clam"` returned *"you are not in a battle"*. I was in a battle, round 1 against the Sewer Clam; the true reason is that a selection screen was open.
- **The card-selection screen cannot show what you selected.** The render says so itself: *"This screen's data feed carries no per-card selection state, so nothing in the list above can be marked as the one you picked. The `Confirm is` line below is the only thing that moves when a pick lands."* Before picking, it printed *"Confirm is not available."* and listed no `confirm` verb. It is usable, but you infer your own selection from a card disappearing from the list.
- **A bomb count disagreed with the Sparks paid out.** Fight 2, round 2, Toadpole (2) printed *"Bomb 8 (buff) — A Set off here deals 8 Pyro damage in total. Bombs here: 2."* Setting it off dealt 8 and granted exactly **one** Spark (Spark 2 → 3). Everywhere else the rate is reliably one Spark per bomb: fight 1 a two-bomb pop took me from Spark 1 to 3, and fight 3 a two-bomb pop granted 2. Its Mine had already self-popped on the previous enemy turn, so only one bomb should have remained — "Bombs here: 2" looks like a stale count.
- **Plating's first decrement did not happen (or did not display).** The Sewer Clam printed *"Plating 8 ... Plating is reduced by 1 at the start of your turn"* in round 1, and still printed **Plating 8** at the start of round 2. From round 3 onward it stepped correctly: 7, then 6, then 5. The first tick appears to be skipped or rendered late.
- **Mine damage is silently scaled by Weak and nothing says so.** The Bomb badge is excellent about this — it reprints as *"A Set off here deals 3 Pyro damage in total, after Weak."* The Mine has no equivalent. Its tooltip reads only *"A Bomb that also goes off when its enemy attacks you, before the hit lands"* — no number, no Weak note. In fight 4 I left the Gremlin Merc at 3 HP under a Mine 3 as a deliberate free kill; the Mine dealt 2 and it survived at 1 and hit me.
- **Big Badda Boom's bonus pays the post-Weak size, but its text and the badge both point at the printed size.** The card reads *"Then deal damage equal to the total size of the Bombs set off"* and the badge read *"Bomb 5"*. With Weak up it dealt 3 (pop) + 9 + **3**, for 15 — not the 17 the two printed numbers imply. That one-point gap left the Fat Gremlin alive at 1 HP.
- **A number I could not reconcile at all.** Fight 5, between round 3 and round 4, the Sewer Clam went from 19... specifically it sat at **23/56 with Block 7**, I dealt 3 with Fish-Flavored Bait, its Plating added 7 Block at end of turn, its Mine self-popped when it attacked, and it arrived at round 4 on **19/56**. I could not work out from anything printed how 4 HP got past that much Block, or whether Mine damage ignores Block. No screen explains the interaction.
- **Weak reached 3 stacks from sources I could not account for.** Doubt prints *"At the end of your turn, if this is in your Hand, gain 1 Weak."* In fight 4 I held Doubt through one end-of-turn and the next screen showed **Weak 3**. The Gremlin Merc's intent printed only *"Aggressive, 6x2"* with no debuff component, and nothing on any screen announced that it applied Weak. From the printed text alone the other 2 stacks are unexplained.
- **"Steady" has no tooltip anywhere, which makes two paid event options unevaluable.** The Waterlogged Scriptorium offered *"Tentacle Quill — Pay 55 Gold. Enchant a card with Steady"* and *"Prickly Sponge — Pay 99 Gold. Enchant 2 cards with Steady."* Nothing on the screen or in any keyword block defines Steady. I had ~245 gold and no other use for it, and still declined both purely because the keyword was undefined.
- **Pounding Surprise and the Spark keyword read as two Spark sources but are one.** Pounding Surprise: *"Whenever a Bomb goes off, gain 1 Spark."* Spark keyword: *"You gain 1 whenever a Bomb goes off."* Word for word the same trigger and the same payout. On my first detonation I predicted 2 Sparks and got 1.
- **Powder Charge is uncastable in every opening hand, by construction.** It costs 1 Spark; Sparks exist only after a Bomb goes off; no Bomb can have gone off before your first play of a combat. It duly printed *"CANNOT BE PLAYED: you have no Spark; and this costs 1"* in fight 4's opening hand. Not wrong, but it is a guaranteed dead card whenever it opens.
- **Awkward refusal wording.** *"CANNOT BE PLAYED: you have no Spark; and this costs 1"* — the trailing "; and this costs 1" reads like a truncated sentence.
