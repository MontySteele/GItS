### Out-of-slot observations

**(a)** It is what it looks like: Quick Fuse detonated the three Bombs, and the starter relic restored one Spark per Bomb, returning the bank from 0 to 3 during the same turn. The board therefore did not isolate the lasting resource consequence, and the blind page omitted a relevant relic. This changes **no registered grade**: P5 asks for the bank immediately after the priced mode, when it was 0, while P1 asks which mode was chosen. It is nevertheless a material board confound outside those slots.

**(b)** It is also what it looks like: the boards systematically failed the intent-sensitivity control. Seven of eight forms were mechanically refused, including three of four deciding forms, because the single-enemy, fixed-telegraph boards gave readers no reason to change otherwise fully playable lines. This does not convert any refusal into a miss or pass; it leaves P2–P4 unreached where specified. It does determine that the instrument needs rework before another evidentiary reading.

The boards only partly did what they were built to do. t01 successfully exercised priced-mode selection and its replay validated the offer list, immediate payment, and Bomb payload. But t02’s intended comparison was refused, neither affordability board reached replay, and the repeated intent-insensitive construction prevented most of the arm’s registered questions from being asked. The uncontrolled starter relic also introduced an unprinted same-turn refund, though it did not alter any slot grade.

The local shadow seat decided nothing and was never replayed; all four shadow forms were refused for intent insensitivity, corroborating an instrument-level pattern without contributing to any threshold.

**Overall judgment: RETURN.**
