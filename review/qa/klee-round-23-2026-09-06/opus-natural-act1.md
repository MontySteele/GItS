## Fight 1 — Toadpole (1) [A] 23 HP, Toadpole (2) [B] 24 HP

Opening hand: Jumpy Dumpty, Defend ×2, Strike ×2. Energy 3. **Spark 1 at the
start of combat**, which no screen had told me to expect before the battle screen.

**Turn 1** — Jumpy Dumpty on A ("Place a Bomb 8. When it goes off, place a Mine 3
on ALL enemies"), then Strike B, Strike B.
*Rejected:* bombing B, the one I was going to kill. The Bomb glossary says it
"grows 4 a turn", so the bomb wants to sit on the body that lives longest, and I
was attacking B. Also rejected a Defend: 7 incoming out of 62 is affordable and I
wanted the bomb ticking. **This was a real decision, made on the turn** — which
body carries the charge is a genuine question the printed rules pose.
*Logging ask 3:* at the end of this turn a Bomb 8 sat on A and **nothing in my hand
could set it off** — the hand was Defends and Strikes. Nothing on the Jumpy Dumpty
face told me which card would do it; I had to wait and find out.

**Turn 2** — drew **Ka-pow!** — "cost 0, Retain. Set off. Deal 4 damage." That is
the answer to turn 1's question and it arrived by luck rather than by anything
the deck told me. Bomb had grown 8 → 12, and A had gained Thorns 2 from its
Empower.
Played Ka-pow! on A (12 Pyro + 4 = 16, A to 7; Thorns hit me for 2; Spark 1 → 2),
then Strike A (A to 1, another 2 thorns), Strike B (B to 6), Defend.
*Rejected:* two Strikes into B for the kill. I chose instead to leave A on **1 HP**
so that its own Mine 3 — placed by Jumpy Dumpty's rider when the bomb went off —
would finish it during the enemy turn. The Mine face says it "goes off before this
enemy's hit, which lands in full unless the Mine kills." Taking A to 1 rather than
0 converted the mine into both a kill *and* a cancelled 3×3 attack. **That is the
best decision I made all run and it was legible entirely from printed text.**

**Turn 3** — A was dead exactly as the Mine text predicted. B had 6 HP and Mine 7
(3, grown by 4 at my turn start). Ka-pow! on B set it off for the kill.
*No alternative rejected:* the mine placed on turn 2 was already lethal. **This is
a plan paying off, not a dead turn** — but it is worth saying that the turn itself
asked me nothing.

Spark at fight end: 3 observed before the last set-off (so 4 with the last bomb).
**No Spark was spent this fight.**

**Card reward (1) — all upgraded by Silver Crucible.** Offered: Chain Fuse+ (each
Bomb on the enemy grows by 9), **Powder Charge+ — the one card printing a Spark
price, "cost 1 Spark"**, Sizzle+ (Set off, 9 damage, +6 if a Bomb triggered an
Elemental Reaction this turn), Mika — Starfrost Swirl+ (8 to ALL, Cryo).
**Took Sizzle+.** One reason: fight 1's binding constraint was that only one card
in my deck could set a Bomb off, and Sizzle+ doubles that while out-damaging a
Strike at the same cost.

## The kit, after 6 fights

### (a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off

1. **Whether to detonate now or let the pile grow.** Fight 3 turn 1 and fight 6 turn
   1, both on the turn. Ka-pow! costs 0 energy and Retains, so holding it is free;
   the only cost of waiting is the damage you did not do this turn, and the only
   gain is +4 per bomb. **This is the kit's signature decision and it is a good
   one** — it is a real number against a real number, and the screen prints both.
2. **Leaving an enemy alive on 1–3 HP so its own Mine kills it before its hit.**
   Fight 1 turn 2, fight 3 turn 3. Trades a card's damage for cancelling a whole
   attack. This is the most satisfying thing in the kit and it is derivable purely
   from the Mine face.
3. **Element ordering.** Fight 5 turn 2: not playing my own Pyro card because it
   would have consumed the Cryo aura I had just spent a card to apply. Made on the
   turn, off printed rules, and it doubled a bomb two turns later.
4. **Which body carries the charge.** Fight 1 turn 1, fight 4 turn 1. Trades
   immediate damage against the fact that a bomb on a body you are about to kill has
   wasted its growth (though the transfer-on-death rule softens this).
5. **At the draft:** taking Rosaria+ over a second set-off card. The Bomb face names
   Vulnerable and a damage cap as the *only* two things that move Bomb damage, so a
   Vulnerable-applier is categorically different from an ordinary attack. That is a
   deck-shaping decision the card faces genuinely teach you.
6. **Grounded on turn 1 of fight 5**, paying 5 HP now for 6 Block a turn forever.

### (b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing

- **Strike and Defend.** In 6 fights the base cards never presented a choice. Every
  turn where I played a Strike, I played it because I had spare energy after the
  interesting cards. Defend was likewise filler except in fight 5, where a specific
  block threshold mattered.
- **The Spark price never once bit.** *Logging ask 2: there was no turn in this run
  where a Spark price stopped me playing a card I wanted.* I ended every fight with
  2–5 unspent Sparks, and Sparks are "Gone after combat", so I threw them away six
  times. Pounding Surprise gives 1 per bomb going off and a big set-off gives 2 or 3
  at once, so income massively outran the 1–2 Spark prices on offer. **Dig In and
  Pocket Match read as free cards, not as priced ones.** That is a live balance
  finding: as a resource, Spark did not exist for me — it was a second, unlimited
  energy pool for a small subset of cards. The one thing it did well was let me play
  a fourth and fifth card on a turn where energy had run out (fight 5 turn 2),
  which is a good feeling; it just never cost me anything.
- **Turns 3 of fights 1 and 3 asked nothing** — the mine placed earlier was already
  lethal. I count these as plans paying off, not dead turns, but the run had two of
  them and both were "end turn and watch".

### (c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text

1. **Hexerei.** *Logging ask 5.* I met the word on five cards (Witches' Circle,
   Witches' Circle+, Coven Errand, Coven Errand+, Fischl — Nightrider) and its
   glossary every time: **"Hexerei — A Companion card that prints the word, and Klee
   herself. Only the ones marked Klee's own pay: 1 Spark a play, up to 3."**
   What I understood the price to be: Hexerei is a *tag*, not a cost. A card is
   Hexerei if it is a Companion card printing the word (Fischl — Nightrider) — and,
   somehow, "Klee herself" is also Hexerei. The **price** attaches only to cards
   additionally marked **"Klee's own"**: those pay **1 Spark on top of their energy
   cost, each time you play them, capped at 3**. So I read Fischl — Nightrider as
   costing 1 Energy and **no** Spark, and Jean — Lion's Fang, Fair Protector (in the
   shop, whose text opens "Klee's own.") as costing 2 Energy **and** 1 Spark despite
   its cost line saying only "cost 2".
   **Three things I could not resolve.** (i) "up to 3" — three *Sparks* per play, or
   the surcharge applies to at most three *plays* per combat, or per turn? I still
   do not know. (ii) "and Klee herself" — is that saying the character is a Hexerei
   for the purposes of Coven Errand's "if you played a Hexerei card this turn", so
   that *any* Klee card satisfies it? If so, Coven Errand is nearly unconditional and
   I mis-valued it twice. If not, it is a dead card in a deck with no Companions.
   (iii) **A Spark price that does not appear on the card's cost line is the one
   place this kit hides a number.** Every other Spark price in the run printed
   "cost 1 Spark" plus a full paragraph explaining that it is a price and not an
   energy cost. Jean's does not, and I would have bought her not knowing.
   This was the only genuinely opaque thing in the kit, and I passed on three
   otherwise reasonable cards because of it.
2. **Flame Dance** — "Set off each enemy **whose aura is not Pyro**." Every set-off
   in this kit applies Pyro, and bombs hit as Pyro, so the enemies with bombs on
   them are overwhelmingly the enemies wearing a Pyro aura. I read this card as
   deliberately refusing to detonate the exact bodies my deck had prepared. I may
   have it backwards, but I could not make it make sense on the reward screen.
3. **An intent number that did not move with Strength.** Fossil Stalker showed
   "the number on its icon is 12" both before and after it gained Strength 3, while
   Corpse Slug's icon *did* move (3×2 → 7×2 at Strength 4). One of those two is
   telling me something the other is not; I planned my block around the higher
   number to be safe.
4. **Rosaria's rider order.** "Deal 12 damage. If the enemy has an aura, apply 1
   Vulnerable" — when the hit itself *consumes* the aura via Melt, is the condition
   checked before or after? I never saw Vulnerable land, and I could not tell whether
   that is the rule or a bug.
5. **The upgrade screen could not show me the basics.** Every Strike and Defend read
   "Upgraded: not shown -- this page has no written face for this card", and
   Dahlia+/Rosaria+ were listed under "Not on this list, and why" as "on the screen's
   list nowhere, and nothing on the feed says why" (they are already upgraded, which
   the page could have said, since it says exactly that for Sizzle+).

### (d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw

- **Never wanted: Defend.** Five block for a whole energy, in a deck that gets 6
  block from Grounded for nothing and 8 from Dig In for a resource I cannot spend.
  I played it as an energy sink. Strike is a close second — it exists to convert
  leftover energy into a number.
- **Happiest to draw: Ka-pow!** 0 energy, Retain, and it turns a growing pile into
  a single big number at the moment of my choosing. It never rots in hand, it never
  costs tempo to hold, and it is the card that made every "wait one more turn"
  decision in this run possible. The runner-up is Rosaria+, purely for the
  **"Reaction preview: Melt ... this card's 12 lands 21"** line.

### (e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?

**Yes, a real one, though a quiet one.** Jumpy Dumpty is Innate so it is always
there, and the question it poses on turn 1 is *which body carries the charge* — the
bomb grows 4 a turn, so it wants the enemy that lives longest, and I was about to
kill one of the two Toadpoles. I put it on the buffer and struck the other. The
second half of the turn (Strike, Strike vs Strike, Defend) was thinner.

The real weakness of turn 1 is different and worth stating plainly: **the deck hands
you a bomb before it hands you any way to set it off.** Jumpy Dumpty's face says the
bomb "goes off only when Set off, or as a Mine" and never names a card that does it.
*Logging ask 3 in full — turns where a Bomb sat on the board with nothing in hand to
set it off: fight 1 turn 1; fight 2 turn 1; fight 5 turn 2; fight 5 turn 3 (resolved
only because Dahlia's "Draw 1 card" happened to find Ka-pow!).* Four turns out of
sixteen. In fight 5 that included a **28-damage** pile sitting under a Cryo aura
printed as expiring in "1 more turn". A starting deck with exactly one detonator in
ten cards is why I spent three of my six card picks on detonators.
