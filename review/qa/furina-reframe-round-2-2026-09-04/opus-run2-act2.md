## Fight 1 — Exoskeleton x3 (25/25, 24/24, 26/26), all with "Hard To Kill 9"

Every enemy carried **Hard To Kill 9 — "Reduce all damage taken and HP lost by
Exoskeleton to 9."** This is the single most important fact of the fight and it
inverts the whole deck: every printed damage number above 9 is a lie, and cheap
multi-hit cards are worth more than expensive big ones.

**Turn 1.** Played Top Billing (free from Vexing Puzzlebox), then Overflowing
Hospitality, then Flood of Emotion on Exoskeleton (2).
*Rejected:* holding Top Billing — it was free this turn only, so there was no
decision there at all. The real decision was Flood of Emotion vs Thunderous
Ovation (7 block). I took the attack because incoming was only 11 against 73 HP.
I deliberately spent Flood of Emotion — printed **14 damage** — as a test of the
cap. It dealt exactly **9** (23 → 14). The cap reads as written.
*Rejected:* Ethereal Spotlight. It costs 2 Encore, I had 3, and it "Spotlights
every Companion card" — but I held no Companion cards, so I let it Ethereal away.

**Turn 2.** Crescendo → Soloist's Solicitation → Applause Line, all on
Exoskeleton (2), killing it; then Surintendante Chevalmarin.
*Rejected:* Stage Presence (6 block). I spent the last energy on Chevalmarin
instead, purely to test whether a second Salon member would do anything. It cost
me ~6 HP and told me something worth more than the block — see below.
**Where the screen and the outcome disagreed:** at the start of this turn my
**Encore had gone 3 → 0** and *nothing had happened*. No enemy lost HP to a Salon
member. The turn before, deploying Chevalmarin had dealt exactly **1** damage
(24 → 23), consistent with "2 Hydro damage" at the "dry members act at
three-quarters" rate. But the standing 3 Encore simply evaporated between turns
with no printed effect and no printed reason.

**Turn 3.** An Invitation (free) → Razor — Lightning Fang → Showstopper+ on
Exoskeleton (1) → High Tide on Exoskeleton (2).
*Rejected:* Stage Presence and Charlotte. **Both enemies were Empowering, so
incoming damage was zero and block was strictly wasted** — the clearest, easiest
real decision the kit gave me all run, and it came from the enemy screen, not
from the kit.
Showstopper+ (printed 15) dealt exactly 9 into the cap, consumed the Hydro aura I
had put up, and the enemy came back wearing **"Poison 5"**. The glossary on that
same screen says Electro-Charged makes the enemy "lose 4 HP at the start of its
turn, 1 less each turn" — which is what Poison does, and my Snecko Skull relic
bumped 4 to 5. So the reaction fired correctly but is *surfaced under a different
name than the glossary uses*.
Then High Tide — a card printed **`[Hydro]`** with an explicit **"Applies Hydro"**
clause — applied **Electro** instead, because Lightning Fang was up. The enemy
ended with Poison, not a Hydro aura. **The card face never stopped saying Hydro.**

**Turn 4.** Crashing Waves (both to 1 and 2 HP) → Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire
on Exoskeleton (2). The kill also finished the other Exoskeleton — Chevreuse's
Pyro hit the Electro aura and Overloaded's "6 damage to ALL enemies" cleaned up.
*Rejected:* Surintendante Chevalmarin / Aria of Recompense. Six Encore had by
then produced two points of damage; I stopped feeding it.
**A targeting hazard:** when Exoskeleton (2) died in turn 2, the survivor
renumbered from (3) to (2). Numbering is positional, not stable, and a queued
plan aimed at "(2)" silently retargets. I worked around it afterwards by always
killing the highest-numbered enemy first.

## Fight 2 — Thieving Hopper (79/79), Escape Artist 5

No damage cap here, and the difference was night and day: **every printed number
was exact.**

**Turn 1.** Matinée Performance (free) → Crashing Waves → High Tide → Flood of
Emotion → Applause Line. 79 → 19, and I predicted the total before playing it.
*Rejected:* leading with Crashing Waves. I played Matinée Performance **first
specifically because it applies Hydro**, and Crashing Waves reads "+5 damage if
the enemy has an elemental aura." Sequencing turned 10 into 15. That is a real,
legible, rewarding decision and it is the best thing the kit did all run.
*Rejected:* blocking. I chose to eat one 17-damage hit rather than split the turn,
because the Hopper escapes on a timer and a slow kill loses the reward outright.

**Turn 1 end — the dead-hand problem.** After spending all 3 energy I held four
cards: Salon Début, Aria of Recompense, Deep Breath, Ethereal Spotlight. **Not
one of them could convert energy into damage, block, or anything I could observe.**
There was no decision to make; I ended the turn.

**Turn 2.** Razor — Lightning Fang → Crescendo (11 damage, applying Electro onto
the Hydro aura → Electro-Charged → Poison 5) → Lisa — Lightning Rose.
*Rejected:* Stage Presence / Thunderous Ovation. The Hopper was Empowering, so
block was again worth zero. I worked out that Crescendo would leave it at 8, Lisa
would tick 5 at end of turn to 3, and Poison 5 would kill it at the start of its
turn before it could act. That is exactly what happened. **This was the single
most satisfying turn of the run** — three systems (a temporary element buff, a
delayed-damage companion, and a reaction-applied DoT) composed into an exact
lethal, and the screen gave me every number I needed to plan it.

## The kit, after 6 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Three kinds, and only three.

1. **Elemental sequencing.** Which element to apply *first* so the second card
   reacts. Matinée-then-Crashing-Waves for the aura bonus (10→15); Chevreuse-then-
   High-Tide for Vaporize; Rosaria-then-Flood for Frozen; anything-then-Electro
   for Electro-Charged's stacking Poison. This is the best thing in the kit. It
   trades immediate damage against a multiplier or a debuff, the numbers are on
   the screen, and I could plan exact lethals with it. Fight 2's turn 2 and fight
   4's turn 3 were both won by ordering, not by card selection.
2. **Damage shape against the enemy's defence.** Hard To Kill 9 made Undercurrent
   the best card in my deck and Showstopper+ one of the worst; Thorns 5 made *one
   big attack* correct and *two small ones* wrong; Personal Hive punished
   multi-hit. This is a real and good axis — but note it is supplied entirely by
   the **enemies**, not by Furina.
3. **Kill-the-attacker vs buy-block.** Fight 5 turn 2 (kill two attackers to drop
   incoming 25→9 rather than block) and fight 6 turn 1 (Weak+Frozen for 17 damage
   over max-damage for 39). Both were genuine, both were close, both turned on
   numbers the screen printed honestly.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

The **entire Encore / Salon / Fanfare / Burst economy**, which is most of the
character's printed surface area.

- **Encore.** Across six fights I never found a use for it beyond one card. It
  arrived in threes and fives and **vanished between turns without printing a
  reason**: 3 → 0 twice in fight 1, with a single point of damage to show for it.
  Aria of Recompense ("Gain 5 Encore") and Lasting Impression ("Gain 4 Encore")
  are, in this deck, blank cards. I declined every Encore card offered after
  fight 1 and never regretted it.
- **The Salon.** I fielded members three times. Total observable output for the
  run: **two points of damage.** The keyword says members "do NOT act on their
  own"; the buff line on my own status bar says "At the start of your turn, each
  Salon Member spends 1 Encore for its act." Those are flatly contradictory, and
  neither predicted what I saw.
- **Fanfare.** It fades 20% per turn and I never once exceeded **3**. Meanwhile the
  cards that read it want **12** (Florid Cadenza, Dramatic Entrance+), **20**
  (Flood of Emotion), or scale at +1 per 2 or per 4. Every Fanfare clause I was
  ever offered was dead text. The reward screen once offered me **"The Final
  Verdict+ — Deal 0 damage"**, which is what that whole subsystem looks like from
  the seat.
- **Burst Energy.** **It never moved off `0 of 70` for the entire run**, across
  six fights and well over a dozen cards printing "Burst +5" and "Elemental Skill:
  playing this card grants 5 Burst Energy." I never saw a Burst card. Either it is
  broken or its display is; from the seat those are the same thing.
- **Top Billing** ("Spotlighted Companions are 25% stronger") I played once, free,
  and could never detect.

Automatic, in the ordinary sense: any turn where the enemy's intent was **Empower**.
Block is worth exactly zero and the turn plays itself — I just fired everything.
That happened five times and was never interesting.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that seemed to contradict its own printed text.**

Ordered by how much it cost me.

1. **Razor — Lightning Fang silently overrides a card's printed element.** With it
   up, **High Tide** (`[Hydro]`, "Applies Hydro") applied Electro, and **Chevreuse
   — Interdiction Fire** (`[Pyro]`, "Applies Pyro") applied Electro. The card faces
   never change. In fight 3 I built a whole turn on Chevreuse's printed Pyro
   triggering Overloaded; it did not, I lost the Weak, and I nearly lost the run
   there. The control case is clean: in fight 4, with Razor *not* up, the identical
   Chevreuse→High Tide sequence Vaporized exactly as printed.
2. **The Prism's intent doubled from 3x3 to 6x3 while I was applying Weak to it**,
   and then a fourth Weak stack moved the printed intent not at all. I died to the
   difference. I cannot reconcile either half with anything on the screen.
3. **"Tainted — Gain 2 Tainted when played."** A circular definition for a keyword
   an Elite forces onto every skill I own. No counter, no visible effect, ever.
4. **The Salon contradiction** in (b): "Members do NOT act on their own" vs "At the
   start of your turn, each Salon Member spends 1 Encore for its act."
5. **Encore draining to 0 between turns** with no printed cause and no printed effect.
6. **Burst Energy pinned at 0/70** while a dozen cards insist they are filling it.
7. **Three status meters nothing explains** — "Spotlight Mode", "Spotlight Moved",
   "Spotlight Plays" — which appeared mid-fight and were never referenced by any card.
8. **Ethereal Spotlight's +50% does not appear on the card faces it buffs.** Dahlia
   kept printing 9 while a separate buff line ("Guest Cast") said it was 50% stronger.
   The buff is real; the cards lie about themselves.
9. **Flood of Emotion printed "Deal 16 damage. If you have at least 20 Fanfare:
   deal 14 damage."** The conditional reward is *smaller* than the base, because
   Strength inflated the base and not the clause.
10. Two screens asked me to choose without telling me what I was choosing: the
    **Self-Help Book** (Sharp / Nimble / Swift, none defined) and the **Tea Master**
    (asks for gold, never prints my gold). **"Bond of Life"** on Arlecchino is
    likewise undefined.
11. **Electro-Charged surfaces on the body as "Poison"**, a word the reaction
    glossary never uses. It behaves correctly; it is just named two things.
12. Enemy list numbering is positional and **renumbers when one dies**, so a plan
    aimed at "(2)" silently retargets.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted:** **Aria of Recompense** — "cost 1, Gain 5 Encore," a full energy
  for a resource I watched evaporate twice without doing anything. It sat in my
  hand in three separate fights and I never once played it. Runner-up, and worse
  in principle: **The Final Verdict+**, offered to me printing "Deal 0 damage."
- **Happiest to draw:** **Crashing Waves+**. It scales with the aura I just applied
  (+5), it hits everything, it is one energy, and against the 9-damage cap it was
  worth four times what my "big" cards were. It rewarded the one Furina system that
  works — element sequencing — and it was honest about its numbers every time.
  Honourable mention to **Undercurrent** after Ember Tea, for the same reason
  inverted.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, but a thin one, and not a Furina one.** The genuine choice was Flood of
Emotion (damage) against Thunderous Ovation (block), and I could only resolve it
by reading the *enemies'* intents (11 incoming vs 73 HP) — nothing about Furina's
own kit informed it. Two of the five cards I played that turn were forced rather
than chosen: Top Billing was free-this-turn-only, and Ethereal Spotlight announced
**"CANNOT BE PLAYED: you have no Encore, and this costs 2"** — the character's
signature relic-generated card, unplayable on turn one of every combat by
construction. The interesting decision (spend 2 of my 3 Encore on Spotlight, or
keep it) was a non-decision because I held no Companion cards to spotlight.

So: a decision existed, the enemy supplied it, and Furina's own three meters
contributed nothing on turn one. That is the round's finding in one sentence.
