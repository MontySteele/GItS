## Fight 1 — Seapunk [A], 44 HP

Deck at this point: 5 Strike, 5 Defend, Jumpy Dumpty (Innate), Ka-pow!.

**Turn 1** (hand: Jumpy Dumpty, Strike ×3, Defend; enemy intends 11). Played
Jumpy Dumpty on Seapunk (Bomb 8), then Strike, Strike. **Rejected:** three
Strikes for 18 instead of 12 + a bomb. I took the bomb because Jumpy Dumpty is
Innate — it is there every fight whether I like it or not — and I wanted to see
what a Bomb does before I decided whether it is worth an energy. I also rejected
Defend; 11 into 62 HP is affordable. Took 11.

**Turn 2** (hand: Strike ×2, Defend ×3; Bomb had grown 8 → 12; enemy 2×4).
Played Strike, Strike, Defend. **Rejected:** three Defends. I had no card that
could Set off the bomb, so the bomb was, at that moment, a number growing on a
screen with no verb attached to it — pure race. Took 3 through 5 Block.

**Turn 3.** Drew **Ka-pow!** — "cost 0, attack. Retain. Set off. Deal 4 damage."
Seapunk was on 20 with Bomb 16. 16 + 4 = 20 exactly. Played it and the fight
ended on one 0-cost card. **Rejected:** Jumpy Dumpty first for a second bomb, or
Strikes. Both were worse and neither was lethal.

**Judgement across the fight:** two flat turns and then a turn where a plan laid
on turn 1 paid off to the point. The turn-3 "decision" was an obvious lethal,
but it was obvious *because* of turn 1, so I count the fight as having one real
decision (spend an energy on a bomb whose payoff card I had not yet seen) rather
than none. Turn 2 genuinely had none: five cards, two of which were the same
card, and a race I could not alter.

**Reward:** 14 gold, Flex Potion, and the card choice Blast Shield (2 Sparks) /
Sizzle / Witches' Circle / Noelle — I Got Your Back. Took **Sizzle** — Ka-pow!
was my only Set off and one setter for one bomb is a coin-flip on draw order.
Witches' Circle and Noelle both key off *Hexerei*, of which I owned zero cards;
Blast Shield keyed off Sparks, of which I had one per combat and no way to make
more except by setting bombs off, which was the thing I could not reliably do.

**Turn 2**, and the turn that showed me the kit's ceiling. 35 HP, Block 10,

Screen: `Bomb 21 (buff) — Set off here deals 21 Pyro damage. Bomb sizes here,

legible was a *different* line, on the card: `*Reaction preview: Melt* — Into

the power block does not.

Played **Pocket Match**: Set off → Melt → 21 into 10 Block = 11 HP, then its own
7 (into a now-bare body, so 7, not the previewed 12 — the aura had just been
eaten by the bomb). 35 → 17, exactly as computed. Then **Sizzle**: Set off fired
the freshly-dropped Mine 3, and its "if a Bomb triggered an Elemental Reaction
this turn" clause paid, so 3 + 6 + 6 = 15 → 2. Then Strike for 6. **Fight over,
zero damage taken.**

**Rejected:** Sizzle first (Sizzle's own Melt preview said its 6 would land 10 —

1.75× on 12 for a 1.75× on 6, and it would also have turned off its own +6
clause). Also rejected **Sorry, Jean...** — 12 Block off the bomb — which was the
turn's real fork and which I declined because the enemy was on 35 and I could

**Reward card:** Powder Charge (1 Spark) / Catalytic Converter / Return to Sender

and no Energy. It is the answer to the structural hole I had been playing around

after the first is a 4-to-7-damage attack with a dead first sentence.**

## The kit, after 6 fights

### (a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off

- **"Grow it or fire it", every turn a bomb was on the field** — made *on the
  turn*, and the only recurring decision the kit has. It is a good one because
  the two sides are printed and both move: the bomb grows 4 a turn (9 with Chain
  Fuse+), and the enemy's Strength/Block/intent moves too. Fight 5 asked it twice
  and I answered opposite ways — wait on turn 1 (nothing incoming, +7 damage
  free), fire on turn 4 (Ritual 5 meant a turn cost more than it paid). That is a
  decision with a real shape.
- **Which Set off card eats the detonation** — on the turn, and only once I had
  two setters. Ka-pow! is free and small, Pocket Match costs a Spark and hits
  harder. When the detonation alone is lethal, you fire with the cheap one and
  keep the expensive tail for a living body. Fight 5 turn 2 and fight 6 turn 2
  both turned on this.
- **Cryo before Pyro** — on the turn, but only because of a *draft* pick. Kaeya
  is 8 damage and a 1.75× multiplier on whatever detonates after him. Playing him
  second is a 34-damage turn thrown away for an 8-damage one. This is the kit's
  best sequencing rule and I got it from one card taken at one reward screen.
- **Bomb-as-damage vs bomb-as-Block** (Sorry, Jean...) — on the turn. I only ever
  declined it, so I am reporting the fork rather than its payoff; but it is the
  one card that made the growing number ambiguous instead of just good.
- **At the draft:** buying Pocket Match (fight 1's shop) and taking Kaeya (fight
  2's reward). Both were bets that a dead line of printed text — "Set off" with
  one bomb in the deck, "if a Bomb triggered an Elemental Reaction" with one
  element in the deck — could be switched on. Both paid, and both fights I won
  without taking damage were won by those two picks.

### (b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing

- **Jumpy Dumpty, turn 1, every single fight.** It is Innate, it is the only bomb
  in the deck for most of the run, and nothing else the kit does works without it
  on the board. Six fights, six identical opening plays. It is not a card, it is
  a start-of-combat trigger with an energy tax.
- **Strike and Defend.** 10 of my 20 cards. Every turn where I did not draw a
  bomb piece was "Strike, Strike, Defend" — fight 1 turn 2 and fight 6 turn 1
  are pure examples. The Spiral Strike (Replay 1) was the only one of the ten I
  was ever glad to see.
- **Both potions, all run.** Flex (5 Strength for a turn) and Dexterity (2 Dex)
  do nothing for a deck whose damage is bomb detonations — Strength adds to
  *Attack hits*, and my big numbers are not attack hits. Reptile Trinket (use a
  potion → 3 Strength) is the same dead relic for the same reason. I held two
  potions and a relic that rewards using them for eleven floors and never found
  a turn where using one was better than not.
- **Snecko Skull.** Never applied Poison once.
- **Set off cards with no bomb on the field.** The screen is honest about it —
  it prints `*No Bomb on the field* — this card is only its own line` — but that
  means Ka-pow! is a 0-cost 4-damage attack and Sizzle is a 1-cost 6, i.e. worse
  than Strike, on any turn the bomb is spent. Before Powder Charge (floor 12)
  that was most turns after the first detonation.

### (c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text

1. **`Bomb 21` vs `Bomb sizes here, oldest first: 12` on the same power block**
   (fight 4 turn 2, fight 5 turn 2). Two different numbers for one bomb, with no
   printed reason. It is 12 × 1.75 = 21, the Melt-adjusted figure, and I only
   worked that out because a *card* in my hand carried a `Reaction preview: Melt`
   line that did the same arithmetic explicitly. The power block should say which
   number is which, the way the card does.
2. **"What reacted this turn" was empty on both turns a Melt fired.** Fight 3
   turn 1 and fight 4 turn 2: the section printed nothing (fight 3 printed the
   header with nothing under it) while the damage arithmetic — 38 → 4, 35 → 17,
   both to the point — only works if Melt resolved. The screen has a slot
   for exactly this and left it blank on the two turns it mattered. On turns
   where nothing happened it says so in words ("Nothing reacted this turn"), so
   the blank is not a "no" — it is a gap.
3. **The enchantment chooser drops enchantments** (floor 8, *Self-Help Book*).
   Ten identical `Strike (n)` / `Defend (n)` rows, no `(Spiral)` tag, on the one
   screen where the tag is the whole basis for choosing. The combat hand prints
   it; the chooser does not. This cost me the pick I wanted.
4. **The Neow row that promised a card with no face.** *Large Capsule* printed
   its own caveat: "this option's own words promise a card and the feed carried
   no face for it — no rules text, no cost, no type". Nothing visibly went wrong,
   but the screen told me it could not fully describe what I was picking.
5. **Minor, and it resolved:** Pocket Match's `Reaction preview: Melt` said "this
   card's 7 lands 12", and it landed 7 — because the Set off in its own first
   sentence had eaten the aura a beat earlier. The preview is computed as though
   the card's own hit meets the aura, which is false for every card that Sets off
   first. It reads as a contradiction until you work out the ordering.

### (d) The card I never wanted, and the one I was happiest to draw

- **Never wanted: Defend.** Five of them, 5 Block, and a kit whose defence is
  supposed to be either killing the thing before it swings (Mines) or converting
  a bomb (Sorry, Jean...). Every Defend I played was an admission that I had not
  drawn a bomb piece. Clumsy is technically worse but it exhausts itself for free
  and feeds Joss Paper, so it asked less of me than Defend did.
- **Happiest to draw: Ka-pow!** — "cost 0, attack. Retain. Set off. Deal 4."
  Free, never discarded, and it is the trigger on the end of every plan the kit
  makes. It killed fight 1 on the nose, and in fights 5 and 6 it detonated a
  36-damage Melt for zero energy and zero Spark, which let the paid setter go
  somewhere useful. Runner-up: **Chain Fuse+**, which is the card that makes the
  bomb worth waiting on.

### (e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?

**Yes, but a thin one, and only because I did not know the answer yet.** The
hand was Jumpy Dumpty + 3 Strikes + Defend against one enemy telegraphing 11.
The choice was 18 damage now versus 12 damage plus a Bomb 8, and it was a real
question *to me* only because I had not yet seen a single card that could set the
bomb off — I was paying an energy for a number on a screen with no verb attached.
Having now played six fights, I know the answer is "always Jumpy Dumpty, always
turn 1", so the honest report is that turn 1 of fight 1 presented a decision to a
blind player exactly once, and the kit will never ask it again. Turn 2 of that
same fight presented none at all.

**The finding, plainly:** as I drafted, Sparks were never scarce and never a
choice. Pounding Surprise refunds a 1-Spark setter out of the detonation that
setter causes, so a 1-Spark card is free and a 2-Spark card is the only real
price in the currency. Between those two facts the bank sat at 1–4 and expired
unspent at the end of every fight, and the printed line "with no cap" describes
a resource I never had to ration. The one turn Spark *did* bite was a card I had
owned for ten minutes, and it bit because I had no bombs on the field — that is,
Spark scarcity in this deck was really bomb scarcity wearing a different name.
Whether that is the kit or my draft I cannot say from one run, but I declined
the two cards (Bang Bang!, Blast Shield) that would have put a genuine 2-Spark
demand on it, on the grounds that I could not afford them, and I was wrong about
that in hindsight — by fight 3 I could have paid either one twice over.
