# Blind seat — KLEEMOD-KOKOMI, lane 2, round 18, natural run

## Identity

- **Model / seat:** Opus (Claude), blind TESTER seat, lane 2.
- **Run seed:** `Y5PU6HE11JAR`. **Ascension:** 3. Opened at HP 64/80.
- **Character:** the screens never printed a character name. What identified the
  kit to me was the starting relic **Tamakushi Casket** ("Start each combat with
  the Bake-Kurage. Whenever you apply a debuff to an enemy, it deals 2 Hydro
  damage to that enemy") and the Bake-Kurage / Plan panel on every combat screen.
- **Act:** 1. The map printed **"At the top of this act: Lagavulin Matriarch"**,
  16 floors ahead at the start.
- **Actions accepted:** 120 of 120.
- **Termination:** action budget. The bridge refused nothing at the end; I spent
  my last two actions on a Strike and an `end turn` inside the Act-1 elite and
  stopped there. Wall clock was nowhere near 5400 s.
- **HP trajectory:** 64 → 62 → 58 → 55 (fight 1) → 47 (fight 2) → 45 → 37
  (fight 3) → 33 → 31 (fight 4) → 25 → 22 → 19 → 18 → 16 → **6/80** (elite,
  still alive, Terror Eel at 15/140).
- **Gold at stop:** 24.
- **Potions held:** none. I drew a Vulnerable Potion, a Skill Potion and bought
  an Energy Potion, and spent all three. A **Duplicator** was offered after
  fight 3 and the bridge declined to claim it, printing: *"Your potion slots are
  full: 3 of 3. A potion claimed now has nowhere to go, and the game says

  (upgraded by Fishing Rod), Barbara — Let the Show Begin♪, Ambush, Well Laid,
  Stolen Chapter, Riptide, Sea-Salt Prayer.
- **Relics:** Tamakushi Casket, Fishing Rod.

**Neow pick: Fishing Rod** ("Every 3 normal combats, Upgrade a random card in
your Deck") over Neow's Talisman and Neow's Bones. Talisman would have upgraded
two vanilla Strike/Defend I expected to stop playing; Bones' curse looked like

## Fight 1 — Toadpole (1) [A] 21 HP, Toadpole (2) [B] 23 HP

Opening hand was five vanilla cards: Defend ×3, Strike ×2. Not one kit card.

| Turn | Plans written | Carried out at start of next turn |

| 3 | Kurage's Oath | Kurage's Oath, 7 — the 7 is damage |

**Turn 1.** Struck A twice, Defended once. **Rejected:** planning something on
the Bake-Kurage — I tried, and learned Strike *cannot* be planned (refusal
below). With a hand of Strikes and Defends there was no other line. **A turn
with no decision in it.**

**Turn 2.** Drew Kurage's Oath and Slack Water — the first kit cards of the run.
Played Kurage's Oath (3 to ALL) then Strike into A for exactly 9, killing it.
**Rejected:** planning the Oath for its 7-to-ALL line, because 3+6 was exactly
lethal on A this turn and A was about to gain Thorns; the Oath's own text made
that arithmetic readable. This was the first real decision of the run and it was
made on the turn.

Then, with 1 energy left, I typed `play "Slack Water" on "B"` — the printed name
of Toadpole (2). The bridge accepted it and **wrote it as a Plan on the
Bake-Kurage** (`"target": "Bake-Kurage"`). See the refusal notes; the screen
itself says *"Either handle aims a card -- `on \"B\"` is the same body as the
full name beside it"*, and it is not.

**Turn 3.** Planned Kurage's Oath (7 to ALL, for the same 1 energy as its 3
now), Struck B, Defended. **Rejected:** playing the Oath immediately for 3 —
planning doubled it and B was at 18 with Thorns 2, so a card I did not have to
land was worth more than a card I did.

**Turn 4.** B at 5. One Strike. **No decision** — but it was the pay-off of the
Plan written on turn 3, not a dead turn.

**Where the screen and the outcome disagreed.** Thorns printed *"When hit by an
attack, deal 2 damage back."* On turn 2 I played Kurage's Oath — a **skill** —
and Strike into a Thorns-2 body and lost **4** HP, i.e. Thorns fired on the
skill too. Vulnerable and Weak both print the clause "a Skill's damage too";
Thorns does not, and behaves as though it did.

**Companion cards played this fight: 0** (I owned none yet).

  conventions.
- The Eel's debuff gave me **"Vulnerable 99 (debuff) — Receive 50% more damage
  from cards for 99 turns."** 99 turns is not a duration anyone designed; and
  "from cards" left me unable to price the swing, since the enemy's 24 is not a
  card. It resolved as **exactly 24 against 14 block = 10 taken**, so player
  Vulnerable did *not* touch enemy attack damage. I could not have known that
  from the text, and I spent a whole turn's planning on the assumption it might.

## The kit, after 5 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

1. **"Play it now, or write it on the jellyfish."** This is the kit, and it is a
   good one. Every Plan card prints both halves, and the Plan half is roughly
   double: Kurage's Oath 3→7, Ambush 5→12, Riptide 9→13, Stolen Chapter 2→4
   draw. You are always buying a bigger number with a turn of tempo, and the
   answer changes with the board: I planned on fight 4 turn 1 (nothing was
   attacking, and clog was coming), and refused to plan on fight 3 turn 1 (I
   needed Slack Water's Weak *that* turn as defence). **Made on the turn.**
2. **"You are Weak; the jellyfish is not."** Fight 2 turn 2 and fight 4 turn 2.
   The card faces rewrite themselves under my Weak and the Plan lines visibly do
   not, and the Plan keyword says so in one clause. That turned a debuff into a
   routing decision instead of a tax. **On the turn, off printed text.** Best
   thing in the kit.
3. **The Shriek race** (fight 5 turn 6). Immediate damage to trip a printed
   threshold *now* versus a bigger planned number that trips it a turn later and
   eats a 22 in between. **On the turn**, but only possible because I had drafted
   two immediate-damage options.
4. **Stacking two Plans on a free turn** (fight 5 turn 7): 27 damage and 4 block
   delivered in one beat, for a turn in which I did almost nothing. **Made a turn
   earlier**, and it felt like it.
5. **The Tamakushi Casket rider as arithmetic** (fight 3 turn 3): "apply a
   debuff → 2 Hydro" turned 16 damage into exactly-18 lethal. **On the turn.**
6. **At the draft:** taking Barbara over Tide Chart, and Riptide over Razor. The
   Razor pass shaped the whole run — I never had a second element and therefore
   never saw an Elemental Reaction, which the screens advertise on every combat
   turn.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Every turn where I drew no Plan card.** Fight 5 turn 4 is the clean example:
  two Strikes, two Defends, a Well Laid printing 2, and a jellyfish panel reading
  *"Nothing is planned. The morning is empty."* Eight of the four starting
  Strikes and four Defends are half the deck and they present nothing. Fights 1
  and 2 opened on all-vanilla hands.
- **Kill turns.** Fight 1 turn 4, fight 2 turn 3, fight 4 turn 4 were each "play
  the lethal the Plan already set up." I count those as the plan paying off, not
  as dead turns — but three of my fourteen turns were them.
- **Turns at low HP against a big telegraph** (fight 5 turns 3 and 5): the block
  arithmetic has one answer and you type it.
- **Never worth playing:** vanilla **Defend** once Barbara (6 block) and Sea-Salt
  Prayer (4 block + Weak + 2 Casket damage) were in the deck — both cost the same
  1 energy. **Well Laid** was near-worthless in three of the five fights: it
  printed 2 or 3 for most of the run and only reached 8 on the one turn two
  Plans landed together.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **Thorns fired on a Skill.** "When hit by an attack, deal 2 damage back" — my
   Kurage's Oath is a Skill and it took 2 back. Vulnerable and Weak both print
   "a Skill's damage too"; Thorns does not and behaves as if it did.
2. **"this morning."** Well Laid says "already including 3 for each Plan carried
   out **this morning**." I assumed all fight, and was wrong: with two Plans
   carried out over the fight it printed 3 (Weakened 5 = 2 + 3), i.e. only the
   **current turn's** carry-out counts. "Morning" turns out to be the phrase the
   panel uses for the start-of-turn beat ("The morning is empty"), which is
   consistent — but it cost me two fights to work out, and "this morning" reads
   like "this run" to a first-time player.
3. **Vulnerable 99 on me**, "for 99 turns", from the Terror Eel; and its text —
   "Receive 50% more damage **from cards**" — did not tell me whether an enemy's
   24-damage swing counts. (It does not: 24 landed as 24.) That is the single
   most decision-relevant number on the screen and I could not price it.
4. **One card face, two damage conventions.** Riptide printed "Deal 9" (base) and
   "Plan: Deal 19" (13 × the enemy's Vulnerable) on the same face; Well Laid
   printed 8 and dealt 12.
5. **Hexerei / Klee.** Two Companion cards in a Kokomi run printed *"**Hexerei**
   — A Companion card that prints the word, and Klee herself. Some are Klee's
   own, some are not. Cards of hers pay when you play one."* I could not tell
   what is paid, by whom, or whether it applies to me at all, so I refused both
   cards partly on that.
6. **Elemental Reactions.** Roughly a third of every combat screen is a
   reaction rulebook — six reactions, an aura-consumption rule, and a
   long paragraph about a reaction that "looks as though it did not happen" —
   ending, every single time, in **"NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE."** Over five
   fights I never once reached a second element. The kit spent more printed
   words on a system I could not touch than on the one I played.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted:** vanilla **Defend**. Also **Well Laid**, which I bought and
  which spent most of the run printing 2.
- **Happiest to draw:** **Ambush**. One energy, and the face says "Deal 5 damage.
  Plan: Deal 12 damage" — the biggest gap on any card I owned, and the clean
  statement of the whole kit. On the elite's turn 7 it landed for 18.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**No.** The opening hand of fight 1 was Defend, Defend, Strike, Strike, Defend —
no kit card at all. I *tried* to make a decision out of it, aiming a Strike at
the Bake-Kurage, and was told **"'Strike' cannot be planned on Bake-Kurage."**
The jellyfish sat on the screen with a four-line rules panel and nothing I could
do with it. The first turn that presented a decision was fight 1 turn 2, once
Kurage's Oath and Slack Water finally appeared.

## Bridge defects and refusals (findings, not play)

1. **The enemy letter handle resolves to the Bake-Kurage.** The combat screen
   prints, on every turn, *"Either handle aims a card -- `on \"B\"` is the same
   body as the full name beside it."* It is not:
   - `play "Strike (1)" on "A"` → refused with *"'Strike (1)' cannot be planned
     on Bake-Kurage, so aiming it there would spend the action and do nothing."*
     (I had aimed at Toadpole (1) [A].)
   - `play "Strike" on "A"` → same refusal.
   - `play "Slack Water" on "B"` → **accepted**, and resolved as
     `"target": "Bake-Kurage"`, writing a Plan I did not want. This one cost me a
     turn of tempo in fight 1 and is the more dangerous half: the letter form is
     silently *wrong* rather than refused. Both letters appear to fuzzy-match
     "**B**a**ke**-Kurage". Full printed names worked every time thereafter and I
     used them for the rest of the run.
2. **My own error, recorded because it looks like the above:** `play "Riptide" on
   "Terror Eel"` was refused (Riptide hits ALL enemies and must be played bare).
   The brief says so; I had suppressed the tool's stdout on that call and did not
   see the refusal, and briefly mis-read the missing 9 damage as a game bug. It
   was not. Re-issued as `play "Riptide"` and it resolved.
3. Total refusals: 3, never three in a row.

## Non-blindness declaration

Commands run outside the two allowed ones, all via the Bash tool:

- `mkdir -p review/qa/kokomi-round-18-2026-09-05` and `mkdir -p` on the session
  scratchpad directory (the scratchpad was created but never written to).
- `sed -n '<ranges>p'` and `head -N` piped over the output of
  `GITS_LANE=2 python -m understudy.blindplay observe`, to re-read one block of a
  screen without reprinting the whole page. On two occasions overlapping sed
  ranges printed the same block twice, which briefly made me think a fight had
  two identically-lettered enemies; a clean re-`observe` showed one body. No
  filter ever added information to a screen.
- `for i in 1 2 3; do ... act ... done` in one shell call, and several
  `act ...; act ...` chains, purely to batch accepted actions.
- `>/dev/null` on some `act` calls to suppress the JSON echo. This is what hid
  the Riptide refusal noted above.

Tools used: **Bash** (as above) and **Write** (once, for this file).

No `harness state`, no `scenario`, no `staged_turn`, no `soak`, no other
understudy subcommand. No `git` command. No search over the repository.

**Repo files read: none.**
