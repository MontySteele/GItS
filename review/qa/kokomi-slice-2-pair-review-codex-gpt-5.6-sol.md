### Arm 1 — Sounding Line

1. **Card involvement.** Played by both graders: “Both energy into damage: Sounding Line…” and “I played Sounding Line… then Coral Guard.”

2. **Change in kind of choice.** Only the numbers changed: both halves presented the same damage-versus-Block allocation, with the prototype simply offering more damage while consuming Charge.

3. **Did the cost bind?** Not concretely this turn. Charge fell from 8 to 2, but “nothing else in the hand competes for Charge”; only speculative future card value was surrendered.

4. **Text problem?** No. Both prototype replays confirmed the stated damage and Charge spend.

5. **Judgment: RETURN.** Return the board, not necessarily the arm: include another current-turn Charge use or reader so spending six Charge creates an observable sacrifice rather than merely a future hypothetical.

### Arm 2 — Fathom the Tide

1. **Card involvement.** Played by both graders; one explicitly identified the steering point: “Both energy into damage, in a specific order… The order is the whole point.”

2. **Change in kind of choice.** Yes. The shipped half only cashed a threshold already reached, while the prototype introduced sequencing between a Charge reader and spender, plus a conserve-versus-spend line.

3. **Did the cost bind?** Yes. Spending reduced Charge from 12 to 6 and made playing Fathom first reduce the following All Streams hit from 11 to 8—a concrete three-damage sequencing penalty.

4. **Text problem?** Yes. The second grader claimed 24 damage after playing the spender first, but replay produced 21; that form misread how the later reader recalculates after Charge is spent.

5. **Judgment: ADVANCE.** The arm produced a steerable sequencing and conservation decision worth testing across a whole fight, although the reader’s displayed wording should be checked for clarity.

### Arm 3 — Twin Tides

1. **Card involvement.** Both graders played its paid mode: “Twin Tides taking the Spend 6 Charge half for 12 Block” and “spending 6 Charge to gain 12 Block.”

2. **Change in kind of choice.** Yes. The shipped pair disagreed between pure offense and formation defense, while the prototype added a chosen price for defense and let both graders combine offense with exact mitigation.

3. **Did the cost bind?** Yes. Six Charge was exchanged for seven additional Block, leaving only 2 Charge and requiring All Streams to be played first to preserve its nine damage; the line also surrendered six immediate damage versus two attacks.

4. **Text problem?** No. Both replays confirmed nine damage, twelve Block, and Charge falling from 8 to 2.

5. **Judgment: ADVANCE.** The alternate payment was deliberately selected against both free Block and retained Charge, producing clear steering worth testing over a whole fight.

### Arm 4 — Watatsumi Levy

1. **Card involvement.** Ignored in both played lines, though weighed: “All Streams… then Watatsumi Levy” was considered and rejected because it traded known damage and Block for “two unknown cards.”

2. **Change in kind of choice.** No. Both shipped and prototype halves chose the identical three cheap cards; replacing Charge gain with a Charge payment did not alter the realized decision.

3. **Did the cost bind?** No. Levy was never played, so no Charge was spent; its six-Charge price only made an already uncertain Muster line less attractive.

4. **Text problem?** No replay contradicted either form. The second prototype form was refused for `intent_insensitive`, not arithmetic or face-text misunderstanding.

5. **Judgment: RETURN.** Return the board: give Muster an observable near-term payoff and a credible window to choose it, so Levy’s Charge payment competes with known value rather than unspecified randomness under incoming damage.

Overall, the round supports spending Charge as steerable when it creates an immediate interaction—especially ordering or a selected alternate price, as in Arms 2 and 3. Arms 1 and 4 did not establish that on their boards. Nothing warrants **ESCALATE**: the one disagreement in Arm 2 is resolved by replay, and Arm 4’s conflicting intent answer was formally refused.
