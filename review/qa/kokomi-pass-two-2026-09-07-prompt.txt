# Doctrine read: Kokomi pool pass two, eight new prototype rows and one lane rule, against the kit charters

Each numbered card below is ONE ARM. For each arm, in order, give FOLLOWS or
REQUIRES_MODIFICATION and name the clause (C1 to C7) you ruled against, and
quote the line of the card and the arithmetic (energy, Block, damage, cards,
turns) that breaches it, in one sentence. A verdict with no clause named is
not a verdict. Read adversarially: the author is a different model family and
the owner wants the row that should not reach a tester named. Do not soften.
Name clauses, not remedies: a number, a face or a rewritten row you volunteer
is DISCARDED.

Why these rows exist. The owner's verdict on Kokomi after an act-1 run
(2026-09-07): the mechanic works, but "the central loop feels too auto-pilot"
-- a free turn is "play every Plan card", with nothing to sequence. The
review that followed (yours) kept Plan and named the test: preparation must
change the value of the decisions that follow it. The ruled path (R265) is
pool first: rows that make the ORDER of the queue matter and rows that make
the queue a RESOURCE to spend, a trial of a second landing moment, and the
two-Plan cap as one lane of the next round rather than the rule. Rule each
row on the charter; separately, at the end, say in two sentences whether the
set as a whole answers the auto-pilot complaint or only adds Plans.

## The charter

- **C1 (owner, 2026-09-04).** "I don't want us to end up gaming the metric
  of 'can an agent beat the game' by printing overly strong cards." A row
  that removes a losing line the kit is meant to keep, instead of pricing
  it, is out of charter.
- **C2 (LAW D3, binding prices).** A benefit carries a binding price. The
  counterfactual test: there must be a real turn in which the card is worse
  than a plain card of its cost. Repeatable energy or draw at Uncommon or
  below is priced by something past the card's own cost.
- **C3 (LAW D2, player-controlled leverage).** The card's value is decided
  by a choice the player makes (play a Plan now or write it; which order;
  cancel or keep), not by a number that rises while you watch.
- **C5 (Kokomi brief section 2 rule 4).** Nothing fires by itself: no
  automatic free attack, no passive feed; every effect is a card the player
  played and paid for. (C4 is Klee's and does not apply.)
- **C6 (not strictly better).** A row may not be a strictly better version
  of a pool row or of a base-game card at the same or lower rarity and
  cost. Reference points: Adrenaline (Rare, 0: 2 Energy, draw 2, Exhaust),
  Skim (Uncommon, 1: draw 3), Offering, Battle Trance, Berserk, Double Tap
  (Uncommon, 1: the next Attack this turn is played twice), Burst (Uncommon
  Skill, 1: the next Skill this turn is played twice), Seeing Red (Uncommon,
  1, Exhaust: gain 2 Energy), Blur (Uncommon, 1: 5 Block, Block is not
  removed at the start of your next turn), Havoc, Hologram.
- **C7 (Kokomi identity Law 4).** In her pool a Common never increases deck
  size.

## The engine (Kokomi, prototype arm, draft 6 rule)

The Bake-Kurage (her jellyfish) sits on her side for the whole combat;
enemies cannot touch it. A card with a Plan line may be played ON the
jellyfish instead of where it would normally go: its cost is paid now, the
card goes to the discard, and the Plan is written to a queue. At the START of
her next turn (after the enemy has acted and after her draw) the jellyfish
carries every queued Plan out IN THE ORDER WRITTEN and the queue is empty
afterwards. A planned hit lands on the front enemy (leftmost alive) unless
the line says ALL. There is no limit on the number of Plans written. Change
of Plans (Common Skill, 1, Exhaust) carries out the FRONT Plan now.
"Whenever the Bake-Kurage carries out a Plan" is once per entry (Treatise:
draw 1 once a turn; Song of Pearls: 3 Block once a turn). Nereid's Ascension
(Rare Power, 2): every Plan is carried out twice. Energy 3, HP 70. Existing
comparators in her pool: Read the Field (Common Skill, 1: 5 Block; Plan: 10
Block); Coral Bulwark (Common, 1: 6 Block; Plan: 8 Block and 1 Weak); Exposed
Flank (Common, 1: 1 Vulnerable; Plan: 2 Vulnerable to ALL); Feint (Common
Attack, 1: 6; Plan: 10); Riptide (Common Attack, 2: 9 to ALL; Plan: 13 to
ALL); Battle Plan (Uncommon, 1: draw 1; Plan: 1 Energy and draw 2); Stolen
Chapter (Common, 1: draw 2; Plan: draw 4); Ripple (Common, 0: 2 Block; Plan:
1 Energy and 4 Block); Tide Wall (Uncommon, 1: 4 Block; Plan: 3 Block per
Plan carried out this morning); Tide Chart (Common, 0: next turn, after the
Plans, draw 1 per Plan carried out).

Two words the new rows use. **The next Plan** means the Plan carried out
immediately after this one in the same morning; if none follows, the rider
does nothing. **Dusk** (trial keyword, two rows): a Dusk Plan is carried out
at the END of this turn, before enemies act, instead of at the start of the
next; it is still a Plan (Treatise, Song of Pearls and Change of Plans see
it), and it is written to the same queue.

## The arms

1. **Opening Gambit** -- Uncommon Skill, 1 energy. Deal 5 damage. Plan: Apply
   1 Vulnerable to ALL enemies. The next Plan deals double damage. Upgrade:
   7 damage now. Arithmetic: written before Riptide, the morning deals
   13 x 2 = 26 to ALL through 1 Vulnerable (39 effective) for 3 energy across
   two cards; written after Riptide, it deals 0 extra. Compare Double Tap.
2. **Second Wave** -- Common Skill, 1 energy. Gain 4 Block. Plan: The next
   Plan is carried out twice. Upgrade: 6 Block. Under Nereid's Ascension the
   next Plan is carried out three times, not four (the rider adds one).
   Compare Burst; and Nereid's Ascension, which this makes a Common taste of.
3. **Scout Ahead** -- Common Skill, 1 energy. Draw 1 card. Plan: Draw 1 card
   for each Plan carried out after this one. Upgrade: costs 0. Written first
   on a three-Plan morning it draws 2; written last it draws 0. Compare Skim
   (draw 3 for 1, no setup) and Stolen Chapter's Plan line (draw 4 for 1).
4. **Second Thoughts** -- Common Skill, 0 energy, Exhaust. Cancel your last
   Plan: its card returns to your hand and you regain its cost. Upgrade: also
   draw 1. The take-back: a Plan written on a turn the board then changed
   (the enemy it aimed at died; a hit is coming that needs Block now) can be
   un-written and the card played for real this turn at no net energy.
   Counterfactual: with no Plan queued it is a dead card.
5. **Ebb Tide** -- Uncommon Skill, 1 energy, Exhaust. Cancel every Plan you
   have queued. Gain 1 Energy and draw 1 card for each. Upgrade: costs 0.
   Arithmetic: three Plans queued (3 energy already spent on them, their
   effects forfeited) become 3 Energy and 3 cards this turn for 1; one Plan
   queued is a net 0 for a card. Compare Adrenaline (2 Energy, draw 2, for 0
   at Rare) and Seeing Red.
6. **Converging Tide** -- Common Skill, 1 energy, targets an enemy. Gain 4
   Block. Every queued Plan aims at this enemy instead of the front. The
   redirect is written on the entries at play; a Plan written after it aims
   at the front as usual. Compare Read the Field (5 Block, Plan 10) for the
   Block and the fact that the aim rule (front, always) is the pool's default.
7. **Breakwater** (Dusk trial) -- Common Skill, 1 energy. Gain 4 Block. Dusk
   Plan: Gain 7 Block. Upgrade: 5 and 9. The dawn comparator is Read the
   Field (5 now, 10 at dawn); the Dusk line pays 3 less for landing before
   the enemy's hit instead of after it. Compare Blur.
8. **Night Watch** (Dusk trial) -- Common Skill, 1 energy. Gain 3 Block.
   Dusk Plan: Gain 5 Block and apply 1 Weak. Upgrade: 5 and 7. The Weak
   lands before the enemy attacks, so it reduces THIS turn's hit; the dawn
   comparator Coral Bulwark's Weak (8 Block and 1 Weak at dawn) reduces the
   next one.
9. **The two-Plan cap (lane rule, not a card)** -- in ONE lane of the next
   round only: the jellyfish carries out at most two Plans a morning; the
   rest wait, in order, for the morning after. Rule it as a rule: does a
   fixed allowance breach C3 (it makes the order a choice) or C1 (it removes
   the many-small-Plans deck the kit was meant to keep)?

Nine verdicts, each with its clause and its breaching line, then the two
sentences on the set. Under 800 words.
