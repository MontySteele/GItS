## Fight 1 — Corpse Slug (1) 25/25 [A], Corpse Slug (2) 27/27 [B]

Opened HP 62/78, relic Salon Solitaire putting **Usher in the lead seat at 3
Fanfare** before I did anything.

**Turn 1.** Hand: Soloist's Solicitation (1, 6 dmg), Stage Presence (1, 6 Block),
Surintendante Chevalmarin (1, summon), Curtain Rise (1, "Deal 7 damage. Spend 3:
deal 13 instead"), Understudy (0, summon random, Exhaust).

I played Understudy first, on the deliberate ground that it is free and I did
not yet know what a "performer" was — it summoned Chevalmarin to the **back**
seat at 1 Fanfare. Then Surintendante Chevalmarin, which read "If she is already
on stage, Raise 3 on her instead" and did exactly that: back seat 1 → 4. Then
Curtain Rise on A, spending 3 from Usher's 3 — Usher emptied, **took a Bow for 4
Block**, and Chevalmarin stepped up to lead at 4. Then Soloist on A.

Rejected: Stage Presence. Incoming was A's debuff plus B's 3×2, and after the
Bow I had 4 Block plus a 4-Fanfare lead against 6 damage; a fourth Block card
would have been dead weight. **The real decision was whether to empty Usher.** I
chose to, because the printed Spend clause said an emptied performer takes a Bow
and Usher's Bow is 4 Block — so emptying him cost me 3 armour and returned 4
armour plus 6 extra damage. That is a decision, and it was made on the turn.

Outcome matched the screen exactly: 13 damage landed, Block 4, lead Chevalmarin 4.

**Turn 2.** Frail 2 on me from A's debuff. A at 4 HP, B at 25 and intending 8.
Hand: Mademoiselle Crabaletta, Grand Entrance (2, "Spend 5: deal 20"), Charlotte,
Understudy, Salon Début.

Understudy again → Crabaletta to the back at 1. Crabaletta card → Raise 3 on her,
4. Then **Grand Entrance on A, spending 5 from a 4 bar** — the card fired in full
for 20 into a 4-HP body.

The alternative I rejected was Grand Entrance into B (25 HP), leaving A alive to
hit me. I chose the massive overkill on purpose, because Ravenous said "when an
enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it, **becoming Stunned** and gaining 4
Strength" — so killing A bought me a free enemy turn. It did: B ended stunned
with Strength 4 and I took nothing.

That is a fight-level plan, not a turn-level one: I spent 16 points of overkill
to buy a turn.

**Turn 3.** B at 20, Strength 4, Hydro Aura 1 (left by Chevalmarin's Bow), and
intending 12. Hand had Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire [Pyro] with a printed
**"Reaction preview: Vaporize — Into that Hydro aura this card's 7 lands 10."**
That preview did all the work: 10 + Soloist 6 + Tidal Flourish 9 (Spend 2 off
Crabaletta's 5) = 25 into a 20-HP body. Lethal, so I rejected both Block cards
and Regal Bearing without hesitation.

**Fight 1 verdict:** three turns, three real decisions (empty Usher or not;
overkill for the Ravenous stun; the reaction-order lethal). No dead turns.

**Reward.** 11 Gold, Stable Serum, and the Glam'd card reward: Understudy (Glam),
Scene Change (Glam), **Ousia Surge (Glam)** — "Deal damage equal to the lead
performer's Fanfare. Replay 1." I took Ousia Surge because it is the only card I
had seen that *pays out* the Fanfare I was being taught to build, and Glam
doubles it. **This was the draft decision that shaped the rest of the run, and
it was wrong** — see (b) and (d).

## What I could not read, or that contradicted itself

1. **"Fanfare" names two unrelated things, and both are defined on screen.** The
   glossary says: *"**Fanfare** — A performer's own bar. Attacks hit your Block,
   then the lead performer's Fanfare, then you. No cap."* From fight 2 onward a
   **status on me** also appeared: *"Fanfare 6 (buff) — Generated by losing HP,
   and by spending, absorbing or paying out Encore — and by Center Stage plays.
   Cards read it; nothing spends it. It fades by 20% at the start of each of your
   turns, never below the baseline your cards have built."* These are different
   mechanics with one name, printed on the same screen. It reached 30 at one
   point and I never found a single card that read it, so I never knew whether I
   was supposed to be doing something about it.

2. **The back seat is described as untouchable and is not.** *"**back
   performer** — The back seat, and the reserve: **nothing hits it**."* Yet in
   fight 4 round 2 and in the elite round 3 the log read
   *"**Crabaletta** left the stage: emptied by a hit, so no Bow"* while she was
   the back performer. Also, in fight 3 turn 3 my newly summoned lead was killed
   by **Thorns** retaliation from my own attack — the Fanfare bar absorbing
   damage I dealt, which the "Attacks hit your Block, then the lead performer's
   Fanfare, then you" line does not obviously cover.

3. **A middle seat exists and is never defined.** The stage printed *"lead: Usher
   3 · middle: Chevalmarin 1 · back: Crabaletta 1"*, but every glossary entry
   only ever names "lead performer" and "back performer" — and **Raise** is
   defined as hitting "the back performer", so with three on stage I could not
   tell from the page whether Raise fed the middle or the back. In the same
   fight, the end-of-turn log then said *"**Chevalmarin** performed from the back
   seat"* about the performer the stage line had just called *middle*. The two
   halves of one screen disagree.

4. **"It moved N" means at least three different things.** *"Usher took a Bow. It
   moved 4"* was 4 Block. *"Crabaletta performed from the lead seat. It moved 5"*
   was 5 damage. *"Chevalmarin performed from the lead seat. It moved 2"* left a
   Hydro aura and dealt 2. One phrase covers Block, damage, and aura application,
   and never says which.

5. **Nothing on any screen tells you what a performer does when it performs.**
   The Bow glossary is complete and useful — *"**Bow** — A departure effect,
   earned by Spend only. Usher: 4 Block. Chevalmarin: Hydro on all. Crabaletta:
   8 damage"* — but performers also act every single end of turn, from every
   seat, and that is where a large share of my damage came from (Crabaletta at
   1 Fanfare in the reserve "moved 11"). There is no equivalent list for it. I
   played six fights without knowing what my own three performers did each turn.

6. **The elite's turn-1 arithmetic did not close.** 16 damage, Weak applied
   (→12), 9 Block on me, Usher at 3. Three damage should have emptied him. The
   next screen showed Usher at 4 and my HP unmoved.

7. **Ousia Surge is silently zero on an empty stage.** *"Deal damage equal to the
   lead performer's Fanfare. Replay 1."* Played three times with no stage, it
   dealt 0 twice and 0×2 once, at full energy cost, with no refusal and no
   preview — while the same screen refuses cards for energy and for hooks with
   explicit "CANNOT BE PLAYED" lines.

8. **The Companion glossary promises a reward slot that never appeared.**
   *"Card rewards after a fight offer a fourth, Companion, choice."* Every card
   reward in this run offered exactly three.

9. **Neow's Lead Paperweight had no readable face**, which the page admitted:
   *"this option's own words promise a card and the feed carried no face for it
   -- no rules text, no cost, no type -- so this page can offer it by name
   only."* An unreadable option is an option I cannot evaluate.

10. **Vulnerable 99 on the player.** *"Vulnerable 99 (debuff) — Receive 50% more
    damage from any attack or card hit for 99 turns."* Ninety-nine turns is not a
    duration, it is a permanent condition wearing a duration's clothes, and it
    read as a display bug until it kept counting down 99 → 98 → 97.

## The kit, after 6 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Three shapes of real decision, and the kit produced all three.

*On the turn:* **how much of the lead's bar to spend.** Every Spend card fires in
full off a short bar, so the question each turn is whether the Fanfare in front of
you is armour or ammunition — it cannot be both, and the card that cashes it also
deletes the thing absorbing the enemy's attack. Fight 2 turn 1 was exactly this
trade: 20 damage and 4 armour, or 15 damage and 7 armour. That is the kit's best
recurring decision and it recurs honestly.

*Earlier in the fight:* **the Shriek turn in the elite.** The Eel printed a
threshold at 70 HP; I was at 94 and had to construct exactly 24+ damage from a
summon, a short-bar Spend and a Bow. The turn where I played it looked obvious;
it was obvious because I had spent turn 2 getting the number within reach. Same
shape as fight 1's deliberate 16-point overkill to buy the Ravenous stun, and as
floor 9's Rotate→Spend→Bow→Ravenous chain that bought a whole free turn at 2 HP.

*At the draft:* **Ousia Surge over Scene Change on the Glam reward.** That pick
set the whole run's texture and I got it wrong; the fact that it mattered this
much is a point in the kit's favour.

I also want to record that the reaction layer produced two decisions that were
purely about *ordering* — apply Pyro, then let my own performer's Bow drop Hydro
onto it for the Vaporize — and those were the most satisfying turns I played.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

Automatic: **every Block card**. Stage Presence 6 and Regal Bearing 3+Weak came
out to the same damage taken twice, which means the choice between them was
noise. Charlotte is the same card with the numbers split across two turns.

Never worth playing: **Ousia Surge**, and I say that having drafted it first. It
demands a fat lead performer, and the kit's own economy attacks that seat from
both sides — enemies hit the lead, and every Spend card empties the lead. Across
six fights it was live once (for 6 damage). **Scene Change** was almost as bad:
0 cost, but useless with fewer than two performers, and my stage was empty or
single most turns. It did exactly one thing all run — and that one thing saved my
life on floor 9, so it is a card that is dead nine turns out of ten and decisive
on the tenth.

Also close to automatic: **summon cards when the stage is empty and something is
about to hit me**, since a 1-Fanfare body is a 1-point shield. I played several
of those with no thought at all.

**(c) What you could not understand, or that seemed to contradict its own printed
text.**

The ten items above. If I had to name the three that actually changed my play:
the **two different Fanfares**, because I spent six fights unsure whether a
resource on my status bar was something I was failing to use; the **undefined
perform effect**, because my performers were quietly doing more damage than my
cards and I could not plan around numbers I could not see; and the **back seat
being hit despite "nothing hits it"**, because it made me distrust the one piece
of positional safety the kit sells.

**(d) The card you never wanted to play, and the one you were happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Ousia Surge (Glam)**. My own first pick. Twice it cost me energy
for zero.

Happiest to draw: **Curtain Rise**. Seven damage for one energy is fine; 13 for
one energy off any bar at all, plus whatever Bow the emptied performer owes, is
the card that killed the Sludge Spinner, killed the third Corpse Slug, and set up
the Shriek. **Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire** is a very close second, entirely
because of its reaction preview: it is the only card that told me, in advance and
in the exact number, whether the turn was lethal.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

Yes, and a good one. Salon Solitaire hands you a 3-Fanfare Usher before you act,
and Curtain Rise's "Spend 3: deal 13 instead" prices him exactly: your entire
opening armour, for +6 damage and a 4-Block Bow. The hand also held a summon and
a free Understudy, so the turn was really "build the stage or cash it," which is
the kit's central question asked on turn one. I did not play a single card that
turn without weighing something.

## The five debrief questions

**1. On turn one of fight one, which line, and why.**

Understudy (0) → Surintendante Chevalmarin → Curtain Rise on A (spending Usher
out) → Soloist's Solicitation on A.

Understudy first because it was free and I was blind: I wanted to see what a
"performer" was before committing energy, and it cost me nothing to look. It put
Chevalmarin in the back at 1, which turned the Chevalmarin card from a summon
into "Raise 3 on her," which was strictly better. Then Curtain Rise's Spend,
because the Spend glossary promised that emptying Usher paid a Bow and the Bow
glossary said Usher's Bow is 4 Block — so the trade was 3 Fanfare of armour for
4 Block of armour plus 6 damage, which is free money. Soloist last with the
leftover energy. I rejected Stage Presence outright: the Bow plus Chevalmarin's
4-Fanfare bar already covered the 6 incoming.

**2. When you spent from a small bar, did you know it was small at the time, and
did the spend feel like a play or a loophole.**

I always knew, because the stage line prints the exact bar and the Spend
glossary is unusually blunt about it: *"It fires in full even if the bar is
short; an emptied performer takes a Bow."* There is no ambiguity to exploit.

The first time — fight 2 turn 3, Curtain Rise spending 3 off a 1-Fanfare body for
the full 13 and lethal — it felt like a **loophole**, and a slightly cheap one:
I had summoned a body that turn for free purely to be spent, and the "cost" of
the Spend was a resource I had conjured seconds earlier. The card's big number
was not gated by anything I had earned.

By the elite it had turned into a **play**, and I can say exactly where it
turned: fight 4 turn 3, when Tidal Flourish spending 2 off a 1-bar both fired at
9-to-all *and* paid Usher's 4-Block Bow, and elite turn 3, when Grand Entrance
spending 5 off a 1-bar delivered 20 plus Crabaletta's 8-damage Bow to hit the
Shriek threshold exactly. Once the Bow is the point, "spend more than you have"
stops being a loophole and becomes the cost line: you are not cheating the bar,
you are **choosing which performer to kill and cashing what they owe on the way
out.** The tell is that I started summoning *specific* performers to be spent —
rotating Crabaletta into the lead on floor 9 purely to buy her 8-damage Bow.

So: loophole at first read, play by the fourth fight. What made the difference
was Bows being *different from each other*. If every Bow were 4 Block it would
have stayed a loophole all run.

**3. Did you want a second performer on stage, and what for.**

Constantly, and for two reasons that turned out to be different.

The reason I *thought* I wanted one was **succession**: the lead is the only seat
attacks reach, so a second body is a second life for the stage — when the lead is
emptied by a hit or cashed for a Bow, the reserve steps up and the Spend cards
stay live. Nearly every turn where I had no second performer, a Spend card
degraded to its base number and the page told me so in as many words.

The reason I *actually* wanted one, which I only learned in the elite, is that
**reserves perform too**. "Crabaletta performed from the back seat. It moved 11"
was more damage than any card in my hand, from a body sitting in a seat the
glossary calls "the reserve". A second performer is not a spare tyre, it is a
second damage source — and nothing printed anywhere told me that. I would have
drafted differently if it had.

Third and smallest: **Raise only feeds the back**, so with one performer every
Raise card parks Fanfare on the exact seat the enemy is about to hit. A second
body is what makes Rising Applause and Warm Reception into anything other than
"give the enemy 5 more damage to chew through."

**4. Which performer did you protect, and could you say why in one sentence.**

**Chevalmarin, and only ever by accident of the seat she was in** — I protected
whoever was in the back, not whoever she was, and when I did make a deliberate
protection decision it was floor 9's Scene Change, where I rotated Chevalmarin's
2-Fanfare bar *out* of the lead and Crabaletta's 1 *into* it, specifically
because Crabaletta's Bow is 8 damage and Chevalmarin's is an aura.

In one sentence: **I protected the performer whose Bow I did not want yet, and
fed the lead seat to whichever performer's Bow I wanted to cash.** Which is to
say I was not protecting a character at all — I was managing a queue, and the
identities only mattered at the moment of departure.

**5. Did the three read as three, or as one Osty three times.**

**Three, but only at the moment they leave.** On the Bow they are unmistakably
distinct, and the glossary line is the clearest text in the kit: *"Usher: 4
Block. Chevalmarin: Hydro on all. Crabaletta: 8 damage."* Defence, element,
damage — three different reasons to empty a bar, and I made real decisions off
that difference (rotating Crabaletta to the lead for lethal; letting Chevalmarin
bow into a Pyro aura for the Vaporize; keeping Usher's Bow for the turns I needed
Block).

**While they are standing on stage they read as one body three times over.** They
all sit at 1 Fanfare when summoned, all regain 1 in the lead, all absorb
identically, and their end-of-turn performs are undocumented and printed in the
same undifferentiated phrase — "performed from the lead seat. It moved N."
Chevalmarin moved 2 and left an aura; Crabaletta moved 11; Usher moved 3. Those
are three genuinely different performers and I could only work that out by
diffing enemy HP across screens. Nothing on any page named what any of them does
while alive.

So: three at the exit, one in the middle. The characterisation is real but it is
all loaded into the last frame — and since Understudy and Salon Début summon at
random, most of the time I could not choose *which* of the three I was getting
anyway, which flattens them further. The one card that let me choose was Scene
Change, and it was the card I nearly cut.
