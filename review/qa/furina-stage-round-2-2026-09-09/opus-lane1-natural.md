# Furina — stage round 2 — lane 1, natural run

## Identity

- **Seat:** Opus 5 (1M context), blind TESTER seat, lane 1.
- **Character:** Furina. **Run:** natural — nothing granted, nothing staged.
- **Seed:** never printed. No screen I saw carried a seed, and I did not go looking
  for one.
- **Ascension:** never printed either. The only evidence is the opening HP: the
  first combat screen of floor 2 opened at **HP 62/78**, i.e. the run began below
  full max HP.
- **Act / boss:** Act 1. The map named **Waterfall Giant** at the top of the act.
  I never reached it.
- **Actions accepted:** 154. (The bridge prints its own counter; the last one I
  read on screen was `actions: 113 of 240`, and 41 further commands were accepted
  after that. Every one of them was `observe` or `act`.)
- **Termination reason:** not a budget. **The run ended — I died.** Killed on
  **floor 11** by the Act 1 elite **Terror Eel**, on the enemy turn after round 8
  of that fight. The bridge then printed:

  > `TOOL-BLOCKED: game_over` / "the run is over; there is nothing left to play"
  > "The run ended on floor 11." — Act 1, HP 0/78, 59 gold.

- **HP trajectory:** 62/78 at the first fight → 60 (fight 1) → 60 → 60 (fights 2
  and 3 cost nothing at all) → 45 (Living Fog) → 39 (three Corpse Slugs) → rested
  to 62 → 54 (event, chose to lose 8 HP) → 56 at the elite (Blood Vial) → 51 → 45
  → 32 → 32 → 27 → 24 → **0**.
- **Gold at the end:** 59.
- **Potions held at the end:** Stable Serum, Droplet of Precognition (a third slot
  was never full at the end).
- **Relics at the end:** Salon Solitaire, Silken Tress, Blood Vial.
- **Deck at the end** (as last printed on a map screen, plus what I took after):
  Charlotte — First-Person Shutter, Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire, Curtain Rise,
  Mademoiselle Crabaletta, Ousia Surge ×2 (one carrying **Glam**), Regal Bearing,
  Rising Applause, Salon Début, Soloist's Solicitation ×2, Stage Combat, Stage
  Presence ×2, The Witness Stand, Tidal Flourish. Sixteen cards.

**Neow pick: Silken Tress** — "Lose all Gold. Enchant all cards in your first card
reward with Glam." I took it because the other two were a generic potion package
and a card the page admitted it could not show me, and because Glam ("this card
has Replay once per combat") was the only option that would teach me something
about the kit's own machinery rather than about Slay the Spire in general. It
paid: my first card reward came up three Glam cards and the one I took, Ousia
Surge, was the best card in the deck for the rest of the run.

A defect on that very first screen: **Lead Paperweight** printed

> "Choose 1 of 2 Colorless cards to add to your Deck.
> *this option's own words promise a card and the feed carried no face for it —
> no rules text, no cost, no type — so this page can offer it by name only*"

so one of the three Neow options was, to a blind reader, unreadable. I could not
have chosen it on its merits.

## Fight 1 — Corpse Slug (1) 25 HP, Corpse Slug (2) 27 HP

Opening board: Usher in the lead seat at 3 Fanfare (from Salon Solitaire).
Slug A telegraphed a debuff, Slug B telegraphed 3×2.

**Round 1.** Hand: Curtain Rise, Stage Presence ×2, Charlotte — First-Person
Shutter, Rising Applause.

The turn-one decision was real and it was about the bar. Curtain Rise reads

> "Deal 7 damage. **Spend 3: deal 13 instead.**"

and my lead had exactly 3 Fanfare — enough to pay, and paying would empty the
performer. The glossary told me an emptied performer takes a Bow (Usher's Bow is
4 Block), so the choice was: 13 damage plus 4 Block and an empty stage, or 13
damage with the bar intact if I raised it first. **I played Rising Applause
first** (3 → 8), then Curtain Rise (8 → 5, 13 damage into A). **Rejected:**
firing Curtain Rise bare for the Bow, because the 4 Block was worth less than
five points of standing shield, and rejected playing it unraised for 7.

Third energy: **Charlotte — First-Person Shutter** (4 Block now, 4 next turn).
**Rejected** Stage Presence (6 Block now) because Charlotte's 8 across two turns
beat 6 across one, and the enemy's 6 damage was going to be partly eaten by the
Usher anyway.

**Round 2.** The board printed a genuinely good decision. Slug A sat at 12,
Slug B at 27, and both carried "Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug
immediately eats it, **becoming Stunned** and gaining 4 Strength." So killing A
would both remove A's attack and stun B: a whole enemy turn deleted, paid for
with +4 Strength on the survivor. I played **Salon Début** (a second performer,
Chevalmarin, arrived in the back seat at 1 Fanfare), then Chevreuse (7) and
Soloist's Solicitation (6) to finish A at exactly 13. **Rejected:** hitting B
instead, and rejected blocking, because the stun made blocking pointless.

That worked exactly as printed. B was Stunned with Strength 4.

**Rounds 3–4.** Round 3 was a smaller version of round 1 — Curtain Rise auto-spent
again and I re-checked whether the trade was worth it (I decided the fight ended
on the same turn either way, so it was near-indifferent; the game spent for me
regardless, see (c)). Round 4 was a clean lethal: Soloist for 6 into a 4 HP body.
**That last turn had no decision, and it did not need one** — it was the round-1
and round-2 plan arriving.

Fight cost: **2 HP.**

Reward: 11 gold, Stable Serum, and a card choice of three Glam cards —
Understudy (0, summon twice), Scene Change (0, rotate twice), **Ousia Surge**
(1, "Deal damage equal to the lead performer's Fanfare. Replay 1."). I took Ousia
Surge: it was the only card that turned the Fanfare bar — which up to then was
purely a shield — into damage, and Glam doubled it. **Rejected** Understudy
because I had not yet seen what a second performer was for.

## Fight 2 — Sludge Spinner 39 HP

**Round 1.** This was the fight where the draft paid off. Rising Applause put the
Usher at 8; Ousia Surge then dealt **8, and its Glam Replay dealt 8 again — 16
damage for one energy** — and, crucially, the bar did not move (8 before, 8
after). Ousia *reads* the bar; Curtain Rise *spends* it. That distinction is the
best thing in the kit and it is legible from the two card faces alone.

Third card: **Regal Bearing** (Block + 1 Weak). **Rejected** a third attack,
because the Spinner was telegraphing 8 and Weak is a permanent discount on it.

**Round 2.** I was Weak myself (Soloist printed "Deal **4** damage" rather than 6,
Curtain Rise printed "Deal 5 damage. Spend 3: deal 9 instead"). The decision was
whether to attack through Weak or bank a turn. **I banked:** Stage Presence +
Charlotte + one Soloist. **Rejected** Curtain Rise, because spending 3 Fanfare
while Weak bought a discounted 9 and cost me three points of the thing Ousia
Surge reads — the exact tension the kit is built on, and the one turn where I
felt it as a decision rather than as bookkeeping.

**Round 3.** Rising Applause (10 → 15), Curtain Rise (spend, 13, bar to 12),
Charlotte. **Rejected** Salon Début: with two performers on stage, Rising Applause
raises the *back* one and Ousia Surge reads the *lead* one, so summoning a second
body would have unhooked my pump from my payoff. That was the single most
interesting thing I worked out all run, and I worked it out from the two glossary
lines alone.

**Round 4.** Soloist for 6 into a 6 HP body. No decision; the plan landing.

Fight cost: **0 HP.** Four rounds, not a point taken.

Reward card taken: **The Witness Stand** (1: Vulnerable + draw 1) over Courtroom
Drama (a reaction payoff I had no second element for) and An Invitation.

## Fight 4 — Living Fog 80 HP (+ Gas Bomb minions)

The long fight, and the one that broke the kit open.

**Round 1.** Applause → Ousia (8) → Stage Presence. **Rejected** the greedy line
(Applause → Ousia → Soloist, 14 damage) because 8 incoming into an 8-Fanfare bar
would have emptied the Usher on turn one — killing my only performer kills Ousia
Surge, and I wanted to see how long I could keep him.

**Round 2.** The Fog answered with **Smoggy 1 — "You can only play 1 Skill per
turn."** That is a clean, legible counter to this kit specifically: Rising
Applause, Salon Début, Stage Presence and Charlotte are all skills, so Smoggy
turns the stage economy off and leaves only attacks. With the Usher at 9 I played
Ousia (Glam, **18**) + Curtain Rise (spend, 13) + Chevreuse (7) — **38 damage in
one turn**, 72 → 34. The best turn of the run, and it happened *because* the
debuff pushed me off the skills and onto the bar I had spent two fights building.
**Rejected:** blocking at all, on the read that 5 incoming into a 6-Fanfare bar
was free.

**Round 3.** A Gas Bomb (7 HP, Death Blow for 8) joined. **The Witness Stand on
the Bomb** made Soloist's 6 into 9, which killed a 7 HP body with one card
instead of two. **Rejected** two Soloists, which also kills it but costs an extra
energy — the cheapest real decision of the run and the one I was happiest with.

**Round 4.** The Usher died and **the whole kit went with it.** The stage line
read:

> "The stage is empty. A *Spend* rider cannot fire at all, so those cards play at
> their base number."

Rising Applause raises nobody, Salon Début was not in hand, Curtain Rise dropped
to 7, and Ousia Surge became a blank. I played Curtain Rise + Chevreuse + Stage
Presence, and there was no decision in it.

**Round 5 was a genuinely dead turn.** Hand: Stage Presence ×2, Regal Bearing,
Charlotte — **four skills**, of which Smoggy let me play one — and Ousia Surge,
which on an empty stage does nothing. Five cards, three energy, **one legal
useful play.** I played Stage Presence and took 10. That is the sharpest negative
finding of the round.

**Round 6.** I spent one energy deliberately to answer a question: with the stage
empty and the status bar reading **"Fanfare 8 (buff)"**, does Ousia Surge — "Deal
damage equal to the lead performer's Fanfare" — read that 8? **It dealt 0.** The
Fog stayed at 14/80. See (c); this is the single worst legibility defect I found.

**Round 7.** Two Soloists took the Fog to 2; the Usher-less stage meant the last
three rounds were vanilla Slay the Spire.

**Round 8.** Soloist for lethal. Killing the leader took the minions with it
("Minions abandon combat without their leader").

Fight cost: **15 HP.**

Reward taken: **Mademoiselle Crabaletta** ("Summon Crabaletta. If she is already
on stage, Raise 3 on her instead") — chosen precisely because I had just spent
four rounds unable to put anyone back on stage.

**Shop (floor 6).** 54 gold. I bought **Tidal Flourish** (51: "Deal 5 damage to
ALL enemies. Spend 2: deal 9 instead") over a third Ousia Surge at 36. The
reasoning was the fight I had just played: more Ousia copies deepen a dependency
that had just collapsed twice, whereas Tidal Flourish has a base number that
works with an empty stage. I stand by it; Tidal Flourish did work with an empty
stage, twice.

## Fight 5 — Corpse Slug ×3 (27 / 26 / 25)

**Round 1.** Tidal Flourish auto-spent 2 (bar 3 → 1) for 9 to all three, then
Curtain Rise spent from a bar of **1** — "It fires in full even if the bar is
short" — for 13, and the Usher left **with** a Bow this time. The log distinguished
the two deaths cleanly, and this is the game at its most legible:

> "Usher left the stage: **emptied by a Spend, so it takes a Bow**."
> "Usher took a Bow. It moved 4."

against the earlier

> "Usher left the stage: **emptied by a hit, so no Bow**."

**Rejected:** holding the bar. With three bodies on the board I wanted the AoE and
the 13 more than five points of shield.

**Round 2.** Slug A at 5. Chevreuse killed it and **both survivors were Stunned**
by their own Ravenous — a full enemy turn deleted for one card. Then Salon Début
rebuilt the stage (Chevalmarin, 1 Fanfare). **Rejected** blocking, again made
free by the stun.

**Round 3.** The best defensive decision of the run, and it was made with a potion
rather than a card. The board showed a Slug intending 7×2 with Strength 4, and I
had **no block card in hand at all** — Crabaletta, two Soloists, Rising Applause,
Salon Début. Two things saved it:

1. **Shackling Potion** ("ALL enemies lose 7 Strength this turn") turned the
   printed intent from `7x2` to **`0x2`**;
2. **Rising Applause used as defence** — with one performer on stage the back seat
   *is* the lead, so +5 Fanfare is +5 of shield. Chevalmarin 2 → 7.

I took **0 damage** on a turn that was telegraphing 14. **Rejected:** summoning
Crabaletta first, which would have moved Chevalmarin off the back seat and made
Rising Applause raise the wrong body — the same trap as fight 2, round 3.

**Round 4.** Chevalmarin's own performance finished the last Slug I had softened,
the survivor ate it (Strength 8, this time *not* stunned — see (c)), and
**Ousia Surge (Glam) off an 8-Fanfare Chevalmarin dealt 16 and ended the fight.**
Two turns of setup cashing in one card.

Fight cost: **6 HP.**

**Rest site (floor 8):** rested for 23 rather than upgrading, at 39/78 with an
elite two floors up. **Event (floor 9):** offered the Spoils Map (an *unplayable*
card, 600 gold next act) against 8 HP for a potion. I took the 8 HP. The reason
was specific to what had gone wrong in fight 4: my failure mode all run was hands

buys more of exactly that. **Treasure (floor 10):** Blood Vial.

## Fight 6 — Terror Eel, 140 HP (elite) — the run ends here

Opening: HP 56/78. The Eel telegraphed **16**, and carried
"Shriek 70 — The first time Terror Eel's HP reaches 70 or below, it becomes
Stunned."

**Round 1.** Rising Applause (Usher to 8) → **The Witness Stand** (Vulnerable) →
Ousia Surge (Glam): 8 × 1.5 = 12, twice = **24 damage for one energy**, 140 → 116.
**Rejected** Stage Presence in that slot; I bought 8 extra damage for 8 HP and at
56 HP that was the right price.

**Round 2.** The Usher was gone (one 16-damage hit is more than any bar I can
build in a turn), so the stage was empty and this was a vanilla turn: Stage
Combat + Curtain Rise + Soloist + Tidal Flourish for 21. No stage decision existed
to make.

**Round 3.** Regal Bearing for Weak, Crabaletta for a body, Soloist. Crabaletta
arrived at **1 Fanfare** and absorbed 1 of a 22-damage hit before dying.

**Round 4.** The one clean piece of arithmetic in the fight. The Eel sat at 84 and
Shriek fires at ≤70; my hand held exactly 14 points of damage (Stage Combat 3 +
Soloist 6 + Tidal 5) **and** exactly 9 points of block (Stage Combat 3 + Stage
Presence 6) against a telegraphed 9. I played all four: **Eel to exactly 70, zero
damage taken.** **Rejected** the Witness Stand line, which deals 21 instead of 14
but takes 9 — I chose the HP because I could see the clock.

**Round 5.** Shriek's stun did not visibly happen (see (c)). Salon Début put a
body on stage purely so that Curtain Rise could spend it: summon at 1, spend for
13, Bow for 4 Block. 70 → 51. **Rejected** the Applause-then-Ousia line, because a
freshly summoned 1-Fanfare performer feeds Ousia 6 at best.

**Round 6.** The Eel applied **Vulnerable 99** to me — 50% more damage taken, for
ninety-nine turns. From here the arithmetic was already lost and I played it out.
Regal Bearing + Stage Presence + Charlotte, 13 Block against a 24 read as 27; took
5.

**Round 7.** Chevreuse + Curtain Rise (7, no stage) + Stage Presence. Eel to 37,
me to 24. **Rejected** the pure race (19 damage, HP to 13) because at 13 with
Vulnerable I die to anything; at 24 I died anyway.

**Round 8.** The Eel telegraphed **33** with Vigor 6, which Vulnerable makes ~50
against my 24 HP, and my hand's total block was 3. There was no decision left. I
played Soloist ×2 + Stage Combat + Salon Début for 15 (Eel to 22) and ended the
turn. **HP 0/78. Run over on floor 11.**

**Judged across the fight rather than turn by turn:** the elite had exactly two
real decisions in it (the round-1 Vulnerable-into-Glam-Ousia burst, and the
round-4 exact-14-and-exact-9 turn). Everything between them was a stage that could
not stay standing.

## The kit, after 6 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Four, and three of them were the same trade seen from different angles.

1. **Spend the bar or read the bar** — *made on the turn*, every turn there was a
   performer. Curtain Rise and Tidal Flourish **spend** the lead's Fanfare; Ousia
   Surge **reads** it without moving it; and the bar is simultaneously my shield.
   So every attack is priced in shield, and the best card in my deck is paid for
   by the card next to it. This is a genuinely good axis and it is legible from
   two card faces.
2. **Do I want a second performer?** — *made at the draft, felt on the turn.*
   Rising Applause says "Raise 5 Fanfare on the **back** performer" and Ousia Surge
   says "damage equal to the **lead** performer's Fanfare." With one body those are
   the same body; with two they are not. So Salon Début actively *unhooks* my pump
   from my payoff unless I also hold Scene Change, which I never drafted. I turned
   down a summon at least three times for this reason. Excellent tension, and it
   is the reason the answer to debrief question 3 is "no."
3. **Whether to kill something now** — *made on the turn*, in both Corpse Slug
   fights. Ravenous stuns every survivor, so a kill is a deleted enemy turn bought
   with permanent Strength. Not Furina's, but it interacted well with her.
4. **The whole Neow/first-reward line** — *made at the draft.* Taking Silken Tress
   put a Glam on Ousia Surge, and a Glam Ousia off a raised bar was 16–24 damage
   for one energy in four separate fights. Fight 2 in particular had exactly one
   decision in it and that decision was made two screens earlier.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Curtain Rise's Spend is not a choice.** The card prints "Deal 7 damage.
  Spend 3: deal 13 instead," but there is no verb for declining. It spent from a
  bar of 8, from a bar of 3, and from a bar of 1, always without asking. A rider
  written as an option that always fires is just a bigger number with a cost
  attached, and it should probably read that way.
- **Stage Presence** was pure filler — 6 Block, no text, no interaction with the
  stage at all in a kit whose whole idea is the stage.
- **Every turn after the last performer died.** Fight 4 rounds 4–8 and elite
  rounds 2, 6, 7 were vanilla Spire: Ousia Surge blank, Rising Applause blank,
  Curtain Rise reduced to 7. The kit does not degrade, it switches off.
- **Salon Début was near-worthless in practice** — the random performer arrives at
  **1 Fanfare**, which absorbs one point of a 22-damage hit and feeds Ousia Surge
  one point of damage. Mademoiselle Crabaletta at least names its body.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

*Quoting only what the bridge printed.*

1. **Two different things are both called Fanfare, on the same screen.** The stage
   line's:

   > "**Fanfare** — A performer's own bar. Attacks hit your Block, then the lead
   > performer's Fanfare, then you. No cap."

   and my own status bar's:

   > "**Fanfare 8 (buff)** — Generated by losing HP, and by spending, absorbing or
   > paying out Encore — and by Center Stage plays. Cards read it; **nothing spends
   > it.** It fades by 20% at the start of each of your turns…"

   These are flatly incompatible ("nothing spends it" vs. "Spend — Pays from the
   lead performer"), they appeared together, and I could not tell which one any
   card meant. **I tested it:** in fight 4 round 6 my stage read "The stage is
   empty," my status bar read "Fanfare 8," and I played Ousia Surge — "Deal damage
   equal to the lead performer's Fanfare" — into an 80 HP enemy at 14 HP. **It
   dealt 0.** So the buff called Fanfare is not the Fanfare any card I owned reads,
   and it cost me a card and an energy to find that out.

2. **"Usher performed from the lead seat. It moved 3." has no visible consequence.**
   This line printed on nearly every turn. When *Chevalmarin* performed I could see
   it: "It moved 5" and the two Slugs dropped 3 and 2 and both gained a Hydro Aura.
   When the *Usher* performed and "moved 3," no enemy HP moved, no Block appeared
   on my Block line, and the bar moved by exactly the +1 the lead regains anyway.
   Fight 2, round 3: Usher "moved 3", the Sludge Spinner went 23 → 19, and the only
   thing I had played into it was a Soloist for 4. I never worked out what the
   Usher's number does.

3. **Arithmetic that did not close.** Fight 4, round 3→4: Block 0, lead at 5
   Fanfare, one 8-damage attack, Usher dies — and my HP stayed at **60/78**, when
   the printed layering (Block → lead's Fanfare → you) says 3 should have reached
   me. Elite round 1→2: Block 0, lead at 8, a 16-damage attack, and I lost **5**.
   I could not reconcile either.

4. **"Bow — A departure effect, earned by Spend only."** But the card **Final Bow**
   (offered as a reward) reads "The lead performer takes a Bow and leaves," and it
   is a skill, not a Spend. The glossary's "only" is wrong on the face of a card
   the same game offered me.

5. **Shriek did not visibly fire.** "Shriek 70 — The first time Terror Eel's HP
   reaches 70 or below, it becomes Stunned." I put it at exactly 70. The Shriek line
   vanished from its status list and its next intent printed as **"Strategic
   (Debuff)"**, not Stunned. Either it fired and the stun was invisible, or it was
   consumed without firing; the screen let me tell neither.

6. **Ravenous fired inconsistently, or the screen hid one case.** Fight 1 and fight
   5 round 2: a death, and every survivor printed "Intent: Stunned." Fight 5 round
   4: a death, the survivor gained Strength 8, and printed "Intent: Strategic
   (Debuff)" with no stun. Same buff text both times.

7. **Printed card numbers fold in *my* debuffs but not the enemy's.** Under Weak,
  Soloist printed "Deal **4** damage" and Curtain Rise "Deal 5 damage. Spend 3:
  deal **9** instead" — helpful. But against a target carrying Vulnerable 1,
  Soloist still printed "Deal 6 damage" and hit for 9. Half the numbers on the card
  are live and half are not, with nothing saying which.

8. **A glossary entry for a mechanic that was not on the board.** With a Gas Bomb
  minion present, the screen printed
  > "**Bomb** — A charge on an enemy: each grows 4 a turn, and goes off when Set off
  > or as a Mine…"
  which describes nothing the Gas Bomb did (it Death Blows for 8 and dies). Word
  collision, not a rule I could use.

9. **The Companion glossary promised a reward slot I never saw.** "Card rewards
  after a fight offer a fourth, Companion, choice." Every one of my five card
  rewards offered exactly three.

10. **The refusal text on a Smog-locked card is honest but unhelpful:**
   > "CANNOT BE PLAYED: something else on the board is stopping you right now.
   > The feed does not say which thing is stopping it."
   It then guessed correctly ("what is on YOU right now is: Smoggy 1"), which was
   fine — but a page that has to guess at why my own card is illegal is worth
   flagging.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted: Salon Début.** "Summon a random performer who is not on stage" —
  random, arrives at 1 Fanfare, and if I already had a performer it made my Rising
  Applause point at the wrong seat. It was a 1-energy card that produced one point
  of shield and one point of Ousia damage.
- **Happiest to draw: Ousia Surge, the Glam copy.** Because it is the only card
  that pays out what the previous four turns built, because it reads the bar
  instead of eating it, and because Rising Applause → Ousia Surge (Glam) for 16,
  or Witness Stand → Ousia Surge (Glam) for 24, was the only thing in the deck
  that felt like Furina rather than like a starter.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a good one.** Turn one, floor 2, I had 3 Fanfare in the lead seat and a
card reading "Deal 7 damage. Spend 3: deal 13 instead" — i.e. the game offered me
its central trade on the first turn I ever took: pay my whole shield for six extra
damage plus a 4-Block departure, or spend a card raising the bar first and have
both. I chose to raise first, and I could explain why at the time. That is a real
opening.

## The debrief, answered

**1. On turn one of fight one, which line, and why.**
Rising Applause → Curtain Rise → Charlotte — First-Person Shutter. Rising Applause
first specifically so that Curtain Rise's Spend 3 would come out of 8 rather than
out of 3: same 13 damage either way, but the difference between a stage that
survives the turn and an empty one. Charlotte over Stage Presence because 4 now
plus 4 next turn beats 6 now against a telegraphed 6, and because the Usher's bar
was there to catch the overflow. I rejected the bare Curtain Rise (13 damage plus a
4-Block Bow, empty stage) — that line trades a permanent resource for a one-turn
one on turn one.

**2. When you spent from a small bar, did you know it was small at the time, and
did the spend feel like a play or a loophole.**
I always knew — the bar is printed on the stage line above the hand, every screen,
in the same place. What I did not have was a *choice*: Curtain Rise spends
automatically whenever it can, and there is no verb to decline. So spending from a
big bar felt like a play (fight 4 round 2: bar at 9, spend 3, 13 damage, still 6 of
shield standing — that was a decision I made two turns earlier). Spending from a
small bar felt like a **loophole**, and specifically twice: fight 5 round 1 and
elite round 5, where I had a performer at **1 Fanfare** and Curtain Rise still paid
out its full 13 because "It fires in full even if the bar is short." Elite round 5
was the naked version — I played Salon Début purely to conjure a 1-Fanfare body so
that Curtain Rise could immediately kill it for 13 damage and a 4-Block Bow. That
is a 1-energy summon converted into +6 damage and +4 Block with no downside, and it
did not feel like the kit's economy. It felt like I had found the seam in it.

**3. Did you want a second performer on stage, and what for.**
**No — and I turned one down repeatedly, on purpose.** The back seat is safe
("nothing hits it") but Rising Applause raises *the back performer* while Ousia
Surge reads *the lead performer*, so the moment a second body exists my only pump
stops feeding my only payoff. Without Scene Change (which I saw twice as a reward
and never took) a second performer is a downgrade. The only thing I ever wanted a
second body **for** was insurance against the lead being emptied — and that want
was real, because losing the lead switched the whole kit off twice. But the cards
as printed make the insurance and the engine mutually exclusive, and I chose the
engine every time. When I finally did put two bodies up (fight 4, round 2) I got
one point of shield out of it.

**4. Which performer did you protect, and could you say why in one sentence.**
The **Usher**, every fight, because Salon Solitaire gives him to me at 3 Fanfare on
turn one and he is therefore the only performer whose bar I can raise before the
first enemy attack lands — protecting him is protecting Ousia Surge's damage
number. One sentence, and I could have said it on floor 2. Where I failed to
protect him it was always the same way: one big hit is worth more than any bar I
can build in a turn (16 into an 8-bar at the elite), so "protecting" him is only
possible against small multi-hit enemies, which is a strange shape for the kit's
central resource.

**5. Did the three read as three, or as one Osty three times.**
**As three, but I only ever met two of them properly, and one of the three is
carried entirely by its glossary line rather than by play.** The Bow glossary is
what differentiates them and it is a single line I saw on almost every screen:

> "**Bow** — A departure effect, earned by Spend only. Usher: 4 Block.
> Chevalmarin: Hydro on all. Crabaletta: 8 damage."

Block / element / damage is three genuinely different jobs. In play:

- The **Usher** read as a body: 4 Block on departure, a "performed… it moved 3"
  each turn that I could never see the effect of.
- **Chevalmarin** read as clearly distinct — her performances actually printed
  ("It moved 5", "It moved 4"), they hit both Slugs, and they hung **Hydro Auras**
  on them, which is the only time in the run one of my performers did something a
  card in my hand could not have done.
- **Crabaletta** I saw once, for one turn, at 1 Fanfare, before she was killed by a
  22-damage hit. I never once saw her Bow's 8 damage. So her identity reached me
  only as text.

So: three on paper, two in the hand, and one of those two (the Usher) whose whole
per-turn contribution was a number I could not trace. They are not one Osty three
times — but the difference between them lives mostly in a departure effect that
only fires when I deliberately kill them, which means I met their identities least
often in exactly the fights where I most needed them alive.
