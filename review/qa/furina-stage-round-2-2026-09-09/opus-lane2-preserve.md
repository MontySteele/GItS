## Fight 1 — Corpse Slug (1) 25 HP, Corpse Slug (2) 27 HP

Opening state: Usher in the lead seat at 3 Fanfare, from Salon Solitaire. The glossary told me what that meant: *"Fanfare — A performer's own bar. Attacks hit your Block, then the lead performer's Fanfare, then you. No cap."* So the Usher is a second block pool that survives the turn.

**Turn 1.** Hand: Soloist's Solicitation (6 dmg), Stage Presence (6 Block), Scene Change (0, rotate), Curtain Rise (7 dmg; *Spend 3: deal 13 instead*), Ousia Surge (damage = lead's Fanfare).

Played **Curtain Rise on B** first. This was the turn's real decision and it was a good one: the Usher sat at exactly 3, so the Spend would empty him, and the Bow glossary said *"Usher: 4 Block"*. I was trading a renewable 3-point shield for 6 extra damage plus 4 Block, one time. **Rejected:** Ousia Surge, which would have read the same bar for 3 damage without spending it — strictly worse now, and the reason I rejected it is the reason the two cards are interesting side by side (one reads the bar, one eats it). Then **Soloist on B** and **Stage Presence**; rejected Scene Change because with one performer a rotation moves nobody.

Result printed exactly as promised: `Usher left the stage: emptied by a Spend, so it takes a Bow. / Usher took a Bow. It moved 4.` B went 27 → 14, I had 10 Block, took nothing.

**Turn 2.** Frail 2 on me. Hand drew **five skills and no attack**. That is the first appearance of the run's defining problem. Played **Salon Début** (summon), then **Warm Reception** (raise 3 on the back performer + draw), which put the resummoned Usher to 4, then **Charlotte** for block. **Rejected:** Let the People Rejoice, which reads "Spend all Fanfare on stage" and would have dealt 0 into an empty stage; and Pneuma Refrain, block equal to a bar that did not exist. Three of five cards in that hand were live only because I had spent a card to make a stage first.

**Turn 3.** B at 8, A at 25. Killed B with Chevreuse (7) + Soloist (6), specifically because both slugs printed *"Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it, becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength."* **The stun was the point:** I paid 4 permanent Strength on the survivor to skip its next turn. **Rejected:** spreading the damage across both, which would have left two live attackers. Then resummoned the Usher with his own card.

**Turn 4.** A stunned as predicted — I took 0. Two Soloists into A, Regal Bearing for the Weak. I also played **Scene Change** here purely to see what a rotation does with one performer: nothing, as the text implies. That was a tester's spend of a free card, not a decision.

**Turn 5.** A at 13, debuff intent, no damage coming. Chevreuse (7) + Curtain Rise (7, base — the stage was empty so the Spend rider could not fire) = 14. Dead.

**Judgement:** the fight had one real decision (spend the Usher on turn 1 or keep him) and one good read (kill for the stun). The two attackless hands in between were not decisions, they were shortages.

## Fight 2 — Sludge Spinner 39 HP

**Turn 1.** Again an all-skill hand. Played **Warm Reception** (Usher 3 → 6, draw 1) over **Gentilhomme Usher** (Usher 3 → 6, no draw) — same raise, one more card, so not really a choice, just a strictly-dominated pair sitting in the same hand. The draw gave Curtain Rise, and **that** made the turn: Usher at 6 meant Curtain Rise could Spend 3 for 13 damage **and leave him alive at 3**. Then Gentilhomme Usher raised him back to 6. That loop — raise past the Spend cost, spend, raise again — is the best thing in the kit and I found it here by accident of the draw. **Rejected:** Stage Presence, because Fanfare persists between turns and Block does not, so banking beat blocking.

**Turn 2.** Took 0. Weak on me shrank the printed numbers on my own cards (Chevreuse read "Deal 5 damage" instead of 7, Soloist 4 instead of 6) — the faces update, which is genuinely good. Chevreuse + Soloist + Stage Presence.

**Turn 3.** Stage empty again. Here I played the **Glam Understudy** for the first time and it summoned **two** performers off one 0-cost card: `Chevalmarin took the lead seat at 1 Fanfare. / Crabaletta took the back seat at 1 Fanfare.` Then **Salon Début** for a third. Rising Applause on the back seat, Charlotte for block. **Rejected:** Ousia Surge, which would have read the new lead's 1 Fanfare for 1 damage — the payoff card is dead exactly when you have just rebuilt the stage.

**Turn 4 — the fight's actual lesson.** The log printed:

> **Chevalmarin** performed from the lead seat. It moved 3.
> **Crabaletta** performed from the middle seat. It moved 5.
> **Usher** performed from the back seat. It moved 3.

Eight damage on the enemy and a Hydro aura appeared on it, with no card of mine having done any of it. **Every seat performs, not just the lead**, and each performer has its own number. Nothing on any card face told me this; I learned it by watching an enemy lose HP I had not spent. That single discovery changed how I played the rest of the run: bodies on stage are the damage engine, and cards are the burst.

Finished with Soloist + Ousia Surge (2, off Chevalmarin's small bar) + Stage Presence; the performers landed the kill on my end turn. **Judgement:** the fight's real decision was made at the *draft* — Understudy off the Neow enchant — and on turn 3 when I chose bodies over burst. Turn 4's kill was that plan paying off, not a dead turn.

## Fight 3 — Toadpole (1) 25 HP, Toadpole (2) 23 HP

**Turn 1.** Salon Début for a second body, then Curtain Rise on B (the Usher at 3 was emptied by the Spend and Bowed for 4), then Regal Bearing. **Rejected:** the pure-block line (Stage Presence + Regal Bearing + summon), because two bodies with no damage against 48 enemy HP is a losing race.

**Turn 2 — the best-printed moment in the run.** Chevreuse's face carried a live rider:

> *Reaction preview: Vaporize* — The triggering hit deals 1.5x damage and consumes the aura. Into that Hydro aura this card's 7 lands 10.

That is the only place all run where the screen did the arithmetic for me before I committed. I played it and it landed 10, exactly as printed.

**And then the fight punished me invisibly.** A carried *"Thorns 2 — When hit by an attack, deal 2 damage back."* My whole stage was Crabaletta at 2 Fanfare. The 2 points of Thorns went through my (zero) Block, into the lead performer's bar, emptied it, and killed my only performer:

> **Crabaletta** left the stage: emptied by a hit, so no Bow.

Two points of reflected damage deleted my entire stage. I had read Thorns and I had read the Fanfare rule, and I still did not put them together, because nothing on the Chevreuse card warns that attacking is how you lose performers. That is the sharpest thing I found this round.

It also cost me the Companion test: I played **Charlotte** immediately after, and by then the stage was empty, so the Companion clause performed nobody.

**Turns 3–4.** Stage empty; Rising Applause, Scene Change and Ousia Surge were all dead cards in the same hand. Killed A with Witness Stand's Vulnerable into Soloist (6 → 9), then a second Soloist off the Witness Stand draw. Turn 4: Understudy for two bodies, then two Soloists killed B.

## Fight 4 — Living Fog 80 HP (+ Gas Bomb minions)

**Turn 1.** Ran the loop deliberately for the first time: Warm Reception (Usher to 6) → Curtain Rise Spend 3 for 13, Usher survives at 3 → Regal Bearing. Took 0.

**Turn 2 — Smoggy.** *"Smoggy 1 — You can only play 1 Skill per turn."* My hand was four skills and one attack. Against a deck that is roughly two-thirds skills this is close to a lockout, and it recurred every turn of the fight. I spent the one skill on Rising Applause (Usher to 8, the biggest renewable shield available) over Witness Stand and Stage Presence; **rejected** Stage Presence because 6 Block for one turn loses to 5 Fanfare that persists and also feeds Spend.

Then I tried to play Scene Change, which costs 0, and got **the run's only refusal**:

> `'Scene Change' cannot be played right now: something else on the board is stopping you right now.`

It did not name Smoggy. Later in the same fight the *card face* did name it — `*Smog* — You cannot play additional Skills this turn` and `what is on YOU right now is: Smoggy 1, Fanfare 4` — so the information exists; it just is not in the refusal that a player actually reads first.

**Turn 3 — the kit's best turn.** Usher at 8, three enemies-worth of pressure. I played, in this order: **Understudy** (0 → Chevalmarin and Crabaletta join, three on stage), **Ousia Surge** (reads the lead's bar *without spending it* — 8 damage), then **Let the People Rejoice** (spends all Fanfare on stage: 10 to everything, every performer Bows, then returns at 1). The order was the whole decision: Ousia had to fire while the bar was still fat, because Rejoice would flatten every bar to 1. The log paid out all three Bows by name — Usher 4 Block, Crabaletta 8 damage, Chevalmarin's Hydro on all — and a Vaporize fired off the back of it. Fog 60 → 34, Gas Bomb dead, and I still had 4 Block. **That is the sequencing puzzle the kit is built around and it felt excellent.**

**Turns 4–6.** Ground it down. Turn 5 was another all-skill hand under Smoggy: five cards, one legal play. I took Stage Presence and let the performers work. Turn 6, Witness Stand's Vulnerable turned Curtain Rise's face into "Deal 10 damage. Spend 3: deal 19 instead" and finished it.

This is the best fight I played.

## Fight 6 — Elite: Terror Eel, 140 HP

The Eel printed *"Shriek 70 — The first time Terror Eel's HP reaches 70 or below, it becomes Stunned."* That is a stated threshold, and building a turn to cross it is the fight.

**Turns 1–4.** Witness Stand's Vulnerable into a Spent Curtain Rise for 19. Then a rebuild turn (Gentilhomme Usher + Salon Début + a free 0-cost Stage Combat, which prints "+3 Block if an enemy intends to attack" and is the one card in my deck that dodges Smoggy). Then Understudy + Tidal Flourish (Spend 2 emptied the Usher, whose Bow gave me the Block back) + Soloist. Then, on a turn where the Eel only telegraphed 9, I banked instead: Rising Applause +5 and Mademoiselle Crabaletta +3 put Crabaletta on **10 Fanfare**, which absorbed the entire next attack by itself.

**Turn 5 — the payoff.** Eel at 79, threshold at 70. Witness Stand (Vulnerable) → Soloist (9) → Tidal Flourish (13, Spend 2 emptied Crabaletta, **whose Bow hit for 12**). 79 → 45, well past 70, and the intent flipped to `Stunned`. That is 34 damage in one turn out of a deck whose cards read 6 and 5, and every point of it came from having decided two turns earlier to fatten a bar. **The decision was made at turn 4, not turn 5.**

**Turn 7 — where it nearly went wrong.** I picked up **Vulnerable 99** ("Receive 50% more damage… for 99 turns") — a permanent debuff, printed without any hint of where it came from — and the Eel telegraphed **33**. At 39 HP that was potentially lethal. I played Chevreuse (Vaporize, 10) + Curtain Rise (Spend, 13) + Regal Bearing (3 Block **and the Weak**, which visibly moved the printed intent from 33 to 24) + Stage Combat (3 damage, 3 Block). Survived on 30. **Rejected:** the pure-damage line, which could not reach lethal (28 max against 40 HP) and would have left me on the wrong side of a 33.

**Turn 8 — the brick.** Eel on 9 HP. My hand: Stage Presence, Let the People Rejoice, Pneuma Refrain, Final Bow, Warm Reception. **Five cards, zero attacks, and an empty stage** — so Rejoice would have dealt 0, Pneuma 0, Final Bow had no lead to bow. The only reason the turn was not a total blank is that Warm Reception draws, and the draw was a summon. I could not kill a 9-HP enemy. The turn after that, the mirror: an all-attack hand, and Chevreuse + Stage Combat ended it instantly.

## Things I could not read, or that contradicted themselves

**1. Two different things are both called "Fanfare", and both were on screen at once.** The glossary says:

> **Fanfare** — A performer's own bar. Attacks hit your Block, then the lead performer's Fanfare, then you. No cap.

and the status line on my own character says:

> **Fanfare 3 (buff)** — Generated by losing HP, and by spending, absorbing or paying out Encore — and by Center Stage plays. Cards read it; nothing spends it. It fades by 20% at the start of each of your turns, never below the baseline your cards have built.

These are two different resources with the same name, contradictory rules ("no cap" vs. "fades by 20%"; a bar cards *spend* vs. a bar "nothing spends"), sitting on the same screen. I never worked out what the second one does. **No card I held or was ever offered mentioned reading it.** It ticked up when I lost HP and decayed when I did not, and it never once affected a decision I made — I could not find the thing it feeds.

**2. "Encore" is glossed but does not exist.** Printed on many screens:

> **Encore** — After Block it absorbs damage before HP. One pool, as each lands: a card pays to resolve, a member spends 1 to perform or acts at 3/4.

I never saw an Encore number, an Encore card, or an Encore pool in ~226 actions. The clause "a member spends 1 to perform" also flatly contradicts what I watched happen: on fight 8 turn 2 the log read `Usher performed from the lead seat. It moved 3` with the Usher going **3 → 3** across the turn, and on fight 2 turn 4 Chevalmarin performed and went **1 → 2**, gaining rather than spending.

**3. The Companion rule is real, load-bearing, and printed nowhere near the cards it governs.** I learned on a floor-3 *card reward screen* that:

> **Companion** — A card titled with a character's name, a dash, then its own. […] On Furina's stage playing one performs the front member, then sends it to the back; an empty stage performs nobody.

I had been holding and playing **Charlotte — First-Person Shutter** and **Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire** since turn 1 of the run without knowing either one rotates my stage and fires a free performance. Neither card's face says "Companion" or mentions the stage. That is two of my six extra cards whose most important clause I did not know I owned. The same gloss also says *"Card rewards after a fight offer a fourth, Companion, choice"* — the screen it was printed on offered three.

**4. "Bow — a departure effect, earned by Spend only" — and then Final Bow.** The Bow glossary says Spend is the only way to earn one. **Final Bow** reads "The lead performer takes a Bow and leaves. Gain Block equal to its Fanfare." That is a Bow that is not earned by a Spend, printed on the same screen as the rule saying it cannot happen.

**5. The stage log rewrites its own seat names.** On fight 2 turn 3, after Understudy, the log said `Crabaletta took the back seat at 1 Fanfare`. One action later, after a third summon, the same log line read `Crabaletta took the **middle** seat at 1 Fanfare`. The history is being restated, not appended. Worse, the log twice printed two different performers as occupying the same seat in the same turn:

> **Chevalmarin** performed from the lead seat. It moved 2.
> **Crabaletta** performed from the **back** seat. It moved 5.
> **Usher** performed from the **back** seat. It moved 3.

**6. "It moved N" is never explained.** The same phrase covers Block (`Usher took a Bow. It moved 4` = 4 Block), damage (`Crabaletta took a Bow. It moved 12`), and a performance (`Usher performed from the lead seat. It moved 3` — 3 damage, I eventually inferred). Sometimes it is simply absent: `Crabaletta performed from the middle seat.` with no number and no explanation.

**7. Numbers that do not reconcile.** Fight 6, round 8 → 9: the Eel sat at **9 HP**, the log said `Usher performed from the lead seat. It moved 3`, and the Eel was still at **9 HP** on the next screen. Fight 1, round 3: I was carrying 4 Block with the Usher on 3 Fanfare against 14 incoming, which should have put 7 through, and I lost 4 HP. I could not reconstruct the arithmetic of Fanfare absorption from anything printed at any point in the run.

**8. Where auras come from.** The screen states flatly: *"An element comes from a CARD that prints one and from nothing else: a potion, a relic or an enemy applies none unless its own face says so."* On fight 3 turn 2 a Toadpole was wearing a **Hydro Aura** and the only thing that had acted was Crabaletta performing. No card of mine prints Hydro; Chevalmarin's *Bow* does, and Chevalmarin was not on stage. Either performing applies elements — which no face says — or the rule as printed is wrong.

**9. Vulnerable 99 arrived unattributed.** Fight 6 round 7 I acquired `Vulnerable 99 (debuff) — Receive 50% more damage from any attack or card hit for 99 turns` with no line in the turn log saying what applied it, on a turn the enemy telegraphed 33 damage. A permanent 1.5× on incoming damage is the single largest state change of the run and I could not tell you what caused it.

**10. The refusal that names nothing.** Quoted in full above: `'Scene Change' cannot be played right now: something else on the board is stopping you right now.` The card *face* on a later screen did better — `The feed does not say which thing is stopping it. A hook is something already on the board, and what is on YOU right now is: Smoggy 1, Fanfare 4.` — but the refusal a player hits first is the one that says nothing.

**11. No seed, and no ascension.** Neither was printed on any screen. I opened at 62/78 with no explanation.

**12. Something I did read well, and should say so.** The reaction preview (`Into that Hydro aura this card's 7 lands 10`), the Frail/Weak/Vulnerable-adjusted numbers printed directly on card faces, the `CANNOT BE PLAYED: you do not have enough energy` line, and the potion-slot warning on reward screens (*"A potion claimed now has nowhere to go, and the game says nothing when one is dropped — so this page will not claim it until a slot is free"*) are all excellent. The problems above are concentrated in the *stage*, not in the general page.

## The kit, after 8 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

- **Spend the bar, or keep it** — on the turn, and the best thing in the kit. Curtain Rise reads "Deal 7 damage. Spend 3: deal 13 instead" and the Spend is automatic if the bar can pay, so the decision is really made one card earlier: *do I raise the Usher to 6 first so the Spend leaves him alive at 3, or do I let the Spend empty him for the Bow?* Fight 2 turn 1 (raise to 6, spend to 3, raise back to 6) and fight 1 turn 1 (spend 3 from exactly 3, take the 4-Block Bow) are the same card making two genuinely different plays.
- **Bank now or burst now** — earlier in the fight, and this is where the kit's best turns are decided. Fight 6 turn 4 I put 8 Fanfare onto Crabaletta on a quiet turn; fight 6 turn 5 that turned into 34 damage and a Shriek stun. The trade is tempo against a bar that is simultaneously my block, my Ousia damage, my Rejoice damage and my Spend fuel. **One resource doing four jobs is what makes the choice interesting**, and the kit is at its best when a turn asks which of the four I need.
- **Ordering within the turn** — on the turn. Fight 4 turn 3: Ousia Surge *reads* the lead's bar, Rejoice *spends* every bar. Playing them in the wrong order costs 7 damage and nothing on either card warns you. That is a real, discoverable, satisfying sequencing puzzle.
- **Which enemy to kill, and when** — on the turn, off printed enemy text. Ravenous ("the survivor is Stunned and gains 4 Strength") turned fight 5 into three consecutive zero-damage turns because I chose to kill the 4-HP slug rather than spread damage. Shriek 70 did the same at the elite. These were the most fun decisions of the run and **none of them came from the kit** — they came from the enemies' faces.
- **At the draft:** taking the Glam Understudy at Neow's card reward over Glam Ousia Surge. That one pick is why the stage ever got to three bodies, and it shaped every fight after it.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Scene Change** (0 cost, rotate front to back) was in my hand perhaps eight times and I played it twice: once to see what it did, once by mistake. With one performer it does nothing; with three, rotating one step moves the *wrong* body forward, and getting the fat back-seat performer into the lead needs **two** rotations, which is two cards. It never once solved a problem.
- **Pneuma Refrain** (Block equal to the back performer's Fanfare) was live exactly once in eight fights. The rest of the time the stage was empty or the back seat was on 1.
- **Ousia Surge** (damage equal to the lead's Fanfare) was live twice. Every time I rebuilt the stage the bars reset to 1, so the payoff card was reliably dead on the turn the stage came back.
- **Salon Début / Gentilhomme Usher / Warm Reception / Rising Applause** are four cards that all do "put a number on the stage", and playing them was almost never a decision — whichever one was in hand got played. Warm Reception and Gentilhomme Usher raise the same 3; Warm Reception also draws. I never once preferred Gentilhomme Usher when I held both.
- **Potions.** I finished the run holding all three and never drank one. Stable Serum (retain hand) and Droplet of Precognition (pull from draw pile) both solve the brick-hand problem, and on the two turns I most needed them — fight 4 turn 5, fight 6 turn 8 — I did not reach for them. That is my mistake to own, but it is also a signal: the brick hands felt like *the deck's* nature rather than a problem a potion could fix.
- **The single biggest source of automatic turns was not a card, it was the hand.** I counted **six turns** in which I drew a hand of four or five stage cards with the stage empty, or a hand of pure skills under Smoggy. Those turns had one legal play and no decision at all.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own text.**

All twelve items in the section above; the four that actually cost me play were: the **two meanings of "Fanfare"** (I still cannot tell you what the buff does), **Encore** being glossed with rules for a resource that does not appear, the **Companion** clause governing two cards I owned from turn 1 without either face mentioning it, and the fact that **performers perform from every seat** — the engine of the entire kit — being discoverable only by noticing an enemy losing HP I had not spent.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted: Scene Change.** Free, and still not worth the click. It is the verb the kit needs (get the banked back-seat bar into the lead where Ousia and Spend can reach it) and it does the job so slowly — one seat per card, two cards for the rotation you actually want — that it never justified itself.
- **Happiest to draw: Let the People Rejoice.** "Spend all Fanfare on stage. Deal that much damage to ALL enemies. Every performer takes a Bow, then returns at 1." It is the one card that cashes every bank at once, pays out all three Bows by name, resets the stage rather than wrecking it, and it won three fights outright: it killed the Gas Bomb at fight 4, softened all three slugs at fight 5, and caught the Fat Gremlin before it escaped with 60 of my gold. Drawing it made me want to spend the next two turns setting it up, which is exactly what a payoff card should do. Honourable mention to the Glam **Understudy** — two bodies for zero energy, and the reason the engine ever started.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a good one.** The opening hand held Curtain Rise ("Deal 7 damage. Spend 3: deal 13 instead") with the Usher sitting on exactly 3 Fanfare, and Ousia Surge ("Deal damage equal to the lead performer's Fanfare") reading the same 3. So turn one asked: read the bar for 3, or eat the bar for 13 damage plus a 4-Block Bow and no shield for the enemy turn? I chose to eat it, and the very next turn punished me for it with an attackless hand and an empty stage — which is a decision *and* a consequence, on turn one, before I knew a single rule. That is a strong opening.

## The five debrief questions

**1. On turn one of fight one, which line, and why.**

**Curtain Rise on Corpse Slug (2), with the Spend firing, then Soloist's Solicitation on the same body, then Stage Presence.**

The Usher had exactly 3 Fanfare and Curtain Rise's rider costs exactly 3, so the Spend would empty him — and the glossary told me an emptied performer Bows, and that the Usher's Bow is 4 Block. So the line converted a 3-point shield into **6 extra damage plus 4 Block**, which is strictly more than the 3 damage that shield would have absorbed. I focused both attacks on B rather than the lower-HP A because both slugs printed Ravenous, and I wanted the eventual +4 Strength to land on the debuffer rather than on the attacker.

I rejected Ousia Surge (3 damage off the same bar) and I rejected saving the Usher, and I was right about the arithmetic and wrong about the consequence: turn two arrived with five skills, no attack, and no stage, and three of those five cards did nothing.

**2. When you spent from a small bar, did you know it was small at the time, and did the spend feel like a play or a loophole?**

**I always knew, and it never felt like a loophole — it felt like a real price, and once like a mistake.**

The bar is printed in two places on every combat screen — `lead: Usher 3` on the stage line, and the card's own rider — so "small" is never a surprise. The clause that matters is *"It fires in full even if the bar is short"*, and that is what makes the spend a **play**: you get the full 13 either way, so the only question is whether the performer survives it. Twice I deliberately raised past the cost first so the spend would leave the body alive (fight 2 turn 1, fight 4 turn 1); several times I spent from exactly the cost and took the Bow as the consolation (fight 1 turn 1, fight 3 turn 1). Both felt bought.

The one time it felt bad was **fight 6 turn 5**, where Tidal Flourish's "Spend 2" fired off Crabaletta's 2 automatically. I had not chosen to lose her; the card decided. That is the one place the design reads as taking a decision away rather than offering one: **the Spend is compulsory whenever the bar can pay it**, so a small bar plus a Spend card is a body you are going to lose whether or not that is what you wanted. And "it fires in full even if the bar is short" combined with "the emptied performer Bows" means a 1-Fanfare performer and a 3-Fanfare performer buy the identical effect — which does read, faintly, as a loophole in the other direction: the cheapest possible bar buys the most expensive rider.

**3. Did you want a second performer on stage, and what for?**

**Badly, and for three separate reasons, only one of which I understood at the time I first wanted it.**

I first summoned a second body defensively — attacks hit Block, then the lead's Fanfare, then me, and a second body is a second turn of that. Then fight 2 turn 4 showed me the real reason: **every seat performs**, so a second performer is a permanent, free, un-blockable ~5 damage a turn that costs no energy and no card after the summon. Against the 140-HP Terror Eel and the 115-HP Gardeners, the performers did more total damage than my cards did. Third, more bodies means a bigger total bar, and Let the People Rejoice reads the *total*: 13 Fanfare across three bodies is 13 to every enemy plus three Bows off one 2-cost card.

And the answer to "what for" is sharpened by what happened when I got them: the back seat is stated to be safe (*"the reserve: nothing hits it"*), so a second and third performer let me **bank on a body that cannot be hit** while a front body soaks. That is a genuinely good structure. What undercuts it is how easily the whole stage falls — fight 5 turn 3 lost all three at once, and fight 3 lost my only one to 2 points of Thorns.

**4. Which performer did you protect, and could you say why in one sentence?**

**Crabaletta, and yes: she performs for 5 where the Usher does 3 and Chevalmarin does 2, and her Bow is 8 damage, so she is worth the most both alive and dead.**

Concretely, I spent Rising Applause and Mademoiselle Crabaletta on her at fight 6 turn 4 to put her on 10 Fanfare, and I chose Mademoiselle Crabaletta out of the fight-4 reward pack specifically because it is the only *named* summon — Salon Début and Understudy are both "a random performer", and randomness is exactly what you cannot afford when one of the three is clearly the best. I protected her by parking her in the back seat where nothing hits, and I lost her twice anyway: once to a compulsory Spend I did not want to make, once when a hit reached the lead.

**5. Did the three read as three, or as one Usher three times?**

**They read as three when they Bowed, and as one Usher three times the rest of the time.**

The Bow line is the only place the game insists they are different, and it is a good line — *"Usher: 4 Block. Chevalmarin: Hydro on all. Crabaletta: 8 damage"* — three genuinely different departure effects, one defensive, one elemental, one violent. When Let the People Rejoice fired all three at once at fight 4 turn 3 and the log paid out block, an aura and 8 damage in three named lines, they were unmistakably three characters.

But a Bow only happens when a performer *leaves*, so the distinction only exists at the moment I lose them. **While they are alive they are interchangeable buckets of Fanfare.** Every card that touches the stage speaks in seats, not names: "Raise 3 on the back performer", "damage equal to the lead performer's Fanfare", "Block equal to the back performer's Fanfare", "the front performer moves to the back seat". Nothing reads *Crabaletta*. Their per-turn performance numbers do differ (3 / 2 / 5), which is the one live distinction — but no card face, tooltip or glossary anywhere states those numbers, so I only learned them by watching the log for eight fights, and I never once got to make a decision on the basis of *which* body was in a seat. Chevalmarin applying Hydro when she performs (which is how Crashing Waves gets its +5, and how Chevreuse gets its Vaporize) is a genuinely lovely piece of identity that is, as far as I can tell, **printed absolutely nowhere**.

Two of the three also have a card that summons them by name (Gentilhomme Usher, Mademoiselle Crabaletta) and Chevalmarin does not, which made her feel like the one I could never ask for.

So: three at the exit, one at the table.
