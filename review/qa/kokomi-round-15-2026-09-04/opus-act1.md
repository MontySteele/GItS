## Fight 1 — Sludge Spinner, 37/37

Opening screen: HP 64/80, energy 3, hand Strike ×3 / Defend / Slack Water.
Enemy intent printed as two parts: `Aggressive (Attack) — the number on its
icon is 8, a part it MAY perform` **and also** `Strategic (Debuff)`.

**Turn 1 — Slack Water on Sludge Spinner, then Strike, then Strike.**
Slack Water prints "Deal 4 damage. Apply 1 Weak. **Plan:** Apply 1 Weak to ALL
enemies." The rejected alternative was writing it as a Plan on the
Bake-Kurage: against exactly one enemy the Plan's whole upside ("to ALL") is
zero, and it would have delayed the Weak by a turn while the enemy was already
telegraphing a hit. Playing it now also fired Tamakushi Casket — the enemy went
37 → 31, i.e. 4 from the card plus 2 from the relic, and the intent number fell
8 → 6 on the same screen, which is the clearest single piece of feedback the
kit gave me all round. Two Strikes followed because a Defend's 5 block is worth
less than 6 damage when I am at 64 and it is at 31.

**Turn 2 — Kurage's Oath onto the Bake-Kurage (Plan), Tide Chart, Strike,
Defend.** The card reads "Deal 2 damage to ALL enemies. **Plan:** Deal 7 damage
to ALL enemies" — 2, not the 3 it prints elsewhere, because I was carrying Weak
1 (see (c)). The rejected alternative was playing Oath directly: 1 energy for 2
damage now versus 1 energy for 7 damage one turn later, and the Plan keyword
explicitly says "your Weak and Strength do not [count]", so the planned copy
dodges the Weak I was wearing. That is a genuine, printed, legible trade and it
was the first turn of the run that felt like a decision rather than a sum.
Tide Chart ("Draw 1 card for each Plan the Bake-Kurage holds") went second on
purpose, after the Plan was written, and drew 1.

**Turn 3 — the Plan carried out for 7** (the screen printed
`Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 7 — the 7 is damage` with `Sludge Spinner lost 7
HP` under it). Enemy at 8. Slack Water (4+2 relic) then Strike (6) killed it.
No alternative rejected here worth the name: 8 HP against 12 available damage
is arithmetic, not a choice.

Screen and outcome agreed everywhere in this fight.

Rewards: 15 gold, Radiant Tincture, and the Glam'd card reward — Cleansing
Wave / Rally / Moon's Reflection / **Sayu — Naptime**, all four carrying
`(Glam)`. I took **Sayu — Naptime** (0 cost, gain 4 Block, next turn draw 2 if
you play no Attacks this turn). The rejected pick was Cleansing Wave, whose
Glam'd Replay makes it 10 block for 1 energy: I chose Sayu because the Plan
engine spends its energy a turn early, so the turn that writes two Plans has
nothing left for a 1-cost blocker, and a 0-cost one is the card that fits the
hole the kit digs.

**Turn 2 — Strike killed Toadpole (1); Sayu — Naptime.** Toadpole (1) had come
back with `Thorns 2` and an intent of 3×3. Killing a 2-HP body for 6 damage
wastes 4, and I paid 2 thorns for it; the rejected alternative was blocking the

for one turn of safety. The interesting part is Sayu: playing it printed
`Naptime 4 (buff) — If you play no Attacks this turn, draw 4 cards at the start
of your next turn`. Glam's Replay had stacked the rider from 2 to 4 — and I had
already played a Strike that turn, so the buff was applied, sat on my status
bar reading like a promise, and paid nothing. The card's own text is honest
("if you play no Attacks this turn"); nothing on the screen flags that the
condition is already dead at the moment it is granted. See (c).

**Turn 3 — Strike, Strike, Defend.** Toadpole at 17 with Thorns 2. Rejected
alternative: one Strike behind two Defends. Rejected because the number of
attacks needed to kill it is fixed at three either way, so the thorns tax is
paid three times regardless and stretching the fight only adds unblocked
enemy turns. Cost 8 HP (4 thorns + 4 through a Defend) exactly as the screen
implied.

**Turn 4 — Strike killed it at 5 HP.** No decision.

Rewards: 12 gold and a card. Choices were Ambush / Tide Wall / Moon's
Reflection / **Thoma — Crimson Ooyoroi**. I took Thoma: 1 energy, "for 2 turns,
whenever you play an Attack, deal 5 Pyro damage to a random enemy and gain 3
Block", because every Elemental Reaction box I had read so far ended in
`NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE: Hydro is the only element this screen can
supply`, and Thoma is the second element. The rejected pick was Ambush (5
damage / Plan 12), the better raw rate — I took it out of the next reward

## Fight 3 — Corpse Slug (1) 27/27, Corpse Slug (2) 26/26

Both carried `Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it,
becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength`. Incoming 14 against my 33 HP.

**Turn 1 — Radiant Tincture, Thoma, Strike, Strike, Defend.** The rejected
alternative was holding the potion: I spent it because a 33-HP body in a
two-body fight is where a 4-energy swing is worth most, and Thoma's window is
only 2 turns, so I wanted the extra energy inside that window. Thoma's first
proc put `Pyro Aura 2` on Corpse Slug (1) — the first non-Hydro aura of the
run.

**Turn 2 — Slack Water on Corpse Slug (1), Strike on Corpse Slug (2), Kurage's
Oath as a Plan.** This is the turn the kit paid off. Slack Water's card face
had grown a new line: `*Reaction preview: Vaporize* — Pyro meets Hydro: this
hit deals 1.5x damage and consumes the aura.` The rejected alternative was
leading with Kurage's Oath (3 to ALL, also Hydro, also a Vaporize): I led with
Slack Water because Vaporize multiplies, so it belongs on the bigger hit, and
Slack Water's 4 beats Oath's 3. Corpse Slug (1) died inside that beat (6
vaporized + 2 casket + two 5-Pyro procs against 17 HP), Corpse Slug (2) ate it
and came out `Stunned` with `Strength 4` at 8 HP. **The reaction preview
appearing on the card face, in the hand, before I committed, is the single best
piece of design I met this round.**

**Turn 3 — Kurage's Oath as a Plan again.** With the slug stunned it could not
punish a turn spent on setup, so 1 energy for 7 later beat 1 energy for 3 now.
The Plan left it at 1 HP; the printed 7 against 8 HP was the one place where I
wished the Plan number were visible next to the enemy's HP on the same screen
so I could see the shortfall without doing it in my head.

**Turn 4 — Strike killed it.** The reward screen printed the Bake-Kurage's last
carry-out under a note explaining it prints there because a Plan whose kill
ends a fight never reaches a battle screen. Good.

Rewards: 14 gold, Power Potion, and **Ambush** (5 damage / Plan 12) over Shell
Guard / Rally / Shikanoin Heizou — Heartstopper Strike. Ambush is 12 damage for
1 energy on the Plan line, the best rate I had been offered, and it feeds the
engine I was already running.

Each carried `Skittish 6 — The first time Phantasmal Gardener is hit each turn,

Power Potion first: Song of Pearls / The Clouds Like Waves Rippling / Treatise.
I took **Song of Pearls** (once per turn, when the Bake-Kurage carries out a

**Turn 1 — Kurage's Oath as a Plan, Ambush as a Plan, Coral Bulwark.**
The reasoning I want on the record, because Skittish makes it a real puzzle:
the 6 Block arrives *after* the first hit each turn, so the first hit of a turn
lands in full and every later hit that turn is 6 worse. That makes Kurage's
Oath's "7 damage to ALL" as a Plan worth 28 into four unblocked bodies, while
the same card played directly is 3 to ALL *and* switches on four Skittish
shields that would eat the rest of my turn. The rejected alternative was
therefore playing Oath now and blocking with the spare energy; I rejected it
because it converts a 28-damage turn into a 12-damage one. I also worked out
the Plan **order** matters and planned Oath before Ambush: the Bake-Kurage
prints `carried out at the start of this turn, front first` and lists them in
the order written, so a small Plan written first would spend the Skittish
trigger and blunt the big one. Result at the top of round 2: 27 → 8, 31 → 24,
26 → 19, 30 → 23. Cost: 9 HP.

**Turn 2 — Strike on Gardener (1), Defend, Defend.** Hand was three Defends and
two Strikes — no Plan card at all. Rejected alternative: two Strikes on two
different bodies for 12 spread. I took 6 + 13 block instead because the next
turn was going to draw the Plan cards back (draw pile was empty) and I wanted
HP to spend on that turn. Note the Skittish arithmetic that made me spread
rather than double up: two Strikes on the *same* gardener is 6 damage, not 12,
because the second one lands entirely into the 6 block the first one bought.

**Turn 3 — Sayu — Naptime, Thoma, Strike (killed Gardener (1) at 2 HP),
Defend.** Took 0 damage. Rejected alternative: skipping Thoma to add a Defend —
rejected because Thoma is 3 block *and* 5 damage per attack for two turns, so
it out-blocks a Defend the moment I play two attacks under it, which I did the

**Turn 4 — Sayu, Strike, Strike (spread across two bodies), Ambush as a
Plan.** Ooyoroi expired at end of turn, so both Strikes had to be spent now;

attack, and I rejected it because 5 now against 12 later, with Song of Pearls
paying 3 block on the carry-out, is a bad trade in a fight I was now winning.

**Turn 5 — Kurage's Oath as a Plan, Coral Bulwark, Defend.** Three gardeners at
12/13/7 with 16 incoming. The rejected alternative was Oath direct for 3 to ALL
(kills nothing, arms three Skittish shields) or Slack Water on a body for 4 and
a Weak. Planning it converted the turn into 14 block now and 21 damage into
fresh bodies next turn; it killed the 7 and left 5 and 6.

**Turn 6 — Strike killed the 5, Coral Bulwark, Defend.** Took 0.
**Turn 7 — Strike killed the last at 6 HP.** Elite cleared at 33/80.

## Fight 5 — Elite: Skulking Colony, 75/75 (incomplete — budget stop)

`Hardened Shell 20 (buff) — Skulking Colony cannot lose more than 20 HP each
turn.` A damage cap facing a deck whose whole idea is one enormous Plan, which
is a nice piece of adversarial design.

**Turn 1 — Thoma, Slack Water, Strike, Sayu — Naptime.** The rejected
alternative was writing Kurage's Oath as a Plan, which I did not hold, and
more importantly: against a 20-per-turn cap the Plan engine is actively worse
than a wide spread of small hits, because a planned 21 is wasted down to 20 and
arrives a turn late. So this was the one fight all round where I deliberately
did *not* plan. Card damage 6+4+2, Ooyoroi 5+5 = 22, and the enemy went 75 → 55
— exactly the cap, correctly applied. Block 3+3+4 = 10 against a Weak'd 14; I
took 0.

**Turn 2 — Coral Bulwark, Defend, Defend.** Hand was Song of Pearls, three
Defends and Coral Bulwark: no attack, no Plan card. Song of Pearls was the
rejected alternative and it is a real rejection — playing a 1-energy power that
only triggers on a Plan carry-out, in a hand with no Plan card, is one energy
for nothing this turn. 16 block against 14; took 0.

**Turn 3 — Sango Isshin.** With one action left in the budget I spent it on the
new card to see which branch it printed. No Plan had carried out that turn, so
it dealt its fallback 8 (55 → 47) — and the enemy's `Hardened Shell 20` line
changed its *number* to `Hardened Shell 12` while its *text* still read "cannot
lose more than 20 HP each turn". So the number is the remaining allowance this
turn, and the text is the cap; they are two different quantities printed as
one. See (c). I then ended turn on action 120 and took 9, ending at 34/90.

## The kit, after 5 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

The Plan/direct fork is a real, recurring, legible decision and it is the best
thing here. Every Plan card prints both lines side by side, so the trade is
arithmetic I can do in the hand: Kurage's Oath is 3-to-ALL now or 7-to-ALL a
turn late; Ambush is 5 now or 12 late; Coral Bulwark is 6 block now or 8 block
and a Weak late. What makes it a *choice* rather than a rate table is that the
tempo cost is real — you pay the energy on a turn where the enemy is already
swinging, and you only collect if you survive. I planned when I could afford
the turn and played direct when I could not, and I was wrong-footed at least
twice.

Three second-order decisions grew out of it, and these are the ones that made
the round enjoyable:

1. **Plan order.** Plans carry out "front first" in the order written, so
   writing a small Plan before a big one against `Skittish 6` wastes the free
   first hit. That is a decision the screen gives you enough to solve and does
   not solve for you.
2. **Whether to plan at all.** `Hardened Shell 20` on the Skulking Colony makes
   a 21-damage Plan strictly worse than three 7-damage hits. One printed enemy
   buff inverted the kit's whole default, and I could see it coming from the
   card faces alone.
3. **Element sequencing.** Once Thoma put a Pyro aura on a body, the
   `Reaction preview: Vaporize` line told me the 1.5× would go to whichever
   Hydro card I played first, so the order of Slack Water and Kurage's Oath
   became a real decision about which hit to multiply.

The elemental layer also gave the run its one genuinely clever moment: the
Tamakushi Casket turns *any* debuff into a Hydro hit, so Slack Water is
simultaneously a debuff, a 2-damage relic proc and an aura application, and
against a Pyro-aura'd body all three interact at once.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

Every Strike and every Defend. They are 40% of the starting deck and not one of
the ~20 times I played one involved a rejected alternative worth writing down.
The kit's decisions live entirely in the six or seven non-starter cards, and
whenever I drew a hand of Strike/Defend/Strike/Defend/Defend — which happened
twice in the Gardener elite — the turn was a sum, not a choice.

**Tide Chart never earned its slot.** "Draw 1 card for each Plan the
Bake-Kurage holds" is 0 cost and I drew it four times; three of those it drew
zero cards, because you only hold Plans on the turn *after* you write them and
Tide Chart wants to be played *before* you spend your energy. Its own
requirement and its own cost fight each other: the turn where you have two
Plans banked is the turn you already had a big carry-out and did not need the
cards. I would rather have had a second Defend.

**Sayu — Naptime's rider never fired once**, across four plays. "Draw 2 cards
if you play no Attacks this turn" is asking a deck with four Strikes, Slack
Water and Ambush to spend a whole turn not attacking, and there was never a
turn where that was the right play. The 4 block is fine; the rider is decoration.

**Song of Pearls felt automatic in the good sense** — I never once regretted it
and never once made a decision about it.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that seemed to contradict its own printed
text.**

Four things, in descending order of how much they cost me.

1. **The multi-part-intent warning is, on this build, wrong.** Every screen with
   a two-part telegraph printed: *"On this build the damage part of a multi-part
   telegraph has repeatedly not landed, so read such a number as a part the
   enemy MAY perform rather than as damage it is about to deal."* I met three
   such telegraphs and **the damage landed in full every time**: Sludge Spinner
   round 1 (printed 8, Weak'd to 6, I took 6), Sludge Spinner round 2 (printed
   6, I took 6 through 5 block for 1), Skulking Colony round 3 (printed 9, I
   took 9). A hedge that tells me to discount a number I should be blocking is
   worse than no hedge; it pushed me toward offence on turns where the printed
   number was the truth.

2. **Weak reduces a card printed as a `skill`, and the two Weak texts disagree
   about that.** The keyword box says *"Weak — The wearer deals 25% less
   damage"*; my own status line says *"Weak 1 (debuff) — **Attacks** deal 25%
   less damage for 1 turn."* Kurage's Oath is printed `cost 1, skill` and its
   face changed from "Deal 3 damage to ALL enemies" to "Deal 2 damage to ALL
   enemies" while I wore Weak. The card face is honest and updates live, which
   is excellent — but the status line told me skills were safe and the card told
   me they were not.

3. **`Hardened Shell 20` counts down while its text does not.** After 8 damage
   it read `Hardened Shell 12 (buff) — Skulking Colony cannot lose more than 20
   HP each turn.` The number is the remaining allowance this turn; the sentence
   is the per-turn cap. Both are useful, but printing them as one line means the
   line contradicts itself the moment you hit the thing.

4. **`Naptime` is granted after its condition is already dead.** I played Sayu
   after a Strike and got `Naptime 4 (buff) — If you play no Attacks this turn,
   draw 4 cards at the start of your next turn` on my status bar, where it sat
   for a full turn looking like a pending draw and paid nothing. The card's own
   text is correct; the buff line reads as a promise. (Also worth noting, since
   it is nowhere printed: Glam's Replay *stacked* the rider from 2 to 4 rather
   than triggering it twice.)

Two smaller ones. The `NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE` sentence is scoped to the
current hand, not the deck — it appeared on a screen where an enemy wore a Pyro
aura and my draw pile was full of Hydro; it is right, but it reads like a
statement about the fight. And the Plan carry-out report prints the damage per
enemy, which is genuinely good, but it prints on the turn *after* the decision,
so the one number I most wanted while choosing — what a planned Oath would do
to each body *given their current HP* — I always had to compute by hand.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Tide Chart**. Three of four draws were literally blank, and the
fourth drew one card. Runner-up is any Defend after floor 6 — Coral Bulwark
strictly outclasses it and the deck has four.

Happiest to draw: **Kurage's Oath**, without hesitation, and it is not close.
It is the card that makes the Bake-Kurage matter: 1 energy, and the difference
between its two lines is 3 damage and 7 damage per body, so on the four-Gardener
elite it was the difference between a 12-damage turn and a 28-damage turn, and
I had to earn that by surviving the turn in between. Honourable mention to
**Thoma — Crimson Ooyoroi**, which is the card that turned the Elemental
Reaction rules from four paragraphs of unreachable rules text into a
`Reaction preview: Vaporize` line on my own cards.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

Yes, but a thin one. The screen offered Slack Water as either a 4-damage
Weak-applier now or a Plan that Weaks ALL enemies next turn, and with exactly
one enemy on the board the ALL clause was worth nothing, so the "decision" was
one I could settle without thinking. The genuine first decision came on turn 2
of fight 1, when Kurage's Oath offered 2-now against 7-later and the Plan
keyword's line *"your Weak and Strength do not [count]"* meant the planned copy
also escaped the Weak I was wearing. That is one turn later than it should be:
the starting hand should contain a Plan card whose two lines are close enough
to argue about on turn 1. As shipped, the starter's turn 1 is Strike, Strike,
Strike.

One structural note to close on. The kit's decisions were consistently good and
consistently *rare* — they lived in Kurage's Oath, Ambush, Coral Bulwark, Slack
Water and Sango Isshin, and in a 16-card deck I saw a Plan card in hand on
roughly two turns in three. The 8 starter Strikes and Defends are not merely
weak, they are the thing standing between the player and the fun part, and the
turns I remember from this round are all turns where I drew around them.
