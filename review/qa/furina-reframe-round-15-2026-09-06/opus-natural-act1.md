## Fight 1 — Sludge Spinner [A], 33/39 when I first saw it (39 max)

The fight was already one performance old when the first screen printed: the
Salon log read *"Crabaletta hit Sludge Spinner for 6 Hydro"*, Fanfare was 2, and
the enemy had lost 6 HP before I had any input.

**Turn 1** (3 energy, 2 Encore, Block 0). Played, in order: **Ethereal
Spotlight** → **Aria of Recompense** → **An Invitation** → **Charlotte —
First-Person Shutter** → **Soloist's Solicitation** on A.

- The Spotlight-first ordering was the turn's real decision and it was made on
  the turn. The screen shouts at you: *"You open a fight with 2 Encore and
  **Ethereal Spotlight** costs 2 -- all of it. Light your Companion cards before
  anything performs: one performance spends an Encore, and the Spotlight is
  locked out for the rest of the combat."* **The alternative I rejected** was
  keeping the 2 Encore as effective HP and never lighting; I rejected it because
  Aria of Recompense was in hand and refunds 5 Encore for 1 energy, so lighting
  cost me nothing but sequencing. That refund is what makes the choice a choice.
- **An Invitation** (0 cost) drew **Charlotte — First-Person Shutter** — *cost
  1, skill, Gain 6 Block. At the start of your next turn, gain 6 Block.
  Performs a member: Playing this performs Mademoiselle Crabaletta, your front
  member.* Rejected: holding energy for Regal Bearing; Charlotte was strictly
  more block plus a free 6-damage performance.
- Ended on Soloist's rather than Stage Presence: the incoming was 8, Charlotte's
  6 Block plus 4 Encore already covered it, so the marginal block was dead and
  6 damage was not.

**Turn 2** (3 energy, 2 Encore, Weak 1 on me). Played **Chevreuse —
Interdiction Fire** on A → **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint** on A → **Soloist's
Solicitation** on A, killing it.

- Chevreuse over Salon Début was the decision, made on the turn: Chevreuse's
  face carried *"Reaction preview: Vaporize — Pyro meets Hydro: this hit deals
  1.5x damage and consumes the aura"*, and the enemy wore Hydro Aura 2. Salon
  Début would have added a member but not finished the fight. **Rejected:**
  Salon Début, on the grounds that a second Crabaletta only pays off over turns
  I did not need.
- **Screen vs outcome:** Chevreuse's face printed **Deal 7 damage** and
  delivered **11**. The 1.5× from the previewed Vaporize is not in the printed
  number. (I later confirmed the printed number *does* include the Spotlight and
  *does* include Weak: unlit base 7 → lit 10 → Weak 7 → Vaporize 11.)
- The last two plays were an obvious lethal, but it was a lethal the Chevreuse
  play set up in the same turn, not a dead turn.

**Result:** won on turn 2 at 62/78 — no HP lost.
**Draft:** took **Ebb and Flow** (*Spend 1 Encore. Gain 3 Encore. Draw 1 card.*)
over Duet, Stage Combat and Freminet — Shattering Pressure, because Encore had
hit 0 by turn 2 and Freminet needed a Freeze source I did not own.

## Fight 2 — Toadpole (1) [A] 21/21, Toadpole (2) [B] 18/24

Again Crabaletta had already performed before my first screen (B at 18/24,

**Turn 2** (3 energy, 0 Encore). Played **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint** on B; the

Applause Line and Lasting Impression — Companion density is the engine, and

## Fight 3 — Seapunk [A], 39/45 (attacking for 11)

**Turn 1** (3 energy, 2 Encore). **Ethereal Spotlight** → **An Invitation** →
**Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire** on A → **Amber — Explosive Puppet** → **Stage
Presence**.

  Encore, and a dry member performs at three-quarters. I lit anyway: +5 on each
  Chevreuse against −2 per performance. **Rejected:** holding the 2 Encore for
  two full-strength performances.
- The screen was exemplary about the consequence. The Salon log printed:
  *"Crabaletta hit Seapunk for 4 Hydro, and it is wearing a Hydro aura (dry: it
  could not pay its Encore, so it acted at three-quarters)."* That parenthetical
  is the single best-written line in the kit — it names the resource, the
  failure and the effect in eleven words.

  an enemy attacks you, take 3 less damage and deal 8 Pyro damage to ALL
  enemies.*). Choosing Amber + Stage Presence over Soloist's + Stage Presence
  was the turn's second decision: Amber is a damage card that pays on the
  enemy's turn and shaves the hit, which suited an 11-damage telegraph.
- Chevreuse printed 10 and delivered 15 (39 → 20 with a 4-damage dry
  performance). Amber's 8 Pyro landed on the enemy turn into a re-applied Hydro
  aura and delivered 12 (16 → 4), 1.5× again, again not in the printed number.

**Turn 2** (3 energy). **Aria of Recompense** (Fanfare 5 ≥ 3 → +10 Encore, both
halves fired and the screen showed `Encore: 10`) → **Salon Début**, whose

- Salon Début over Lynette was a deliberate probe as much as a kill: I wanted to

**Result:** won on turn 2 at **53/78** — 2 HP lost.
**Draft:** took a second **Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire** over Limelight,

## Fight 5 — Living Fog [A], 74/80

**Turn 1** (3 energy, 2 Encore; hand was all utility, no attacks). **Ebb and
Flow** → **Ethereal Spotlight** → **An Invitation** → **Chevreuse — Vanguard's
Valor** → **Freminet — Pers, Deploy!** on A → **Aria of Recompense+**.

- Vanguard's Valor before Freminet was the decision. Its face reads *"Your next
  Attack deals 3 additional damage, plus 3 if an Elemental Reaction triggered
  this turn."* No reaction had triggered yet, so I got only the base 3 — and
  the card is structurally awkward about it, because the reaction you would want
  to bank is the one your *attack* triggers, which is after the buff is spent.
  **Rejected:** Freminet first for the reaction, then Valor — which banks +6
  for an attack I no longer had.
- The screen handled the buff well: after Valor, the state read `Passion
  Overload 3 (buff) — Your next Attack deals 3 additional damage`, and
  Freminet's face **updated from Deal 9 damage to Deal 12 damage**. Printed
  numbers move for buffs; they do not move for reactions.
- Freminet's Cryo into the Hydro aura Froze the Fog — `Frozen 1 (debuff) — Its
  next action deals 50% less damage` — and its telegraph dropped from 8 to 4 on
  the same screen. 74 → 50.
- Aria+ with Fanfare 6 gave 16 Encore, and that 16 is what carried the rest of
  the fight: I took **zero** HP damage across three turns; the 4-damage hit went
  into Encore (16 → 12) with HP untouched.

**Turn 2** (3 energy; `Smoggy 1 (debuff) — You can only play 1 Skill per turn`).
**Courtroom Drama** (a power, not a skill) → **Chevreuse** on A → **Lynette (1)**
as my one skill.

- Smoggy made the ordering a genuine puzzle: my hand was four skills and one
  attack, and I had to spend the skill slot on the Companion that performs
  rather than the block. That is the best-designed decision I met all act, and
  the enemy — not the kit — supplied it.
- **The turn's biggest surprise, and a screen/outcome disagreement:** Chevreuse
  took the Fog from **50 to 19 — 31 damage from one 1-cost card**. Printed face:
  10. Vaporize took it to 22, Courtroom Drama's *"first Elemental Reaction you
  trigger each turn applies 1 Vulnerable and 1 Weak to its target"* fired on that
  reaction, and the Vulnerable it applied then amplified **the very hit that
  triggered it** (10 × 1.5 × 1.5 = 22), plus a Crabaletta performance for 9
  (6 × 1.5, Vulnerable). Nothing on any face says the debuff lands in time to
  boost its own trigger. It is a large, invisible multiplier.

**Turn 3.** **Chevreuse** on A killed the Fog at 10 HP; the summoned Gas Bomb
[B] — `Minion 1 (buff) — Minions abandon combat without their leader` — left
with it, so its telegraphed 8-damage Death Blow never landed. Reading the Minion
line was the decision: kill the leader, ignore the bomb.

**Result:** won on turn 3 at **53/78** — no HP lost.
**Draft:** took **Noelle — Breastplate** over Grand Salon, Stage Combat and The
House Holds Its Breath, for Companion density again.

## The kit, after 6 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

1. **Ordering around a single pool, made on the turn, every fight.** Encore is
   armour, the price of a performance, and the price of the Spotlight, and the
   three compete inside one number. Fight 2's *Ebb and Flow before the
   Spotlight* (or the card bricks), fight 4's *Chevreuse before Aria* (or the
   Fanfare-3 clause never fires), fight 3's *light anyway and accept dry
   performances* — these are all the same decision wearing different clothes,
   and it is a good one, because the screen gives you everything you need to
   solve it and the wrong order is visibly punished. Fight 2's 7 lost HP was
   me getting it wrong.
2. **"Block" cards that are also damage, made at the draft.** Because a
   Companion play performs the front member, Lynette (7 Block) and Noelle
   (9 Block) are attack cards in disguise. That turned the elite's block turns
   into 21-damage turns. Every card reward where I chose Companion density
   (Lynette #2, Vanguard's Valor, Noelle, Bogglecat Box) over a stronger
   standalone card was a real draft decision and each one paid.
3. **Aiming the element, made on the turn.** Chevreuse on the aura-wearer and
   the Cryo/Electro card on the bare body (fight 2), Freminet's Freeze to halve
   a telegraph (fight 5). The reaction previews on the card faces make this
   readable *before* you commit, which is the kit's best legibility feature.
4. **Kill the leader, not the minion** (fight 5 turn 3) — supplied by the
   enemy's printed `Minion 1` line, not by the kit, but a real read.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **The Spotlight opener is not a decision, it is a ritual.** Five of six fights
  opened `Ethereal Spotlight` on turn one, and the screen itself tells you to,
  in a boxed tip, every single time. A card the game nags you to play in the
  same slot every fight is a tax with a keyword on it.
- **Crabaletta's opening performance happens before I exist.** Every fight's
  first screen already showed 6 damage dealt and Fanfare 2. Free, but it also
  means the "before anything performs" warning is already false when you read
  it — something performed, and the Encore did not move.
- **Regal Bearing** (3 Block, 1 Weak, cost 1) I never once played in six
  fights. Next to Stage Presence+ (9 Block) and Lynette (7 Block *and* a
  7-damage performance) it has no case.
- **Fanfare is nearly inert.** It sat between 2 and 13 all act. Two cards read
  it (Aria's ≥3 clause, which is on within one performance of any fight
  starting, and the +1-per-10 member bonus, which fired exactly once). The
  state line even says so: *"Cards read it and none spends it."* It is a number
  that goes up.
- Late-fight lethal turns were automatic, but that is the plan paying off, not
  a dead turn.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicts its own printed text.**

1. **Skittish 6 never fired.** Four elite bodies each printed *"The first time
   Phantasmal Gardener is hit each turn, it gains 6 Block."* A 12-HP body died
   to a 13-damage hit; a 25-HP body took exactly 4 from a dry performance. I
   saw no Block appear on any Gardener at any point. Either the buff does
   nothing or performances and card hits both dodge it, and the face says
   neither.
2. **Printed damage excludes the reaction multiplier.** Chevreuse printed 7 and
   delivered 11; printed 10 and delivered 15; printed 10 and delivered 22.
   The Spotlight and Weak and Passion Overload are all folded into the number on
   the face; the previewed 1.5× Vaporize never is. The face is right about four
   modifiers and silent about the biggest one.
3. **Courtroom Drama's Vulnerable boosts the hit that triggered it.** 50 → 19
   from one 1-cost card. Nothing printed suggests the debuff lands early enough
   to amplify its own trigger.
4. **Weak cut a member performance from 6 to 4**, twice. The Salon text says in
   as many words: *"A performance is not an Attack and not a hit: Vulnerable
   moves it, but no Shatter and no when-hit power fires."* It names Vulnerable
   as the exception and does not name Weak; my Weak debuff line reads *"Attacks
   deal 25% less damage."* The performance was reduced anyway. Whichever
   behaviour is intended, one of those two sentences is wrong.
5. **Both copies of a card show a one-shot buff.** With Passion Overload 3
   banked, *both* Chevreuses printed `Deal 13 damage`; only one could claim it.
6. **`Hexerei`**, printed bare on Razor — Claw and Thunder's face, is defined
   nowhere on the screen that prints it.
7. **"Upgrade 2 random cards" never says which two.** I found out two rooms
   later, by accident, from a card-removal list.
8. **"If you moved the Spotlight this turn"** (Director's Cut) and *"Lasts
   until the Spotlight moves"* (Guest Cast) use a verb — *move* — that no card
   I saw performs. I could not tell what would satisfy it.
9. Minor: I initially thought Charlotte's 6 Block proved An Invitation's cards
   dodge the Spotlight. It did not — Charlotte's base is 4 and 6 is the lit
   number; Razor at 12 and Freminet at 9 later confirmed fetched Companions are
   lit. I record my wrong read because the confusion was real: with no base
   values visible anywhere, a lit number and an unlit number are
   indistinguishable on sight.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted:** **Regal Bearing** — 3 Block and 1 Weak for a full energy, in
  a deck where every other 1-cost skill also performs a member. I drew it in
  four fights and played it in none. (Runner-up: **Ethereal Spotlight**, which I
  played every fight and never *wanted* to — it is homework.)
- **Happiest to draw:** **Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire.** It is the whole kit
  in one card: it hits, it triggers the reaction, it performs a member, and
  because Crabaletta keeps re-applying Hydro it stays vaporising all fight. It
  went from 7 printed to a 31-damage swing once Courtroom Drama joined it.
  **Chevreuse — Vanguard's Valor** at 0 cost is close behind for the same
  reason — a free card that performs a member is free damage.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes — one, and a good one, but it was a sequencing decision rather than a
strategic one.** The screen handed me two facts in tension: the Spotlight costs
exactly the Encore I start with, and Aria of Recompense in the same hand refunds
five of it. Working out that lighting first and refunding second is free — and
then spending the freed 0-cost An Invitation into a Companion that both blocks
and performs — was a genuine four-card puzzle on turn one. What it was *not* was
a choice about how to fight: there was no "aggressive line vs. safe line" on
offer, only a right order and several wrong ones. Fight 5's Smoggy turn (one
skill per turn, four skills in hand) is the only turn all act that made me give
something up.
