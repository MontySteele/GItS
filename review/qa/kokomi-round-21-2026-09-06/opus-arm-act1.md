## Fight 1 — Toadpole (1) [A] 21 HP, Toadpole (2) [B] 25 HP

Opened HP 64/80, 12 in draw, 5 in hand: Defend ×2, Tide Wall, Nereid's
Ascension, Battle Plan. **The whole opening hand had no attack in it.**

**Turn 1 — Plans written: 1 (Battle Plan).**
Played Nereid's Ascension (2) then Battle Plan on the Bake-Kurage (1).
*Rejected:* two Defends for 10 Block against B's telegraphed 7. I rejected it
because A was buffing — the fight was going to get harder, not easier — and
because with no attack in hand the only way to make the turn produce anything
was to spend it on the engine. Took 7 (HP 57).

**Turn 2 — Plans written: 1 (Kurage's Oath, the skill).**
The morning printed the doubling working:
`Bake-Kurage: Battle Plan, 1 — the 1 is Energy.` twice → **Energy 5/3, hand of
8.** Well Laid's face had rewritten itself to `Deal 8 damage` with
`this morning: 2` underneath.
Played Well Laid ×2 into A (16, taking 2 Thorns each), Strike A (lethal),
Kurage's Oath *the power*, Kurage's Oath *the skill* as a Plan, Slack Water
into B, Strike into B.
*Rejected:* dumping everything into B, who had more HP. I aimed at A because A
carried `Thorns 2 — When hit by an attack, deal 2 damage back`, and Thorns
punishes a deck that wants to hit many times; the cheapest way to stop paying
it is to kill the body wearing it. That decision was made **on the turn**, off
printed text.

**Turn 3 — Plans written: none (fight ended in the morning).**
The reward screen printed the kill:
`Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 7 … Toadpole lost 6 HP, and died`.
The last turn of this fight was a turn I never played: the Plan I wrote on turn
2 finished it. **That is the plan paying off, not a dead turn.**

Won at HP 51/80.

morning). I rejected it because the exact-lethal line was on the table now and

act and it was made **on the turn**, entirely from printed numbers.
I aimed at A rather than B after reading both intents: A's 3×2 becomes 7×2=14
with the +4 Strength, B's 8 becomes 12, so killing A leaves the weaker
survivor.

**Turn 3 — Plans written: 3 (Battle Plan, Slack Water, Tide Wall).**
B was Stunned, so the turn was free. I wrote all three.
*Rejected:* playing Slack Water and Well Laid for ~8 immediate damage.
Rejected because a stunned enemy makes tempo worthless and Plans worth triple.

**Turn 4 — Plans written: none.** The morning printed:

Bake-Kurage: Slack Water, 1 — the 1 is Weak. Inside the same beat: Tamakushi Casket 2 on Corpse Slug (2).

Played Strike, Strike, Slack Water, Kurage's Oath (skill) — 21 damage, B to 3.
*Rejected:* writing Kurage's Oath as a Plan for 7 instead of playing it for 3.
Rejected because B was at 24 and I wanted it dead this turn cycle, not next.

**Turn 5 — Plans written: none.** B at 3, I played one Strike. **This turn had

is the finding, but the fight as a whole had two real decisions (T2's kill
order, T3's stun-window plan-dump).

Won at HP 47/80.

**Turn 4 — Plans written: none.** Seapunk 24 HP behind 7 Block, attacking 12; I
was at 23 with 6 Block. I found exact-ish lethal: Shinobu (13, because

## The kit, after 6 fights (5 won, 1 stopped at 1 enemy HP)

### (a) Which decisions felt like real choices

1. **"Play it, or write it on the jellyfish?" under Weak** — *on the turn*,
   fight 3 T2 and fight 6 T3. The Plan keyword says "your Weak and Strength do
   not [count]", and the hand shows it: Ambush prints `Deal 3 damage` while the
   line under it still prints `Plan: Deal 12 damage`. Trades a turn of delay for
   a debuff-proof payload. This is the best-designed decision in the kit and it
   is legible from the card face alone.
2. **"Play it now, or write it, against a telegraphed Defend"** — *on the turn*,
   fight 4 T3 and fight 5 T1. A Plan resolves at your next morning, which is
   *after* the enemy has raised Block; a card played now resolves before it. The
   intent line is what decides it. Trades payload size against Block timing.
3. **Kill order under Ravenous** — *on the turn*, fight 2 T2. Two identical
   bodies, and the printed buff ("when an enemy dies, Corpse Slug eats it,
   becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength") turns "which one" into a real
   question with a computable answer (kill the 3×2, leave the 8). It also turned
   a kill into a stun, which bought the free turn that turn 3 spent on Plans.
4. **The draft pick that shaped fight 3** — *at the draft*. Taking Ambush over
   Undertow on floor 2 is why fight 3 ended in two turns; 12 doubled is 24 and
   nothing else on offer scaled with the jellyfish.
5. **Spending the whole free turn writing** — *earlier in the fight*, fight 2 T3
   and fight 6 T1. Whenever the enemy telegraphs a stun, a buff, or a status
   dump, the Plan queue converts an empty turn into a double turn. The kit
   rewards reading intents more than most decks do.

### (b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing

- **Defend, always.** Four copies, `Gain 5 Block`, no Plan line, no interaction
  with the jellyfish at all. Every time I held one it was the card I played last
  or discarded. Tide Wall and Read the Field both do its job *and* scale with
  the morning; Defend is dead weight in a deck whose whole idea is the queue.
- **Strike ×4**, same complaint one degree softer — they at least deliver
  Weak-free damage the turn I need it.
- **Cleanup turns.** Fight 2 T5 and fight 1 T3 presented nothing: one lethal, no
  alternative. That is a normal cost of a plan-ahead deck (the decision was made
  two turns earlier), not a defect, but it is real: the kit front-loads its
  decisions and then pays them out on turns you barely play.
- **Slack Water's Plan line** — `Apply 1 Weak to ALL enemies` — never once felt
  worth a card. Against one enemy it is 1 Weak and 2 relic damage delivered a
  turn late.
- **Song of Pearls, bought for 73 gold, was never drawn** in the 3 fights after
  I bought it. That is a deck-size problem the kit hands you: rewards are
  generous, thinning is 75 gold, and by floor 8 I was at 23 cards with two
  engine powers I mostly could not find.

### (c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its printed text

1. **Kurage's Oath (the power) appears to do nothing. Three independent
   observations.** Its face reads "Whenever the Bake-Kurage plays a card from
   its memory, gain 3 Block"; once up, the status line restates it as "Each
   Bake-Kurage pulse also grants 3 Block."
   - F4 morning of turn 3: power up, **2 carry-outs**, Block **0**.
   - F4 morning of turn 4: power up, 2 carry-outs, one of them
     `Tide Wall, 6 — the 6 is Block`, and Block read **exactly 6** — Tide Wall's
     amount alone, with nothing added for the two pulses.
   - F6 morning of turn 4: power up, 2 carry-outs, Block **0**.
   Expected 6 Block in each case. This is my highest-confidence defect, and it
   cost me a real decision: in F4 T2 I chose the power over a Defend and took 5
   more damage for nothing.
2. **The same title, "Kurage's Oath", is printed on two completely different
   cards** — a 1-cost power (block on pulse) and a 1-cost Hydro skill (`Deal 3
   damage to ALL enemies. Plan: Deal 7`). They sit in hand together as
   `Kurage's Oath (1)` / `(2)`, numbered by list position, so which one I get
   depends on draw order and I twice had to observe the hand before I dared
   name one. The bridge's own disambiguation note admits it cannot always tell
   two same-named cards apart.
3. **Nereid's Ascension is missing from the map's deck list.** The map printed
   16 titles, and the fight two floors later dealt me an 18-card deck including
   Nereid's Ascension. The card was in the deck the whole time; the list on the
   deck screen simply does not carry it.
4. **The same reaction paid two different amounts.** Shinobu into a Hydro aura,
   twice, with the preview both times reading "the reacted enemy loses 4 HP at
   the start of its turn, 1 less each turn": fight 4 landed `Poison 8`, fight 6
   landed `Poison 4`. I cannot tell from any screen what made the difference.
5. **Two damage overshoots I could not reconcile** (F4 T4, ~2–4 HP; F5 T2, ~6
   HP), both in my favour, both in beats where the relic fired off a
   reaction-applied debuff. Related to the glossary's own capitalised warning
   about the relic re-applying the aura inside the beat — which I did observe
   live, twice, exactly as written.
6. **The Status cards I was given are invisible.** Haunted Ship telegraphed
   "give you 5 Status cards"; the pile counts jumped by six; no screen after
   that named a single one, and none reached my hand before I stopped. I have no
   way to know what is in my own deck.
7. **`Artifact` silently eats a debuff.** Slack Water's Weak was negated and the
   relic did not fire; nothing said so. I inferred it from 4 damage instead of 6.
8. **You cannot hold a card for a morning.** Well Laid's whole design is "be in
   hand on a turn with a big morning behind it", but the hand discards at end of
   turn, so you cannot set that up — you can only build a big morning and hope.
   That is not a contradiction of its text, but it is a design promise the rules
   quietly withdraw, and it is why Well Laid read `this morning: 0` on 4 of the
   8 times I saw it.
9. Minor: `skip` is refused on the post-fight reward list even though the card
   screen it leads to says "You may skip this."

### (d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw

- **Never wanted: Defend.** Vanilla 5 Block in a deck where Tide Wall costs the
  same and pays 3 per carry-out. I never once chose it over a Plan when both
  were legal.
- **Happiest to draw: Ambush.** `Deal 5 damage. Plan: Deal 12 damage.` is the
  clearest expression of the kit's one idea — a 2.4× multiplier for one turn of
  patience, immune to Weak, and doubled again by Nereid's Ascension. Both times
  I wrote it, the morning printed `Ambush, 12` and the fight bent around it.
  Honourable mention to **Well Laid** for being the card that taught me what a
  "morning" was, by rewriting its own damage number in my hand.

### (e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?

**Yes, and a sharp one, precisely because the hand was bad.** Defend, Defend,
Tide Wall, Nereid's Ascension, Battle Plan — not one attack. The turn was a real
fork: spend 3 energy on 15 Block and deal nothing, or spend it on a power plus a
Plan and eat 7. Reading `At the start of your turn, the Bake-Kurage carries out
every Plan twice` next to `Plan: Gain 1 Energy and draw 2 cards` and working out
that those two multiply is the moment the character taught me what it is. A
kit whose opening hand contains no attacks and is still interesting has done
something right.
