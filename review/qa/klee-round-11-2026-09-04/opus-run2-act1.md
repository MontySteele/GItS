## Fight 1 — Toadpole (1) 25/25, Toadpole (2) 23/23

Opening screen printed the whole vocabulary up front: Spark 1, Bomb, Set off,
Mine, Vulnerable, and the relic Pounding Surprise. That is a lot of words, but
each one was defined on the same page.

**Turn 1.** Played Jumpy Dumpty on Toadpole (1) (*"Place a Bomb 8. When it goes
off, place a Mine 3 on ALL enemies."*), Strike on Toadpole (2), Defend.
*Rejected:* Defend ×3 into a 7-damage intent, i.e. block everything and place
nothing. I rejected it because the bomb's whole printed economy is *"grows 4 a
turn, goes off only when Set off"* — a turn spent not placing a bomb is a turn
the bomb does not grow, and the only cost of placing early is the tempo I was
already going to spend. This was a genuine decision and the card text is what
decided it.

**Turn 2.** Bomb badge read `Bomb 12`. Drew **Ka-pow!** — *"Retain. Set off.
Deal 4 damage."* — my first detonator. Played it on Toadpole (1): the badge
resolved for exactly 12 Pyro plus the 4, 25→9, and the Jumpy Dumpty rider put
`Bomb 3 (…including 1 Mine)` on both Toadpoles as advertised. Then Strike ×2 to
kill Toadpole (1) and Strike on Toadpole (2).
*Rejected:* holding Ka-pow one more turn to detonate a `Bomb 16`. I rejected it
because killing Toadpole (1) **moves its Mine to the survivor** (*"Kills move it
on"*), stacking two Mines on one body — a printed rule I could plan around, and
the most interesting line on the screen.

**Turn 3.** Toadpole (2) at 11 HP wearing `Bomb 14 (Bombs here: 2, including 2
Mines)` and intending 3×3. I **played nothing and ended the turn**, betting the
Mine text — *"goes off when its enemy attacks you, before the hit lands"* —
would kill it before its own attack landed. It did: the next screen was the
reward screen and I took 0 damage that turn.
*Rejected:* two Strikes to kill it by hand (it had Thorns 2, so that line costs
4 HP). This is the best turn of the run: the screen printed a fact, I made a
prediction from it, and the fact held.

Reward: 14 Gold, Fruit Juice, and a card pick of Quick Fuse / Fish-Flavored Bait
/ Sizzle / Gorou. Took **Fish-Flavored Bait** because I had exactly one bomb
source (Jumpy Dumpty) and three ways to set one off on offer; placement, not
detonation, was the bottleneck.

**Event (Self-Help Book).** Put Sharp 2 on **Ka-pow!** rather than a Strike:
Ka-pow is 0-cost and Retain, so it is the attack I expect to play most often.
The card immediately reprinted as `Ka-pow! [Pyro] (Sharp 2) — Retain. Set off.
Deal 6 damage.` — the enchantment is legible on the face.

## Fight 3 — Sludge Spinner 37/37

**Turn 1.** Pop! (free Bomb 5), then Defend, Defend, Strike into 8 damage +
debuff.
*Rejected:* Pop! + Strike ×2 + no block. The enemy's second intent line was
`Strategic (Debuff)` with no number, so I could not price the turn; when the

**Turn 2.** Weak reprinted itself **on every card face**: `Strike — Deal 4
damage`, `Ka-pow! (Sharp 2) — Deal 4 damage`, `Fish-Flavored Bait — Deal 3
damage`. That is the single best legibility touch I saw. And the Bomb badge did
**not** move — consistent with *"Not an Attack: only Vulnerable and a cap move

**Turn 3.** Badge read `Bomb 21 (Bombs here: 2)` — exactly 9+4 grown by 4 each,

*Rejected:* nothing. One card, lethal.

*Rejected:* Bait first then Diona, which is the same three cards for 3 less

*Also unexplained here:* **Spark went 1 → 2 with no Bomb going off.** My only

**Turn 2.** Intent 6×2 with `Strength 3` on the body. Played Pop! and Jumpy

*Rejected:* putting the bomb on the Cultist instead. It genuinely did not matter

**Turn 4.** Strike ×3 to kill the Cultist, then Tinder Toss — which, with only

**Turn 5.** Strike for the kill.

Reward pick: Rapid Fire / Pocket Fireworks / Chain Fuse / Fischl. Took **Rapid

## The kit, after 6 fights (5 finished, 1 abandoned on budget)

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

One decision carried the whole kit and it is a good one: **when to spend the
detonator.** Every turn the badge says `Bomb N`, N grows by 4 per charge per
turn, and *"goes off only when Set off"* — so holding Ka-pow is a loan against
the enemy's clock. In fight 4 I held it for two turns and it paid 40 damage on
a 0-cost card; in fight 2 I spent it immediately because a Mine was going to
finish the job. That is a real, legible, recurring tension, and the badge
arithmetic is exact enough that I could predict `Bomb 33` and `Bomb 21` on the
nose before the turn came.

Second, smaller, and also real: **ordering for reactions.** Diona before Ka-pow
rather than after turns 33 raw into 41. It is a decision the screen supports
with aura badges — but only if you already know Melt multiplies the *first*
bomb hit, which nothing prints.

Third: **Mine placement as a defensive card.** *"Goes off when its enemy attacks
you, before the hit lands"* let me win fight 1's last turn by playing nothing at
all. Turning an offensive charge into pre-emptive defence is the most Klee thing
in the kit.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

The base cards. Strike and Defend are 8 of my 16 cards, and every turn where the
hand was Strikes and Defends — fight 2 turn 1, most of fight 5 turn 4 — was a
turn the kit was not present at all. With one detonator in the deck, the
statistically most common Klee turn is "place a bomb, block, pass," which is a
turn with one decision (which of two identical bomb cards) and no interesting
one.

**Rapid Fire was never worth playing.** Two energy, `Set off each enemy hit`,
random targets: against four bodies it hit the bombed one zero times out of
four, and its 3-damage chunks are precisely the size Skittish 6 eats. Its
printed selling point cannot be aimed.

**Tinder Toss is worth playing only when one enemy is left**, at which point its
randomness is moot — the same failure, twice, in the same fight.

I never used either potion. That is a self-criticism as much as a finding: I was
saving Fysh Oil for a boss I never reached, and Fruit Juice's 5 Max HP never
looked urgent enough on any single screen.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **Spark income has an unprinted source.** Twice — fight 4 turn 1 (1 → 2 with
   no bomb going off) and fight 6 turn 2 (1 → 2 across a turn in which I *spent*
   one and no bomb went off, so net +2) — my Spark count rose with nothing on
   screen to explain it. My only Spark relic reads *"Whenever a Bomb goes off,
   gain 1 Spark."* Either something grants Spark on Elemental Reactions and
   never says so, or the counter is wrong. As a blind player I could not tell
   which, and I could not budget my Spark-priced cards because of it.

2. **Reactions fire silently.** Melt is printed as a keyword and its 1.75× is
   printed, but at the moment a Melt happens the screen says nothing — the aura
   badge just disappears and the HP is lower than the card faces predict. I
   reconstructed all three Melts by arithmetic. The word "Melt" appeared on my
   screen exactly once all run, in Diona's *"Reaction preview"* line, and that
   line is a warning that a reaction will be **wasted**. The game warns me about
   the bad case and stays silent on the good one.

3. **Bombs go off in placement order and only the first one gets the reaction
   multiplier.** In fight 5 turn 3 the Bomb 5 melted (8.75) and the Bomb 8 did
   not, for 22 total. `Set off` says *"Every Bomb on the target goes off first,
   one at a time, each a Pyro hit for its size"* — "one at a time" is doing
   enormous, unstated work there. Which charge eats the multiplier is worth real
   damage and is nowhere on any face.

4. **`Set off` on a random-target card is close to a lie.** Rapid Fire and
   Tinder Toss both print `Set off`; across three plays against multiple enemies
   they set off a bomb zero times. Nothing warns that the card cannot be aimed
   at the charge you built.

5. **`Kills move it on` quietly deletes a decision.** Because a bomb always
   transfers to a survivor, "which enemy do I bomb" has no wrong answer in a
   normal fight. The card looks like it is asking a targeting question and isn't.

6. **Diona's face and Diona's buff disagree in tense.** The card reads *"If a
   Bomb goes off this turn, gain 5 Block"* (which reads as either direction); the
   buff it leaves reads *"The **next time** one of your Bombs goes off this
   turn"* (forward only). I had to guess the ordering on first play. The buff is
   right and the card is ambiguous.

7. **Minor, and to the mod's credit rather than against it:** Weak reprinting
   itself into every card's damage number, and the Bomb badge visibly *not*
   moving under Weak, matched the Bomb keyword's *"only Vulnerable and a cap move
   it"* precisely. That is the clearest rules-to-screen agreement in the kit.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Rapid Fire.** Two energy for four unaimable pinpricks whose one
distinguishing clause never fired.

Happiest to draw: **Ka-pow!** — and not close. 0 cost, Retain, and it is the
card that converts a two-turn setup into 40 damage. The Retain is what makes it
a plan rather than a lottery: I could commit to a three-card build-up on turn 2
*knowing* the payoff would be in hand on turn 3. Runner-up: **Jumpy Dumpty**,
because its Mine rider is the only card that let me win a turn by doing nothing.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a real one.** Turn 1 of fight 1 dealt me Jumpy Dumpty alongside three
Defends and a Strike against a 7-damage intent, and the bomb keyword told me on
that same screen that a charge placed now is worth 4 more next turn. Place-vs-
block was live on the very first turn, I could price both sides off printed
text, and I got it right. That is a good opening hand and a good opening screen.

The caveat is fight 2's turn 1, which dealt Strike ×2 and Defend ×3 and
presented no Klee decision at all. With 10 kit cards in 16, roughly one opening
hand in five or six is going to be the vanilla one.
