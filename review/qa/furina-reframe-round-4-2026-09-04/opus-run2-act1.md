## Fight 1 — Corpse Slug (1) 26 HP, Corpse Slug (2) 25 HP

Both wore *Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it,
becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength*, which makes the order of the kills a
real question rather than a formality.

**Turn 1** (3 energy; incoming 3x2 and 8 = 14).
Played **Salon Début**, then **Freminet — Pers, Deploy!** on Slug (2), then
**Soloist's Solicitation** on Slug (2).
*Rejected:* three attacks for 18 damage, and Regal Bearing for 3 Block + Weak
on the 8-hitter. I rejected pure tempo because Salon Début is the only card in
the opening hand that looks like an engine, and I wanted to know what it did
before the fight was decided; I rejected Regal Bearing because 3 Block + 2
saved damage is worse than 6 damage when nothing is lethal.

**The screen and the outcome disagreed here.** Salon Début prints *"Add 1
random Salon Member to your Salon. Burst +5. Elemental Skill."* — it says
"add", and the Salon keyword on the same card says *"Members do NOT act on
their own."* Playing it gave me 2 Block immediately. Only the resulting buff
line explains why: *"Salon Member 1 — A joining member performs at once."* The
card that adds the member does not say the member performs; the buff you only
see afterwards does. The 2 Block also encodes a second rule I could only
reconstruct: Gentilhomme Usher performs for 3 Block, and *"a member with no
Encore to spend performs at three-quarters"* → 3 × 0.75 = 2.

Freminet is a Companion, so playing it performed the Usher again (+2 Block,
Fanfare 2 → 4). Slug (2) 25 → 13, Cryo Aura 2 on it.

**Turn 2** (3 energy; only one attack in hand).
Played **An Invitation** (0) → drew *Charlotte — Framing: Freezing Point
Composition*. Played **Aria of Recompense** → Encore 5. Played **Ethereal
Spotlight** (0, costs 2 Encore) → *Guest Cast 1: Companion cards are
Spotlighted*. Played **Charlotte — Framing** on Slug (2), then **Charlotte —
First-Person Shutter**.
*Rejected:* Stage Presence and a straight 6-damage Soloist. I rejected them
because this was the turn to find out what Encore and the Spotlight actually
do, and because the enemy that was going to hurt me was already at 13.

**This is where I learned the biggest thing on the sheet, and not from a card.**
Aria of Recompense prints exactly *"Gain 5 Encore."* Nothing on that card, and
nothing on the card-removal screen, says what Encore is. The moment I held
some, the status line told me: *"Encore: a buffer and not a bank: after Block
it absorbs incoming damage before HP. Cards spend it, and a Salon member spends
1 each time it performs."* Encore is simultaneously a second health bar, the
Spotlight's price, and the members' per-performance fuel — three jobs — and a
player reading only the card face learns none of them.

**Second disagreement, and this one I could measure.** Under *Guest Cast 1*
(50% stronger printed damage and Block on Companion cards), Charlotte — Framing
printed "Deal 6 damage" and dealt exactly 6 (13 → 7). No Weak was on me;
nothing else was in the way. The next turn, under the *same* Guest Cast buff,
**Freminet printed "Deal 9 damage"** — 6 × 1.5. Two Companion cards, one
Spotlight, one boosted and one not. The difference is where the card came from:
Freminet is a deck card, Charlotte — Framing was generated in combat by An
Invitation. (Corroborated twice more; see fight 2 and (c).)

**Turn 3** (3 energy). Played **Freminet** (printed 9) on Slug (2) to kill it,
then **Soloist** and **Charlotte — Framing** onto Slug (1).
*Rejected:* Salon Début for a second member. I rejected it because killing Slug
(2) triggered Ravenous on Slug (1) — Stunned, +4 Strength — and buying a free
turn was worth more than a second member I could not yet aim. That worked: Slug
(1) spent its turn Stunned and I took 0. Fanfare hit 10, which the Salon
keyword says is the threshold for *"+1 per 10 Fanfare"* on member numbers.

**Turn 4.** Played **Aria**, **Salon Début**, **Freminet** on the Slug. The
joining member's performance plus Freminet's 9 finished it. I never saw the
killing blow — the tool cut straight to the reward screen.

*Turns that presented a decision: 1, 2, 3. Turn 4 was cleanup.*

## Fight 3 — Toadpole (1) 25 HP, Toadpole (2) 21 HP

**Turn 1 is the finding.** My hand was Stage Presence, Regal Bearing, Charlotte
— First-Person Shutter, Freminet, Soloist, and an Ethereal Spotlight marked

Companion card in the kit collapses into a vanilla 6-damage attack or a vanilla
4-Block skill, and the relic that hands me a free card each turn hands me a
brick. I played Freminet → Soloist → Charlotte because there was nothing else
to play.
*Rejected alternative: none existed.* That is the finding — a turn with no
decision in it.

**Turn 2.** **Aria** → **Spotlight** → **Salon Début** (Usher again, fourth
time) → **Fischl** on Toadpole (1) for 10. Thorns 2 bit 2 off my block.
*Rejected:* killing the 9 HP Toadpole (2) with Fischl. I rejected it because
Superconduct's 2 Vulnerable is wasted on a corpse and Toadpole (1) at 15 was
the one I would still be hitting next turn.

**Turn 3 — Endless Waltz, and two things it does not do.** I played **Aria**
then **Endless Waltz** (*"Add 1 Mademoiselle Crabaletta and 1 Gentilhomme Usher
to your Salon. Salon Member numbers are 3 higher."*).
*Rejected:* Freminet for 9 damage plus a Superconduct on Toadpole (1)'s Electro
aura — pure tempo. I rejected it because I wanted to see the kit's stated
payoff at least once.

Encore went 5 → 3, so both joining members performed at **full** price, not
three-quarters. They performed for **6 damage and 3 Block** — their base
numbers. With *Grand Salon 3* live they should have been 9 and 6. **The card's
own "+3" does not reach the two members the card itself deploys**; the buff
lands after they have already performed. I confirmed the contrast a fight later
(fight 5, turn 2: with Grand Salon already standing, a Salon Début member
performed for exactly 9).

Crabaletta's Hydro met Toadpole (1)'s Electro aura and the reaction fired — but

reaction **Electro-Charged**. Same mechanic, two names, and the name the
glossary teaches is not the name the enemy wears. I only knew the reaction had
happened because the HP moved.

**Turn 4.** **Fischl** (now printing 10, Spotlight applying normally — so
*reward* Companions are fine; only An Invitation's are not) killed Toadpole
(2); **Soloist** killed Toadpole (1).

*Turns that presented a decision: 2, 3, 4. Turn 1 presented none.*

**Turns 3–4.** Soloist finished the Merc; the split arrived Stunned; Freminet +

## Fight 6 (Elite, floor 8) — four Phantasmal Gardeners, 26 + 30 + 31 + 29 = 116 HP

Each wore *Skittish 6 — The first time Phantasmal Gardener is hit each turn, it
gains 6 Block.*

**This is the kit's worst case and it found it.** Furina's damage is a spray of
small single-target hits — a 6-damage card that triggers a 6-damage member
performance — and Skittish taxes 6 off the first hit on each body every turn.
Against four bodies that is up to 24 damage a turn of pure tax, which is more
than my entire turn's output.

**Turn 1.** Salon Début (Usher, again) → Freminet on Gardener (3) → Charlotte.
Net damage into 116 HP: **6**. Took 5.
**Turn 2.** Aria → **two Soloists onto the same Gardener**, because the tax is
per-target-per-turn and the second hit is untaxed. Net 6. Took 8.
*Rejected:* Endless Waltz. I rejected it on the strength of a defect I had
already measured — its members perform at base numbers, and Skittish would eat
those numbers whole.
**Turn 3.** Crescendo (8) → Fischl (7) → Soloist (6), all stacked on Gardener
(3) so only the first was taxed. Net 15, the best turn I had. Took 17.

After three rounds: 27 damage dealt into 116, 30 HP lost out of 44. Gardener
(3) on 4 HP, the other three untouched at full. Next turn's declared intent was
9 + 7 + 9 = 25 into my 14 HP. **I stopped at the action cap here rather than
play a losing turn to reach a tidier ending.**

I want to be plain about my own error: I was offered **Undercurrent** (2 energy,
2 damage to ALL enemies 3 times) as the second card of the run and passed it for
Fischl. Fischl was right for five fights and catastrophically wrong for this
one. But a kit whose only offered answer to a four-body elite is one uncommon I
saw once is worth looking at.

## The kit, after 6 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Four, and they were good ones.

1. **Aria of Recompense against a block card.** Encore is a damage buffer, the
   Spotlight's price, *and* the members' performance fuel, so "gain 5 Encore"
   is competing with "gain 6 Block" on three axes at once. Every time I chose
   Aria it was a real judgement about which of its three jobs I needed.
2. **Which member is at the front.** *"A Companion card you play performs the
   Usher"* versus *"...performs Crabaletta"* silently converts my whole hand
   between block mode and damage mode, and I cannot reorder the queue — only
   Evoke through it. Fight 4 turn 1 (Crabaletta front, three performances off
   one deploy, 18 damage from a 6-damage hand) and Elite turn 1 (Usher front,
   6 damage total) are the same play with opposite outcomes.
3. **Fight 5 turn 1: 16 damage or 25.** The 25 line left me at 17 HP against a
   12-damage attacker. Taking the smaller number to stay in the race is exactly
   the decision I want a kit to put in front of me.
4. **Sequencing the Spotlight.** Aria → Ethereal Spotlight → Companions is a
   real ordering puzzle, because the Spotlight is free in energy but costs 2 of
   the same Encore the members are spending, and it is Ethereal so it is
   use-it-this-turn-or-lose-it.

The Elite also produced a genuine tactical read — **stack hits on one body,
because Skittish taxes only the first hit per target per turn** — which is a
good piece of design even though I lost the fight.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Stage Presence** (1, gain 6 Block) I played **once in six fights**, and
  only because I had a spare energy. Regal Bearing at 3 Block + 1 Weak, and
  Charlotte — First-Person Shutter at 4 + 4 *plus* a member performance, both
  dominate it. It is the card I removed a copy of at Neow and I would remove
  the other.
- **An Invitation → play whatever it gave me** was automatic. It costs 0 and
  Exhausts; there is no turn on which you think about it.
- Once Crabaletta was at the front, **every Companion card became "press for 6
  more damage"** and the choice between them stopped mattering.
- **Aria → Spotlight → Companions** became a rote opening by fight 3. I played
  that exact sequence four times.
- **Fanfare** is decorative. It fades 20% a turn, *"cards read it and none
  spends it"*, and in six fights exactly one card (Crescendo, taken on floor 5)
  ever read it. The +1-per-10-Fanfare member bonus crossed its threshold once,
  in fight 1, and I never noticed the difference.
- **Burst Energy never happened.** The keyword is printed in full on Salon
  Début on every single screen — a 70-point meter, a Burst card that arrives in
  hand when it fills, "energy past full is lost at the cast" — and I ended the
  run having **never exceeded 5 of 70**. Salon Début grants 5 and reactions
  grant 5, so a full meter needs roughly fourteen of those in one combat.
  I read that paragraph perhaps thirty times for a mechanic I never once saw.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **Companion cards generated by *An Invitation* are not Spotlighted.**
   Measured twice. *Charlotte — Framing: Freezing Point Composition* printed
   "Deal 6 damage" and dealt exactly 6 under Guest Cast with no Weak on me,
   while **Freminet printed 9** under the same buff in the same fight.
   *Shinobu — Grass Ring of Sanctification* printed "Gain 6 Block" and moved my
   Block by exactly 6 under an active Guest Cast. Deck Companions (Freminet,
   Charlotte — First-Person Shutter) and reward Companions (Fischl, which
   printed 10 for a base 7) all take the buff correctly. This is my highest
   confidence defect.
2. **Endless Waltz's "+3" does not apply to the two members Endless Waltz
   deploys.** They performed for 6 damage and 3 Block at full Encore price
   (Encore 5 → 3, so no three-quarters reduction). With *Grand Salon 3* already
   standing, a later Salon Début member performed for 9. The buff is applied
   after its own deploys resolve.
3. **The Electro-Charged reaction shows on the enemy as "Poison".** The
   glossary printed on the same screen teaches the name *Electro-Charged*; the
   body wears *Poison 4* with a matching description. Two names for one thing,
   and the taught name never appears.
4. **Salon Début does not say its member performs.** The card says "Add 1
   random Salon Member to your Salon"; the Salon keyword on the same card says
   "Members do NOT act on their own"; the member performs immediately. Only the
   buff you acquire afterwards discloses it.
5. **"Gain 5 Encore" never says what Encore is** — not on the card, not on the
   deck-removal screen. The definition only appears in the status line once you
   already hold some, and it is a large definition (buffer, card cost, member
   fuel).
6. **"Spotlighted... no Fanfare"** on Ethereal Spotlight — I could not verify
   this and I think it may be false. Fanfare rose by 2 per member performance
   throughout, including on turns where the triggering Companion card was
   Spotlighted.
7. **Fischl's "If Oz is out, he deals 5 Electro damage to a random enemy."** No
   keyword anywhere explains who Oz is or how to get him out. Oz was never out.
8. **The Sunken Treasury's "Receive Greed"** — no indication whether that is a
   curse, a relic, or a debuff, on a screen asking me to price it against 281
   gold.
9. **Gold is never displayed.** Six fights, several reward screens, and I never
   saw a running total. When *Thievery 20* stole gold from me I had no way to
   know how much I had left.

Two pieces of text I want to praise, because they are the standard the rest
should meet: **"Reaction preview: Superconduct — Electro meets Cryo: the
reacted enemy gains 2 Vulnerable"**, printed on the card, before I spend the
energy; and **"CANNOT BE PLAYED: you have no Encore, and this costs 2"**, which
told me why rather than just greying out.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted: Stage Presence.** One play in six fights. It is out-classed
  by every other block card I own, including my own starting Regal Bearing.
- **Happiest to draw: Fischl — Nightrider.** It prints its own reaction
  preview, it triggers a member performance, and in fight 4 it delivered 20
  damage in one card off a printed 7 (card 10 + Crabaletta 6 + Electro-Charged
  4). It is the only card in the deck where the kit's three systems — the
  element reactions, the Salon, and the Spotlight — all fired at once, and it
  is the only time the kit felt *designed* rather than assembled. Close second:
  **Salon Début when it rolls Crabaletta**, which is also its problem — the
  same card was a dead 2 Block on four of six plays.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a good one.** The opening hand held Salon Début (an engine I could
not read), two vanilla 6-damage attacks, Freminet, and Regal Bearing, against
14 damage of incoming intent. Engine versus tempo, with a genuine cost either
way, on turn one, with no correct answer visible from the card faces. That is
the right question to open on.

The counterweight is fight 3's turn 1, which presented **no** decision at all:
without Aria, Salon Début or Endless Waltz in hand, the kit has no Encore, no
member on stage, and every Companion card degrades to a vanilla 6/4 — I played
the only three cards that could be played, in the only order that mattered. In
a 12-card deck holding 3 enablers, that hand is not rare.
