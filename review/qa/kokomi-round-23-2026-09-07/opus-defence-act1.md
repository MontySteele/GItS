## Fight 1 — Twig Slime (S) 11/11, Leaf Slime (M) 34/34, Leaf Slime (S) 13/13

Opening: HP 64/80, 3 energy, hand Second Thoughts / Defend / Strike ×2 / Second Wave.
Intents: Twig attacks for 4; both Leaf Slimes give Status cards (2 and 1).

**Turn 1.** Played **Second Wave** onto the Bake-Kurage (writing its Plan, *"The next Plan
is carried out twice"*), then **Strike ×2** into Twig Slime, killing it (12 into 11).
*Rejected:* Defend. With only 4 damage incoming and both Leaf Slimes on Status intents,
5 Block was worth less than removing the only attacker; and writing Second Wave cost the
same 1 energy as the Block card while promising a doubled Plan later.
*Also rejected:* writing Second Wave **and** a Strike as two Plans, taking the 4 damage
undefended for a doubled 12 next turn — 12 doubled damage aimed at an 11-HP body is
waste, so killing it now was strictly better.
This was a real decision and it was made on the turn.

**Turn 2** (HP 64, Block 0, three Slimed statuses now in the deck; Leaf M attacks 8, Leaf S 3).
Here the first defect landed. Second Wave's Plan had resolved and the screen printed
*"Bake-Kurage: Second Wave — no enemy lost HP"*, then *"Nothing is planned."* I had **no
Block from it** (Block 0 at turn start, though the card's face reads "Gain 4 Block. Plan:
…"), and **no buff row anywhere** saying a doubling was pending. I could not tell from any
printed thing whether the doubling was still live.
Played **Coral Bulwark** as a Plan (8 Block + 1 Weak next turn) and **Defend ×2** for 10
Block against 11 incoming. *Rejected:* Coral Bulwark now (6 Block) plus a Defend plus a
Strike — that blocks exactly and deals 6, but it would not have tested whether the
doubling flag survived, and testing it was worth 6 damage to me.

**Turn 3.** The test came back negative: the log read *"Coral Bulwark, 8 — the 8 is Block.
Inside the same beat: Tamakushi Casket 2"* — **8, not 16**, and one Weak, not two. So the
Second Wave I paid for on turn 1 produced literally nothing: no Block, no doubling, no
visible state. I took 1 damage on the turn (HP 63).
With no damage incoming, I wrote **Kurage's Oath** (Plan: 7 to ALL) and **Read the Field**
(Plan: 10 Block) and played **Slack Water** on Leaf Slime (S) for 4 + the Casket's answering
2 = 6, leaving it on 7. *Rejected:* playing Kurage's Oath now for 3-to-all instead of
writing it for 7-to-all. Since nothing was attacking me, the tempo I gave up by planning
cost nothing, so the 3→7 upgrade was free. That is the Plan system working as advertised.

**Turn 4.** The plan paid exactly: *"Kurage's Oath, 7 — Leaf Slime (S) lost 7 HP, and died"*,
then 10 Block. Leaf M at 25 and attacking for 8, with 10 Block up.
Played **Night Watch** as a **Dusk** Plan (resolves at end of *this* turn, before enemies
act: 5 Block + 1 Weak, and the Weak fires the Casket for 2), **Strike** into Leaf M, and one
**Slimed** to cycle. *Rejected:* holding Night Watch to play face-up for 3 Block — the Dusk
line gives 5 Block *and* a Weak *and* 2 relic damage for the same energy, which is not
really a choice; the Dusk keyword makes the face-up half of that card dead.

**Turn 5.** Leaf M at 17. Second attempt at the Second Wave combo, this time with a Plan
written *after* it in the same queue: **Strike** into Leaf M (→11), then wrote **Second Wave**
and **Kurage's Oath** in that order. *Rejected:* Kurage's Oath face-up for 3 plus a Defend —
slower, and it would have left the Second Wave question unanswered a second time.

The kill screen answered it:

```
  - Bake-Kurage: Second Wave        - no enemy lost HP
  - Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 7   - Leaf Slime (M) lost 7 HP
  - Bake-Kurage: Kurage's Oath, 7   - Leaf Slime (M) lost 4 HP, and died
```

So **Second Wave works only when another Plan is written behind it in the same
carry-out**; written alone it evaporates silently and refunds nothing. Where the screen
and the outcome disagreed: on turn 2 the card's own text ("The next Plan is carried out
twice") reads as a lasting promise, nothing printed said the promise had lapsed, and the
turn-3 log printed the un-doubled Coral Bulwark without a word about it.

**Fight 1 verdict:** real decisions, and most were made a turn ahead rather than on the
turn. Rewards: 11 gold, Skill Potion, and a card.

**Draft:** took **Kujou Sara — Crowfeather Cover** (0-cost, next Attack +4 and applies
Electro) over **Treatise** (draw on Plan carry-out), **Change of Plans**, and
**Cleansing Wave**. Treatise is probably the stronger card; I took Sara because Electro
was the only second element on offer and the Elemental Reaction block had been telling me
on every screen that *"NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE: Hydro is the only element this screen
can supply."* I wanted to see the reaction layer.

## Fight 2 — Fuzzy Wurm Crawler 55/55

**Turn 1** (HP 63, hand Strike ×3 / Sara / Defend; it attacks for 4). Played **Sara**
(0 cost) then **Strike ×3**: 10 + 6 + 6 = 22, taking the 4 unblocked.
*Rejected:* Defend over the third Strike. Against a 55-HP body with a 4-damage intent,
trading 4 HP for 6 damage is the right side of the exchange.
*Note:* the hand contained no Plan card at all and the screen said so — *"No Plan card in
hand: the jellyfish waits."* That was an honest, legible way to tell me my engine was
offline this turn, but it also meant this turn's only decision was block-or-race.

**Turn 2.** The Electro landed and the payoff was immediate and beautifully signposted:
Kurage's Oath now carried an extra line on its own face —
*"Reaction preview: Electro-Charged — Hydro meets Electro: the reacted enemy loses 4 HP at
the start of its turn, 1 less each turn."* This is the single most legible thing in the
kit: the card told me what it would do to *this* body, right now.
The genuine decision followed: **Electro Aura 1** meant the aura expires in one turn, so
writing Kurage's Oath as a Plan (7 damage, but carried out next turn) would have missed
the reaction window, while playing it face-up (3 damage) caught it. I played **Kurage's
Oath face-up** for the reaction, then wrote **Night Watch** and **Breakwater** as Dusk Plans.
*Rejected:* the 7-damage Plan. 3 + ~10 of Electro-Charged beats 7 with no aura, and the
enemy's Empower intent meant I did not need the tempo elsewhere. This is the best decision
the kit offered me all round, and it was made on the turn, off information the card face
gave me for free.
It also produced my first refusal: `play "Kurage's Oath" on "A"` was refused —
*"\"Kurage's Oath\" does its own aiming, so it takes no `on \"A\"`. The form that resolves:
play \"Kurage's Oath\""*. Correct and self-correcting, but worth noting: the same card is
`on "Bake-Kurage"`-able and not `on "A"`-able, which took a beat to internalise.

**Turn 3.** Electro-Charged had become **Poison 3** on the panel, and the enemy had
**Strength 7** and an 11-damage intent. Its HP had gone 33 → 22 across the beat; the parts
I could account for from printed numbers were Oath 3 + Casket 2 + a poison tick, which
leaves about 2 HP I could not reconcile from anything the screen printed. Played
**Slack Water** on it (4 + Weak, and the Weak fired the Casket for 2), **Strike**, and one
**Defend**. *Rejected:* double Defend for a clean zero-damage turn. I chose to take 3 HP for
6 extra damage because the poison clock was already running and shortening the fight was
worth more than the HP.

**Turn 4.** Enemy on 7. **Strike ×2** for the kill. No alternative and none wanted: this is
the two-turn Electro-Charged plan paying off, not a dead turn.

**Fight 2 verdict:** one excellent on-the-turn decision (aura window vs. Plan value), one
draft decision paying (Sara), and two turns of arithmetic. HP 63 → 56.

**Draft:** took **Vanguard** (0-cost, apply 1 Vulnerable, Exhaust) over **Rally**,
**Ebb Tide** and **Gorou — Crystal Collapse**. Vanguard is a free Casket trigger and a free
damage multiplier; Gorou wants a Companion density I did not have (one Companion card),
and Ebb Tide is an anti-synergy card that unwrites the thing the character is built to do.

## Fight 3 — Shrinker Beetle 38/38

**Turn 1** (HP 56; it intends a strong Debuff, no damage). Played **Slack Water** on it
(4 + Casket 2), **Strike**, and wrote **Coral Bulwark** as a Plan.
*Rejected:* Defend — nothing was attacking me. With a no-damage intent the turn has no
defensive question at all, so the only decision was which card to bank as a Plan.

**Turn 2.** The debuff arrived: **Shrink -1 — "While Shrinker Beetle is alive, you deal 30%
less damage with every hit you land."** And here the bridge did something genuinely good:
**every card face in my hand re-printed its own reduced number.** Kurage's Oath now read
"Deal 2 damage to ALL enemies" and Strike read "Deal 4 damage". I did not have to do the
arithmetic; the kit did it in front of me.
Played **Vanguard** (Vulnerable + Casket 2), **Strike**, wrote **Kurage's Oath** as a Plan,
and played a **Defend**. *Rejected:* playing Kurage's Oath face-up for its now-shrunken
2 damage — the Plan line printed 7. And *rejected:* saving Vanguard's Vulnerable to
amplify the planned Oath, because Vulnerable's own keyword text says one stack falls off at
the end of the enemy's turn, so it would be gone before the Plan resolved. That is a real
piece of reasoning the printed text supported end-to-end.

**Turn 3.** The most interesting disagreement of the round. The log read
*"Kurage's Oath, 7 — Shrinker Beetle lost 7 HP"* — **the full 7, undiminished**, while my own
copy of that same card in hand was printing 2 because of Shrink. So **the Bake-Kurage's
carry-outs ignore the debuffs on me**. That is a large strategic lever — plan through your
own damage debuffs — and I could not find it printed anywhere. The Plan keyword text goes
out of its way to tell me what *does* carry across ("Your Strength folds in as you write it;
the enemy's Vulnerable counts next turn") and is silent on whether my own damage-down
carries, which is exactly the case that came up.
Enemy on 8; played **Sara** then a **Strike**, which killed it before I could play the second
Strike (my next command was refused with "you are not in a battle" — refusal #2, and a fair
one).

**Fight 3 verdict:** the fight's decisions were front-loaded — the Plan written on turn 2
did the work. HP 56, untouched. Rewards: 13 gold, Block Potion, a card.

**Draft:** took **Feint** (6 damage, Plan: 10) over **Ebb Tide**, **Song of Pearls** and
**Shinobu — Grass Ring of Sanctification**. Straight damage, and given the Shrink finding a
Plan-side damage card is better than its face suggests.

## The kit, after 5 fights

### (a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off

Four kinds, and three of them are the Plan system:

1. **Play now vs. write as a Plan** — the spine of the kit, and it is a good one because
   the trade is legible on the card face: Kurage's Oath is 3-to-all now or 7-to-all next
   turn, Feint 6 or 10, Read the Field 5 Block or 10. The exchange rate is roughly *double
   for a turn of delay*, which means the decision genuinely swings on whether this turn is
   safe — a no-damage enemy intent makes planning free, and a live threat makes it
   expensive. **Made on the turn**, every turn there was a Plan card in hand.
2. **Aura window vs. Plan value** (fight 2, turn 2). Electro Aura 1 meant the reaction
   expired before a written Plan could resolve, so 3 damage now with an Electro-Charged
   rider beat 7 damage later without it. This is the best decision the kit gave me: two
   printed numbers (the aura's remaining turns, the Plan's damage) pointing opposite ways.
   **On the turn.**
3. **Reaction ordering** (fight 5, turn 3). Kaeya-then-Sara-then-Strike vs.
   Sara-then-Kaeya: the Shatter only attaches to the first Attack after the freeze, so the
   order is worth about 10 damage. **On the turn**, and it was the turn the whole fight
   turned on.
4. **Spending a debuff to burn Artifact** (fight 5, turn 1). A cheap Dusk Weak paid the
   Artifact counter so later debuffs and the Casket would land. **On the turn.**
5. And one **at the draft**: taking Sara and then Kaeya turned a mono-Hydro deck — where
   every screen kept telling me *"NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE"* — into one with two
   reactions. Fights 2, 4 and 5 all hinged on that pick. The kit's ceiling is visibly a
   drafting question, not a turn question.

**Dusk** deserves a call-out: a Plan that resolves *before* the enemy acts rather than
after collapses the usual "planning costs you a turn of defence" problem, and it is what
let fight 4's turn 4 be both an offensive turn and a survival turn.

### (b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing

- **Defend, and the basics generally.** Fight 4's turn 3 was Defend ×4 plus Vanguard and
  had literally no decision in it. Every turn where the hand was mostly basics was
  arithmetic, not play.
- **The face-up half of every Dusk card.** Night Watch face-up is 3 Block; as a Dusk Plan
  it is 5 Block *plus* a Weak *plus* 2 relic damage, resolving before the enemy acts, for
  the same energy. Breakwater is 4 vs 7 the same way. I never once played either face-up
  and cannot construct the board where I would. That is not a decision, it is a card with
  a dead half.
- **Second Thoughts.** Drawn three times, played zero. "Cancel your last Plan: its card
  returns to your hand and you regain its cost" is a fix for a mistake I never made,
  because Plans are written and resolved inside two turns and nothing surprised me hard
  enough to want one back. It is a 0-cost blank in practice.
- **Slimed.** 1 energy to draw 1 card and exhaust is a wash; I played one purely to not
  waste a floating energy.
- Lethal turns (fight 2 turn 4, fight 4 turn 5, fight 5 turn 4) were automatic, but each
  was the payoff of a plan set two turns earlier, not a dead turn.

### (c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text

1. **Second Wave, written alone, does nothing and says nothing.** Its face reads "Gain 4
   Block. Plan: The next Plan is carried out twice." Written as a Plan on fight 1 turn 1,
   it produced no Block (Block 0 at turn start), the log said *"Second Wave — no enemy lost
   HP"*, the buff list showed nothing pending, and the very next Plan I wrote (Coral
   Bulwark, the following turn) came out **un-doubled**. The doubling evidently lives only
   within a single carry-out queue — write another Plan behind it on the *same* turn and it
   works perfectly (proved twice, fight 1 turn 5 and fight 5 turn 2). Nothing on the card,
   in the Plan keyword, or in the Bake-Kurage block says that. I burned 1 energy and a
   turn of tempo on a card that failed silently. **This is the round's clearest defect.**
   Related, smaller: when a card with a "Gain N Block" first clause is written as a Plan,
   the face-up Block is simply not gained, and only the Plan clause happens. That is
   probably intended, but the card face reads as two sentences, not as an either/or.
2. **The Bake-Kurage ignores debuffs on me, unannounced.** Under Shrink -1 ("you deal 30%
   less damage with every hit you land"), my hand re-printed Kurage's Oath as "2 damage" —
   and the same card, carried out by the jellyfish, dealt **7**. That is a big lever and I
   found it by accident. The Plan keyword is precise about what *does* transfer ("Your
   Strength folds in as you write it; the enemy's Vulnerable counts next turn") which made
   its silence here read as "nothing else changes".
3. **HP arithmetic I could not close from printed numbers.** Three times an enemy lost
   about 2 more HP than the printed card numbers accounted for (fight 2 round 3: 33 → 22;
   fight 4 turn 4: 36 → 18 on 16 of card damage; fight 3's Electro-Charged beat). I suspect
   an unlogged Casket trigger or the reaction's own initial tick, but the screen never told
   me, and the log's otherwise-excellent "Inside the same beat: Tamakushi Casket 2"
   annotation appears only for Plan carry-outs, never for cards I play myself.
4. **Intent numbers vs. my own debuffs.** The page says outright it does no arithmetic on
   the intent figure, so facing "21 damage" while Vulnerable 2 I could not tell whether the
   real number was 21 or 31. I over-blocked to be safe. Honest, but it makes the biggest
   decision in a fight a guess.
5. Minor: on fight 1 turn 1 the Bake-Kurage block printed *"No Plan card in hand: the
   jellyfish waits"* while Second Thoughts was in my hand carrying the *Plan* keyword
   block. The keyword glossary attaches to cards that are not themselves Plans.

### (d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw

- **Never wanted:** **Second Thoughts**. A 0-cost Exhaust skill that undoes a Plan, in a
  system where Plans resolve fast enough that I never regretted one. Drawn three times,
  never played, never close. (Second Wave is the more *interesting* failure, but I did want
  to play it — it just needed a rule nobody printed.)
- **Happiest to draw:** **Kaeya — Frostgnaw**, once I had it. It is 8 damage and 3 Block on
  one 1-cost card, and against a body wearing my own Hydro aura its face grows a
  *"Reaction preview: Frozen"* line that turns it into a 20-damage two-card combo with any
  Strike. The card that teaches you the combo on its own face is the card I want in this
  kit. Runner-up: **Kurage's Oath**, which is the card the Plan system is built around and
  the one whose write-or-play decision was live almost every turn.

### (e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?

**Yes.** Three enemies, 4 damage incoming, 3 energy, and a hand holding both a Plan card
and exactly enough Strikes to kill the only attacker. The choice was whether to spend the
turn killing Twig Slime (12 into 11 — no margin) or to bank the turn into the Bake-Kurage
and eat the damage, and the third energy was a genuine third option (Defend, which turned
out to be worthless because the surviving enemies were on Status intents). I made the
