## Fight 1 — Shrinker Beetle (40 HP)

**Turn 1.** Hand: Strike, Defend, Slack Water, Defend, Strike. Enemy intent was
*Strategic (DebuffStrong)*, so it was not going to hit me. Played Strike, Strike,
Slack Water (all on A) for 18 — 6 + 6 + 4, plus 2 more because Slack Water's Weak
set off Tamakushi Casket's "whenever you apply a debuff to an enemy, it deals 2
Hydro damage".

*Rejected:* writing Slack Water on the Bake-Kurage as its **Dusk Plan** ("Apply 1
Weak to ALL enemies"). Against one body, ALL is one body, so the Plan buys the
same Weak for none of the 4 damage — the Plan line is strictly worse the moment
the fight has a single enemy. Also rejected Defend, because the printed intent
said the enemy was not attacking. **This was a real decision, made on the turn,
and the screen decided it**: the intent line and the "ALL enemies" wording were
both legible enough to settle it without guessing.

**Turn 2.** I now wore **Shrink −1** ("While Shrinker Beetle is alive, you deal
30% less damage"), and the card faces had *re-printed themselves*: Strike now
read "Deal 4 damage", Kurage's Oath "Deal 3 damage to ALL. Plan: Deal 7 damage to
ALL". That is the single best piece of legibility in the whole run — the debuff
was applied to the numbers on the cards, not left for me to compute.

Played Kurage's Oath **onto the Bake-Kurage** (1 energy, writes the Plan), then
Strike, Strike for 8. Enemy 22 → 14.

*Rejected:* Kurage's Oath face-up for 3 now. The Plan pays 7 next turn for the
same 1 energy — 2.3× the damage for a turn of delay, against a 22-HP body I was
not killing this turn either way. This is the kit's central trade and it read
clearly the first time I met it.

**Turn 3.** The Plan fired at the start of the turn for exactly 7 (14 → 7), and
Strike + Strike (8) killed it. **No rejected alternative on this turn, and that
is not a dead turn** — the lethal was set up two turns earlier by the choice to
bank Kurage's Oath. The decision that won the fight was made on turn 2.

Reward: 10 Gold, Liquid Memories, and a card. Took **Sayu — Yoohoo Art: Fuuin
Dash** [Anemo] over Exposed Flank, Tide Chart and Stolen Chapter. Reason: every
card I owned was Hydro, every battle screen kept printing "NO REACTION IS
REACHABLE HERE: Hydro is the only element this screen can supply", and a tester
who never gets a second element never finds out whether the reaction layer works.
Exposed Flank (Plan: 2 Vulnerable to ALL) was probably the stronger card.

only arithmetic that fits is 12 − 9 = 3, i.e. **the Vulnerable I was wearing did
not multiply the damage I took**. Either the intent number already had everything
folded in (the page warns elsewhere that it does no arithmetic on the intent
figure) or player-side Vulnerable does not do what its own gloss says. I could
not tell from the screen, and that is the finding.

## Fight 7 (elite, floor 12) — Bygone Effigy (127 HP)

**Booming Conch fired**: 7 cards in hand and Energy 4/3. The Effigy was
*Sleeping* on turn 1 and wore **Slow 0 — "Whenever you play a card, this enemy
receives 10% more damage from Attacks this turn. It counts the cards played
BEFORE this one."**

This is the best-designed turn in the run, and it is not a Kokomi card that makes
it — it is the enemy. Slow turns every turn into an *ordering* puzzle: cheap and
non-damaging cards first, biggest attack last.

**Turn 1** (free — it was asleep). Wrote War Council and Ambush as Plans (cards 1
and 2, which cost me nothing in multiplier since they deal no face-up damage),
then Strike as card 3 (×1.2) and Sayu as card 4 (×1.3) — Sayu last because 8 > 6.
Predicted 7 + 10 = 17; screen came back 127 → 110. Exact.

**Turn 2.** The two Plans paid 19 at the start (Ambush 12 + War Council 5 + 2
Casket). That meant Sango Isshin printed its big mode. Wrote Kurage's Oath as a
Plan (card 1) and played **Sango** as card 2 into Slow 10.

**Second place where the screen and the outcome disagree.** Sango's big clause is
"deal a quarter of your Max HP to ALL enemies" = 20 at 80 Max HP. As card 2 with
Slow at 10, an Attack should land 22. Across the beat the Effigy went 110 → 91 →
64, i.e. 19 from the two Plans and **exactly 27 from Sango + the Oath Plan (7)**,
which forces Sango = 20, un-amplified. On the *very next* turn I checked the same
mechanism with ordinary cards — Feint as card 2 and Strike as card 3 against Slow
— and got 11 and 7 from printed 10 and 6, i.e. ×1.1 and ×1.2 exactly. So Slow
demonstrably works, and **Sango's Max-HP clause is the thing that ignores it**,
while Sango's card face calls itself an attack and Slow's face says "from
Attacks". I could not reconcile that from any screen.

(Later, in fight 9, Sango's big mode dealt **22** while I carried Strength 2 —
so the Max-HP clause *does* take Strength. It takes Strength and not Slow.)

**Turn 3.** Wrote **Breakwater** (card 1), Feint (11), Strike (7). The
Bake-Kurage's own badge then read *"Plan 1 — Carries out 1 Plan at the start of
your next turn"*, which **contradicts Breakwater's own Dusk line** ("at the end of
this turn, before enemies act"). The Dusk line was the true one; the badge's
boilerplate is wrong for Dusk plans. Numbers below in the Debrief.

**Turn 4.** 23 incoming, Strength 10 on the Effigy, me at 37. Used Mazaleth's Gift
(Ritual 1), wrote **Ripple first** and Kurage's Oath second, played Nereid's
Ascension+. *Rejected:* writing Oath first. Nereid doubles only the **first**
Plan, so the order of two writes is a real choice: Oath-first is 14 damage,
Ripple-first is +2 Energy and +8 Block. At 37 HP against a 23 I took the energy
and the block, and I would take it again — it is what made the kill turn possible.

**Turn 5** (kill). Energy 6, Strength 1 from Ritual, Slow live. Played Defend
(card 1, ramp + insurance), War Council face-up (card 2, Weak — its 23 became 17
on the screen), Slack Water (card 3), Strike (card 4) — 39 → 20 — then
**Gigantification Potion** and the last Strike as card 5 into Slow 40: 7 × 3 ×
1.4 ≈ 29 against 20. Dead.

*Rejected:* using Gigantification earlier, or on Sango. Holding it for the card
with the highest Slow multiplier was the whole point; the elite's own debuff told
me when to spend it. **This fight had a decision on every single turn, and none
of them were the same decision.**

Rewards: 38 gold, **Happy Flower**, and **Shinobu — Grass Ring of Sanctification**
(cost 0, 4 Block, +4 more if I lost HP this turn) over The Moon, Second Wave and
Sea-Salt Prayer — chosen at 20/80 HP for the free block.

## The kit, after 10 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

1. **Face-up or written, every single turn a Plan card is in hand.** This is the
   kit's spine and it is a genuinely good one, because it is not a fixed answer:
   in fight 4 turn 1 I wrote War Council and in fight 4 turn 4 I played the same
   card face-up, for reasons the screen made checkable both times (body count,
   whose turn the Weak needs to land on, whether the enemy will be standing in
   Block when the carry-out arrives). *Made on the turn.*
2. **Which Plan goes first, once Nereid's Ascension+ is down.** Nereid doubles
   only the first Plan, so writing Ripple-then-Oath and writing Oath-then-Ripple
   are different turns: +2 Energy/+8 Block versus +7 damage. I took each at least
   once and both were right at the time. *Made on the turn, enabled at the draft.*
3. **Shaping enemy HP to a number I have already committed to.** Fights 2 and 9
   were both won by aiming face-up attacks so that a banked 7 or 12 would be
   exactly lethal. This is a kind of decision I have not met in this genre often
   and it is the best thing here. *Made on the turn.*
4. **Card ordering under the Bygone Effigy's Slow.** Cheap cards first, biggest
   attack last, potion timed to the highest multiplier. *Made on the turn — and
   supplied by the enemy, not the kit.*
5. **Whether to play a Block card at all**, because Orichalcum pays 6 for holding
   none and Defend pays 5. Every small block card in the deck became a trap. *Made
   on the turn, created at the treasure chest.*
6. **The draft itself.** Taking Ripple (a 0-cost Plan writer) is what made Feint
   and Sango live cards; taking Sango without enough Plan writers is what made it
   a 2-energy 8-damage brick for half the run. Fight 3 had no decisions in it *at
   all* and that was a draft outcome, not a turn outcome. *Made at the draft.*

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

Every turn where no Plan card was in hand. Fight 3 turns 1 and 2 were "play your
three attacks" with nothing to weigh, and Feint and Sango both sat in hand
printing their small numbers. The kit has two modes — engine on, engine off — and
engine-off is a plain, dull deck of Strikes.

Slack Water's **Dusk Plan** was never worth writing against a single enemy, and I
met single enemies in five of ten fights: the Plan line trades 4 damage for
nothing when "ALL" is one body. It was excellent against four bodies in fight 4.
That is fine as design; I only note that the card reads as a choice more often
than it is one.

**Defend** stopped being playable the moment I picked up Orichalcum. So did
Shinobu — Grass Ring of Sanctification, the card I chose *for* its block, which
at 4 was below the relic's 6 and only worth playing stacked on top of other block.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

- **Sango Isshin's big clause ignores Slow but takes Strength.** Under Slow 10 as
  the second card it dealt 20, not 22; in fight 9 with Strength 2 it dealt 22, not
  20. Slow's face says "10% more damage from Attacks" and Sango's face calls
  itself an attack. Feint and Strike took the same Slow correctly on the very next
  turn (10→11, 6→7), so the mechanism works and this card is the exception.
- **Player-side Vulnerable did nothing I could measure.** Wearing "Vulnerable 2 —
  Receive 50% more damage", a 12-damage attack against 9 Block cost me exactly 3
  HP. Enemy-side Vulnerable, by contrast, was clearly visible (my hits went ×1.5).
- **The Kin Priest lost 32 HP on boss turn 1 where the printed faces total at most
  29.** I could not source the remainder.
- **The Bake-Kurage's Plan badge lies about Dusk plans.** With only Breakwater
  written it read "Carries out 1 Plan at the start of your next turn", while
  Breakwater's own line says end of this turn. The card was right; the badge is
  boilerplate that does not know about Dusk.
- **Breakwater's "plus 3 for each Plan the Bake-Kurage is holding" is unreadable
  from the screen.** The badge counts Breakwater itself among the Plans held, but
  the block it paid says it does not count itself. Numbers in the Debrief.
- Minor: the observe page duplicated whole blocks (every enemy line printed
  twice) on several screens. Cosmetic, but it made a couple of reads slower.

Everything else was *unusually* legible and I want that on the record as loudly
as the defects. Debuffs re-print the numbers on the card faces (Shrink turned
"Deal 6" into "Deal 4"; Frail turned "Gain 5 Block" into "Gain 3"; Tangled turned
Slack Water's cost from 1 into 2). Weak visibly moved enemy intent numbers. The
end-of-fight page prints the Bake-Kurage's last carry-out line by line with the
HP each body lost, precisely because a Plan that kills never reaches a battle
screen. I predicted damage totals to the point six or seven times and the screen
agreed to the point every time except the three cases above.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Sango Isshin.** Two energy is a third of a turn, and off-engine it
is 8 damage — worse per energy than a Strike. I bought it for 72 gold and it was
a brick in fights 4 and 6, then a 20-to-ALL bomb in 7, 9 and 10. A card whose
whole value is a coin-flip on whether a *different* card showed up first is the
least pleasant thing in the deck. (Runner-up: Defend, which Orichalcum retired.)

Happiest to draw: **Ripple.** Zero cost, writes a Plan, and the Plan hands back
energy and block — so it turns Feint on, turns Sango on, and pays for itself. When
Nereid doubled it (Energy 5/3, Block 8, in fight 6) it was the best moment in the
run. **Nereid's Ascension+** is the more powerful card; Ripple is the one I was
glad to see, because it is the one that makes the engine start.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a good one.** Turn 1 of fight 1 put Slack Water in my hand against a
single Shrinker Beetle telegraphing a debuff, with the choice of playing it
face-up (4 damage, 1 Weak, and the Casket's 2) or writing its Dusk Plan (1 Weak
to ALL, resolving before the enemy acts). Both halves were printed on the card,
the Casket's rider was printed in the relic line, and the enemy's intent told me
its attack was not coming — so the choice was decidable from the screen and it had
a right answer for a reason (ALL is one body here). That is exactly what a first
turn should be. The *second* decision on that same turn — whether to Defend
against an enemy that was not attacking — was free, but the first one was real.

## Debrief

> Name every turn where you held Breakwater or Slack Water. For Breakwater: how
> many Plans the Bake-Kurage was holding when the turn ended, by your own count,
> and the Block it paid, checked against each other; and whether you wrote the
> other Plans before or after it. For Slack Water: whether you played it face-up
> or wrote it, the number of bodies, what was telegraphed, and when the Weak
> landed relative to the enemy's attack.

**Breakwater** — drafted at the end of fight 6, so it existed for fights 7, 8, 9
and 10. I held it on four turns and wrote it on three of them.

1. **Fight 7 (elite), turn 3.** Wrote it **first** of my three cards; **no other
   Plan was written that turn, before or after** — Feint and Strike were both
   played face-up. By my count the Bake-Kurage was holding **exactly one Plan
   (Breakwater itself)** when the turn ended, and the badge agreed: it read
   "Plan 1". **Block paid: 5.** The check: the Effigy hit for 23; I went 49 → 37,
   so 11 Block stood. I was holding no Block from any card, so Orichalcum's 6
   fired, leaving 5 for Breakwater. 5 = 5 + 3×0. **So Breakwater counted zero
   Plans held, even though the badge counted itself as one.** It does not count
   itself.
2. **Fight 8, turn 3.** Wrote Kurage's Oath **first**, Breakwater **second**, so
   there was **one other Plan written before it** and none after; the badge read
   "Plan 2" when the turn ended, and by my own count the jellyfish was holding
   two (Oath + Breakwater). **Block paid: not isolable, and I say so rather than
   guess.** I had already stacked 17 Block from Ripple's doubled carry-out (8),
   Shinobu (4) and Defend (5); the Shambler's hit was 16 reduced to 12 by Slack
   Water's Weak, so the 17 alone covered it and I took 0. Whether Breakwater added
   5 (5+3×0) or 8 (5+3×1) is invisible in that outcome. On the reading that fight
   7 established — it does not count itself, and it *should* have counted Kurage's
   Oath — the expected figure is **8**.
3. **Fight 10 (boss), turn 3.** Wrote it **first**; **no other Plan written that
   turn**, before or after. Holding **one (itself)** at end of turn. **Block paid:
   consistent with 5, not separable from 8.** I had 14 from two Defends plus a
   carried-over 4; incoming was 17; I took 0, so anything ≥ 3 from Breakwater
   would have done. On fight 7's rule the figure is **5**.
4. **Fight 10 (boss), turn 4** — Breakwater was *not* in hand; I have no fourth
   write. (The fourth turn I *held* the card was fight 9 turn 2, where I had it in
   the discard rather than the hand — I did not hold it. So: three writes, three
   turns held, and I am reporting all of them.)

**The honest summary on Breakwater:** the only turn where its number was
separable from the rest of my Block gave **5 with the badge showing "Plan 1"** —
which means the "+3 for each Plan the Bake-Kurage is holding" clause and the Plan
badge disagree about whether the card counts itself. I never once managed to write
two *other* Plans before it, so I never saw the clause pay more than its base, and
across three writes I could confirm a bonus of exactly **zero**. As a card
experienced blind, it is a 1-energy 5-Block skill with a rider I could not make
fire — and one that is, in this deck, *strictly worse than holding no Block at
all*, because Orichalcum pays 6 for the empty turn.

**Slack Water** — a starter, so it was in every fight. I drew and held it on
**eight** turns, and here is each one:

1. **Fight 1, turn 1** — one body (Shrinker Beetle 40). **Played face-up.**
   Telegraphed: *Strategic (DebuffStrong)* — no attack coming. Weak landed
   immediately, on my own turn, and was therefore **wasted** — it decayed at the
   end of the enemy's turn without an attack to reduce. I played it for the 4 + 2
   damage and knew the Weak was dead on arrival. *Rejected the Dusk Plan because
   with one body, "ALL" is one body.*
2. **Fight 1, turn 3** — held it, did not play it; the fight ended on the Plan and
   two Strikes.
3. **Fight 2, turn 2** — one body (Twig Slime (M) 19). **Played face-up.**
   Telegraphed: Attack for 11. Weak landed **on my turn, before its attack**, and
   the screen showed the intent drop **11 → 8**. Then Strike and Sayu killed it,
   so the Weak never actually had to matter.
4. **Fight 3, turn 3** — one body (Nibbit, 9 HP behind 5 Block). **Played
   face-up**, third of three cards. Telegraphed: *Empower (Buff)*. Weak landed and
   the Casket's 2 was part of the exact lethal; the Weak itself did nothing
   because the body died.
5. **Fight 4, turn 1** — **four bodies** (27/32/11/8). **Written, as its Dusk
   Plan.** Telegraphed: three *StatusCard* intents and one Attack for 4. The Dusk
   carried out **at the end of my own turn, before the enemies acted**, exactly as
   its face says: Weak landed on all four **ahead of the attack**, the Casket
   answered each Weak for 2, and that 2 is what killed the 8-HP Twig Slime I had
   Struck down to 2. This is the one turn where the card was unambiguously worth
   writing, and the body count is the whole reason.
6. **Fight 5, turn 1** — one body (Mawler 72). **Played face-up.** Telegraphed:
   Attack 4×2. Weak landed on my turn, before the attack; I took 6 instead of 8.
7. **Fight 6, turn 1** — two bodies (Twig Slime 28, Flyconid 47). **Played
   face-up**, on the Flyconid. Telegraphed: Flyconid Attack 8 + Debuff; Twig Slime
   StatusCard. Weak landed before its attack and took the 8 down to 6, which
   Orichalcum's 6 then absorbed entirely — I took **0** that turn. *Rejected the
   Dusk Plan* even at two bodies, because only one of the two was attacking, so
   the extra Weak was worth less than 4 damage.
8. **Fight 7 (elite), turn 5** — one body. **Played face-up**, third of five cards
   in the Slow ramp. Telegraphed: Attack 23 (Strength 10). Weak had already been
   applied by War Council one card earlier, so Slack Water's Weak was the second
   stack; the intent read 17 by then. The fight ended that turn.
9. **Fight 8, turn 3** — one body (Vine Shambler). **Played face-up**, at cost 2
   because Tangled re-printed it. Telegraphed: Attack 16. Weak landed on my turn
   and cut it to 12, which my 17 Block ate whole.
10. **Fight 10 (boss), turn 2** — three bodies. **Played face-up**, into the Kin
    Priest, to spend its Vulnerable window (×1.5) rather than to spread a Weak.
    Telegraphed: 7 / 2×2 / 8 + Debuff. The Weak landed on the Priest before its
    attack.

**The pattern, plainly:** nine face-up, one written. The Dusk Plan is the right
side of the card only when the board has three or more attacking bodies, which
happened once in ten fights. Every time I played it face-up the Weak landed on my
own turn, ahead of the enemy's attack — so the Dusk clause's advertised advantage
("before enemies act") is **not** an advantage over playing it face-up at all;
face-up gets the Weak there just as early *and* keeps the 4 damage. The Dusk line
only buys reach across bodies. That is a much narrower card than its face
suggests.
