### Pair 1 — mutually exclusive

1. **Card involvement.** The prototype was played and seriously weighed by both graders: one called the Block line “real,” while the other actually chose “Gain 3 Block.”

2. **Change in kind of choice.** Yes. The shipped card bundled maximum damage with Block and dominated the alternatives; the prototype forced a genuine choice between advancing the damage race and retaining 3 Block.

3. **Did the cost bind?** Yes. Choosing defence cost 8 damage, the Pyro application, and the resulting reaction; choosing damage cost all 3 Block and left the player exposed at effectively 2 HP after the attack.

4. **Did the repair work?** Yes. Removing the routing attack and lowering HP made both modes credible enough to split the graders, directly correcting round 3’s noncompetitive Block mode.

5. **Judgment: RETURN.** Return the arm for an implementation repair, not a board redesign: the game incorrectly demands a target for the targetless Block mode, so the grader’s defensive line could not be mechanically replayed. Re-run this repaired board after fixing that defect.

### Pair 2 — priced in cost

1. **Card involvement.** The prototype was seriously weighed but not played: one grader “checked it and dropped it fast,” while the other explicitly “seriously considered” it.

2. **Change in kind of choice.** Yes. The shipped alternative combined Block and AOE with a follow-up attack; the prototype’s price turned defence into a whole-turn commitment opposed to the two-kill offensive line.

3. **Did the cost bind?** Yes. Paying three energy for the defence sacrificed both single-target plays, two enemy removals, prevention of the Status cards, and removal of the Small Leaf’s 3-damage attack.

4. **Did the repair work?** Yes. Raising the bodies to 6 HP removed the AOE’s decisive-removal role: it killed nothing, so considering the prototype meant considering whether 4 Block justified abandoning the board clear.

5. **Judgment: ADVANCE.** The repaired board isolated the intended pricing question and showed a concrete change from bundled defence-plus-follow-up to an all-in defensive turn; that warrants testing in whole-fight play.

Round 4 supports both intended decision shapes: exclusivity created a genuinely contested mode choice, and added price converted defence into a distinct turn-level commitment. Nothing warrants **ESCALATE**: Pair 1’s divergent choices demonstrate closeness rather than an irreconcilable contradiction, while its failed replay is a specific mechanical defect that must be fixed before advancement.
