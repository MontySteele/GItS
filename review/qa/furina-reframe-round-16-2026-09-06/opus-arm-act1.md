## Fight 1 — Toadpole (1) 23 HP, Toadpole (2) 21 HP

**First turn, stage and arrival (logging ask 4):** the stage held one member,
**Crabaletta**, marked FRONT. The arrival performance **did** print, under
*What your Salon did this turn*: "**Crabaletta** hit Toadpole (1) for 6 Hydro,
and it is wearing a Hydro aura." Encore read 2 and Fanfare read 2 after it —
so the arrival performance minted its 2 Fanfare but appears not to have charged
Encore for itself.

### Turn 1 (3 energy, Encore 2)

The screen made a hard claim twice — once on the Ethereal Spotlight card face
and once as a standalone italic line under the hand:

> *You open a fight with 2 Encore and **Ethereal Spotlight** costs 2 -- all of
> it. Any performance spends one, so it is this turn's first action or not this
> fight.*

I did not believe it, because **Aria of Recompense** was sitting in the same
hand reading "Gain 5 Encore," and gaining Encore is not performing. So:

1. **Aria of Recompense.** Encore 2 → **7**. The tip is false.
   *Rejected:* playing Ethereal Spotlight first, which is what the screen told
   me to do. Rejecting it was strictly better — I got the Spotlight *and* five
   spare Encore.
2. **Ethereal Spotlight.** Encore 7 → 5, Guest Cast 1 lit. Confirmed legible:
   Lynette's face changed from "Gain 5 Block" to "Gain 7 Block" the moment it
   resolved.
3. **An Invitation** (0 cost) → drew Barbara — Let the Show Begin♪, whose face
   read 9 Block (base 6, spotlit).
   *Rejected:* nothing — a free card with no downside is not a decision.
4. **Barbara** on Toadpole (1). 9 Block, and it performed Crabaletta for 6.
   *Rejected:* Lynette (7 Block, also performs). Barbara was strictly more
   Block, and Lynette's Swirl was inert — the screen said so itself: "NO
   REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE: Hydro is the only element this screen can
   supply."
5. **Soloist's Solicitation** on Toadpole (1), 6 damage, → 11 HP.
   *Rejected:* Lynette for 6 more damage plus 7 more Block. I took the aimed
   6 over the random 6 because members "pick their own enemy at random," and
   the extra Block was dead against 7 incoming that 9 Block already covered.

**Verdict on the turn:** a real decision, and the best one of the run — but it
was a decision about *disbelieving the screen*, not a designed trade.

### Turn 2 (3 energy)

Toadpole (1) had gained **Thorns 2** ("When hit by an attack, deal 2 damage
back"). This is logging ask 2 and I got a clean read on it.

1. **Curtain Cue** (0). I had not moved the Spotlight this turn, so it took the
   "Otherwise" branch for 1 Encore. Free, so not a decision.
2. **Soloist's Solicitation** on A, 6 damage — killed it.
   **Thorns fired on a card hit:** my HP stayed 62/78 and **Encore fell by 2**
   (5 → 3). So the retaliation landed on Encore, not HP, exactly as the Encore
   text promises ("After Block it absorbs damage before HP").
3. **Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire** on B (21 HP, no aura). Printed order in
   the Salon log and the HP numbers together: B went 21 → **2**, i.e. 19 in one
   card. Chevreuse's own hit did 10 and applied Pyro; *then* the member
   performance resolved and printed "**Crabaletta** hit Toadpole (2) for **9**
   Hydro, and left no aura on it" — 6 base, ×1.5 for **Vaporize** into the Pyro
   Chevreuse had just applied.
   Two facts fell out of this and both held for the rest of the run: **the
   card's own effect resolves before the member performance it triggers**, and
   **member performances do apply elements and do trigger Elemental
   Reactions**, even though the Salon text insists a performance "is not an
   Attack and not a hit."
   *Rejected:* Chevreuse on the already-wounded A. Aiming at the fresh body was
   the play because Chevreuse's Pyro would be self-comboing with the Hydro my
   own member was about to apply.
4. **Curtain Rises** on B for lethal.

**Fight judged as a whole:** two turns, zero HP lost. The turn-2 Chevreuse line
was a genuine plan — apply Pyro with the card so that my own member's Hydro
vaporizes it — and it is the most satisfying thing the kit did all run. The
lethal on turn 2 was that plan paying off, not a dead turn.

**Reward:** took Razor — Claw and Thunder (Companion, Electro) over Duet,
Shared Billing and a second Second Course. Reason: my deck was mono-Hydro and
nothing in it could react with itself except through Chevreuse; a second
element is what makes the reaction layer live.

## Fight 2 — Sludge Spinner, 39 HP

**First turn (logging ask 4):** stage held **Crabaletta** (FRONT). Arrival
performance printed: "hit Sludge Spinner for 6 Hydro."

### Turn 1 (3 energy, Encore 2)

1. **Aria of Recompense.** Encore → 7. (Fanfare was 2, so the "at least 3
   Fanfare" clause did *not* fire — only 5, which is correct and legible.)
2. **Ethereal Spotlight.** Encore → 5.
3. **Salon Début.** Deployed a second Crabaletta; it performed for 6.
4. **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint.** 7 Block, performed the front Crabaletta for 6.
   *Rejected:* Soloist's Solicitation (same 6 damage, no Block) and Stage
   Presence (6 Block, no damage). Lynette did both for the same energy, so this
   was not really a decision — it was a strictly-dominant card. The Swirl half
   was inert again: one enemy, so "copy the aura onto ALL enemies" copies it
   onto itself.

### Turn 2 (3 energy)

The Spinner's turn-1 debuff had landed **Weak 1** on me. This is logging ask 3
and it produced the run's cleanest good news and one genuine oddity.

- **The card face folded Weak in correctly.** Razor, which had read "Deal 8
  damage" on the reward screen, now read **"Deal 12 damage"** at one point and
  **"Deal 9 damage"** while Weak was on me. 8 × 1.5 (Spotlight) × 0.75 (Weak)
  = 9. I could read my own debuff straight off the card. That is very good.
- **Weak did not touch a performance.** With Weak 1 still on me, Salon Début's
  deploy printed "**Crabaletta** hit Sludge Spinner for **6** Hydro" and the
  Spinner lost exactly 6. Base 6, no 0.75. This is consistent with the Salon
  text ("A performance is not an Attack and not a hit") but it means **Weak is
  a much smaller punishment for this character than the card faces imply**,
  because most of the kit's damage is performances that Weak cannot see.

1. **Rain of Roses.** Hydro to all, Encore → 7.
2. **Salon Début.** Third Crabaletta; stage now full at 3. Performed for 6.
3. **Razor — Claw and Thunder** on A. I played it *believing* a full stage
   would make a Companion card Evoke the front member — see (c); it did not.
   The Spinner was on 15 and Razor's 12 plus the ordinary 6 performance was
   exactly lethal, so the fight ended and I could not tell from the result that
   my reading was wrong. **The screen never printed anything at all about how
   the fight ended** — the reward page simply replaced the combat page, taking
   the Salon log with it.

**Fight judged as a whole:** two turns, zero HP lost, and effectively one
decision (Rain of Roses vs. more Block), made at the turn. The draft decision
that shaped it — taking Razor — mattered more than anything I did on a turn.

## Fight 3 (Elite) — Phantasmal Gardener ×4, 29 / 27 / 31 / 28 HP

Every one of the four carried **Skittish 6**: "The first time Phantasmal
Gardener is hit each turn, it gains 6 Block." This is logging ask 2 and it gave
the cleanest possible reading of the performance/hit distinction.

**First turn (logging ask 4):** stage held **Crabaletta** (FRONT); the arrival
performance printed — "hit Phantasmal Gardener (4) for 6 Hydro" — and
Gardener (4) went to 22/28 **with no Block gained**. So a member's performance
did *not* fire Skittish, on the very first line of the fight.

### Turn 1 (3 energy, Encore 2)

1. **Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire (1)** on D, *deliberately before* the
   Spotlight. This was the best-designed decision I made all run: playing a
   Companion card first pushed Fanfare from 2 to 4, which crosses Aria's "at
   least 3 Fanfare" threshold and doubles its payout. The price was playing
   Chevreuse un-spotlit (7 base instead of 10).
   *Rejected:* the standard Aria → Spotlight → Chevreuse order, which would
   have done 5 more damage to D but left me on 6 Encore instead of 11 going
   into a four-body elite. I traded 5 damage for 5 Encore, and against 15
   incoming per turn Encore is the armour.
   Result: D 22 → **12**, i.e. **10 damage from a printed 7** — Vaporize into
   its Hydro aura, ×1.5. **And D gained Block 6.** So: *a card hit fired
   Skittish; the block was granted after the damage, not before.*
2. **Aria of Recompense.** Encore 1 → **11**. The Fanfare≥3 clause paid the
   full extra 5 exactly as printed.
3. **Ethereal Spotlight.** Encore → 9.
4. **Curtain Cue** — this time *after* moving the Spotlight, so it took the
   good branch: **+3 Encore and draw 1** (Encore → 12, drew Charlotte). The
   card's own tip explains this ("a card asking this wants the Spotlight played
   first, this turn") and it is accurate.
   *Rejected:* nothing; 0 cost.
5. **Chevreuse (2)** on C, which was wearing Hydro from my own member's
   arrival hit. Vaporize again: C 25 → 10.
   *Rejected:* Charlotte's 2 Vulnerable. I skipped it because members pick
   their own target at random, so Vulnerable on one body is a coin-flip whether
   the follow-up performance even lands on it.

### Turn 2 (3 energy, Encore 0)

**Encore 12 → 0 in a single enemy turn.** I took 15 across three attacks and
lost 12 Encore and 4 HP. That is the honest shape of Encore: it is a large,
fast-draining pool, not a wall, and it evaporates on exactly the turn you most
want it for performances.

1. **An Invitation** (0) → Gorou — Inuzaka All-Round Defense, face reading
   "Deal 12 damage. Gain Block equal to half the damage dealt."
2. **Gorou** on D (12 HP). Killed it. **My Block before: 0. My Block after: 0.**
   *Where the screen and the outcome disagree:* the card promises Block equal
   to half the damage dealt, 12 damage was dealt, and no Block arrived. Caveat
   I want on the record — **both** times I played Gorou this run it was a
   killing blow, so I cannot separate "the clause is broken" from "the clause
   does not fire on a kill". It needs one non-lethal test.
   *Rejected:* Gorou on B (27 HP, non-lethal — which would have tested exactly
   that). I chose the kill because D was about to hit me for 7 and I had 0
   Encore. Correct play, and it cost me the measurement.
   Also printed here, and excellent: "**Crabaletta** hit Phantasmal Gardener (2)
   for 4 Hydro ... **(dry: it could not pay its Encore, so it acted at
   three-quarters)**". That single parenthetical is the only thing that made
   "acts at 3/4" comprehensible — see (c).
3. **Applause Line** on C, 4 damage. Its face read "Deal 4 damage, already
   including Fanfare" at Fanfare 5, having read 3 in the shop at Fanfare 0.
4. **Salon Début** — second member — and **Stage Presence** for 6 Block.
   *Rejected:* a third attack. With 0 Encore and 10 incoming, Block was the
   only thing standing between me and HP.

### Turn 3 (3 energy, Encore 0)

1. **Rain of Roses.** Encore → 5, Hydro onto everything.
2. **Salon Début.** Stage full at 3, performance for a full 6.
3. **Razor** on A. **This is where my misreading was exposed.** The status line
   said "A full stage Evokes the front member. A Companion card you play
   performs Crabaletta," and I read that as *full stage ⇒ Companion card
   Evokes*. It does not. The log printed an ordinary performance — "hit
   Phantasmal Gardener (3) for 6 Hydro" — the Salon still held three
   Crabalettas afterwards, and no Evoke line appeared. I had spent the whole
   turn setting up an Evoke that was never going to happen.
   Razor itself delivered its printed 12 into A's Hydro aura and consumed it;
   the reaction was **Electro-Charged** ("loses 4 HP at the start of its turn"),
   which duly showed up next turn as A dropping 11 → 7 unprompted. A also
   **gained Block 6** — Skittish firing on a card hit again, in the same turn
   that my performance on another body fired nothing.

### Turn 4 (3 energy, Encore 0) — the first real Evoke

Re-reading the Deploy keyword — "*deploying* onto a full stage Evokes the front
member first" — told me what actually triggers it.

1. **Curtain Rises** on A: a Deploy, onto a full stage.
   **Logging ask 1, Evoke #1.** Encore held immediately before: **0**.
   Printed, in this order:
   > - **Crabaletta** took its final bow — an EVOKE, so it left the stage. It
   >   hit Phantasmal Gardener (2) for **10** Hydro, and minted 5 Fanfare, its
   >   Focus counting 3 times.
   > - **Chevalmarin** hit Phantasmal Gardener (2) for 2 Hydro, and it is
   >   wearing a Hydro aura (dry: it could not pay its Encore, so it acted at
   >   three-quarters).
   **The Evoke line printed first, the joining member's line second** — which
   is exactly what "Evokes the front member first" promises. Delivered: 10, not
   the printed 14, because Encore was 0 and the Evoke went dry (14 × 0.75 =
   10.5 → 10). Fanfare went 9 → 16 (+5 minted by the Evoke, +2 from
   Chevalmarin).
   *Rejected:* Gorou on A for a guaranteed kill. I took Curtain Rises first
   because A had 6 Block on it and 1 HP under that, and Gorou's 12 would kill
   A whether or not the Evoke landed there — so leading with the Evoke cost me
   nothing and might have saved a card.
2. **Soloist's Solicitation** on B (5 HP) — lethal.
3. **Gorou** on A — lethal. Block gained again: **0**.

**Fight judged as a whole:** four turns, 15 HP. The best decision was at turn
1 (Chevreuse-before-Aria, buying 5 Encore with 5 damage) and it was made on the
turn. The worst was turn 3, and it was caused by the screen, not by me.

**Rewards:** Captain's Wheel and Duplicator — see (c), the reward screen names
relics but prints no text, so both picks were blind. Card: **Hearts Swelling**
(Innate, 7 Encore) over Showstopper, Macaron Break and Lynette — Bogglecat Box,
because the elite had just shown me that running dry on Encore is what turns
the kit off.

## Fight 5 — Sewer Clam, 56 HP, Plating 8

Plating ("At the end of your turn, gain 8 Block") makes this a burst check —
chip damage is deleted every turn.

**First turn (logging ask 4):** stage held **Crabaletta** (FRONT); arrival
performance printed, 6 Hydro — and the Clam still showed 56/56 with Block 2,
because Plating's 8 Block ate the whole performance. The screen made that
readable *only* because it prints the Clam's Block; the Salon log itself says
"hit ... for 6 Hydro" whether or not any of the 6 arrived. **The Salon log
prints the number thrown, not the number landed.**

### Turn 1 (3 energy, Encore 2)

1. **Hearts Swelling** → Encore 9, 3 Block. 2. **Ethereal Spotlight** →
Encore 7.
3. **Duplicator potion**, then **Razor** on A.
   The bridge printed the interaction outright and it is the clearest line in
   the whole tool: "**Razor — Claw and Thunder** was played an extra time,
   **and the extra play performed as well.**" Three Crabaletta performances
   appear in the log for the turn (arrival + two from the doubled Razor).
   Clam 56 → **22**: 34 damage in one turn.
   *Rejected:* saving the Duplicator for a Companion with a bigger printed
   number. Against Plating, front-loading is the whole point — 8 Block a turn
   punishes exactly the slow game.

### Turn 2 (3 energy, Encore 0)

Clam on 14 behind 8 Block, and its intent was a Buff — so it would deal me no
damage. That inverts the usual calculus: Block was worth nothing, so every
energy went to damage.

1. **An Invitation** (0) → Barbara.
2. **Rain of Roses.** Encore 0 → 5.
   *Rejected:* a third damage card. This is the decision of the fight: with
   Encore at 0 my performances were dry at 4, and one energy spent on Encore
   turned each subsequent performance from 4 into 6 (and, once Charlotte
   landed, into 9). Paying a card to un-dry the members beat playing that card
   as damage.
3. **Charlotte — Snappy Silhouette** on A. Applied Vulnerable 2, drew 1, and
   performed: "**Crabaletta** hit Sewer Clam for **9** Hydro." Base 6 × 1.5.
   **Vulnerable does move a performance**, exactly as the Salon text says it
   would.
4. **Duplicator**, then **Barbara** — two more 9s — lethal through the Block.

**Fight judged as a whole:** two turns, 2 HP. Turn 2 was a real decision made
on the turn (buy Encore vs. buy damage), and it was legible because the "dry"
annotation told me precisely what running on empty was costing me.

## The kit, after 6 fights (5 completed)

### (a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off

- **Chevreuse-before-Aria, fight 3 turn 1 — on the turn.** Play a Companion
  card first to lift Fanfare over 3 and double Aria's Encore, at the cost of
  that Companion resolving un-spotlit. A clean 5-damage-for-5-Encore trade with
  a real wrong answer. This is the best thing in the kit.
- **Vulnerable-before-Evoke, fight 6 turn 3 — on the turn.** Ordering Charlotte
  ahead of the Deploy turned a 10 into a 15. Sequencing that pays 50%.
- **Buy Encore or buy damage, fight 5 turn 2 — on the turn.** With members dry
  at three-quarters, spending an energy on Rain of Roses raised every
  subsequent performance. The "dry" annotation is what made this decision
  visible; without it I would never have known the members were underperforming.
- **Kill-priority vs. measurement, fight 3 turn 2 — on the turn.** Gorou at the
  12-HP body that was about to hit me, or the 27-HP body. Ordinary but real.
- **Aria+ or The Gallery Stirs, fight 6 turn 1 — on the turn.** Armour now vs.
  cards forever, against a 140-HP elite.
- **Taking Razor, fight 1 reward — at the draft.** A second element is what
  makes the reaction layer exist at all; it shaped every later turn. Likewise
  buying Charlotte, which is what made both Evokes worth more than their face.
- **Disbelieving the Ethereal Spotlight tip, fight 1 turn 1 — on the turn.**
  A real decision, but a bad kind: the game asserted something false and the
  correct play was to catch it.

### (b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing

- **The opening is a script.** By fight 4 every fight began: Hearts Swelling →
  Ethereal Spotlight → Curtain Cue. Three cards, two-thirds of my energy, zero
  decisions, identical every time. The Spotlight in particular is not a choice
  — it is a toll you pay at the top of each fight for the relic to stop nagging
  you, and its "Ethereal" clause plus the relic re-adding it every turn means
  ignoring it isn't even an option you can settle.
- **Curtain Cue's "Otherwise" branch** (gain 1 Encore) is never the branch you
  want and never worth a card slot; the card is a Spotlight tax rebate.
- **Stage Presence** — 6 Block for 1 energy, no element, no performance, no
  Encore. I played it once in six fights and regretted the energy.
- **Soloist's Solicitation** — 6 damage, touches none of Encore, Fanfare, the
  Salon or the elements. It is the card I played when I had a spare energy and
  nothing to think about.
- **Regal Bearing** I never played at all, in six fights, including after it was
  upgraded.
- **Fanfare is very nearly inert.** Every screen prints "Cards read it and none
  spends it," which advertises a resource. In practice it peaked at 16, members
  gain "+1 per 10 Fanfare," and the only two things that ever read it were
  Aria's ≥3 threshold and Applause Line, which converted Fanfare 5 into
  **+1 damage**. I paid 50 gold for Applause Line specifically to find out what
  Fanfare does, and the answer was: almost nothing.

### (c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text

1. **The Ethereal Spotlight tip is simply false.** "You open a fight with 2
   Encore and this costs 2. Any performance spends one, so it is this turn's
   first action or not this fight." Aria of Recompense and Hearts Swelling both
   grant Encore without performing. I falsified it on the first turn of the run
   and then broke it deliberately in every subsequent fight. It is printed as a
   hard rule, in italics, twice per screen.
2. **"A full stage Evokes the front member" sits directly beside "A Companion
   card you play performs Crabaletta,"** and the natural reading of the pair is
   that a Companion card played into a full stage Evokes. It does not — only a
   **Deploy** onto a full stage Evokes. I planned and wasted a whole turn of an
   elite on that reading (fight 3 turn 3), and I only recovered because the
   *Deploy* keyword, on a different card, spells it out. The status line should
   say which action Evokes.
3. **Chevreuse's face understates its own damage, and its reaction preview
   quotes the wrong base.** Face: "Deal 10 damage." Delivered into a Hydro
   aura, spotlit: **15**. Separately, when the preview line does appear it
   reads "Into that Hydro aura this card's **7** lands **10**" — quoting the
   un-spotlit base 7, and landing on 10, which is *also* exactly the spotlit
   non-reacting number. Two unrelated 10s on one card, and neither is what
   happens. Contrast this with Weak, which the same card faces fold in
   perfectly — so the machinery to show the true number clearly exists.
4. **Gorou's "Gain Block equal to half the damage dealt" granted 0 Block,
   twice.** 12 damage dealt, Block 0 before and Block 0 after, on both plays.
   Caveat: both were killing blows, so this may be "does not fire on a kill"
   rather than "does not fire." It needs one non-lethal test.
5. **"acts at 3/4" / "Evokes at 3/4" is unreadable as written.** The Encore
   keyword says "a member spends 1 to perform **or acts at 3/4**," which parses
   as a fraction, a ratio, or a page reference. I did not understand it until
   the combat log spelled it out — "(dry: it could not pay its Encore, so it
   acted at three-quarters)". That log line is excellent; the keyword should
   say what the log says.
6. **"Focus" is never defined anywhere.** The Evoke line prints "its **Focus**
   counting 3 times"; the Evoke keyword calls the same thing "its **Fanfare
   bonus** counts 3 times." Two names, one of them undefined.
7. **Rounding is inconsistent within a single turn** — the same member, same
   base, same two multipliers, printed 6 and 7 (fight 6 turn 3).
8. **The relic reward screen prints names with no text.** After the Gardener
   elite I had to choose between "Duplicator" and "Captain's Wheel" with
   nothing else on the page; Captain's Wheel's actual text ("At the start of
   your 3rd turn, gain 18 Block") appeared only after I had taken it. That is
   an uninformed choice presented as a decision. Cards, potions and upgrade
   previews all print their text properly, so this is a gap, not a policy.
9. **The Salon log prints damage thrown, not damage landed.** Against Sewer
   Clam's Plating, "hit Sewer Clam for 6 Hydro" appeared while the Clam's HP
   did not move at all. I could only reconstruct what happened by watching the
   enemy's Block field.
10. **A fight that ends gives you nothing.** Twice (fights 2 and 5) the killing
    blow swapped straight to the reward page and took the combat log with it. I
    could not see what the last action delivered — which is how I mis-read
    fight 2 as containing an Evoke it did not contain.
11. **I never learned what the Arcane Scroll gave me.** No screen ever said
    which rare was added.
12. Minor: I opened the run at **62/78**, not full, with nothing on screen
    accounting for the missing HP.

### (d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw

- **Never wanted: Stage Presence** (and Soloist's Solicitation just behind it,
  with Regal Bearing never played at all). Six Block for an energy, touching
  none of Encore, Fanfare, the Salon or the elements. In a kit whose whole
  pleasure is that cards trigger members and members trigger reactions, a card
  that talks to nothing is dead weight.
- **Happiest to draw: Chevreuse — Interdiction Fire.** One energy that aims
  real damage, performs a member, carries the off-element that makes my own
  Hydro reactive, and sets up its own Vaporize. It is the only card that
  touched three of the kit's systems at once. Razor is a close second.

### (e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?

**Yes — and an unusually sharp one, for the wrong reason.** The screen told me,
in italics and twice, that Ethereal Spotlight was "this turn's first action or
not this fight." Aria of Recompense was in the same hand, gaining 5 Encore
without performing. The whole turn hinged on whether I believed the game about
its own rules, and the right answer was not to. I also had a genuine sequencing
choice underneath that (An Invitation before committing my last energy, aimed
damage vs. random-target performance), so the turn would have had a decision
even if the tip had been true. But the *biggest* decision on turn 1 of the run
was catching an error, and that is not a design win.
