## Fight 1 — Corpse Slug (1) 27/27, Corpse Slug (2) 25/25 (floor 2)

Both carried `Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it,
becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength`. That one line shaped the whole fight.

**Turn 1** (hand: Defend, Strike, Slack Water, Strike, Kurage's Oath; incoming
3x2 + 8 = 14). Played Slack Water **on the Bake-Kurage** (its Dusk Plan applies
1 Weak to ALL, and Dusk means "carried out at the END of this turn, before the
enemies act"), then Defend, then Kurage's Oath on the Bake-Kurage.
**Rejected:** playing Kurage's Oath and both Strikes face-up for 3+6+6 now. I
rejected it because the printed Plan lines are strictly larger (Oath 3 → 7 to
ALL, Strike 6 → 8) and because the Dusk line was the only way to shrink an
incoming 14 in the same turn I wrote it. Real decision, made on the turn.
Result: Block 7 (Defend printed "Gain 5 Block, plus 2 if the Bake-Kurage is
holding a Plan" and paid the 7 — I wrote the Plan first on purpose), took 3.

**Turn 2** (hand: Strike, Defend, Strike, Defend, Defend; slugs at 18 and 16).
Wrote both Strikes as Plans (8+8), played Defend. **Rejected:** playing both
Strikes face-up for 12. Rejected because 16 > 12 and I could not kill either
slug this turn regardless, so the delay cost nothing. Decision on the turn.

**Turn 3** (slug A at 2, slug B at 16, A now intending a debuff). Killed A with
a face-up Strike, then wrote the other two Strikes as Plans (16), ended.
**Rejected:** killing A with a Plan next turn instead. Rejected because
Ravenous *stuns* the survivor when it eats a corpse, so killing A **now** bought
a free enemy turn, and 8+8 = 16 was exactly B's remaining HP. This was the one
genuinely satisfying decision of the fight: the kill order was the play, and the
lethal two turns later was the plan paying off, not a dead turn.

Result printed on the reward screen: `Bake-Kurage: Strike, 8 … Corpse Slug lost
8 HP, and died`. Screen and outcome agreed exactly.

## Fight 2 — Sludge Spinner 38/38 (floor 5)

4 additional damage and applies Electro"), then Strike face-up for 10 (Electro
aura applied), then the other Strike as a Plan, then Defend for 7.
**Rejected:** planning both Strikes for 16. Rejected deliberately to set up a
reaction: the Kurage's carry-out is a **Hydro** hit, so a Hydro Plan landing on

element this screen can supply"* while **Kujou Sara, in my hand on that very
screen, applies Electro**. The next turn printed `Electro-Charged on Sludge

and then did it.

**Turn 2.** I was Weak 1; Strike printed **4** and Kurage's Oath **2**, while
the Plan lines still printed 8 and 7 — the Bake-Kurage panel had told me exactly
why ("Every planned HIT is the jellyfish's, so Shrink or Weak on you never cuts
it"). Wrote both Strikes as Plans, played Defend.
**Rejected:** any face-up damage. Under Weak that is a strictly dominated line
and the screen made that legible without arithmetic. Good turn: the debuff on me
changed which half of every card I was reading.

Turn 3 never happened — the 16 killed it at carry-out.

## Fight 3 — Seapunk 44/44 (floor 6)

**Turn 1.** Wrote Opening Gambit as a Plan, then Strike as a Plan, then Defend.
**Rejected:** Strike first, then Opening Gambit. Rejected because Opening
Gambit's Plan reads "Apply 1 Vulnerable to ALL enemies. **The next Plan carried
out with this one** deals double damage" — the order I write them in is the
order they resolve, so the rider only pays if the payload is written *after*.
That is the best decision the kit offers and it is made entirely at
plan-writing time.

Carry-out printed: `Opening Gambit, 1 — the 1 is Vulnerable. Inside the same
beat: Tamakushi Casket 3 … Strike, 24 — the 24 is damage; the clause asked for
8`. 8 → ×2 → ×1.5 = 24, and the screen showed its work. 27 damage off two 1-cost
cards.

**Turn 2** (Seapunk 17, Vulnerable 1 live). Played Slack Water **face-up** for 9
(4 ×1.5, plus the Casket's 2 ×1.5 for the Weak), then two Defends.
**Rejected:** writing Slack Water's Dusk Plan. Rejected because the Plan gloss
says "the enemy's Vulnerable counts **next turn**", and Vulnerable 1 would be
gone by then — the multiplier was only available face-up. A genuine turn-level
decision created by a printed timing rule.

Also on this turn: I tried `play "Defend (1)" on "Bake-Kurage"` and was refused —
*"'Defend (1)' cannot be planned on Bake-Kurage, so aiming it there would spend
the action and do nothing."* **This is a legibility defect.** Defend's face
prints the ***Plan*** keyword gloss underneath it, exactly as Strike and Slack
Water do, and its own text ("plus 2 if the Bake-Kurage is holding a Plan")
mentions Plans; nothing on the card says it has no Plan line. The refusal was
correct and well-worded, but I only learned the rule by being refused.

**Turn 3** (Seapunk 8, intending Buff + Defend). Sara + Strike for 10, killed it.
**Rejected:** planning the kill. Rejected on the printed rule "A Plan is carried
out before you play anything, so it lands in whatever Block the enemy is still
standing in from its own turn — you cannot strip that Block first." Killing it
before it Blocked was the whole point. Another decision the screen handed me.

## Fight 5 — Skulking Colony 75/75, ELITE (floor 9)

`Hardened Shell 20 of 20 left this turn — Skulking Colony cannot lose more than
20 HP each turn.` This is the best enemy in the run for testing this kit,
because it **directly punishes the plan-stacking combo** the rest of the deck is
built around: a 51-damage carry-out would have been shaved to 20. The counter
even printed its remaining allowance every turn ("4 of 20 left this turn"),
which let me price my turn instead of guessing.

**Turn 1.** Sara → Slack Water face-up (8 damage, Electro applied by Sara, then
the Weak triggered the Casket's Hydro 2 into the Electro aura → `Electro-Charged`
→ Poison 4), then two Defends for 10 vs a Weakened 14. Took 0.
**Rejected:** the Opening Gambit / Riptide burst. Rejected on the shell cap.
Turn-level decision, forced by a printed enemy rule — the best kind.
Then used **Powdered Demise** (9 HP at the end of each of its turns) because
against a per-turn cap, free chip damage costs no cards.

**Turn 2.** Two Strike Plans (16) + Defend. **Rejected:** three Strike Plans
(24) with no block — the shell would have eaten 4 of the 24 anyway.

**Turn 3.** The shell read `4 of 20 left this turn`, so I stopped attacking,
wrote Riptide+ as a Plan (17) and blocked. **Rejected:** dumping Kurage's Oath+
into a 4-point ceiling. This is the one place where the kit's numbers had to be
read against an enemy's numbers rather than just maximised, and I enjoyed it.
The plan killed it at the next carry-out.

One thing I could not reconcile: on turn 1 I did 10 damage and the shell dropped
to `10 of 20 left`, yet across the enemy's own turn it lost 15 (Poison 4 +
Demise 9 + a Casket 2). Either the cap resets between phases or it does not apply
to end-of-turn ticks; **no printed line says which**, and the shell's own text
("each turn") does not disambiguate.

## Fight 7 — Punch Construct 55/55 (floor 14)

Started on 5 energy (Venerable Tea Set, from the rest site). `Artifact 1 —
Negates 1 debuff`, intending to Block.

**Turn 1.** Wrote **Second Wave → Opening Gambit → Riptide+** in that order, then
Defend. **Rejected:** OG → Second Wave → Riptide, and the pure face-up line (23
damage while it still had no Block). I chose the order I did because I read
"next Plan carried out with this one" as strictly the following entry, so a
doubler pointed at a doubler needed the *no-damage* rider first.

The carry-out proved the reading and taught me something:

```
Bake-Kurage: Second Wave — no enemy lost HP
Bake-Kurage: Opening Gambit, 1 — the 1 is Vulnerable.        (Artifact ate it)
Bake-Kurage: Opening Gambit, 1 — the 1 is Vulnerable. Inside the same beat:
             Tamakushi Casket 3 on Punch Construct.          (this one stuck)
Bake-Kurage: Riptide+, 51 — the 51 is damage; the clause asked for 17.
```

Second Wave doubled **Opening Gambit**, not Riptide — so the "twice" and the
"double" landed on different cards, and that is the sharpest trap in the kit: two
riders that both say "the next Plan" chain in a way that is only legible after
you have watched it once. It happened to be right (the doubled OG punched
through the Artifact), but I got there by luck, not by reading.

**Turns 2–3** were mop-up: Scout Ahead drew a blank, Slack Water plus Coral
Bulwark+ left it on 2, one Strike finished it. Turn 3 presented no decision worth
the name — 2 HP and five playable cards — but it was the tail of a plan written
two turns earlier, so I count the fight as having had one big decision (the
write order) rather than none.

## Fight 9 — Waterfall Giant 240/240, ACT 1 BOSS (floor 17)

Opened at 80/80 on 5 energy.

**Turn 1 — the worst opening hand of the run, and the potion was the decision.**
Hand: Defend, Defend, Defend+, Kujou Sara, Slack Water, against a boss that was
only buffing. Three Defends are dead cards against a turn with no incoming
damage. I played Sara + Slack Water for 8 and Electro, then spent **Snecko Oil**
(draw 7, randomise costs). **Rejected:** ending the turn with three energy
unspent. The hand itself offered no decision; the potion manufactured one.

Snecko then produced a bug worth reporting. The re-priced cards printed
explanations, and two of them are **false**:

> **Riptide+** — cost 1 … *The cost printed on this card is 2; it is showing 1
> here, **because this copy is upgraded — that is permanent.***

Riptide+ costs **2**; I had been playing it at 2 for six floors. The screen
attributed a Snecko Oil discount to the upgrade and called it permanent. The
same false line appeared on Coral Bulwark+ and Kurage's Oath+, while the
*un*upgraded Strike and Read the Field got the correct explanation ("the cut is
this turn's board and not the card"). So the page decides *why* a cost moved by
checking whether the card is upgraded, which is wrong whenever both are true.

**Turn 1, continued — the payoff turn.** With four energy and cheap cards I wrote
six Plans in order: **Opening Gambit → Riptide+ → Kurage's Oath+ → Strike →
Coral Bulwark+ → Read the Field.** The carry-out:

```
Opening Gambit, 1 — the 1 is Vulnerable. Inside the same beat: Casket 3
Riptide+, 51 — the 51 is damage; the clause asked for 17.
Kurage's Oath+, 15 — the clause asked for 10.
Strike, 12 — the clause asked for 8.
Coral Bulwark+, 11 — the 11 is Block. Inside the same beat: Casket 3
Read the Field, 10 — the 10 is Block.
```

98 damage and 21 Block out of one turn's writing. **Rejected:** putting Opening
Gambit later in the queue, and spending the last energy on a face-up Defend.
This is the fight where the kit's central idea actually sang: the whole turn was
a *sentence* I composed, and reading it back on the next screen was the most fun
I had. It is also where the honest worry lives — the fun was in the writing, and
the turn it resolved I mostly watched.

**Turns 2–6.** Treatise + Second Wave → Coral Bulwark (doubled block). Then a
Weak-shrunken hand where the Plan lines (8) beat the face-up lines (4) again.
Then, with `Vulnerable 1` live, I switched to **face-up** Riptide+ (12 + 6 for
having debuffs, ×1.5 = 27) plus a Strike, because Vulnerable would not survive to
the next carry-out — the same timing decision as fight 3, and it never got old.
Then Exposed Flank → Second Wave → Kurage's Oath+ for two 15s.
`Steam Eruption 27 … 30 — When killed, deals N damage at the end of your next
turn` grew +3 every turn, which is a clean, legible reason to hurry.

**Phase change.** At the kill the boss printed `changing phase (its HP is not a
number this turn)`, then `Death Blow — It will attack you for 30 damage before
being destroyed`. I answered with Slack Water face-up **first** (Weak → 30 ×0.75
= 22) and then Coral Bulwark+ and Coral Bulwark for 15 Block. **Rejected:**
three block cards. Rejected because a Weak applied before blocking removes more
damage than a third Coral Bulwark adds — the fight's last decision, and a good
one. Killed it on 52/80.

## The kit, after 10 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

1. **Face-up now vs. write it as a Plan** — on the turn, every turn, in every
   fight. The trade is a whole turn of delay against a bigger number (Strike 6/8,
   Oath 3/10, Riptide 12/17), and it is *not* a fixed answer: it flipped toward
   face-up whenever a Vulnerable was expiring (fights 3, 4, 9), whenever a kill
   was available before the enemy Blocked (fight 3) or before it escaped, and
   whenever an enemy's Strength was compounding (fight 8); it flipped hard toward
   Plans whenever I was Weak, because the panel told me plainly that Weak on me
   never touches the jellyfish's hits. This is the kit's spine and it works.
2. **The order I write Plans in** — at plan-writing time, which is a decision
   point most deckbuilders do not have. Opening Gambit, Second Wave and Nereid's
   Ascension all say "the next Plan", so the queue is a small program. Getting
   `Second Wave → Opening Gambit → Riptide+` right in fight 7 and the six-card
   sentence in the boss fight were the two most satisfying moments of the run.
3. **Kill order under a printed consequence** — Ravenous (fight 1), Heist vs
   Escape (fight 4), Ritual (fight 8). These are the enemies' doing, not the
   kit's, but the Plan delay makes them sharper, because committing to a target
   a turn early is a real bet.
4. **Spending the turn against a ceiling** — the elite's Hardened Shell was the
   single best decision-generator in the run, precisely because it *turned off*
   the combo and made me price a turn instead of maximising it.
5. **At the draft:** taking Kujou Sara (the only Electro in a mono-Hydro deck,
   which is what made reactions reachable at all), and taking Opening Gambit over
   the raw Ambush. Both changed how turns played, not just how big they were.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Defend is close to automatic.** Eighteen of them; the only decision it ever
  posed was whether I had a Plan written before I played it, and since I write a
  Plan almost every turn, the answer was yes 12 times out of 18 without my
  thinking about it. It is a rider that almost never fails.
- **AoE vs. multiple bodies** was automatic: with three rats, the ALL lines and
  the Casket's per-debuff hit are so far ahead that there is nothing to weigh.
- **Change of Plans** I bought for 50 gold and **never played once**. Every time
  I drew it, it read `CANNOT BE PLAYED: no Plan is written` — because I draw it
  at the start of a turn, before I have written anything, and by the time I have
  written a Plan I would rather it carry out with its friends next turn than
  alone now. It may be a fine card in a hand I never had; on the evidence of this
  run it is a dead card.
- **Second Wave / Scout Ahead face-up** (4 Block, draw 1) were never worth a
  card; they exist only as Plan lines, and that is fine, but it means a turn
  where I cannot afford to write is a turn where they are blanks.
- **Clumsy** is a non-event: Ethereal, so it exhausts itself. I never regretted
  taking the relic for it.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **"NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE: Hydro is the only element this screen can
   supply"** printed on a screen where **Kujou Sara was in my hand applying
   Electro**. It later printed the full reaction rules and `Electro-Charged`
   fired. The claim is false whenever a companion card is holdable.
2. **Snecko Oil's cost explanation is wrong for upgraded cards** — quoted in
   fight 9. "Riptide+ … showing 1 here, because this copy is upgraded — that is
   permanent" is simply untrue; Riptide+ costs 2. The page appears to test
   `upgraded?` rather than comparing against the card's actual current cost.
3. **Defend prints the Plan keyword and cannot be planned.** The refusal text was
   excellent; the card face is misleading.
4. **Hardened Shell's "each turn"** — I could not work out whether the cap
   resets between my turn and the enemy's; the numbers suggested it does, nothing
   printed says so.
5. **The first card chooser closed on `choose` while telling me it would not.**
   The Brain Leech screen printed *"Choosing here arms a pick; it does not close
   the screen. Say `confirm` after `choose` to take it"* and *"Confirm is not
   available."* — two contradictory lines at once. My `choose` took the card
   outright and the following `confirm` was refused as "nothing waiting to be
   confirmed". The rest-site Smith chooser behaved the *other* way (choose
   toggled, confirm was needed). Same verb, two behaviours.
6. **`WhatMod: KleeMod`** printed inside the Tamakushi Casket's text on the
   Orobas event screen — a debug string leaking into player-facing text, and it
