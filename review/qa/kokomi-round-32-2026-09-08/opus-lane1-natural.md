## Fight 1 — Leaf Slime (S) 11, Leaf Slime (M) 34, Twig Slime (S) 7

**Opening hand:** Defend, Defend, Strike, Defend, Strike. Held a Plan line
(every one of those five prints one).

**Turn 1.** Wrote both Strikes as Plans on the Bake-Kurage, then played Defend.
*Rejected:* playing the Strikes face-up for 6+6 now. The board's whole threat
was Twig Slime's 4; the two Leaf Slimes only intended Status cards, so the turn
was cheap and buying `Plan: Deal 8` twice (16) instead of 12 now was free value.
The order was on purpose: with the Plans written, the Defend in hand **reprinted
itself** — before, `Gain 5 Block, plus 2 if the Bake-Kurage is holding a Plan`;
after, `Gain 7 Block, plus 2 if the Bake-Kurage is holding a Plan`, and I gained
7. *(That re-print is a defect: the folded number and the unfolded conditional
clause are both on the card at once, so the card reads as if it will pay 9.)*

**Turn 2.** The two carry-outs both landed on Leaf Slime (S) — 8 killed it down
to 3, the second 8 killed it with 5 wasted. **They did not retarget.** That is
the fight's real lesson and it cost me: with the front body at 11 and each Plan
at 8, the second Plan was dead the moment I wrote it, and nothing on the Plan
screen warns you. Then: Strike face-up on Twig Slime (7→1), Slack Water written
as a **Dusk** Plan, Kurage's Oath written as a Plan. *Rejected:* Defend. The
Dusk line was the point — it resolves "at the end of this turn, before enemies
act", and the Casket's answering 2 Hydro for the Weak finished the Twig Slime
before it swung. It did: Twig died, and the surviving Leaf Slime's 8 came in
weakened at 6.

**Turn 3** (enemy on Status intent, no damage). Slimed (draw 1, Exhaust), then
Plan Strike, Plan Kurage's Oath. *Rejected:* Defend against a turn with no
attack in it — the choice here was whether to spend the free energy on a draw or
on a third Plan, and the Slimed was strictly better than a Block I'd never use.

**Turn 4.** Enemy at 10. Wrote Strike as a Plan (8 next turn, which kills it
through the 8 it would otherwise take two face-ups to reach), Defend for 7,
Slimed for the draw. *Rejected:* Strike face-up for 6 plus a Defend — that
leaves it at 4 and me one turn further behind. Took 1 damage.

**Turn 5.** Enemy at 2 after the carry-out. Played Strike; it died. **No
decision on this turn** — but it is the two-turn plan paying off, not a dead
turn.

**Reward:** War Council (`Plan: Deal 5 damage and apply 1 Weak to ALL`), taken
over Coral Bulwark, Moon's Reflection and Bennett. With Tamakushi Casket, the
Weak on a Plan is itself worth 2 Hydro to every body hit, so the card's real
planned line is 7-to-all for 1.

## Fight 2 — Fuzzy Wurm Crawler 55

**Opening hand:** Kurage's Oath, Strike, Strike, War Council, Defend. Held a
Plan line.

**Turn 1.** Wrote all three — Strike, Strike, War Council — as Plans and ended
the turn on 0 Block against a 4. *Rejected:* Defend for 7. The fight's shape was

**Turn 2.** Enemy on Empower, so free. Plan Strike, Plan Strike, and Slack Water
face-up. *Rejected:* writing Slack Water as a Dusk Plan again — with no attack

had misread them:** Slack Water played face-up *did* refresh the Hydro aura to 2,
even though Strike's own rider says "Its own hit applies no aura; the
Bake-Kurage carries out the Plan as a Hydro hit, which does." Two Hydro cards,
two different rules about their own face-up hit, and only one of them says so.

**Turn 3.** Enemy at 10 with Strength 7 and a 4x3 coming. Two Strikes killed it.
The only decision was made two turns earlier.

**Reward:** **Shinobu — Thundergrust [Electro]**, taken over Treatise, Cleansing
Wave and Pincer. Every combat screen so far had printed "NO REACTION IS
REACHABLE HERE: Hydro is the only element this screen can supply" — this was the
first card offered that could change that sentence, and I wanted to see what the
kit did with a second element.

## Fight 4 (elite) — Bygone Effigy 127

**Opening hand:** Defend, Slack Water, Defend, Kurage's Oath, Strike. Held a
Plan line.

This body carries `Slow`: *"Whenever you play a card, this enemy receives 10%
more damage from Attacks this turn. It counts the cards played BEFORE this
one."* That single line made this the most interesting fight of the run, because
it is the first thing I met that **punishes the Plan queue**: a carry-out
happens at the start of the turn, before any card is played, so it always lands
at Slow 0. Against this one body, face-up beats planned.

**Turn 1** (asleep). Kurage's Oath, then Slack Water, then Strike — cheapest and
non-Attack first, biggest Attack last, to sell the Slow ramp. *Rejected:* the
usual two-Plans-and-a-Defend opening, precisely because of the Slow reading. 16
damage, and the badge read `Slow 30` at the end of the turn.

**Turn 2** (Empower). War Council, Read the Field (took Nereid's Ascension off
the top three), then Shinobu last at Slow 20. **First reaction of the run:**
`Electro-Charged on Bygone Effigy, off Shinobu — Thundergrust`, and it applied
`Poison 4`. Note what happened next and is not written anywhere: the body still
wore `Hydro Aura 2` afterwards, because applying Poison is applying a debuff,
and the Casket answers a debuff with a Hydro hit — so the reaction **re-seeded
its own aura**. That loop is the strongest thing in the kit and no screen says
it exists.

**Turn 3.** 23 incoming, me at 49. Plan Strike, Plan Strike, Defend for 7.
*Rejected:* Treatise (engine) and face-up Strikes (Slow). I chose 16 banked
damage and 7 Block over 14 face-up damage and 5, knowing the Slow argument cut
the other way — with a 23 on the board the Block mattered more than the 20%.

**Turn 4.** War Council, Slack Water, Strike face-up: Weak cut the 23 to 17, and
the Casket paid 2 per debuff. HP 33→16 anyway. By this point the arithmetic said
I lose: 72 HP left on it, ~15 a turn from me, 17+ a turn from it.

**Turn 5.** Drew Shinobu, Nereid's Ascension and three Defends. Used **Lucky
Tonic** (Buffer) so the 17 would be eaten whole, then played Nereid's (2) and
Shinobu (1) instead of 15 Block. *Rejected:* three Defends for 15 Block — that
survives the turn and loses the fight. This was the real decision of the run,
and it was a decision about which loss to take.

**Turn 6.** The board came back at **23/127**: Electro-Charged had stacked
`Poison 8`, Nereid's had doubled nothing yet, and Buffer had held me at 16.
Strike, Strike, Shinobu last (Slow 20, and Shinobu prints +5 below half HP) —
28 into a 23. Dead.

**Reward:** Undertow (`Deal 7; if the enemy has a debuff, deal 10`), taken over
Feint, Moon's Reflection and Yae Miko. With the Casket spraying debuffs, its
condition is always on; I passed on Yae's Electro engine because at 16 HP I
needed a card that pays the turn it is played.

## The kit, after 10 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

- **On the turn, constantly: write it or play it.** Every Plan line is a trade
  of *tempo for rate* — Strike is 6 now or 8 next turn; Kurage's Oath+ is 3-to-all
  now or 10-to-all next turn. Because the Plan is *paid now*, the queue is really
  a way to bank energy on a cheap turn and spend it on an expensive one, and I
  used it that way in fight 2 (three Plans on a free turn, 23 damage the next).
  The trade has a real cost and I paid it twice: a Plan cannot answer a threat
  that exists *this* turn, which is why War Council went face-up against
  Byrdonis's 19.
- **On the turn: the order of the writes.** This is the best decision in the kit
  and it only exists once you own Nereid's Ascension and Opening Gambit. Nereid's
  doubles *the first Plan written*; Opening Gambit's plan doubles *the next Plan
  behind it*. Writing Opening Gambit first and Strike second was worth 36 damage
  in a turn on the boss, and writing them the other way round would have been
  worth about 14. Nothing prompts you; the reward is entirely for reading two
  cards against each other.
- **On the turn: Plan-then-Defend, or Defend-then-Plan.** Small, constant, and
  genuinely a decision the first three times you meet it (see the Debrief).
  After that it is a habit.
- **Earlier in the fight: which body the aura sits on when the Electro lands.**
  Slack Water face-up refreshes Hydro, Shinobu consumes it for Electro-Charged,
  and the Casket answers the resulting Poison with another Hydro hit that
  *re-seeds the aura*. Sequencing those three inside one turn was worth ~33
  damage in the Mawler fight and it is the single highest-leverage thing I did.
- **At the draft: elements.** Taking Shinobu over Treatise in fight 2 changed
  what every later turn could do, because the deck otherwise cannot make the
  screen's own "NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE" line go away.
- **Made by the enemy, not by me, and the better for it:** `Slow` (rewards
  face-up, punishes carry-outs), `Hard To Kill 9` (makes the small Plan the
  efficient card and Sango Isshin junk), `Flutter` (makes the *skill* Plan
  better than the attack), `Imbalanced` (makes exact Block better than damage),
  `Ringing 1` (one card). Five different printed enemy rules each inverted a
  different one of my defaults. That is where most of this run's thinking went.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **The basic Defend is the most automatic card in the deck**, and the +2 clause
  makes it *more* automatic rather than less: since I write a Plan on nearly
  every turn anyway, the conditional is on by default, so the card is just "gain
  7" with a sentence attached. It never once changed what I did — it changed
  only the order in which I did it, and only for the first few turns.
- **Strike into the Bake-Kurage is the default line**, to the point that "plan a
  Strike" was my answer on maybe half the turns of the run before I looked at
  anything else.
- **Toric Toughness (2 energy, 5 Block now + 5 on each of the next 2 turns) I
  never played once** after transforming a Defend into it. Two energy on a
  three-turn drip is the wrong shape for this kit, which wants both energy and
  the Plan written on the same turn.
- **Sango Isshin never got played** — the two boards I drew it on were the
  9-damage-capped Exoskeletons and a fight where I could not spare 2 energy.
  A quarter of max HP to ALL sounds enormous and never once was.
- **Read the Field** earned its slot twice (both times digging Nereid's) and was
  a blank the rest of the time.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **Defend's number is folded and its clause is not.** With a Plan held the
   card reads `Gain 7 Block, plus 2 if the Bake-Kurage is holding a Plan` and
   pays 7. The clause has already been applied to the number printed beside it.
   Read literally, the card promises 9.
2. **Vulnerable folding is inconsistent between cards.** On the boss with
   `Vulnerable 1` up, Undertow printed 10/15 and Shinobu printed 12 (both ×1.5,
   folded). One round later with `Vulnerable 2` up, **Strike still printed
   "Deal 6 damage"**, unfolded. Two cards on the same screen against the same
   body, two different conventions.
3. **Strike's own face-up hit leaves no aura; Slack Water's does** — and only
   Strike's rider says anything about it. Both carry the `[Hydro]` tag and both
   carry the identical `*Applies Hydro*` glossary block. I played the whole of
   fight 3 on the assumption that no face-up Hydro card seeds an aura.
4. **Plans do not retarget.** Two Strike Plans written at an 11-HP front body
   both hit that body; the second wasted 5 damage into a corpse rather than
   moving to the next enemy. The Plan rules block explains at length which body a
   Plan takes (front non-Minion, or ALL) but never says *when* that is decided.
5. **The Casket / Electro-Charged loop is invisible.** Applying a debuff makes
   the Casket deal 2 Hydro, which re-applies the Hydro aura, which the next
   Electro hit consumes for another reaction. The reward screen even printed
   `Electro-Charged` **twice off one Shinobu**. Nothing anywhere says this
   is a loop; I found it by reading a status list.
6. **The Pael room printed no options at all** — a header, the "this room prints

11. Without: 1** (F6 Byrdonis T3, where the turn's whole energy went face-up
into Weak + Undertow and nothing was written).

On purpose, honestly: **yes on all eleven, but it stopped being a decision after
about the third.** The first time, on fight 1 turn 1, I wrote the Plans first
specifically to watch the card re-print itself from 5 to 7, and that was a real
choice with a real unknown. From roughly fight 3 onward "Plans first, Defend
last" was simply the correct order in every case, because the Plan is paid
whether or not the Defend follows and there is no board state in which writing
second is better. The clause therefore never traded anything off: it never once
made me *not* write a Plan, and it never made me play a Defend I would otherwise
have skipped. What it does do is punish a player who plays them in the intuitive
order (block the incoming hit, then plan), silently, by 2 — and because the
printed number folds the bonus in but the clause stays on the card, the screen
gives you no way to notice you were punished.

**10 of 10 opening hands held a Plan line.** With a natural deck of 4 Strikes,
4 Defends, Kurage's Oath and Slack Water — every one of which prints a Plan — a

opening hand I drew (fight 9) still held three. So the "do I open the queue?"
question is never *gated* by the draw; it is only ever a question of which card
goes in it. That is good for consistency and it is the reason the mechanic feels
like a default rather than an opportunity: it is never absent, so it is never
noticed.

thing that is already dead". Against that, the turns I would name as the

attack) and Bowlbug T2 (block *exactly* to Stun). The kit's decisions are real,
and almost all of the best ones were created by an enemy's printed rule rather
than by the Plan queue itself.
