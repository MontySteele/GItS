## Fight 1 — Corpse Slug (1) [A] 27 HP, Corpse Slug (2) [B] 26 HP

Both wore `Ravenous 4` — "When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it,
becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength."

**Turn 1** — hand was Strike, Strike, Defend, Defend, Defend, and *nothing else*.
Played Strike, Strike into B, then one Defend. **Rejected:** three Defends (15
Block, zero progress) and two Defends plus a Strike. I picked the middle because
the incoming was 14 and I wanted B, the 8-damage one, dead first.
**This turn presented no Klee decision at all** — the choice was "how many of my
five vanilla cards are attacks", which is a decision the base game makes, not
this kit. Ended 53/62. *Sparks held: 1. Nothing went off.*

**Turn 2** — this is where the kit arrived: Jumpy Dumpty ("Place a Bomb 8. When
it goes off, place a Mine 3 on ALL enemies") and Ka-pow! ("Retain. Set off. Deal
4 damage"). Played **Jumpy Dumpty on B → Ka-pow! on B → Strike on B**. The bomb
went off for 8, Ka-pow! added 4, the Strike finished, and B's death stunned A
through its own Ravenous, which cancelled A's 8-damage attack.
**Rejected:** planting the bomb on A and letting it grow to 12 for next turn —
better damage per energy, but it left B alive to debuff me and A swinging. I
also rejected killing B with the bomb *after* two Strikes, which would have
thrown away four points of Ka-pow! and six of bomb.
This was a genuine three-way plan, and the payoff — the enemy's own eat-and-stun
rule turned into a skipped turn — is the best thing that happened in the run.
*Sparks held: 2 (+1, Pounding Surprise, when the bomb went off). Bomb went off:
yes; Mine 3 landed on both bodies.*

**Turn 3** — A now had `Mine 14` on it ("Bombs here: 7 / 7, including 2 Mines")
and intended 12. Played **three Defends, 15 Block**. **Rejected:** two Strikes
plus a Defend, which after the mines would have left A on exactly 1 HP and cost
me 7. I could read from the Mine's own text — "A Mine also goes off before this
enemy's hit" — that 14 damage was going to land for free, so the Strikes were
worth nothing and the Block was worth everything. Took 0. *Sparks: 2 at my turn's
end, 4 after the enemy's turn (both Mines went off).*

**Turn 4** — A at 13. Strike, Strike, Ka-pow! for exactly lethal.
**Rejected:** Jumpy Dumpty into Ka-pow! for 18. Both killed; I took the cheaper
one. This turn had no decision, but it is the *cash-out* of turn 3's decision,
not a dead turn.
*Sparks: 4, entirely unspent when the fight ended and thrown away.*

**Where screen and outcome disagreed:** nowhere. Every number I predicted off the
printed text — 8 from the bomb, 14 from the two mines, the stun off Ravenous —
landed to the point.

## Fight 2 — Sludge Spinner [A] 38 HP

**Turn 1** — hand: Defend, Defend, Strike, Razor — Claw and Thunder+, Ka-pow!.
Played **Strike → Ka-pow! → Razor**, in that order, then both Defends.
Two separate riders paid for the ordering: Razor's "If this is the **third**
Attack you played this turn, gain 1 Energy" (Energy read 2/3 after three plays
that should have cost 3), and Ka-pow!'s Pyro sitting on the body so that Razor's
Electro triggered **Overloaded** — "6 damage to ALL enemies and 1 Weak on the
reacted enemy". 38 → 11 in one turn, with the intent falling 8 → 6.
**Rejected:** Razor first for tempo, which would have cost me both the energy
refund and the reaction; and holding Ka-pow! on its Retain, which would have cost
me the third attack.
This was the best turn of the run and **the decision was made entirely in the
ordering** — same five cards, three quite different outcomes.
*Sparks: 1. Nothing went off (no bomb existed).*

**Turn 2** — I was Weak 1, and the screen re-printed my Strikes as "Deal 4
damage" rather than 6, which I want to note approvingly: the card face, not a
buff icon, told me my numbers had moved. Spiral Strike (4, Replay 1 → 8) plus a
Strike (4) for 12 into an 11-HP body. **Rejected:** nothing real — this was a
cleanup turn. *Sparks: 1, unspent.*

## Fight 3 (elite) — Skulking Colony [A] 75 HP

`Hardened Shell 20 of 20 left this turn — Skulking Colony cannot lose more than
20 HP each turn.` This is the fight that taught me the most about the kit,
because a per-turn damage cap is exactly the thing a growing bomb is bad at.

**Turn 1** — Jumpy Dumpty (Bomb 8), Pocket Fireworks (9), Strike (6). 15 damage,
under the cap, and a bomb banking 4 a turn for free while the cap holds my card
damage down. **Rejected:** Pocket Fireworks + Strike + Defend (same damage, 5
Block, no bomb). I paid 9 HP for the bomb. Took 14 → 45/68.
*Sparks: 1. Nothing went off.*

**Turn 2** — the bomb read `Bomb 12`. Played **Ka-pow! (0 Energy) → Razor**.
Ka-pow! set the bomb off for 12 and hit for 4, i.e. **16 damage for zero
Energy**, and Jumpy Dumpty's rider put a `Mine 3` on the body. Razor's 11 then
had only 4 of the shell left to spend — the screen said so directly, "Hardened
Shell **4** of 20 left this turn" — but I played it anyway *for the Overloaded
Weak*, which cut the incoming 14 to 10. Then Defend+.
**Rejected:** holding Ka-pow! (it Retains) to detonate a 16-point bomb next turn.
Against a 20-per-turn cap a bigger bomb is worth nothing, so the growth I paid 9
HP for on turn 1 was in fact *worth less* the longer I held it. Rejected also
Strike-for-4 instead of Razor-for-4: same damage, no Weak.
*Sparks: 2 at my turn's end; 3 after the Mine went off before its hit.*

**Turn 3** — 37 HP left, intent 9 + a buff. Dig In+ (11 Block, **1 Spark, no
Energy**), Spiral Strike (12), Strike (6), Defend. **Rejected:** skipping the
Defend to bank an action — I was at 40 HP with a boss on the map. Took 0.
Dig In is the card that made the Spark counter stop being decoration.
*Sparks: 3 → 2 (Dig In). Nothing went off.*

**Turn 4** — 19 HP left, cap 20, so it was killable in one. **Bang Bang!+ (2
Sparks, no Energy, 11) + Pocket Fireworks (9) = 20.** Dead, at the cost of one
Energy for the whole lethal turn.
**Rejected:** Strike + Fireworks (15, one short of lethal, and it hands the
Colony a 9×2 turn). The decision here was made two turns earlier — banking
Sparks instead of spending both of them on Dig In — and it paid exactly.
*Sparks: 2 → 0.*

**Rejected:** blocking the 22 with Defend+ and a Spark I did not have; and

**Rejected:** Razor first (loses the third-attack refund *and* wastes the Pyro

*Sparks: 1, unspent — Bang Bang!+ never came up. Bomb 4 planted on turn 5 was

Zero damage taken in this fight, from a 140-HP elite hitting for 33.

**Where a screen and the outcome disagreed:** once, mildly. `Vulnerable 99` on me

## The kit, after 5 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and where they were made.**

- **Ordering inside one turn** — this is the kit's best decision and it recurs.
  Ka-pow!-before-Razor to spend a Pyro aura on an Overloaded (fight 2 T1, fight 5
  T2), and third-attack-last for Razor's Energy refund (fight 2 T1, fight 5 T5).
  Same five cards, ±10 damage and a Weak depending on sequence. *Made on the
  turn.*
- **Detonate now or let it grow** — Jumpy Dumpty banks 4 a turn, and the answer
  genuinely changes: against the Corpse Slugs the right answer was "now, to buy a
  stun"; against the capped Colony it was "now, because a bigger bomb is worth
  nothing through a 20-per-turn shell"; against the Eel it was "hold, the mine
  will find its own moment". *Made on the turn, but shaped by the enemy's printed
  rule.*
- **Sparks are a second, tighter budget** — Bang Bang!+ (2) versus Dig In+ (1)
  versus Dig In (1), from an income of one at combat start plus one per
  detonation. Fight 3 turn 3 was exactly this: spend a Spark on 11 Block now, or
  hold two for a zero-Energy 11-damage lethal next turn. I held. *Made a turn
  early, and it is the most interesting recurring tension in the kit.*
- **The draft choice that shaped a fight** — taking Dig In+ over Pocket
  Fireworks+ is why the Thorns kill in fight 4 and the whole Eel fight cost zero
  HP. *Made at the draft.*

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Strike and Defend turns.** Fight 1 turn 1 and fight 4 turn 1 were pure
  arithmetic: pick the subset of five vanilla cards that adds to the enemy's HP.
- **Ka-pow! with no bomb on the board** is a 4-damage attack that Retains, i.e.
  a worse Strike. It happened twice (fight 4 T2, fight 5 T2). The card is
  brilliant when a bomb exists and filler when one does not, and with one bomb
  *placer* in the deck for most of the act, "does not" was common.
- **Bang Bang!+ was unplayable more often than playable.** It printed `CANNOT BE
  PLAYED: you have no Spark, and this costs 2` in two of the three fights it
  appeared in. It cost me nothing to hold, but a 2-Spark card in a deck whose
  Spark income is one plus one-per-detonation is a card you draft and then wait
  for.
- **Jumpy Dumpty against a damage cap** was close to a trap. I paid 9 HP on
  fight 3 turn 1 for growth that the Hardened Shell then made worthless.
- **Freminet — Shattering Pressure** ("Your Shatters deal 4 additional damage")
  was offered to me and I could not have played it: nothing in the run ever
  printed a Shatter, and the word is not in the glossary on that screen.

**(c) What I could not understand, or what contradicted its own text.**

- **`Hexerei` — "A Companion card that prints the word, and Klee herself. Some
  are Klee's own, some are not. Cards of hers pay when you play one."** I read
  this a dozen times across five fights and I still do not know what it *does*.
  "Cards of hers pay" — pay what, to whom, and when? I played Razor four times
  and never saw anything I could attribute to Hexerei. Later a *different*
  screen printed the missing half — "Sparks from your Companion: Playing one of
  Klee's own Companions makes 1 Spark, 1 more if it triggered an Elemental
  Reaction and 1 more if it is upgraded" — which is a clear rule that Hexerei's
  own glossary entry does not contain, and which still does not tell me whether
  Razor is "one of Klee's own".
- **"in 1 hit for as many Sparks"**, on every Bomb and Mine block. I never
  worked out what that clause means. "Set off here deals 14 Pyro damage, **in 2
  hits for as many Sparks**" — as many Sparks as what? Nothing I did ever cost
  me Sparks for a detonation, and nothing gave me two.
- **Bomb growth versus the Mine.** The Bomb entry says "goes off **only** when
  Set off"; the Mine entry says a Mine "also goes off before its enemy's hit".
  Both are on screen at once and the second silently contradicts the first
  unless you notice "Mine" is a subtype. It cost me one wrong plan before I read
  it properly.
- **Silver Crucible's counter ran ahead of me.** It read (3), then (2) at the
  first reward screen, (1) at the second, and blank at the third — but I only
  ever saw upgraded rewards on the first *three* card packs, which is what it
  promised. The counter appears to tick when the pack is generated rather than
  when I open it, which made me think I had lost a charge to nothing.
- **`Vulnerable 99` on me, "for 99 turns"** reads like a placeholder for
  "permanent".
- One page-level oddity worth naming: the `Elemental Reaction` glossary is ~200
  words and reprints in full on almost every screen, including a paragraph about
  a relic interaction I do not have, and including the line "NO REACTION IS
  REACHABLE HERE" on screens where it *was* reachable two cards earlier.

**(d) The card I never wanted to draw, and the one I was happiest to see.**

- **Never wanted:** plain **Defend**. 5 Block for an Energy, in a deck that also
  holds Dig In+ (11 Block for a Spark and no Energy). Every Defend I played felt
  like a card I would rather have removed. Runner-up: **Ka-pow!** on a bombless
  board.
- **Happiest to draw:** **Ka-pow!** *with* a bomb on the board. "Retain. Set off.
  Deal 4 damage" at zero Energy turned into 16 damage on fight 3 turn 2 and 14 on
  fight 5 turn 4, and it left all three Energy for Block. A free card that is
  sometimes the biggest attack in the deck is a very good card. **Dig In+** is the
  close second and the reason I took no damage in three of five fights.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight present a decision?**

**No.** My opening hand was Strike, Strike, Defend, Defend, Defend and my
starting relic was Pounding Surprise, which does nothing until a Bomb goes off —
and no card in that hand could place one. The only question was how many of the
five to spend on attack, which is a base-game question. **The kit does not appear
until turn 2**, when Jumpy Dumpty and Ka-pow! came up together and the fight
became interesting immediately. With a 10-card deck holding exactly two Klee
cards, a five-card opener misses both about a quarter of the time and misses the
*pair* far more often than that — and the pair is the point, because either card
alone is filler.
