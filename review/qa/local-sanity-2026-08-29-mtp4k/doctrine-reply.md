E1 — THE QUEUE  
1. PARTLY. It directly attacks issue (c) by making the Companion played a remembered object with future consequence, and it helps issue (a) by giving the bank a visible destination rather than a number to watch. It does not by itself fix issue (b), because the fuel rule is what stops Companion defence from feeding the finisher.  
2. FOLLOWS. Clause D2: “Every persistent resource and every automatic engine must feed a decision the player can steer: timing, targeting, placement, acquisition, conversion, or forgoing.” The queue is steered by which Companion is played and when, so the queue itself is a decision surface rather than a passive accumulator.  
3. RISK. If Companion access is thin, late, or unreliable, the queue can become an empty promise and the automatic engine can still read as a conveyor the player is waiting on rather than steering.

E2 — THE FUEL  
1. YES for issue (b), PARTLY for the rest. Removing Charge accrual from Companions directly breaks the loop where blocking with Companions also advances the finisher. It helps issue (a) by making Charge a narrower, more deliberate resource, but it does not by itself create the spend decision; that comes from the threshold.  
2. REQUIRES_MODIFICATION. Clause: the starter relic’s printed text: “Whenever a card is Exhausted, gain Charge and N Burst Energy.” That sentence still says every Exhaust pays Charge, so a rule that Companions pay none cannot stand unless that printed clause moves. I agree this is one of the two expected owner-side amendments.  
3. RISK. The narrowed fuel source can make the engine feel starved in Companion-heavy turns, and it may push the player toward burning her own cards for tempo even when the better play is to keep them, which can distort the deck-as-second-HP-bar identity.

E3 — THE THRESHOLD  
1. YES for issue (a), PARTLY for the rest. A threshold that spends Charge gives the bank a target, a cost, and a cadence, so the player is no longer merely watching a number rise. It also gives the queued Companion a payoff, which helps issue (c). It does not by itself fix issue (b); that depends on the fuel rule.  
2. REQUIRES_MODIFICATION. Clause R80: “CHARGE IS NEVER SPENT.” A threshold that reduces Charge by a fixed amount is a spend, so this element cannot be legal under the shipped standing law as written. I agree this is the second expected owner-side amendment.  
3. RISK. Even with a visible queue, the fire is automatic and one-card-per-turn, so the player may still feel the engine is happening to them rather than being chosen by them, especially if the queue is long, the threshold is easy to cross, or the front card is not the one they wanted.

E4 — THE PULSE  
1. PARTLY. Removing the Charge multiplier from the pulse directly removes the “watch it rise until the number is large” finisher, which helps issue (a). Keying the pulse to the last card type gives the player a small steering lever, which helps issue (c) by making card type matter. It helps issue (b) only indirectly, by stopping the pulse from reading the bank that Companion defence used to feed.  
2. FOLLOWS. Clause D2: “Every persistent resource and every automatic engine must feed a decision the player can steer: timing, targeting, placement, acquisition, conversion, or forgoing.” The pulse is still automatic, but its branch is steered by the type of the last card played, so the engine feeds a decision the player can make before the pulse fires.  
3. RISK. The Power branch is still an open pick in the element as stated, and until that branch is fixed the pulse’s consequence is not fully determinate. That can leave the player forecasting a menu rather than a known outcome, which weakens the legibility the rest of the design depends on.

E5 — THE UI  
1. PARTLY. The strip helps issue (a) by making the queue, the threshold, and the upcoming pulse visible, so the player can plan against the engine instead of discovering it. It helps issue (c) by making the queued Companions visible as objects with order and consequence. It does not by itself fix issue (b); that is a rules change, not a display change.  
2. FOLLOWS. Clause D4: “At the decision point the player can perceive and forecast the consequences that matter, through the card, a keyword, a persistent UI element or a character rule — not necessarily verbatim on every face.” The strip is exactly the kind of persistent UI element D4 allows for making the automatic engine forecastable.  
3. RISK. Visibility can make an automatic action more legible without making it more steerable. If the queue is long, the threshold is easy to reach, or the front card is not the one the player wanted, the UI may only make the lack of choice easier to see.

OVERALL  
A. PARTLY. The proposal as a whole addresses all three noted issues more directly than the shipped design: it gives Charge a spend and a target, stops Companion defence from feeding the finisher, and gives Companions a second consequence as queue contents. But the steering is indirect, and the automatic fire still leaves some of the old “engine happens to you” feeling unless the queue is reliably full and the player can shape it early.  

B. REQUIRES_MODIFICATION. The complete list of clauses that must move for this to be legal is:  
- R80: “CHARGE IS NEVER SPENT.”  
- The starter relic’s printed text: “Whenever a card is Exhausted, gain Charge and N Burst Energy.”  
I agree those are the two expected owner-side amendments. I do not think any additional clause must move for legality, but D2 and D4 are the clauses the design lives or dies on in practice.  

C. The visible queue can satisfy D2, but only because the steering happens before the fire, not at the fire. D2 does not require the player to choose the automatic action itself; it requires the resource or engine to feed a decision the player can steer, and the queue gives the player acquisition, conversion, and timing decisions by choosing which Companion to play and when. D4 is better satisfied: the strip can show what will fire and what the pulse will do, so the player can forecast the consequences that matter. Clause D2: “Every persistent resource and every automatic engine must feed a decision the player can steer: timing, targeting, placement, acquisition, conversion, or forgoing.” Clause D4: “At the decision point the player can perceive and forecast the consequences that matter, through the card, a keyword, a persistent UI element or a character rule — not necessarily verbatim on every face.” An automatic action is still automatic, but it is not automatically illegal if the player can steer its input.  

D. The proposal’s claim that the visible queue makes the automatic fire “steerable” is supported by D2 only if the queue is treated as the decision surface, not as a display of an unsteerable conveyor. Its claim that the strip fixes the shipped gauge’s “no bar” defect is supported by D4 once a threshold exists. Its claim that the pulse is no longer a Charge finisher is supported by the pasted sheet once the per-Charge multiplier is removed. The one internally inconsistent claim is the suggestion that the design is not Encore-shaped merely because the player never chooses to spend Charge at the moment of play: the threshold fire is still a spend, and R80’s own warning says a bank that can be spent is a different resource wearing the same name.
