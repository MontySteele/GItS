## Fight 1 — Fuzzy Wurm Crawler (57/57)

**Turn 1** (3 energy; hand Defend, Slack Water, Kurage's Oath, Shockwave, Read
the Field). Played **Read the Field** face-up first, deliberately before spending
the other two energy, so that whatever it handed me could still be paid for.
Then wrote **Kurage's Oath** on the Bake-Kurage (Plan: 7 to ALL) and played
**Slack Water** at the enemy.
*Rejected:* writing Slack Water as a Plan too. Both plans would have paid 9 next
turn against 13 over two turns for the split, and playing Slack Water now also
put Weak on a body that was telegraphing "Attack for 4" — the intent line is what
decided it. Also rejected Shockwave: 2 energy of debuff on a body hitting for 4
is a waste of Vulnerable, and it Exhausts.

**Turn 2.** The Plan resolved for 7 ("Kurage's Oath, 7 — the 7 is damage"). Hand
came up **three Defends and two Strikes** against an Empower intent, i.e. no
attack incoming. Played both Strikes.
*Rejected:* nothing, honestly. **This turn presented no decision** — Block does
nothing against a Buff intent, and the two Strikes were the only cards that did
anything at all. That is the finding: a starter hand with no Plan card in it is a
turn the kit does not participate in.

**Turn 3.** Enemy at 32 with Strength 7, telegraphing 11. Played three Strikes
(18).
*Rejected:* writing Read the Field as a Plan for 10 Block. I worked out that a
Plan resolves at the *start* of my next turn, so its Block would cover the attack
*after* the incoming one — one turn late. Trading 6 damage for block that arrives
late in a race I was winning was wrong.

**Turn 4.** Enemy at 14, telegraphing 11, Strength 7. Played Slack Water (Weak,
so its 11 fell), then two Defends for 10 Block.
*Rejected:* Shockwave + Slack Water as a burst — I counted it at 10–13 against a
14 HP body, so it would not have killed, and I would have eaten the hit for
nothing. Taking 0 and killing next turn was strictly better.

**Turn 5.** Took 0 damage (the block plan worked). Two Strikes for the kill.
*Rejected:* nothing — but this is the "plan paying off" case, not a dead turn:
the block that made this turn free was bought on turn 4.

**Across the fight:** decisions on turns 1, 3 and 4; turns 2 and 5 were
consequences. Reward: took **Deep Current** over Moon's Reflection, Stolen
Chapter and Gorou — the fight had just shown me the deck's problem was raw
damage per energy, not card flow.

## Fight 2 — Shrinker Beetle (40/40)

**Turn 1.** Intent was "Strategic (DebuffStrong)". Played **Scout Ahead**
face-up first to see one more card before committing, then two Strikes (12).
*Rejected:* Shockwave. Weak on a body that is not attacking this turn buys
nothing, and I wanted to keep the Exhaust card for a turn where Vulnerable would
be multiplying something bigger than a 6.

**Turn 2.** The debuff landed: **Shrink -1 — "While Shrinker Beetle is alive, you
deal 30% less damage with every hit you land"**. The screen re-printed every card
face with its reduced number (Strike now "Deal 4", Kurage's Oath "Deal 2") —
that is the single most legible thing the bridge did all round. Wrote **Kurage's
Oath** as a Plan (still printing "Plan: Deal 7"), played Deep Current and Strike.
*Rejected:* playing Oath face-up for its shrunken 2. Writing it was partly a
probe: I wanted to see whether the Plan's 7 was shrunk too.

**Turn 3.** **The probe answered: the Plan carried out for the full 7** while
Shrink was still on me. The Plan rule prints "A Plan carries the numbers you
wrote; a debuff on you afterwards does not change it" — but Shrink was on me
*before* I wrote it, and it still paid 7. So Plans dodge Shrink entirely. That is
a real, useful, non-obvious rule that no card face states. Then played Slack
Water (Weak, cutting its 13), Strike, Defend.
*Rejected:* writing Read the Field for 10 Block again — same one-turn-late
problem, and I needed the enemy dead before Shrink cost me another turn.

**Turn 4/5.** Two Strikes finished it. Reward: **Opening Gambit** over Ripple,
Moon's Reflection and Freminet, because its Plan line ("Apply 1 Vulnerable to ALL
enemies. The next Plan carried out with this one deals double damage") is the
first card I had seen that made *writing two Plans in one turn* a thing worth
doing.

*Rejected:* three attacks. 13 damage across two turns with 4 taken beat 12 now

**Turn 2.** Nibbit showed a two-part intent, "Attack for 6" **and** "Defensive

**Turn 3.** Enemy at 13 with 5 Block, as telegraphed. Strike + Deep Current

the rest on the bottom"; with fewer than 3 available it made the choice for me

**Turn 4.** Deep Current killed it. Reward: **Sango Isshin** ("Deal 8 damage. If
the Bake-Kurage carried out a Plan this turn, deal a quarter of your Max HP to
ALL enemies instead") over Undertow, Second Wave and Sayu — this is the card that
turned the Plan engine from a tempo trick into a win condition.

## Fight 4 (Elite) — Bygone Effigy (127/127)

Opened with **Slow 0 — "Whenever you play a card, this enemy receives 10% more
damage from Attacks this turn. It counts the cards played BEFORE this one"**, and
a Sleeping intent.

**Turn 1** (it does nothing). Scout Ahead face-up (drew Clumsy — the curse from
the event), then Slack Water and Deep Current, deliberately last, to sit as high
on the Slow count as I could. 13 HP came off a 127 HP body. That is the honest
number for this kit's turn-one output.
*Rejected:* writing plans — with a Sleeping enemy the free turn is better spent
on damage that lands before it wakes.

**Turn 2.** Wrote **Kurage's Oath** as a Plan and played **Shockwave** (Weak 3 +
Vulnerable; the relic answered twice).
*Rejected:* Oath face-up for 3 and a Defend. Against a body that would still be
alive in six turns, three turns of Weak on a 17-damage attack was worth more than
3 damage now.

**Turn 3.** The Plan carried out and the screen printed **"Kurage's Oath, 10 —
the 10 is damage; the clause asked for 7."** That single line is the best piece
of writing in the kit: it told me, without my having to test it, that the
enemy's Vulnerable multiplies a Plan's damage. Then Strike (Vulnerable, so 9) +
Cleansing Wave+ + Defend for 13 Block against a 17.
*Rejected:* Read the Field to dig. Blocking 13 of 17 at 62 HP against an elite
that was climbing (17 → 23) was the safer half of the trade.

**Turn 4.** Vulnerable still up for one more turn. Drank **Radiant Tincture**
(+1 energy now and for 3 turns) to buy a fourth card, then three Strikes and Deep
Current: **39 damage in one turn** (90 → 51). This was the turn the run was
decided by, and the decision was made two turns earlier when I bought Vulnerable
with Shockwave.
*Rejected:* holding the potion for the boss. At 58 HP against a body that had
just started hitting for 23, the elite *was* the emergency.

**Turn 5.** No damage in hand worth the name (Slack Water 4, Oath 3). Wrote
**Kurage's Oath** as a Plan and blocked with Cleansing Wave+ and a Defend; played
Slack Water for the Weak.
*Rejected:* full block (18) and no Plan. I paid 4 HP for 7 damage **and** for the
right to fire Sango Isshin next turn, which needs a Plan to have been carried out.

**Turn 6 — the combo turn.** Wrote **Opening Gambit** (Plan: Vulnerable to ALL)
and **Scout Ahead** (Plan: draw 1 per Plan carried out) and blocked with two
Defends. I dealt **zero damage** this turn on purpose, at 37 HP against a 23.
*Rejected:* Strike + Opening Gambit face-up for 11. I counted it out: 11 now left
the enemy at 27, which I could not kill next turn either, so I would eat two more
23s. Spending the turn to guarantee a Vulnerable, two extra cards and a live
Sango was the only line that killed.

**Turn 7.** The screen paid the plan out exactly as written: *"Opening Gambit, 1
— the 1 is Vulnerable. Inside the same beat: Tamakushi Casket 3 on Bygone
Effigy"* and *"Scout Ahead, 2 — the 2 is cards drawn."* Sango Isshin came off
those two extra cards, fired for 21 base into Vulnerable — **35 → 4** — and a
Strike finished it. HP 24/87.

**Across the fight:** the two real decisions were both made turns before they
paid — Shockwave on turn 2 and the zero-damage plan turn on turn 6. The last turn
looked automatic and was the payoff of the second one.

Rewards: 36 gold, Speed Potion, **Horn Cleat** (14 Block at the start of the 2nd
turn), and a **second Sango Isshin** over Feint, Pincer and Barbara.

## The kit, after 8 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

- **On the turn:** *play it now, or write it on the jellyfish.* This is the kit's
  whole engine and it is a genuinely good decision, because the two halves are
  differently shaped — Kurage's Oath is 3 now or 7 next turn; Feigned Retreat+ is
  6 Block now or 6 Block **and** 8 damage next turn; Ripple+ is 2 Block now or
  1 Energy and 7 Block next turn. Every one of those trades tempo for rate, and
  the enemy's telegraphed intent is what settles it. Fight 5 turn 3 (Tangled made
  attacks cost +1) and fight 1 turn 1 were both decided this way.
- **Earlier in the fight:** *paying a whole turn for a future one.* Elite 1 turn
  6 — I dealt zero damage at 37 HP to write Opening Gambit and Scout Ahead — was
  the best turn I played all round, and it was only legible because the Plan
  block prints what will be carried out and Sango prints its condition. The same
  shape won fight 6 in one turn.
- **At the draft:** Sango Isshin is the pick that reorganises the deck. Once it
  is in, "did a Plan resolve this turn?" is a question you are asking on *every*
  turn, including the ones where you have no Plan cards, and that retroactively
  makes cheap Plan-writing worth doing. Breakwater+'s **Dusk** was the other
  draft-level decision: it fixes the kit's structural weakness (Block from a Plan
  arrives one turn late) and I could tell that from the keyword alone.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Defend and Strike.** Nine-tenths of my "no rejected alternative" turns were
  hands of Strikes and Defends with no Plan card in them. Fight 1 turn 2 was
  literally three Defends and two Strikes against a Buff intent: there was one
  legal use of the energy. The kit is interesting exactly in proportion to how
  many Plan cards are in the hand, and the starter deck's basics are inert
  filler that the jellyfish cannot even use.
- **Read the Field** written as a Plan (10 Block) I never once wanted — see (d).
- **Shockwave** was worth it exactly twice (elite 1, where Vulnerable multiplied
  a 21) and never in a small fight; a 2-cost Exhaust debuff is a boss card.
- **Clumsy** and the three **Infection** statuses: pure dead draws, as designed.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **Read the Field on a short draw pile silently made my choice for me.** The
   card says "Look at the top 3 cards of your draw pile; put one into your hand".
   With 1 card in the pile it produced no selection screen, put a Defend in my
   hand, and charged the energy. Nothing on the screen said "there was only one
   candidate". I spent a turn unsure whether the card had whiffed.
2. **Shrink did not apply to a Plan I wrote while shrunk.** The Plan glossary
   says "A Plan carries the numbers you wrote; a debuff on you *afterwards* does
   not change it" — but Shrink -1 ("you deal 30% less damage with every hit you
   land") was on me *before* I wrote Kurage's Oath and the carry-out still paid
   the full 7. Either "a hit you land" excludes the jellyfish's hit (plausible —
   the same page says "Every planned HIT is the jellyfish's") or Plans are simply
   immune. The rules text does not say which, and the difference matters.
3. **Opening Gambit's doubling clause is unreachable more often than it looks.**
   "The next Plan carried out with this one deals double damage" requires a
   second *damage* Plan written in the same turn. I held it four times with only
   block/draw Plans in hand and never once got the doubling. The screen did tell
   me afterwards — *"Opening Gambit: no Plan followed"* — which is honest, but
   the card face does not warn you before you spend the energy.
4. **Two-part intents.** "Attack for 6 … and also: Defensive (Defend)" comes with
   a disclaimer that the feed "carries nothing that says which of those parts
   resolve, in what order, or on what condition". I had to guess how much Block
   would be there. That is the bridge's honesty, not the kit's fault, but it is
   the one place I could not plan properly.
5. **Elemental reactions never happened.** Every screen carried a long
   Elemental Reaction paragraph and every screen also said "NO REACTION IS
   REACHABLE HERE: Hydro is the only element this screen can supply". Eight
   fights, hundreds of lines of aura rules, and the aura never once did anything
   I could act on. It reads as a system that is printed but not present.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted:** **Read the Field**, in both halves. Face-up it is a 1-energy
  look at 3 cards in a deck whose cards are mostly Strike and Defend, and the one
  time it mattered it silently auto-picked (see (c)1). As a Plan its 10 Block
  arrives at the *start* of my next turn, i.e. after the hit I was trying to
  survive — I rejected it for exactly that reason on fight 1 turn 3, fight 2
  turn 3 and fight 5 turn 4. Breakwater+'s Dusk clause exists specifically to fix
  the flaw Read the Field has, which rather makes the point.
- **Happiest to draw:** **Sango Isshin**. "If the Bake-Kurage carried out a Plan
  this turn, deal a quarter of your Max HP to ALL enemies instead" turns a
  1-energy Plan written last turn into 21 damage to everything, and drawing it on
  a turn I had already armed felt like collecting on a bet I had placed two turns
  earlier. Runner-up: **Ripple+**, because a 0-cost card whose Plan hands the
  energy back makes the whole engine free.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes — a real one, and a good one.** The opening hand was Defend, Slack Water,
Kurage's Oath, Shockwave and Read the Field against a body telegraphing "Attack
for 4", and the question "which of these do I write on the jellyfish instead of
playing" was live on all four of the non-Defend cards. I played Read the Field
first *because* I wanted information before committing the other two energy, then
split: Oath written (3 now vs 7 later) and Slack Water played (its Weak was worth
more against a telegraphed attack than one turn later). Nothing was forced, the
alternatives were all defensible, and I could reason about them entirely from the
printed faces. That is the strongest single thing I can say about this kit.
