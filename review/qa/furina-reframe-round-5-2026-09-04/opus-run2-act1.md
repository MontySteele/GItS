## Fight 1 — Leaf Slime (S) 13, Leaf Slime (M) 35, Twig Slime (S) 9

Opening hand: Ethereal Spotlight (0, **"CANNOT BE PLAYED: you have no Encore, and
this costs 2"**), Regal Bearing, Stage Presence, Freminet — Pers, Deploy!, An
Invitation, Salon Début. 3 energy.

**Turn 1.** Played An Invitation first (0 cost, "Add 1 random Common Companion
card to your hand") — it gave Freminet — Pressurized Floe: Backstroke (2 energy,
10 damage + 6 Block). Then Salon Début, then Backstroke on Leaf Slime (S).

*Rejected:* Salon Début + Freminet — Pers, Deploy! + Stage Presence, which is
15 damage and 6 Block against Backstroke's 14+6. I took the Backstroke line
because Backstroke's title has the character-dash shape the **Companion** glossary
entry defines, so I expected it to perform the salon member as well — 10 + a
perform beat 6 + a perform.

The turn-1 decision that actually mattered was **where** to point Backstroke.
Salon Début had already deployed Crabaletta, and Crabaletta's deploy-perform had
hit the Twig Slime for 4 (9 → 5) and left Hydro Aura 2 on it. So I knew members
hit *something*, but not what governs the target — nothing printed says. I pointed
Backstroke at Leaf Slime (S) (13 HP, i.e. more than Backstroke's 10) as a test:
if the member followed my card's target, 10 + 4 kills it; if the member kept
hitting the Twig, I eat 4 damage and learn the rule. It followed the card's
target and Leaf Slime (S) died. **That rule — a Companion card's perform lands on
the Companion card's target — is nowhere in the printed text**, and I had to spend
a real turn discovering it.

Ethereal Spotlight sat dead in hand all turn, and the refusal line was the most
useful text on the screen: it told me a resource called Encore existed and that I
had none of it, before any card in my opening hand made any.

**Turn 2.** Aria of Recompense (reframe) — "Gain 5 Encore. If you have at least 6
Fanfare, gain 5 more" — was the answer. Played it (Fanfare 3, so 5 Encore), then
Ethereal Spotlight (paying 2 Encore, leaving 3), then Lynette — Enigmatic Feint
on Twig Slime, then Soloist's Solicitation on Leaf Slime (M).

*Rejected:* Aria + two Soloist's Solicitations for 12 flat damage and 5 Encore
sitting as absorb. I turned that down to see what the Spotlight actually buys.
It buys a real, visible thing: the instant Spotlight resolved, Lynette's printed
Block changed from 5 to 7 in my hand, and next turn Backstroke read 15 instead of
10. That is the clearest, most legible effect in the whole kit — the card text in
hand rewrites itself.

**Here is where the screen and the outcome disagreed.** Guest Cast, the buff the
Spotlight applies, prints: *"Companion cards are Spotlighted: 50% stronger printed
damage and Block, **no Fanfare**."* I played Lynette with Guest Cast up; Crabaletta
performed; **Fanfare went 3 → 5.** The Fanfare buff itself prints "2 when it stays
and 5 on an Evoke", and +2 is exactly one staying perform. So the Spotlight's
"no Fanfare" clause did not fire. I saw this again in the Wriggler fight (Fanfare
0 → 6 across three Spotlighted performs) and again in the elite (0 → 4 across two).
Three fights, three confirmations, no exception.

**Turn 3.** Backstroke (now reading 15) then Freminet — Pers, Deploy! (now 9) on
Leaf Slime (M), 29 HP. Killed it. *Rejected:* Stage Presence — the enemy's intent
was 2 Status cards, not damage, so Block was worth nothing and I could see that
from the intent line.

Fight 1: **zero HP lost.**

Reward: Kujou Sara — Tengu Stormcall (1, deal 5, next turn Attacks +5, Electro).
*Rejected:* Quick Change+ (already upgraded, first Attack each turn draws 2) and
Director's Cut, whose condition — "If you moved the Spotlight this turn" — I could
not price, because after two fights I still did not know what "moving" the
Spotlight means or whether I control it. I took Sara because by then I knew a
Companion card is a free extra member perform stapled to whatever else it does,
and that is the kit's best rate.

## Fight 2 — Nibbit 45

**Turn 1.** An Invitation (gave Kaeya — Frostgnaw), Salon Début, Freminet — Pers,
Deploy! on Nibbit, then Kaeya — Frostgnaw on Nibbit.

This is the first turn of the run that felt like the kit doing something only it
does. The order was the whole decision: Salon Début deploys Crabaletta, who
performs 4 Hydro and leaves Hydro Aura 2 on Nibbit. Freminet is [Cryo] — Cryo
onto that Hydro aura is **Frozen**, and the reaction preview appeared on the card
in my hand the moment the aura existed, reading *"Reaction preview: Frozen — Hydro
meets Cryo: its next action deals half damage."* Nibbit's intent dropped from 12

a Companion card) put Hydro back on, so Kaeya — also Cryo — re-froze it and, being
an Attack against a Frozen target, Shattered for an extra 6.

*Rejected:* Stage Presence for 6 Block on the last energy. Halving a 12 into a 6
via Frozen is strictly better than blocking 6, and it also set up the Shatter.
The kit let me build that; **no card printed it.** I got there by reading the
glossary's Frozen entry against the element tags, not from anything on the cards.

Nibbit 45 → 15 in one turn, and its hit was halved. I took 6.

**Turn 2.** Sara — Tengu Stormcall on Nibbit (5 + Crabaletta's perform) then
Soloist's Solicitation. Exactly lethal. *Rejected:* Aria for Encore — the enemy
had 15 HP and I could count 15 damage, so building a resource I would not spend
was the worse line. This was a genuine "do I finish it or do I set up" decision

Reward: Gorou — Inuzaka All-Round Defense (1, deal 8, Block equal to half the
damage dealt). *Rejected:* **Poised Riposte — "Deal 6 damage, already including
Encore."** I could not parse that sentence and still cannot. Encore is defined as

**Turn 1.** Aria (5 Encore) → Ethereal Spotlight (−2) → Salon Début → Freminet —
Pers, Deploy!. 21 damage (Crabaletta's deploy-perform at full 6 because Encore
paid for it, Freminet at 9, Crabaletta again at 6).

*Rejected:* skipping the Spotlight to keep 2 more Encore as absorb, which is 21
damage → 21 damage this turn but every later Companion card 33% smaller. Against

**The payoff was defensive, not offensive, and I did not plan it.** Freminet's Cryo
landed on the Hydro aura Crabaletta had just left, Byrdonis went Frozen, and its
intent fell from **17 to 8**. I took 7 damage on an elite's opening turn. That is
the best moment the kit produced all act — but I want to be honest that *the kit
did not show it to me*. Freminet's card printed no reaction preview when I played
it (there was no aura yet); the Frozen appeared because of the order I happened to
play two cards in, and I only knew to want that order because I had stumbled into
the same interaction in fight 2.

**Turn 2.** An Invitation (gave Neuvillette), Crowfeather Cover (0 — free
Crabaletta perform, and next Attack +4/Electro), Gorou (16 with the +4) —
24 damage, 8 Block. Then Neuvillette and Soloist. 63 → 39 → 18.

*Rejected:* Stage Presence for 6 more Block. Byrdonis was at 39 with Strength
climbing; a turn spent blocking is a turn where its next attack is bigger. The
race said push.

**And here is the second place the screen and the outcome disagreed.** Crowfeather
Cover promised "your next Attack this turn deals 4 additional damage and **applies
Electro**". Gorou (a Geo card, whose own preview read "Crystallize") then hit a
target wearing Hydro Aura 2. The +4 damage arrived, exactly as printed. What
appeared on Byrdonis was **"Poison 4 (debuff) — At the start of its turn, loses 4
HP, then reduce Poison by 1."**

There is no Poison anywhere in this kit's glossary. But the glossary *does* define
**Electro-Charged — "Hydro meets Electro: the reacted enemy loses 4 HP at the start
of its turn, 1 less each turn"** — which is word-for-word the same effect. So the
Electro did apply, it did react with the Hydro, and Electro-Charged fired: **the
game just calls it "Poison" on the body while every card and every glossary entry
calls it Electro-Charged.** I saw the identical thing again in fight 6. This is
the one confusion in the act I could not resolve from the screen alone; I resolved
it by matching the numbers, and a player without the glossary open would simply
conclude their reaction had not happened.

Two smaller things on that same beat: Gorou's own Crystallize preview did not fire
(Block was exactly half the damage, with no +4), presumably because Crowfeather
overrode its element — sensible, but not printed anywhere. And the Hydro aura was
still showing afterwards, because a later Crabaletta perform re-applied it — the
exact "THAT LAST RULE CAN HIDE THE FIRST" case the glossary warns about at length.
The warning is there because the system needs it, which is itself the finding.

**Turn 3.** Crowfeather Cover (free perform, 18 → 14) then Sara — Tengu Stormcall
(11 with the +4, plus a perform). Dead on the number. *Rejected:* Lynette + Deep
Breath to survive a 19-damage hit — but I could count 19 damage against 18 HP, so

**Elite cleared at 67/78 — 11 damage taken across three turns**, against a 17-per-

**This reward screen is also the first time the run mentioned Burst Energy.** The

## The kit, after 6 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Three, and they are all the same shape: *spend Encore now, or hold it as armour.*

1. **Ethereal Spotlight, every fight.** 2 Encore for a permanent +50% on every
   Companion card's printed damage and Block. On a two-turn fight it is a loss; on
   the elite it was the fight. The card even tells you the price and refuses
   itself when you cannot pay, which is the clearest gate in the kit.
2. **Aria versus a kill.** Fight 2 turn 2 and fight 3 turn 3 both came down to
   "build the pool, or count the damage and end it." Both had a countable answer,
   which is what makes them decisions rather than guesses.
3. **Play order for reactions.** Salon Début → Freminet is Frozen; Freminet →
   Salon Début is not. On the elite that ordering was worth 9 points of incoming
   damage on turn 1. This is the deepest thing in the kit and the least signposted
   (see (c)).

The fourth, weaker one: **which enemy a Companion card points at**, since the
member's perform follows it. Once I knew the rule it turned every Companion play
into a two-hit targeting question.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

The Block cards. **Stage Presence** (6, later 7) and **Regal Bearing** (3, later 4)
were the cards I played when I had a spare energy and nothing else, and I skipped
them at every real decision point in the act. Six fights, 120 actions, and I used
**zero potions** — I held a Block Potion and an Explosive Ampoule through an
elite and never wanted either. That is the loudest thing I can say about this act's
pressure: nothing threatened me, and the defensive half of the kit was therefore
inert. I finished the elite at 86% HP.

**Soloist's Solicitation** (1 energy, 6 damage, no element, no character-dash, so
no perform) is the plain vanilla card, and it exists mainly to be the thing you
play when your Companion cards are gone.

Also automatic: **An Invitation** at 0 cost. It is always correct and never a
decision — it just hands you a card.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

Five, in order of how much they cost me:

1. **Guest Cast prints "no Fanfare" and Fanfare goes up anyway.** Three fights,
   every time, +2 per staying perform, exactly as if the Spotlight were not there.
   Either the clause is broken or it means something other than what it says.
2. **Electro-Charged shows up on the enemy as "Poison".** The card, the reaction
   preview and the glossary all say Electro-Charged; the body says "Poison 4",
   with Electro-Charged's exact numbers. Twice. If I had not been matching numbers
   I would have written down that my reaction failed.
3. **Nothing tells you a Companion card's perform follows the card's target**, or
   what a member does when you play a Companion card with an empty stage (nothing
   — half the card silently does nothing). I spent a turn in fight 1 discovering
   the first and only inferred the second.
4. **"Poised Riposte — Deal 6 damage, already including Encore."** I skipped this
   card because after six fights I still cannot tell you what that sentence means.
5. **Burst Energy exists and I never saw it.** A full glossary entry on a reward
   card I did not take, describing a meter that no combat screen displayed and no
   card I owned could fill. And relatedly: **what a second Salon member is for.**
   The Salon holds 3, but a Companion card performs "the front member" only, and
   nothing in my deck Evokes, so I passed on two reward cards (Casting Call+, a
   second Crabaletta) purely because I could not price them.

Smaller: Deep Breath's "Choose one" offered one option with no explanation; the
Smith screen listed a card in my deck (Neuvillette) under "on the screen's list
nowhere, and nothing on the feed says why"; and the fight-6 reward screen offered
"18 Gold" and "15 Gold" as two separate rows.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Regal Bearing** — 3 Block (4 with Dexterity) and 1 Weak for a full
energy, in a deck where that energy buys a Companion card worth 9 damage plus a
free member perform. I played it zero times in six fights.

Happiest to draw: **Kujou Sara — Crowfeather Cover**, and it is not close. Cost 0,
a Companion card, so it performs a member for free, *and* it hands the next Attack
+4 and an element. It is the card that made turns feel like they had a shape —
you play it first, then decide which attack it is buying. **Aria of Recompense+**
is the honourable mention: making it Innate changed every opening turn in the act.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, but a smaller one than it looked, and not the one the kit wants.** The real
first-turn question was where to point Backstroke, and that was a decision only
because I did not yet know the targeting rule — it was an experiment, not a play.
The kit's actual signature decision, spending Encore, **could not be made on turn
1 at all**: Ethereal Spotlight sat in my opening hand printing "CANNOT BE PLAYED:
you have no Encore, and this costs 2", and Aria was not in the hand. So turn 1 of
fight 1 was three cards and 3 energy with no resource to spend and no member on
stage. It presented arithmetic, not a choice.

That is fixed later in the run and the fix is visible: once Aria was upgraded to
Innate, turn 1 of fight 6 opened with 8 Encore in hand and the Spotlight
immediately live, and *that* turn had the real decision in it. The kit's opening
turn gets good exactly when Encore is guaranteed to be there.
