three so a natural run stayed natural — Neow's Sacrifice buys a strong potion
but jams a dead Guilty into a 10-card deck for five combats, and Neow's Torment

the only look at the starting deck, which is information the screen is entitled

I upgraded **Aria of Recompense** (5→8 Encore) over Soloist's Solicitation
(6→9 damage). Encore does double duty — it absorbs damage after Block *and*
pays for member performances — so a point of it is worth more than a point of
Block, which expires. **This is the round's first legibility finding:** the
pre-pick preview line read only `Upgraded: Gain 8 Encore...`, but the card I
actually got was **`Innate. Gain 8 Encore...`**. The upgrade silently added
Innate, and the preview did not print it. That hidden keyword turned out to be
the single most load-bearing thing in the deck — it guaranteed 8 Encore on
turn 1 of every fight for the rest of the run — and I chose the card without
being shown it.

## Fight 1 — Nibbit (45 HP), intent 12

**Turn 1.** Aria of Recompense+ (Encore 2→10) → Ethereal Spotlight (0 energy,
−2 Encore; Freminet 6→9, Charlotte 4→6) → Freminet on A (9) → Soloist's (6).
*Rejected:* Charlotte — First-Person Shutter for 6+6 Block instead of
Soloist's. That was the real decision of the turn and it was close: Charlotte
lines up 12 points of prevention against Soloist's 6 points of damage, which
is better value per card, but a 45 HP body at 15/turn dies in three turns and
at 9/turn in five, and each extra turn is another 12 damage arriving. I took
the race. It cost exactly 4 HP.

The Encore rule resolved *precisely* as printed: 12 incoming, 0 Block, Encore
8 absorbed 8, 4 to HP, Encore to 0. No ambiguity anywhere.

**Turn 2.** An Invitation (0) → Salon Début → Chevreuse — Vanguard's Valor →
Soloist's → Stage Presence.
*Rejected:* leading with Soloist's. Salon Début had to go first because
Crabaletta's Hydro landing on the Cryo aura Freminet left last turn is a
Frozen, and Frozen both halves the incoming attack and primes a 6-damage
Shatter for my next Attack. That sequencing — Cryo from a card on turn 1,
Hydro from a member on turn 2 — is the best thing in the kit and it is
genuinely mine to find.

Crabaletta printed `hit Nibbit for 4 Hydro ... (dry: it could not pay its
Encore, so it acted at three-quarters)`. That line is excellent: it told me
*why* the number was 4 and not 6 without my having to work it out, and it is
what made me draft for Encore at the next reward.

Chevreuse then read `Your next Attack deals 3 additional damage, plus 3 if an
Elemental Reaction triggered this turn` — the reaction had triggered, so
Soloist's went in at 6+6=12 and the Frozen Shatter added 6 on top. 22 damage
in a turn off a 6-damage card. Nibbit 26 → 4.

**Turn 3.** Freminet on A. *No alternative rejected* — 4 HP behind 5 Block,
and Freminet is one energy that both hits for 9 and performs Crabaletta. But
this is the plan from turn 2 paying off, not a dead turn: the Cryo I applied
on turn 1 is what made turn 2's 22-damage swing, which is what made turn 3
trivial. The fight's decisions were all made on turns 1 and 2.

*Companion cards played: 3 (Freminet ×2, Chevreuse). Turns with an empty
stage: 1 (turn 1).*

## Fight 2 — Shrinker Beetle (39 HP), opening intent: debuff

**Turn 1.** Aria+ → Ethereal Spotlight → Freminet on A (9) → Soloist's (6).
*Rejected:* any Block at all. The intent line said Debuff, not Attack, so
every point of Block was strictly waste — the screen made that free to read.
I also held An Invitation rather than playing it, because with no member on
stage a Companion card is `only its own line`, and the tool says so on the
card face.

**Turn 2.** Salon Début → Charlotte — First-Person Shutter → Suffering for Art.
*Rejected:* Soloist's Solicitation. The Beetle had applied **Shrink** —
"While Shrinker Beetle is alive, your Attacks deal 30% less damage" — and my
Soloist's face had visibly rewritten itself from `Deal 6 damage` to
`Deal 4 damage`. But the Salon rules print that **"a performance is not an
Attack"**, so I gambled that Crabaletta would dodge Shrink entirely.

**She did.** The log printed `Crabaletta hit Shrinker Beetle for 6 Hydro` —
the full 6, not 4.2. That is a real, findable, printed-rules-support tactical
read, and it is the best decision I made in the run: against an
Attack-debuffing enemy, the correct answer is to stop attacking and route all
damage through the stage. Nothing told me to do this; the rules let me work it
out.

Crabaletta's Hydro also consumed the Cryo aura for Frozen, halving the
Beetle's 7 to 3.

**Turn 3.** An Invitation → Neuvillette — Sourcewater Droplets → Salon Début.
*Rejected:* two Soloist's for 12 (also lethal). The Companion route killed
just as dead for one less energy while generating Fanfare, and the second
Salon Début deployed a *second* Crabaletta whose join-performance was itself
lethal. Beetle 12 → 0.

Zero HP lost. *Companion cards played: 3 (Freminet, Charlotte Shutter,
Neuvillette). Turns with an empty stage: 1 (turn 1).*

## Fight 3 — Twig Slime S (9), Twig Slime M (27), Leaf Slime S (12)

The multi-enemy shape I had knowingly declined to prepare for at reward 2.

**Turn 1.** Aria+ (Sown refunded the energy) → Ethereal Spotlight → Salon
Début → Freminet on **B** → Stage Presence.
*Rejected:* Freminet on A (3 HP after Crabaletta's random hit). Killing the
9 HP slime removes 4 incoming, but spends a 9-damage card on a 3 HP body.
I put it into the 27 HP slime instead, on the reasoning that Freminet applies
Cryo and Crabaletta's follow-up Hydro would Freeze whatever it hit.

It worked — B went 27 → 12 and came out Frozen with a Shatter primed — **but
I should be honest that half of that was luck, not plan.** The Salon rules
say plainly that "a performing member picks its own enemy, never the card's
target," and Crabaletta chose B at random. I set up a combo I could not aim.

**Turn 2.** An Invitation → Soloist's on A (kill) → Diona — Icy Paws →
Charlotte — First-Person Shutter.
*Rejected:* Regal Bearing, and rejected spending energy on damage instead of
the two Companion cards. Killing A first was the point: it removed 4 incoming
*and* narrowed the random-target pool so the two performances could not be
wasted on a 3 HP body. Both then hit B and killed it. 16 Block against 15
incoming.

This is the turn where the random targeting stopped being a pure downside and
became something I could *manage* — not by aiming, but by clearing the bodies
I did not want hit. That is a genuine, non-obvious skill the kit rewards.

**Turn 3.** Charlotte — Framing on C. *Rejected:* nothing meaningful — 6 from
the card plus 6 from the performance is exactly 12 on a 12 HP body. One card,
one energy, lethal. Again the payoff of an earlier turn, not a decision.

Zero HP lost. *Companion cards played: 4. Turns with an empty stage: 0 —
Salon Début came up on turn 1, and this was by some distance the smoothest
fight of the run.*

## Fight 4 — Vine Shambler (61 HP), 6×2

**Turns 1 and 2 are the round's most important negative finding: the stage
held no member for both of them.** Salon Début sat in the draw pile, so every
Companion card in my hand printed `No member on stage: performs nobody. Deploy
a member first, or this card is only its own line.` Freminet was a vanilla
9-damage attack, Charlotte a vanilla Block card. The kit's entire engine was
absent for a third of the fight and there was no decision available about it —
I could not choose my way out, only wait for a draw.

**Turn 1.** Aria+ → Spotlight → Freminet (9) → Soloist's (6) → Charlotte
(6 Block +6 next). *Rejected:* Stage Presence over Charlotte — Charlotte gives
12 points of Block across two turns for the same energy.

**Turn 2.** Charlotte — Framing (6, draw 1) → Suffering for Art → Freminet (9)
→ Soloist's (6). *Rejected:* Stage Presence for Block. Suffering for Art
trades 1 HP for 3 Encore, and since Encore absorbs 1:1, that is a net +2 HP
*and* fuel — a small, clean, always-correct-looking sum, which is a mild
criticism of the card.

**Turn 3 — the best decision in the fight.** The Shambler telegraphed **16**
and had applied **Tangled** ("Attacks cost an additional Energy this turn"),
which had visibly rewritten Soloist's from cost 1 to **cost 2** on its face.
I played Salon Début (Crabaletta's Hydro onto the Cryo aura → Frozen, 16 → 8)
→ An Invitation → Neuvillette (9 Block, performance) → Charlotte — First-Person
Shutter (6 Block, performance).
*Rejected:* Soloist's Solicitation. Tangled made my Attack cost 2 for 6
damage, while two Companion cards cost 2 for **12 damage of performances plus
15 Block**. The debuff taxed exactly the half of my deck that the stage
bypasses. That is a good interaction: the enemy's counter-play pushed me off
cards and onto the salon, and the screen gave me everything I needed to see it.

Frozen (halving 16 to 8) plus 15 Block meant a 16-damage turn cost me nothing.

**Turn 4.** Freminet+ on A for lethal at 7 HP. The payoff turn.

One HP lost, and that was Suffering for Art. *Companion cards played: 7.
Turns with an empty stage: 2 (turns 1–2) — the worst of the run.*

## Fight 5 — ELITE: Bygone Effigy (127 HP)

**Turn 1.** Asleep, no damage incoming. It carried **Slow** — "Whenever you
play a card, this enemy receives 10% more damage from Attacks this turn. It
counts the cards played BEFORE this one."

That single line turned an otherwise empty turn into the most interesting
sequencing puzzle of the run. I played Aria+ → Spotlight → An Invitation →
Chevreuse → Stage Presence → Soloist's → **Freminet+ last**.
*Rejected:* playing Freminet+ early, and rejected skipping Stage Presence.
Stage Presence's 6 Block was pure waste against a sleeping enemy — I played it
purely to add another 10% to the two Attacks behind it. And I worked out that
with a flat +3 from Chevreuse available, the bigger base attack belongs on the
bigger multiplier: 6@1.5 + 12@1.6 beats 12@1.5 + 6@1.6. Predicted ~32 damage;
the screen showed 127 → 95. **Exactly 32.**

Slow is a genuinely good piece of design — it rewards card *order* rather than
card choice, which is a decision axis nothing else in this kit uses.

**Turn 2 — the engine at full tilt, and the best turn of the run.** Salon
Début → Charlotte — Framing → Freminet last. Three Crabaletta performances
(18 unblockable-by-Shrink Hydro) plus two Slow-boosted Attacks plus a Shatter:
**95 → 55, forty damage in one turn.** The Salon log printed
`Crabaletta hit Bygone Effigy for 6 Hydro, and left no aura on it` three times
— each Hydro consuming the Cryo my own Attack had just applied, looping Frozen
off my own two-element deck.

This is the kit working as I assume it is meant to, and it required all three
of: a member on stage, two Companion cards in hand, and correct ordering. When
those line up it is excellent.

**Turn 3 — the mirror image, and the honest cost.** The Effigy's two Empower
turns had bought it **Strength 10**; it telegraphed 23. And I drew *no
Companion card*. Crabaletta stood on the stage and did nothing for a whole
turn, because members "do NOT act on their own" and I had no card to perform
her.

Speed Potion (5 Dexterity) → Regal Bearing (Weak: 23 → 17) → Stage Presence →
Soloist's last for Slow. Dexterity turned 3+6 Block into 8+11 = **19** against
a Weakened 17.
*Rejected:* Breathless. It reads `Spend 4 Encore, or HP for any shortfall.
Deal 9 damage` — but Encore was the only thing standing between me and a
23-damage hit, so converting my armour into 3 extra damage was clearly wrong.
Good card, wrong turn — and knowing that is the decision.

Zero damage taken.

**Turn 4 — lethal, planned three cards deep.** 48 HP left. Salon Début →
Charlotte — Framing (its Attack lands, *then* its performance's Hydro meets
the fresh Cryo → Frozen, halving 23 to 11 and priming Shatter) → Chevreuse
(performance, and now +6 because a reaction had triggered this turn) →
**Freminet+ last** (max Slow, +6, plus the 6 Shatter it was the first Attack
to collect). 48 → dead.

*Rejected:* Soloist's in place of Salon Début, and rejected playing Chevreuse
first. Chevreuse had to come *after* the reaction so its conditional second
+3 was live, but *before* Freminet+ so the buff landed on the biggest number.
That is a four-card ordering with three separate reasons, and it is the most
satisfying thing I did all round.

**127 HP elite killed for zero HP.** *Companion cards played: 7. Turns with an
empty stage: 1 (turn 1).*

## The kit, after 5 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

The best ones were all *fight-level or draft-level*, not turn-level:

1. **Route damage through the stage to dodge an Attack debuff** (fight 2, *on
   the turn*). Shrink cut my Attacks 30% and visibly rewrote Soloist's to
   "Deal 4"; the Salon rules say "a performance is not an Attack." Choosing to
   stop attacking and deploy instead traded tempo for immunity, and the
   printed rules were sufficient to work it out in advance. Same shape again
   in fight 4 when **Tangled** taxed Attacks +1 energy and two Companion cards
   bought 12 damage and 15 Block for the price of one 6-damage Soloist's.
2. **Two-element sequencing across turns** (fights 1–5, *earlier in the
   fight*). Cryo from a card, Hydro from a member, Frozen on the seam — then
   an Attack to collect the Shatter. Trades immediate damage for a halved
   enemy turn plus 6 free damage later.
3. **Card ordering under Slow** (elite, *on the turn*). Cheap cards first,
   biggest Attack last, and the flat buff onto the biggest base. Predicted 32
   damage, got exactly 32. Pure ordering, no card choice at all.
4. **Declining Curtain Rises** (*at the draft*). Traded a fix for my worst
   structural problem against permanently diluting my performance rotation.
5. **Kill order to shape random targeting** (fight 3, *on the turn*). I can't
   aim performances, but I can delete the bodies I don't want them hitting.
6. **Encore as armour vs. Encore as ammunition** (elite turn 3). Refusing
   Breathless because the 4 Encore it burns was the only thing between me and
   a 23-damage hit. The card is good; that turn it was wrong. That tension is
   the best thing about the Encore design.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

*Automatic:* **Aria of Recompense+ on turn 1, every single fight, without
thinking.** Innate + Sown made it cost nothing and give 8 Encore, so it was
never a decision — it was a ritual. **Ethereal Spotlight** likewise: it is
Ethereal, so it is use-it-or-lose-it each turn, and once I had two Companion
cards the answer was always "play it turn 1." Two of my first two plays in
every fight were foregone conclusions. **Suffering for Art** is also close to
automatic — 1 HP for 3 Encore where Encore absorbs 1:1 is a visible net gain
almost always.

*Never worth playing:* **Regal Bearing** (3 Block and 1 Weak for a full
energy) was the worst card in the deck and I played it exactly twice all run —
once purely as a Slow counter, once for the Weak against a 23-damage hit.
**Stage Presence** was filler I played only when nothing else existed; twice I
played it for reasons entirely unrelated to Block. And **any turn with no
Companion card in hand** was automatic in the bad sense: Crabaletta stands on
the stage doing nothing, because members "do NOT act on their own."

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

Very little contradicted itself — this kit is unusually well-printed, and
every rule I gambled on (Encore absorbing after Block, performances not being
Attacks, Frozen priming Shatter, Slow counting cards before) resolved *exactly*
as written. Specific complaints:

- **The Aria upgrade preview omitted `Innate`.** The preview showed only the
  number change; the card I received had a keyword the preview never
  mentioned, and it was the most important keyword in my deck.
- **`Upgraded: not shown -- its upgrade rewrites the sentence` was untrue in
  spirit** for Salon Début, whose upgrade appends "Gain 2 Encore."
- **One arithmetic discrepancy I could not resolve.** End of fight 2 turn 1,
  Encore read 7; after taking A's 4 and C's 3 through 6 Block I expected 6,
  and the next screen showed **5**. One point unaccounted for. I could not
  reconstruct it from printed information and I am not confident enough to
  call it a defect — but I could not explain it either.
- **I never once saw Fanfare do anything.** It ticked 0→2→4→7→6→5, every
  screen dutifully said "cards read it and none spends it," and the Salon rule
  says "Member numbers gain +1 per 10 Fanfare you hold" — but Fanfare peaked at
  **7** and decays 20% a turn, so I never crossed the threshold that makes it
  matter. Aria+'s "if you have at least 3 Fanfare, gain 8 more" also never
  fired, because Aria is Innate and gets played on turn 1 when Fanfare is
  always 0. **An upgraded card's conditional half was structurally dead in
  every fight I played**, and the whole Fanfare axis was decorative for an
  entire act.
- **I never saw an Evoke.** Crabaletta "Evokes for 14 Hydro damage," a full
  stage Evokes the front member, and Chevalmarin/Usher Evokes are printed
  everywhere — but with a 3-slot stage and at most 2 members, I never triggered
  one. A visible, repeatedly-explained mechanic that act 1 never reaches.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

*Never wanted:* **Regal Bearing.** One energy for 3 Block and 1 Weak is worse
than Stage Presence's flat 6 in almost every spot, and the Weak only earned its
keep once all run.

*Happiest to draw:* **Salon Début** — and that is itself the finding. The card
I was most pleased to see is not a payoff, it is the *enabler*; it is the card
that switches the rest of my hand on. Drawing it turns three other cards from
vanilla into engine pieces, and not drawing it (fight 4, turns 1–2) makes the
deck a mediocre pile of 6-damage attacks. Honourable mention to **Charlotte —
Framing**, which attacks, cycles, applies Cryo, *and* performs a member for one
energy, and was lethal on its own twice.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes — one real one, plus two rituals.** Aria+ then Spotlight were forced (I
needed Encore before the Spotlight could charge me 2 for it, which is itself a
small, pleasing ordering constraint the screen makes discoverable). The actual
decision was the last two energy: **Freminet + Soloist's for 15 damage, or
Freminet + Charlotte for 9 damage and 12 points of prevention.** With a 45 HP
enemy hitting for 12 and 8 Encore already banked, I took the race, and it cost
exactly the 4 HP I predicted. That is a genuine, legible, correctly-priced
choice on turn 1 of the run.

The caveat is that it was a decision about *Block versus damage*, which is a
decision any deck has. The kit's own signature decision — the stage — could
not appear on turn 1, because Salon Début was in the draw pile and every
Companion card in my opening hand read "performs nobody."

**Summary judgement.** The kit is legible to an unusual degree — the printed
rules were sufficient to predict outcomes to the point, repeatedly, and twice
let me find a non-obvious tactical answer (dodge Shrink via performances,
dodge Tangled via Companion cards) with no hints. Its ceiling is high and
genuinely fun: 40 damage in one turn off correct ordering, and a 127 HP elite
killed for zero HP. Its floor is the problem — **the engine is entirely
draw-gated on one card.** Across five fights the stage stood empty for 5 turns,
and on those turns the deck has no decisions at all, only vanilla cards and
waiting. Fanfare and Evoke, meanwhile, were visible in every rules block and
functionally absent from the whole act.
