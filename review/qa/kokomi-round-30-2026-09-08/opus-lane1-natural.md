## Fight 1 — Sludge Spinner 37/37

**Turn 1.** Hand was Strike ×3, Defend ×2 — no Plan card, and the jellyfish
printed *"No Plan card in hand: the jellyfish waits."* Played 3 Strikes (18) and
1 Defend (5). **Rejected:** 2 Strikes + 2 Defends. That is arithmetic against a
telegraphed 8, not a decision; the turn presented none.

**Turn 2.** Drew Kurage's Oath and Slack Water. Wrote **Kurage's Oath as a Plan**
(7 to ALL next turn) and played **Slack Water** at the enemy (3 damage + 1 Weak,
and Tamakushi Casket answered the debuff for 2 — HP fell 19→14, which is exactly
3+2), then Defend. **Rejected:** playing Oath now for 3, and writing Slack
Water's Dusk Plan. Slack Water's Dusk Plan applies Weak to ALL at end of turn;
playing the card outright applies the same Weak *this* turn *and* deals damage,
so the Dusk line was strictly worse against one body. The intent line moved from
11 to 8 the moment the Weak landed — the screen restated it rather than making
me compute it.

**Turn 3.** Plan resolved for 7 (14→7). Two Strikes killed it. The kill ended
the fight, and the reward screen printed *"The Bake-Kurage's last carry-out"*
with the Plan and the HP lost under it, which is the one place that beat would
otherwise have been invisible.

**Judged across the fight:** one decision — turn 2's Plan-versus-play — and it
was a real one. Turns 1 and 3 were execution.

## Fight 2 — Corpse Slug (1) 25/25, Corpse Slug (2) 26/26

Both carried *"Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it,
becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength."* That one line shaped the whole fight.

**Turn 1.** Wrote Kurage's Oath (7 to ALL) and Ripple (1 Energy + 4 Block) as
Plans, Struck A, and double-Defended. **Rejected:** playing Oath now for 3 to
all. 7 next turn beat 3 now because neither slug could kill me through 10 block.

**Turn 2.** The interesting turn. Feint read *"deal 10 damage instead"* because a
Plan had carried out, so I put it on **B**, then Slack Water on B, then two
Strikes to kill **A exactly** (12 HP, 6+6). Order was the decision: I wanted A to
die **last**, after B had already been chipped to 3, so that B's Ravenous eat
landed on a body I had finished with and the Stun cost me its whole turn.
**Rejected:** killing A first and then spending the rest on B, which hands B a
free 4 Strength while it is still at 19 and lets it act. The board after: B at
3/26, `Intent: Stunned`, Strength 4 — exactly what the printed rule promised.

**Turn 3.** One Strike. Dead. A plan paying off, not a dead turn.

**Judged across the fight:** the decision was turn 2's ordering, made on the turn
off a printed enemy buff. Good fight.

## Fight 3 — Seapunk 44/44

## Fight 4 — Calcified Cultist 41/41, Damp Cultist 53/53

**Turn 1.** Both intents were `Empower (Buff)`; my hand was Defend ×3, Strike ×2,
no Plan card. I played two Strikes at A and ended. **There was no alternative to
reject.** Block prevents nothing against two buffs, and with no Plan card the
jellyfish had nothing to write. This is the clearest dead turn in the run, and it
was dead because the kit's engine was simply absent from the hand.

**Turn 2.** Now Ritual showed: A had *Ritual 2*, B had **Ritual 5 — At the end of
its turn, gains 5 Strength.** That is a snowball clock, so I chose damage over
Song of Pearls: Strike, Strike, Feint (5, no Plan had carried out) at A.
**Rejected:** playing Song of Pearls, whose earliest payout was two turns away in
a fight I needed to end in three; and rejected Defending, because block does not
answer a body that gains 5 Strength a turn.

**Turn 3.** A at 12 wearing Hydro Aura 1; B bare at 53. Played **Amber** first:
it Vaporized on A (6+4+4 = 14, killing it at 12) and put 12 on B in the same
card. Then wrote **Ambush** as a Plan and **Ripple** as a Plan, then **Slack
Water** at B. Slack Water is Hydro into the Pyro aura Amber had just left on B —
a second Vaporize, 6 instead of 4, then the Casket's answering 2 re-applied Hydro
in the same beat. B fell 53→33; the reaction log named both Vaporizes by card.
**Rejected:** writing Ambush while A was still front. I did write it — the
printed rule *"next turn: front non-Minion"* told me the target is chosen at
carry-out, so the Plan would land on B once Amber killed A. It did.

**Turn 4.** Ambush carried out for 12 into B; Ripple returned 1 Energy. Two
Strikes, two Defends, Ripple written again. **Rejected:** spending everything on
damage — B's intent was 11 and I was at 35.

**Turn 5.** B at 9 with Strength 15 and a 16-damage intent. Amber for 12. Dead.
**Rejected:** writing Ambush's 12 as a Plan — it would have arrived after B's 16
landed on my 35 HP.

**Judged across the fight:** three real decisions (turn 2's tempo-over-engine
call, turn 3's Amber-into-the-aura ordering, turn 5's kill-now), one wholly dead
turn.

## Fight 6 — Sewer Clam 56/56 with Block 8

*"Plating 8 — At the end of your turn, gain 8 Block."* plus my Hand Drill
(*"Whenever you break an enemy's Block, apply 2 Vulnerable"*) made this a
burn-through-the-shell fight.

**Turn 1.** No Plan card in hand at all. Amber, Strike, Feint, Defend. The Clam
fell 56→**26**: 30 damage through 8 Block. I had predicted 22 and was wrong by 8,
because Hand Drill's Vulnerable landed mid-sequence and two Vaporizes fired.
**Rejected:** holding Feint for a turn with a carried-out Plan — I had no Plan
card, so there was no such turn coming.

**Turn 2.** `Smoggy` had not yet landed; the Clam was buffing, so no incoming.
This is where the printed Plan rule decided the turn: *"A Plan is carried out
before you play anything, so it lands in whatever Block the enemy is still
standing in from its own turn — you cannot strip that Block first."* Against
Plating that makes a Plan strictly worse than the same card played now, so I
played **Ambush directly** rather than writing it, alongside Slack Water and
Strike, all at ×1.5 from a still-live Vulnerable. 26→9. **Rejected:** writing
Ambush for 12 next turn, which would have met 8 fresh Block and delivered 4.

**Turn 3.** Clam at 9 behind 7 Block, intent 14, me at 45. Struck to thin the
Block, played **Ayaka — Soumetsu** and Ripple. Ayaka's end-of-turn 8 Cryo killed
it before the enemy phase. **Rejected:** the pure-block line (Defend + Ripple,
kill next turn), which would have let a Strength-4 Clam hit me twice more.

**Judged across the fight:** the real decision was turn 2's, and it was decided
by a rules paragraph rather than by a card — the best thing the kit's screen did
all run.

**Judged across the fight:** two real decisions, both created by Smoggy forcing a
choice between writing a Plan and defending. Smoggy made this the most

## Fight 9 (Elite) — Skulking Colony 75/75

*"Hardened Shell 20 of 20 left this turn — cannot lose more than 20 HP each
turn."* That cap, not its 75 HP, is what the fight is about: it forces a minimum
of four turns and turns the question into "can you survive four turns of 14".

**Turn 1.** Shinobu — Sanctifying Ring (lose 3 HP, then 3 turns of 5 Electro AoE
and 5 Block), then Grass Ring — which read *"gain 4 additional Block if you lost
HP this turn"*, and the Ring's self-damage had just satisfied that, so it paid 8
— then Defend and two Strikes. 18 block against a 14 intent: took nothing.
**Rejected:** opening with damage. Against a damage cap, surplus damage is
deleted, so the correct opening is block.

**Turn 2.** Used **Radiant Tincture**. Played Ayaka, Strike, Defend, and wrote
Ripple as a Plan. **Rejected:** playing Amber. This is the sharpest read the cap
produced: Ayaka's 8 plus the Ring's 5 plus a Strike already came to 19 of the
allowed 20, so a 14-damage Amber would have had 13 of it thrown away. I held it.

**Turn 3.** Soumetsu was on its last turn (*"8 ... then 16 when it ends"*), so
the end-of-turn effects alone would exceed the cap. I therefore played **no
damage cards at all**: Slack Water purely for the Weak (which cut a 9 intent),
Song of Pearls, and two Defends. **Rejected:** Feint's 10 and any Strike — all of
it would have been eaten by the cap.

**Turn 4.** Colony at 14 with `Frozen 1 — an Attack Shatters it for 6
unblockable`. Strike (9 + 6 Shatter) then Kurage's Oath. Dead. **Rejected:**
Sara/Ambush — unnecessary.

**Judged across the fight:** three real decisions, all of them about *when not to
deal damage*. The damage cap is a good constraint and the screen stated it
plainly enough to play around. Cost: 7 HP.

**Judged across the fight:** turn 1's play-don't-write call was real and printed;

## Fight 11 (Boss) — Lagavulin Matriarch 222/222, Block 12

*"Asleep 3 — Awakens upon losing HP or after 3 turns"*, `Plating 12`.

**Turn 1.** Amber, Strike, Ayaka. Amber's 12 exactly consumed the 12 Block —
breaking it fired Hand Drill for 2 Vulnerable — and Strike woke it. 222→193.
**Rejected:** the stall line (block up and let Asleep run its 3 turns). With a
222-HP body and an action budget I could already see ending, tempo was worth more
than three free turns of nothing.

**Turn 2.** Slack Water for the Weak (19 → the number I actually took was 3),
Sara, then every block card I had: Grass Ring, Ripple, Defend. Soumetsu ended
this turn for 8 + 16 into a live Vulnerable. 193→**125**. **Rejected:**
Defend-only; and rejected saving Sara, since her +5 wanted to be live on the
following turn's Strike.

**Turn 3.** Fantastic Voyage 5 was up and the boss wore **Vulnerable 6**. Strike
(9+5, ×1.5 = 21), Gorou (8+5, ×1.5, plus half as Block), Kirara (8 Block now, 10
damage next turn). 125→**67**, and I took nothing. **Rejected:** Song of Pearls —
no Plan was going to carry out, so it was a dead card in this hand.

**Turn 4.** Boss at 67 with no Block and intending to Defend next. Strike,
Ambush **played not written**, Opening Gambit **played not written** — third time
the same printed rule about Plans landing in standing Block decided a turn.
67→**38**. Took 12. **Rejected:** the Opening-Gambit-plus-Ambush double, which
would have delivered ~36 into 12+ Block a turn later instead of 27 now.

**Turn 5.** 38 behind 12 Block, no attack intent. Strike, Feint, Shinobu —
Sanctifying Ring. 27 damage plus 3 turns of Electro and Block bought for 3 HP.
**Rejected:** Opening Gambit over the Ring — the Ring's Block mattered against a
21 intent.

**Turn 6.** Boss at 12 with Poison 4 ticking and me carrying `Strength −2` and
`Dexterity −2` from its debuff turn. Strike (7, ×1.5) and Ambush. Dead.

**Judged across the fight:** the boss was decided at turns 2–4, and the recurring
decision was the same one: **write or play**. Against a body that blocks on its
own turn, this kit's whole Plan engine turns off, and the printed rule tells you
so honestly enough that you can play around it. Whether "the engine is off
against half the bosses in the game" is a good design is not mine to say — but I
never once wrote a Plan against the Matriarch and I still won.

## The kit, after 11 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

1. **Write or play — made on the turn, decided by printed text.** The line
   *"A Plan is carried out before you play anything, so it lands in whatever
   Block the enemy is still standing in from its own turn — you cannot strip
   that Block first"* is the single most load-bearing sentence in the kit. It
   turned fight 6 turn 2, fight 10 turn 1 and boss turns 4–5. Against a body
   that blocks, the Plan bonus (Ambush 5→12) is worth less than the block it
   walks into, so you play the card for 5 and keep tempo. Against a body that
   attacks, you write. That is a genuine, legible, repeatable choice and it is
   the best thing here.
2. **Write order — made earlier in the fight, paid at the carry-out.** Fight 8
   turn 1: Opening Gambit before Kurage's Oath, because "the next Plan carried
   out with this one deals double damage" makes sequence the whole play. 7
   became 21. The cost was a turn of no damage.
3. **Which body dies last — made on the turn.** Fight 2, against Ravenous. The
   trade was 5 damage of overkill for a full stunned enemy turn.
4. **When not to deal damage — made on the turn.** The elite's 20-per-turn cap
   made "hold Amber" the correct play twice. Trade: a slower fight for zero
   wasted cards.
5. **Amber, taken at the draft.** One off-element card in a mono-Hydro deck
   generated every Vaporize in the run, including the 18-from-a-12 Ambush. The
   game itself told me the choice existed by printing
   *"NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE: Hydro is the only element this screen can
   supply"* on the first battle screen — an unusually honest thing for a screen
   to say, and it made the draft pick obvious in a good way.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

Strike and Defend, always. More seriously: **any turn with no Plan card in hand
is an automatic turn**, because the jellyfish is the only thing in the kit that
takes a decision. Fight 4 turn 1 (Defend ×3, Strike ×2 against two buffing
bodies) had *no* alternative to reject at all, and fight 6 turn 1 and fight 1
turn 1 were nearly as empty. That happened in 3 of 11 fights' opening turns.

Never worth playing: **Feint without a Plan carried out**. A 1-cost attack for 5
in a deck where Strike costs 1 for 6 (later 9) is strictly a worse Strike, and I
played it at 5 twice out of an empty hand and regretted it both times. Also
**Kurage's Oath's direct half** (3 damage to ALL) — I played it once, to finish a
14-HP elite, and otherwise its front face is a card you never want; only its
Plan line is real. **Slack Water's Dusk Plan** I rejected on all three occasions
it was available: playing the card outright applies the same Weak *sooner* and
adds damage, so I never found a board where writing it was right.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

- **Kamisato Ayaka — Soumetsu.** The card reads "At the end of your turn, deal 8
  Cryo damage to ALL enemies. After 2 turns, deal 16 Cryo damage to ALL
  enemies." The buff it makes reads "deal 8 Cryo damage to ALL enemies, then 16
  when it ends. Lasts for 1 turn." I played it three times and never once knew,
  before ending the turn, what number was about to land. Against a damage cap
  that is a real problem.
- **"Vaporize on Sewer Clam, off Bake-Kurage"** in fight 6, a fight in which I
  wrote **no Plan at all**. The hit was, I believe, the Tamakushi Casket's
  answering strike after Hand Drill applied Vulnerable — but the log attributed
  it to the jellyfish, which had done nothing. The source name is wrong or at
  best deeply misleading.
- **Kujou Sara — Tengu Stormcall** prints "Next turn, your Attacks deal 5
  additional damage"; the power it creates is called **Fantastic Voyage**. Two
  names for one thing, and neither screen connects them.
- **Sewer Clam's Plating.** *"Plating 8 — at the end of your turn, gain 8 Block.
  Plating is reduced by 1 at the start of your turn"* — and its Block line read 8,
  then 7. I could never tell whether a Plan next turn would meet 7, or 7 on top
  of what was left. I guessed and avoided Plans entirely.
- **Amber's reaction preview appears only when an aura is present.** With an aura
  it prints "this card's 4 lands 6"; with none it prints nothing, so the fact
  that hits 2 and 3 also land at 4 (because hit 1 consumed the aura) is something
  you learn by arithmetic after the fact, not from the card.
- **"Surprise 1 (buff) — Something is off about this creature..."** told me
  nothing, and the Gremlin Merc split into two fresh bodies on death. I do not
  mind a secret; I mind that the line pretends to inform.
- **The reward screen refused `skip`** with "there is nothing here to skip",
  while the card screen above it printed "You may skip this." `proceed` worked.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Feint** on a turn with no carried-out Plan. Happiest: **Ambush**.
It is a 1-cost that reads 5 and delivers 12 written, 18 through a Vaporize, or
24 doubled by Opening Gambit — and the carry-out log tells you which of those
happened and why. Second place: **Amber — Fiery Rain**, which is the card that
makes the elemental half of the kit exist at all.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**No.** The hand was Strike ×3 and Defend ×2 against a single 37-HP body
telegraphing 8 damage. There was no Plan card, and the jellyfish printed
*"No Plan card in hand: the jellyfish waits."* The only question was three
Strikes and one Defend versus two and two, which is arithmetic against a stated
number. The kit's entire mechanism was absent from its own opening turn, and the
first decision of the run did not arrive until round 2.
