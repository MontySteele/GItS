## Fight 1 — Toadpole (1) [A] 21 HP, Toadpole (2) [B] 24 HP

Opening hand was **Strike ×3, Defend ×2** — the unmodified basic deck. The Bake-Kurage panel was up and said in as many words: *"No Plan card in hand: the jellyfish waits."*

- **Turn 1:** three Strikes into A (the one telegraphing `Empower`). Rejected: 2 Strikes + Defend, which would have eaten the 7-damage attack for 2 net. I took the 7 because the third Strike was the Kusarigama trigger and the relic's 6 went into B. **This was a decision, but only because of the relic** — with no Kusarigama the third Strike vs a Defend is the whole choice, and it's a thin one.
- **Turn 2:** hand finally showed the kit — Slack Water, Kurage's Oath. A sat at 3 HP with **Thorns 2** and B at 18. I played **Kurage's Oath first** (3 to ALL) specifically because it killed A for exactly lethal and spent none of the 3 overkill a Strike would have wasted, then Strike + Slack Water into B. Rejected: Strike-kills-A-then-Oath, which does the same 15 to B minus 3 wasted; and rejected writing Slack Water's Plan on the Kurage (1 Weak to ALL next turn) because A was dying this turn and B's next attack was a buff. **Real decision, made on the turn, about overkill routing.**
- **Turn 3:** B at 3 with Thorns; one Strike, take 2 thorns, done. No alternative worth the words.

Verdict: two of three turns presented a choice; the choice on turn 2 was about *ordering* AoE before single-target, which is a kit-shaped decision (Oath is a 1-cost skill that hits ALL).

## Fight 2 — Corpse Slug (1) [A] 25 HP, Corpse Slug (2) [B] 27 HP

Both carried **Ravenous 4** — *"When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it, becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength."* That printed line is the whole fight.

- **Turn 1:** Slack Water into **B** (the 8-damage body, not the 3x2 one) to hang Weak where it removed the most damage, then Riptide for 9 to both, +4 to the debuffed one. Rejected: Weak on A (Weak on 3x2 saves ~1.5, on the 8 it saves 2, and Riptide's +4 lands the same either way), and rejected Riptide-then-Slack-Water, which loses the +4 entirely. **Decision on the turn: which body wears the debuff, priced against a card that pays for debuffs.**
- **Turn 2:** the printed Ravenous line said killing one *stuns* the other. So I deliberately killed B with two Strikes and put the third into A — 3 attacks for the Kusarigama 6, A left at 4 and **Stunned**, and I took zero. Rejected: Oath + Strike to kill B (leaves A at 7 with no Kusarigama), and rejected killing nothing to avoid feeding +4 Strength — which is the trap the card text baits, since the Stun is worth more than the Strength when the eater is nearly dead. **The best turn of the run, and the decision was made off enemy text, not my own cards.**
- **Turn 3:** Feint into a stunned 4-HP body. Automatic; it is the plan from turn 2 paying off.

## The kit, after 9 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

1. **Aura ordering — on the turn (fight 4, 8, 9).** Playing a Hydro card and *then* Shinobu's Electro is worth roughly double the poison of the reverse, because Tamakushi Casket's answering 2-damage Hydro hit re-reacts with the fresh Electro aura. Trade-off: the Hydro card is usually my weakest attack, so the "correct" order spends a worse card first while the enemy is at full strength. This is the kit's best decision and it lives entirely in two printed paragraphs.
2. **Write the Plan or play the card — on the turn (fight 3, turn 2).** Under Weak, every card's immediate number was redrawn smaller on its face while its Plan line stayed full-size. Writing Riptide as a Plan converted a 6 into a 13 at the cost of a turn. Trade-off: tempo, and the enemy keeps whatever Block it's standing in, which the panel states plainly.
3. **Which body wears the debuff — on the turn (fight 2, 6).** Slack Water's Weak, Riptide's "+4 to each enemy with a debuff" and the casket's 2 all key off the same application, so one card's target choice moves three numbers.
4. **Kill order against printed enemy powers — on the turn (fight 2's Ravenous, fight 7's Suck).** Feeding the Corpse Slug a corpse to *stun* it, and blocking to zero so Suck never ticks, were both decisions read off the enemy panel rather than my hand.
5. **At the draft: taking Shinobu — Thundergrust over Deep Current on floor 4.** That single Electro card converted an all-Hydro deck (whose own screens kept telling me `NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE`) into a poison engine that killed a 140-HP elite. The whole run's shape came from one card-reward pick, and both later Shinobu appearances were takes I made for the same reason.
6. **Potions as tempo — on the turn (Poison Potion in the elite, Vulnerable Potion vs the Ritual cultist).** Both were "spend it now or lose to compounding", and both were correct.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Three Strikes into the front body was automatic all run** — and it was frequently my *best* turn, because Kusarigama pays 6 for the third Attack. That's a relic making a boring line optimal, and it crowded out the Plan system: a turn spent writing a Plan is a turn that does not reach three Attacks.
- **Defend** was never a decision, just arithmetic against the intent number.
- **Kurage's Oath** at 3 damage to ALL was close to worthless in single-target fights, which was most of them; its Plan line (7 to ALL) I never once had a free beat to write.
- **Breakwater never justified its slot.** In two plays it produced 5 Block — exactly a Defend — because its scaling clause ("plus 3 for each Plan carried out this turn") requires a Plan-dense turn I was never able to build. It is a card that is only good in a deck I never got.
- **Read the Field was a trap twice** in the same specific way: it costs 1, and the card it finds costs energy I have already spent. Both times I paid a full energy to look at three cards I could not use.
- **Feint** almost never got its "if a Plan was carried out this turn" condition, so it was a 5-damage attack for 1 in a deck with 6-damage Strikes.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

- **Breakwater's Dusk timing is printed three different ways** (card: "end of this turn"; my status bar: "at the start of your next turn"; the fight-over summary: "carried these out at the start of this turn"). The behaviour appeared to match the card, but no two surfaces agree.
- **Poison arrived at 8 when Electro-Charged prints 4,** repeatedly, and the panel never showed the intermediate step. The reaction paragraph does pre-warn that a relic can consume-and-reapply "inside the same beat" so the reaction "looks as though it did not happen" — but the player has to reconstruct a double proc from a number that is simply twice what the keyword says. I only trusted my reading because it reproduced four times.
- **Two Electro cards in a row add nothing,** because the second hits its own aura. This is derivable from the aura rules and is stated nowhere on the cards, and it silently halves the value of drawing two Shinobus.
- **The Haunted Ship's `5 Status cards` intent never resolved.** Disclaimed by the page, but it still made me plan for clog that never came.
- **Steam Eruption's growth is legible but its interaction with a Poison kill is not.** I could see 33; I could not tell from any screen whether killing the boss with poison at the *start of its turn* would end the fight or hand it a Death Blow phase. That single unknown is what killed the run.
- **Hand renumbering under Snecko Oil** is documented in the page's own italics and still caught me twice; the cost annotations ("The cost printed on this card is 1; it is showing 0 here") are excellent, but the index I read and the index I typed were one screen apart.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted:** Kurage's Oath — 1 energy for 3 damage to a single body, which was the board in seven of nine fights. Breakwater is a close second for producing exactly a Defend.
- **Happiest to draw:** **Shinobu — Thundergrust.** 13 damage below half HP, and it was the only key I had into Elemental Reactions in a deck the game kept telling me could not react. Runner-up: **Riptide**, whose "+4 more to each enemy with a debuff" quietly rewarded everything else the kit does.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Barely, and not from the kit.** The opening hand was Strike ×3 / Defend ×2 — no Plan card, and the Bake-Kurage panel printed *"No Plan card in hand: the jellyfish waits."* The only question was three Strikes versus two Strikes and a Defend, and that question existed only because Neow's relic paid for the third Attack. The kit's own first decision did not arrive until **round 2 of fight 1**, when Slack Water and Kurage's Oath finally showed up and the Kurage became addressable.
