## Fight 1 — Corpse Slug (1) 26/26, Corpse Slug (2) 27/27

Both wore `Ravenous 4 — When an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it, becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength.` Won on round 3, 62 → 48.

**Round 1** (3 energy; hand Strike ×3, Defend, Ammo Scavenging; intents 3×2 and 8, so 14 incoming).
Played **Ammo Scavenging on Slug (2)** → Bomb 4, then **Strike ×2 into Slug (1)** (26 → 14). No block.

*Rejected:* three Strikes for 18 (would not have killed either slug — 26 and 27 — so the 18 bought nothing a bomb doesn't), and Defend-plus-two-Strikes (5 block against 14 incoming is 5 HP for 6 damage forgone; with a 26 HP target I wanted the tempo). Also rejected putting the Bomb on Slug (1), the one I was killing — though the Bomb keyword promised "Kills move it on", I did not trust a rule I had not seen fire, so I put it on the survivor. That was the safe read and it cost me nothing.

Two things the screen told me correctly here. Ammo Scavenging prints "Draw 1 card for each of your Bombs that went off this turn" and it drew me nothing, because none had — the card did exactly what it said, and I want to note that a card whose second clause is dead on the turn you'd naturally first play it reads, at that moment, like a card that does nothing but place a bomb.

**Round 2** (48 HP after taking the full 14; hand Defend ×2, Jumpy Dumpty, Run Away!, Strike; Slug (1) at 14 intending 8, Slug (2) intending a debuff).
Played **Jumpy Dumpty on Slug (1)** (Bomb 8, "When it goes off, place a Mine 3 on ALL enemies"), **Strike on Slug (1)** (14 → 8), **Run Away!** (3 block — its "If a Bomb went off this turn, gain 4 additional Block" was dead, nothing had gone off), **Defend** (5). 8 block against 8 incoming, exactly.

*Rejected:* holding Jumpy Dumpty to detonate a bigger bomb later — but nothing in this kit retains a skill, so "holding" a card means discarding it, and the choice was really *play it now or throw it away*. That is a decision the deck makes for you, not one you make. I also rejected Defend ×2 + Strike (10 block for 8 incoming is 2 wasted block; I preferred to buy the bomb).

The bomb I had placed on round 1 read **Bomb 8** on this screen. It had grown 4, exactly as the keyword said.

**Round 3** — the payoff, and the first turn of this fight that felt like a puzzle.
Screen: both slugs held **Bomb 12**; Slug (1) was at 8 HP; Slug (2) at 27 with Bomb 12. Hand: **Ka-pow!** (cost 0, Retain, Set off, 4 damage — my first detonator, seen on round 3 of the run), **Stoke the Fuse** (cost 1 Spark, "Spend all your Sparks. Your largest Bomb grows by 3 per Spark spent"), Defend, **Countdown** (Set off. Draw 1 card), Catalytic Converter.

Played **Ka-pow! on Slug (1)** — the 12 killed it outright, and the printed consequences all landed at once: Jumpy Dumpty's rider put a Mine 3 on the survivor, `Ravenous` ate the corpse so Slug (2) went Stunned with Strength 4, and Pounding Surprise paid me a Spark. The survivor's line then read `Bombs here: 12 / 3, including 1 Mine`, which is the clearest single line the bridge printed all run — it told me the parts, the total, and which part was a Mine.

Then **Stoke the Fuse** (2 Sparks → largest Bomb 12 becomes 18) and **Countdown on Slug (2)** → 18 + 3 = **21**, and the screen went 27 → 6. Then **Strike** for the kill.

*Rejected:* Ka-pow on Slug (2) first (it would have spent a 12 bomb on a 27 HP body while an 8 HP body sat next to it); and holding the whole turn to let both bombs grow another 4 — Slug (2) was about to be Stunned, so a wasted turn was cheap, but growing 12 → 16 and paying a whole turn for +4 was worse than spending 2 Sparks on +6 immediately. I also rejected playing **Catalytic Converter**: the screen said in capitals `NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE: Pyro is the only element this screen can supply`, and Catalytic Converter only pays on a reaction. I want to record that this is the first card in the run I was told, by the game, not to play.

This was the first turn of the run with a genuine ordering problem: Stoke must come before the detonator, because after the detonator there is no bomb left to grow. Nothing on either card says so; you have to work it out. I liked working it out.

## Fight 2 — Sludge Spinner 38/38

Won on round 2, 48 → 40.

**Round 1** (hand Perfect Timing, Defend, Strike, Jumpy Dumpty, Ka-pow!; the Spinner telegraphed 8 *and* a debuff, and the page warned "read such a number as a part the enemy MAY perform").
Played **Jumpy Dumpty** (Bomb 8) then **Perfect Timing** (Set off → the 8 fired, Mine 3 landed, then 8 damage; 38 → 22, exactly as printed), then **Strike** (22 → 16).

*Rejected:* holding Perfect Timing so the bomb could grow. Same non-choice as before — Perfect Timing did not retain yet, so holding meant discarding. I also rejected spending my last energy on Defend instead of Strike: the page had told me the damage half of a multi-part telegraph often does not land, so I bought damage instead of block. **The page was wrong: the 8 landed, plus Weak.** That is the first place the screen and the outcome disagreed.

*Rejected, and this is the interesting one:* playing Ka-pow! after Perfect Timing to fire the fresh Mine 3 for 3 + 4. I kept it instead, because the Mine's own keyword says it "also goes off before its enemy's hit" — so its 3 was coming to me for free whether or not I spent a card on it, and Ka-pow Retains. That read was right: round 2 opened with the Spinner at 13, having eaten Strike 6 + Mine 3.

**Round 2** (40 HP, Weak 1 on me, Spinner 13 HP, 3 energy, 3 Sparks; hand Ka-pow!, Ammo Scavenging, Stoke the Fuse, Strike, Catalytic Converter, Run Away!).
Played **Ammo Scavenging** (Bomb 4) → **Stoke the Fuse** (3 Sparks → Bomb **13**) → **Ka-pow!** for exactly lethal. One energy spent on the whole kill.

*Rejected:* Strike + Strike + anything (12 damage, one short of 13, and Weak had cut Strike from 6 to 4 on the printed face). Rejected Catalytic Converter again, for the same printed reason.

Two legibility notes from this turn. First, **Weak rewrote the card faces** — Strike printed "Deal 4 damage" and Ka-pow "Deal 3 damage" — so I never had to do the multiplication. Second, **Weak did not touch the Bomb**: the 13 bomb dealt 13. The Bomb keyword says "Not an Attack", and the numbers honoured it. Both of those are good.

## Fight 3 — Toadpole (1) 25/25, Toadpole (2) 21/21

Won on round 2, 40 → 36. This was the best fight of the round.

**Round 1** (hand Stoke the Fuse, Jumpy Dumpty, Perfect Timing *(Steady)*, Catalytic Converter, Run Away!).
Played **Jumpy Dumpty on Toadpole (1)** (Bomb 8) → **Stoke the Fuse** (1 Spark → Bomb 11) → **Catalytic Converter** → **Run Away!** (3 block). Retained Perfect Timing.

*Rejected:* detonating anything. Perfect Timing now carries **Steady/Retain** (bought at the Scriptorium, see below), which for the first time in the run made "hold the detonator and let the bomb grow" an actual option rather than a fiction. That single enchantment changed how the kit plays more than any card I drafted. I also stopped rejecting Catalytic Converter here — I had Barbara in the deck by then, so a second element was reachable, and I had spare energy.

**Round 2** — Toadpole (1) at 25 with **Bomb 15** and Thorns 2; Toadpole (2) at 21. Hand: Perfect Timing *(Steady)*, Countdown, Defend ×2, Ka-pow!, **Barbara — Front Row Seat** (Gain 5 Block. Apply Hydro twice. Whenever a Bomb goes off this turn, gain 3 Block).

Played **Barbara on Toadpole (1)**. The moment it resolved, Perfect Timing and Ka-pow both grew a new line on their faces: `Reaction preview: Vaporize — Pyro meets Hydro: this hit deals 1.5x damage and consumes the aura.` I did not have to remember the reaction table; the cards told me.

Then **Ka-pow!** (0 energy) into the Hydro: the 15 bomb hit as Pyro on Hydro → 22, Ka-pow's own 4 finished the 25, `Ravenous` moved the mines onto the survivor, and the survivor's line read `Bombs here: 3 / 3, including 2 Mines`. Then **Perfect Timing on Toadpole (2)**: Set off both mines (6), 8 damage, and its rider — "If a Bomb triggered an Elemental Reaction this turn, play this again" — fired and gave me another 8. 6 + 8 + 8 = 22 against 21 HP. Fight over on turn 2 for two cards and one energy.

*Rejected:* leading with Perfect Timing instead of Ka-pow. Both would have Vaporized the 15, but Perfect Timing costs 1 and Ka-pow costs 0, and I wanted Perfect Timing alive *after* the reaction so its replay clause would be live. That ordering is the whole turn, and again nothing prints it.

*Rejected:* Diona was not in hand yet; if she had been I would have used Cryo (Melt, 1.75x) over Barbara's Hydro (Vaporize, 1.5x) on the big bomb.

## Fight 4 — Haunted Ship 63/63

Won on round 5, 36 → 20. The longest fight and the one where the kit's shape showed.

**Round 1** (hand Catalytic Converter, Stoke the Fuse, Strike, Defend, Diona; ship telegraphing a Debuff *and* 5 Status cards).
Played **Catalytic Converter**, **Strike** (63 → 57), **Diona on the ship** (Cryo aura, 6 block).

*Rejected:* Defend over Diona — Diona's 6 beat Defend's 5 and carried an aura besides. *Rejected:* Stoke the Fuse, because there was no Bomb on the board for it to grow, and it was discarded unplayed. **I had no bomb-placer and no detonator in this opening hand at all.** That is the finding: the hand was Catalytic Converter (needs a reaction), Stoke the Fuse (needs a bomb), Strike, Defend, Diona — three of five cards were conditional and two of those conditions were unreachable.

The debuff landed as Weak 3, and **the 5 Status cards landed too**, despite the "MAY perform" hedge. I only worked that out by counting piles: 8 draw + 5 hand + 9 discard = 22 against a 17-card live deck.

**Round 2** (28 HP; hand Ammo Scavenging, Defend, Strike ×3, all Strikes reading 4 under Weak; ship at 57 intending 13; **Cryo aura down to 1 turn and no detonator in hand**).
Played **Ammo Scavenging** (Bomb 4), **Defend**, **Strike**. The Cryo aura I had bought on round 1 expired unused — that is 1 energy and a card spent on nothing, because the aura's 2 turns and the deck's willingness to show me a detonator are unrelated clocks.

*Rejected:* three Strikes for 12 and eating the full 13. At 28 HP against a 57 HP body I could not race.

**Round 3** (20 HP; hand Jumpy Dumpty, Run Away!, Defend, Barbara, Ka-pow!; ship at 53 with my Bomb 8, intending 4×3).
Played **Jumpy Dumpty** (a second Bomb 8), **Barbara** (Hydro, 5 block), **Defend** (5), **Run Away!** (3) — 13 block against 12 incoming, took nothing — and **retained Ka-pow!**.

*Rejected, and this was the round's real decision:* firing now. Ka-pow into a Hydro'd Bomb 8 + Bomb 8 was 12 + 8 + 3 = 23 immediately. Holding meant two Bomb 12s next turn under a Hydro aura that lasts 2 turns, i.e. 18 + 12, and a free 0-cost detonator waiting in hand so that *all three* of next turn's energy could go to more damage or more block. I counted 23-now against 33-later, and took later. Deciding that is exactly the kind of turn I want from a kit.

**Round 4** — ship 53, `Bomb 24 (Bombs here: 12 / 12)`, Hydro Aura 1. Hand: Ka-pow!, Defend, Perfect Timing *(Steady)*, Countdown, **Dazed** (`CANNOT BE PLAYED: has unplayable keyword`), Strike.
Played **Perfect Timing** first: 12 Vaporized to 18, the second 12 for 12, its own 6 (Weak), then the replay clause fired off the reaction and gave me a Mine-hit plus another 6 — **45 damage, 53 → 8, from one card.** I had predicted 45 before pressing it and the screen paid 45. That is the strongest thing I can say about this kit's legibility: by fight four I could compute the payoff turn to the point before playing it.

Then Ka-pow (3), Strike (4), Defend (5). The ship survived on **1 HP** — I was one point short of ending it, and had to take a 13 to the face for it.

*Rejected:* opening with Ka-pow instead of Perfect Timing (identical bomb damage, but the attached 6 and the replay would have been wasted on an empty board); and Countdown, whose only body here was "draw 1 card" with no energy left to play what it drew.

**Round 5** — killed the 1 HP ship with a Strike.

The Dazed cards did **not** follow me out of the fight; the next combat's draw pile was 14 against a 19-card deck. But the map screen between the two said `Dazed × 5` was in my deck. See section (c).

## Fight 5 (Elite) — Skulking Colony 75/75, `Hardened Shell 20 — cannot lose more than 20 HP each turn`

Won on round 6, 38 → 17. I went in at 38/62 having rested, and chose the Elite over a plain Monster because I had 39 actions left and wanted the kit tested under pressure.

This enemy is a printed answer to the kit. Everything I had learned — grow a bomb, aura it, fire one enormous multiplied hit — is worth exactly 20 against a 20-per-turn cap. The Bomb keyword had been telling me this since the Neow screen ("only Vulnerable and **a cap** move it") and I did not understand the word "cap" until I met one.

**Round 1** (hand Strike ×3, Countdown, Defend; 14 incoming). Played Strike, Strike, Defend. *Rejected:* three Strikes for 18 — nearly the cap, but 14 unblocked at 38 HP against a 75 HP body I could not out-race was the wrong trade. *Rejected:* Countdown, which with no bombs on the board is a 1-energy "draw 1 card".

**Round 2** (29 HP; hand Defend, Perfect Timing *(Steady)*, Ammo Scavenging, Ka-pow!, Strike). Played **Ammo Scavenging** (Bomb 4), Strike, Defend, and **retained both Ka-pow! and Perfect Timing**. 6 damage in a turn, deliberately.

*Rejected:* Ammo Scavenging + Ka-pow for an immediate 8. Under a per-turn cap the correct play is to source your 20 from the cards that cost nothing — bombs, Ka-pow at 0, Fwoosh at 0 energy — and spend all three energy on block. Spending Ka-pow on an 8 today to avoid a 12 tomorrow is backwards. The cap turned the kit from a burst deck into a *free-damage* deck, and I thought that was a genuinely good fight.

**Round 3** (20 HP; hand Perfect Timing, Ka-pow!, **Diona**, Fwoosh!, Defend, Run Away!, Jumpy Dumpty; colony 57 with my Bomb 8).
Played **Diona** (Cryo — Melt is 1.75x, better than Barbara's 1.5x) → **Jumpy Dumpty** (second Bomb 8) → **Ka-pow!**: 8 Melted to 14, plus 8, plus 4 = 26, **delivered as 20**, and the shell line on screen changed to `Hardened Shell 0`. Then **Run Away!** (3 + 4, its bomb clause finally live) and **Defend**: 11 + 7 + 5 = 23 block against 9. Took nothing.

*Rejected:* Fwoosh! and Perfect Timing, both of which would have been pure waste once `Hardened Shell 0` was on the screen. The shell counting down in public is the single best piece of UI in the whole round — it converted "have I hit the cap?" from arithmetic into reading.

*Rejected:* skipping Jumpy Dumpty to save it. Same non-choice: it does not retain.

**Round 4** (20 HP; colony 34 with Strength 2, intending 9×2; hand Perfect Timing, Defend, Catalytic Converter, Stoke the Fuse, Barbara, Ammo Scavenging; 5 Sparks).
Played **Ammo Scavenging** (Bomb 4) → **Stoke the Fuse** (5 Sparks → Bomb **19**) → **Barbara** → **Perfect Timing**: 34 → 14, capped at 20 again. Then drank **Heart of Iron** (7 Plating).

*Rejected:* Defend instead of Barbara for the last energy. Barbara gives 5 block plus 3 per bomb that goes off, so with one bomb she is 8 against Defend's 5 — and here Barbara's own face told me the rest: `Reaction preview: Vaporize — This card deals no damage. Pyro plus Hydro is still consumed, and there is no hit here for the 1.5x to multiply.` The card volunteered that its aura would be spent for nothing if I mis-sequenced it. I have not seen a card warn me off its own combo before and it is the best line of text in this kit.

**Round 5** (17 HP; colony 14, intending 16; hand Strike ×2, Defend ×2, Countdown; Plating 5).
Played **Strike, Defend, Defend** — 6 damage, 10 block + 5 Plating = 15 against 16.

*Rejected, with arithmetic:* Countdown-then-two-Strikes, hoping to draw a free attack (Ka-pow or Fwoosh were both live in an 8-card draw pile) and kill on the spot. I put that at about one in four, and priced the two branches at 17 HP if it hit and 7 HP if it missed, against a flat 17 HP for blocking out and killing next turn off a colony at 8. I took the flat line. *Rejected also:* Strike + Strike + Defend (colony to 2, me to 12) — 5 HP for a marginally safer kill next turn.

**Round 6** — colony at 8; **Ka-pow! (0 energy) + Fwoosh! (0 energy, 1 Spark)** = 10, killed it without spending a single energy. That is the sentence I would use to describe what this kit is for.

## The kit, after 5 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

Four kinds, and three of them are good.

1. **Fire now or grow.** A Bomb grows 4 a turn and does nothing until a Set off card arrives, so every turn asks whether this turn's 23 beats next turn's 33. Fight 4 round 3 is the clean example: I counted both and took the later number. This is the kit's spine and it is a real, arithmetic, readable choice — but it is only a choice **when a detonator retains**. Before I bought Steady, "hold it" meant "discard it", and the decision was fake. See (b).
2. **Ordering inside the payoff turn.** Stoke before the detonator, aura before the bomb, the 0-cost detonator before the one with a replay rider. Nothing on any card prints the order; you deduce it, and getting it right is worth ten to twenty damage. Fight 3 round 2 and fight 4 round 4 both turned entirely on sequencing and both felt like solving something.
3. **Which aura to spend.** Barbara's Hydro is 1.5x, Diona's Cryo is 1.75x, and both also give block, so the choice is "which multiplier on which bomb, and do I need the block this turn". Good tension, and the reaction-preview line on the detonator's face makes it readable without memorisation.
4. **Spark against energy.** Stoke the Fuse spends *all* Sparks; Fwoosh! and Powder Charge each spend one. They compete for the same pool, and the pool is fed by bombs going off — so spending Sparks to make a bomb bigger costs you the Sparks that would have paid for the card that sets it off. I never had to resolve that hard because I only ever held one spark-priced card at a time, but I could see the shape of it.

The Hardened Shell elite deserves its own line. A per-turn damage cap inverts the whole kit — the right play stops being "one enormous hit" and becomes "source your 20 from the zero-cost cards and put all three energy into block". That the same deck has a second, coherent mode under a cap is the most interesting thing I found.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

*Automatic:* every turn where I held a bomb-placer and no detonator, or a detonator and no bomb. There is no decision in those turns — you place the bomb, you play your block, you end. Fight 4 rounds 1 and 2 were both like that, and round 1 was worse than automatic: three of the five cards in hand (Catalytic Converter, Stoke the Fuse, and the aura half of Diona) were conditional on things that were not on the board.

*The bigger structural point:* **almost nothing in this deck retains, so "hold it for the payoff turn" is usually not available.** Jumpy Dumpty, Ammo Scavenging, Stoke the Fuse, Barbara, Diona, Perfect Timing-before-I-enchanted-it all discard at end of turn. So the choice the kit constantly seems to be offering — bank it, or spend it — is mostly an illusion; the deck decides, and you play what you drew. The 55 gold I spent on Steady for Perfect Timing bought more decision-space than any card I drafted, which is a strange thing to be true of an event.

*Never worth playing:* **Catalytic Converter** in any mono-element fight, and the screen said so itself in capitals (`NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE`). Across five fights it produced exactly one extra Spark. A 1-energy power that is dead unless you happen to hold one of two Companion cards is a card I would cut. **Countdown** was close behind — with no bomb on the board it is "pay 1, draw 1", and I declined it four separate times.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

- **The "MAY perform" hedge was wrong both times it mattered.** The Sludge Spinner's telegraphed 8 landed in full. The Haunted Ship's "5 Status cards" landed too — and I only discovered that by adding my pile counts and finding five cards more than my deck holds. The page says "on this build the damage part of a multi-part telegraph has repeatedly not landed"; on this run it repeatedly did, and I under-blocked because of the warning.
- **The map screen's deck list is not my deck.** After the Haunted Ship it listed `Dazed × 5`, which are combat-only status cards and were gone by the next fight. Earlier, after I played Catalytic Converter as a Power, the same list dropped Catalytic Converter entirely. Both directions of error, in the one place I go to plan a draft.
- **The enchant confirmation reads as two cards.** After I picked Perfect Timing at the Scriptorium, the "What you have picked" block printed **Perfect Timing (1)** and **Perfect Timing (2)** and marked *both* `PICKED`, while the list above it held only one Perfect Timing. It is a before/after preview, but I paid for one enchantment and the screen showed me two rows; I had to reason my way to that.
- **Stoke the Fuse's cost line is misleading in a specific way.** It prints "cost 1 Spark" and then, in its body, "Spend all your Sparks". The 1 is a price of admission, not the price. It cost me 5 Sparks in the elite and I only knew that because I had read the body twice.
- **Bomb, Set off and Mine are three keywords for what is nearly one idea,** and the Mine's difference — it goes off by itself before its owner's hit — is buried in the middle of a sentence. I did not trust it until I watched a Mine detonate on its own in fight 2, and I had already spent a card considering whether to trigger it manually.
- **"a cap" in the Bomb keyword** meant nothing to me for five fights, then met Hardened Shell and turned out to be load-bearing. It reads like boilerplate until it isn't.

What was *not* confusing, and should be said: card faces rewrite themselves under Weak (Strike printed 4, not 6), Frail (Defend printed 3, not 5), and reachable reactions (`Reaction preview: Vaporize`). The multi-bomb line prints its parts (`Bombs here: 12 / 3, including 1 Mine`). `Hardened Shell` counts down to 0 as you spend it. By fight 4 I could predict a 45-damage turn to the point before pressing it. That is a high standard and this kit mostly meets it.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

*Never wanted:* **Catalytic Converter**. One Spark in five fights, dead by printed admission in three of them, and it competes for the same energy as a block card on the turns you are trying to survive.

*Happiest to draw:* **Perfect Timing**, once it carried Steady. It is a detonator that retains, so it turns "grow the bomb" from a hope into a plan; its replay clause pays you for having built the reaction; and it produced the single best moment of the round, a 45-damage turn I had computed in advance and watched land exactly. Runner-up, unglamorously: **Ka-pow!** — 0 energy, Retain, and it is the card that makes the whole engine free.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

Yes, though a shallower one than it looked. The hand was Strike ×3, Defend, Ammo Scavenging against two slugs whose combined telegraph was 14 into my 62. The choice was real — spend the energy on a Bomb 4 that would do nothing for two turns, or on 18 flat damage, or on block — and I chose the bomb over the damage on the reasoning that 18 killed neither 26 nor 27 HP body, so the tempo bought nothing.

But I want to be honest about how much of that decision I was actually equipped to make. **I placed a bomb without knowing whether my deck contained a single card that could set one off.** The Bomb keyword says bombs "go off only when Set off"; nothing in my opening hand, and nothing in the bundle I had picked at Neow, could Set off. I made the bomb play on the assumption that a kit built on a keyword must ship a card for it, and I did not see Ka-pow! until round 3. If that assumption had been wrong I would have spent the first two turns of the run gift-wrapping nothing. A first turn that offers a decision you cannot yet price is only half a decision.
