## Fight 1 — Toadpole (1) [A] 21 HP / Toadpole (2) [B] 24 HP

**Turn 1.** Played Jumpy Dumpty on A (Bomb 8), then Defend, Defend (10 block vs B's printed 7). Rejected: playing Ka-pow! immediately for 8 + 4. Rejected because Ka-pow! prints *Retain* and costs 0 — waiting costs literally nothing and the Bomb line prints "grows 4 a turn", so the same card was worth 4 more next turn. That is a real decision and the card text alone settles it, which is good.

**Turn 2.** Bomb had grown to 12 exactly as printed. Played Ka-pow! on A: 12 Pyro + 4 = A 21→5, Mine 3 landed on both bodies, Spark went 1→2 (Pounding Surprise), and I took 2 from A's new Thorns 2. Then Strike into A for the kill, then Strike, Strike into B. Rejected: holding again for a 16-point bomb. Rejected because the payload was already lethal-adjacent and the Mine 3 the detonation drops on **ALL** enemies then grows 4 a turn on **each** body — detonating early with two enemies up compounds faster than one bomb growing alone. That was the draft-level idea of the kit clicking, on turn 2 of my first fight.

**Turn 3.** B at 12 with "Mine 14 … in 2 hits", intent 3×2. I played nothing and ended the turn: the Mine goes off *just before its enemy's hit*, 14 > 12, so B killed itself and I took zero. The alternative — spending three Strikes into it — was rejected because it was strictly worse (Thorns 2 per hit for damage the mines were already going to deal). **This was the best turn of the run and it was a turn where I played no cards**, which is a strange and good thing for a kit to be able to produce.

Judged across the fight: two real decisions (when to detonate; reading the mine as lethal), both legible off printed text.

## Fight 2 — Sludge Spinner [A] 39 HP

**Turn 1.** Jumpy Dumpty on A, then Strike, Strike (12). Rejected: Jumpy + two Defends. Rejected because the enemy printed only 8 damage and 39 HP — racing was worth 8 HP. I took the 8 and a Weak 1, which is printed plainly on my own status line ("Weak 1 (debuff) — Attacks deal 25% less damage for 1 turn").

Here the screen and my expectations first disagreed: every number on my cards silently changed — Strike 6→4, Ka-pow! 4→3, Bombs Away! 3→2. The faces are re-printed with the *post-Weak* number, which is correct and honest, but the first read is disorienting because nothing beside the card says why. The status line does say why, one block up. I call this legible-but-only-just.

**Turn 2.** Ka-pow! on A (Bomb 12 + 3, and The Boot quietly lifted the 3 to 5), then Bombs Away!, then Strike for the kill at exactly 0. Rejected: waiting one more turn for a 16 bomb. Rejected because 27 HP was inside one turn's reach and waiting bought another 6-damage hit plus a buff for the enemy. Decision made on the turn, arithmetic all readable off the faces.

## Fight 3 — Corpse Slug (1) [A] 27 HP / Corpse Slug (2) [B] 25 HP, both "Ravenous 4"

**Turn 1.** Jumpy Dumpty on A, Ka-pow! on A (detonate immediately), Strike A, Defend. Rejected: holding the bomb to 12 again. Rejected specifically *because there were two bodies* — Jumpy's Mine 3 lands on ALL enemies, so early detonation starts two growing charges instead of one. Same card, opposite correct play from fight 1, decided by the board. That is the clearest evidence in this round that the kit's core has real depth.

**Turn 2.** A was at 5 and I killed it with a Strike knowing "Ravenous 4 — when an enemy dies, Corpse Slug immediately eats it, becoming Stunned and gaining 4 Strength". Rejected: leaving A alive and hitting B. Rejected because the Stun buys a whole turn and the +4 Strength only matters if the fight runs long — and it did not. Then Bombs Away!, then Strike. B came out Stunned with Strength 4, exactly as printed.

**Turn 3.** B at 14 with Mine 11 + Bomb 6, intending 7×2. I played **Strike to put B at 8 — under its own Mine 11** — then two Defends as insurance. B's mine killed it before its hit and I took zero. The Strike was not for lethal and not for tempo; it was to move the enemy below a number the enemy is carrying. That is the single most interesting decision the kit offered me all round, and it is a decision the printed Mine text fully supports.

## Fight 4 (Elite) — Skulking Colony [A] 75 HP, "Hardened Shell 20 of 20 left this turn — cannot lose more than 20 HP each turn"

This enemy is a direct answer to the kit and it made the fight the best-designed one I played.

**Turn 1.** Jumpy Dumpty, Pop! (free Bomb 5), Defend, Defend. Rejected: detonating anything. Rejected because the shell caps me at 20 a turn, so a single huge payload is *wasted* — the kit's whole instinct (stack one enormous bomb) is the wrong instinct here, and the counter is printed on the enemy's face.

**Turn 2.** Ka-pow! set off Bomb 21 → exactly 20 dealt, and the screen then read "Hardened Shell **0 of 20** left this turn", which is an excellent piece of feedback. With the cap spent, every further point of damage that turn was worthless, so I spent the rest of the turn on things that are not damage: Fish-Flavored Bait for its Bomb 4, Chain Fuse (+6 per bomb), Dig In (8 block for 1 Spark, no energy) and Defend — 13 block vs 14 incoming. Rejected: Strikes. Rejected because the cap made them literally zero. A cap that turns "deal damage" into "build and block" is a good turn.

One thing I could not settle from the screen: the Mine 9 went off during the enemy's turn while the shell still read **0 of 20 left this turn**, and it dealt its full 9 (55→46). So the cap's "turn" is the *player's* turn, not the round — nothing printed says that, and I only learned it by watching a number move.

**Turn 3.** Perfect Timing set off Bomb 14 (+8) = 20, capped again, plus Bombs Away! to re-seed and a Defend. **Turn 4.** Chain Fuse (6→12) then Perfect Timing (12 + 8 = exactly 20) and a Defend; took 13. **Turn 5.** Strike for the last 6. Across the fight the decision was the same one repeated — "assemble exactly 20, spend the remainder on defence" — which is one good decision the first time and a routine the fourth time.

## Fight 5 — Punch Construct [A] 55 HP, Artifact 1, opens by Blocking

**Turn 1.** It intended Defend, so I built: Jumpy Dumpty, Pop!, Strike. Rejected: nothing meaningful — with no incoming damage and no detonator in hand this turn was close to automatic.

**Turn 2.** The turn of the run. Enemy at 49 with **Block 10** and my Bomb 21 on it, and the Bomb glossary prints "**Block stops it**". So the order was forced and interesting: **Amber — Fiery Rain** first (4 damage ×3) purely to strip the 10 block, then **Chain Fuse** (+6 per bomb → 18/15), then **Perfect Timing** to set off 33 + 8. 49 → 3. Rejected: leading with Perfect Timing, which is the natural-looking play and would have thrown the entire 21-point payload into 10 block. The card that decided it was Amber — a card I drafted for damage and used as a can-opener.

**Turn 3.** Ended the turn with no cards played: enemy at 3 with Mine 3, which kills before its hit. Zero damage taken in the whole fight.

## The kit, after 5 fights

**(a) Real choices, and where they were made.**
- *When to detonate* — every fight, on the turn. Trades a bigger payload later against damage taken now and against the Mine-3-on-ALL that a detonation seeds. Fight 1 turn 1 (hold) and fight 3 turn 1 (detonate now) are the same card played opposite ways for a printed reason. This is the kit's real decision and it is a good one.
- *Order of operations against Block* — fight 5 turn 2, on the turn. "Block stops it" makes the detonator's position in the sequence load-bearing; leading wrong wastes 20+ damage.
- *Hitting an enemy down under its own Mine* — fight 3 turn 3, on the turn. Genuinely novel: the attack is aimed at a threshold, not at a health bar.
- *Spending a capped turn on not-damage* — the elite, on the turn, because the enemy's face made the kit's instinct wrong.
- *At the draft:* taking Chain Fuse and Big Badda Boom commits to "one giant charge", which the elite's Hardened Shell punishes; taking Bombs Away!/Pop! commits to spread. I could feel that trade at each card screen, which is the good kind of draft pressure.

**(b) Automatic, and never worth playing.**
- **Defend is close to dead.** 5 block (3 under Frail) against 14-to-18 incoming is not a decision; I played Defends only because the energy had nowhere else to go. Dig In — 8 block for a **Spark** and no energy — showed exactly what Defend should feel like.
- **Strike is filler** that occasionally becomes interesting only when it is aimed at a Mine threshold.
- Turns where I have a detonator and no bomb, or a bomb and no detonator, are pure automatic: you play the one card that does anything.
- Sparks accumulated to 5 in the elite with only one sink in my deck. Until I drafted Dig In, "Spark" was a number that went up and did nothing — the relic prints a reward I could not spend.

**(c) Could not understand / contradicted itself.**
- The Hardened Shell cap's "turn": it read **0 of 20 left this turn** and the Mine still dealt its full 9 during the enemy's turn. Nothing printed reconciles that.
- **Perfect Timing** prints "If a Bomb triggered an Elemental Reaction this turn, play this again" and **Catalytic Converter**, **Razor**, **Sucrose** all refer to reactions — but every fight I saw printed "**NO REACTION IS REACHABLE HERE: Pyro is the only element this screen can supply**". Half of the rules text on Klee's own cards was unreachable for the whole act. I never once saw a reaction.
- A stray glossary entry: the card-reward screen after fight 2 printed **"Spend — Chosen on play, never automatic. Pays the lead performer … an emptied performer takes a Bow. On an empty stage the Spend mode is not offered at all."** Nothing on that screen — Perfect Timing, Stoke the Fuse, Tinder Toss, Amber — has a performer, a stage or a Bow. That looks like another character's keyword leaking into Klee's screens. Definite defect.
- The Smith screen prints "Upgraded: **not shown** — this page has no written face for this card" for Strike and Defend, i.e. for exactly the cards a new player is most likely to consider upgrading, while every Klee card shows its upgrade in full.
- Minor: **Stoke the Fuse** prints "cost all your Sparks (1 to play)" — I could not tell from that line whether playing it with 5 Sparks costs 5 or 1, and I skipped it partly for that reason.

**(d) Never wanted / happiest to draw.** Never wanted: **Defend** — 5 block is noise at act-1 elite damage. Happiest: **Perfect Timing** — 1 energy, it both detonates and adds 8, and in fight 5 it turned a stripped-block enemy from 49 to 3 in one card.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight present a decision?** **Yes, a small but real one.** Jumpy Dumpty is Innate and Ka-pow! is Retain, cost 0, so turn 1 asks "detonate the Bomb 8 now for 12, or retain the free detonator and take 16 next turn?" — and the answer is readable off the two faces (Bomb "grows 4 a turn"; Ka-pow! "Retain", cost 0). The remainder of the turn, two Defends against a printed 7, was automatic.
