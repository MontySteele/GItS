## Fight 1 — Nibbit [A], 42 HP

**Turn 1** (3 energy, Spark 1). Played **Witches' Circle** → **Fischl — Oz, at
Your Side** → **Jumpy Dumpty** on A.

The order was the decision. Witches' Circle prints *"Whenever you play a Hexerei
card, place a Bomb 3 on a random enemy"*; Fischl prints **Hexerei** on its face.
Playing the Power first turned Fischl from "a 5-damage-a-turn raven" into "a
raven plus a Bomb 3", and the screen confirmed it: after the two plays the enemy
carried `Bomb 11 … Bomb sizes here, oldest first: 3 / 8`. Spark went 1 → 2, which
is Fischl's Hexerei grant, un-upgraded, no reaction: exactly the +1 the rider
promised.

*Rejected:* **Strike** for 6. Two Powers that compound beat a flat 6 when the
enemy is not threatening enough to need the tempo, and I had exactly 3 energy for
the two Powers plus the placer.

*Rejected:* **Ka-pow!** (0 cost, *Retain. Set off. Deal 4*). Setting off 11 now
against 42 HP throws away growth: the Bomb keyword prints *"each grows 4 a
turn"*, so two bombs are worth +8 a turn, and Ka-pow **Retain**s, so waiting
costs me nothing but one enemy attack I was going to eat anyway.

**Turn 2.** Took 12. Enemy telegraphed *Attack 6* **and** *Defend* on the same
move. That flipped the plan: the Bomb keyword prints *"Block stops them"*, so
the block it was about to gain would eat the whole stack. Played **Ka-pow!** on
A, then **Strike**, **Strike**.

Ka-pow read 29 damage off a 19-point stack, and the screen had told me why in
advance: the enemy wore `Electro Aura 1` (from Oz's end-of-turn hit), and Ka-pow's
face carried *Reaction preview: Overloaded — Pyro meets Electro: 6 damage to ALL
enemies and 1 Weak*. 7 + 6 + 12 + 4 = 29, 37 → 8. Spark 2 → 4, matching
`in 2 hits for 2 Sparks` on the Bomb line. Two Strikes finished it.

**Verdict on the fight:** one real decision (detonate now vs. grow), and it was
forced by a *telegraphed Defend*, not by my hand — which is the good version of
that decision. The two Strikes at the end were a plan paying off, not dead turns.
The first turn's decision was the **ordering** of two Powers, and it was legible
from the printed text alone.

**Reward:** 19 gold; took **Tinder Toss** over Coven Errand, Chain Fuse and the
Companion **Barbara — Melody Loop**, because *"Its 1 Spark is a price, not an
Energy cost"* plus a relic that mints Sparks off my own bombs reads like a card
that costs nothing in the resource I was actually short of.

## Fight 2 — Twig Slime (S) [A] 7 HP, Twig Slime (M) [B] 26 HP, Leaf Slime (S) [C] 11 HP

**Turn 1.** **Bombs Away!** → **Jumpy Dumpty** on B → **Strike** on A.

The decision was where the big charge goes. A and C were both small; B at 26 was
the body worth an 8. A died to the Strike, and the screen then showed exactly

`2 / 2`: *"If this enemy dies with it still on, it moves to a survivor."* Screen
and outcome agreed.

*Rejected:* **Ka-pow!** to clear A. It would have done the same kill, but it is
my only Retained detonator and its value is the stack it eventually eats.
A 1-energy Strike I had spare did the job.

*Rejected:* **Alice's Introduction Magic** (*All cards in your hand count as
Hexerei cards this turn*). I had no Spark-priced card in hand and no Witches'
Circle out, so the word would have bought nothing.

**Turn 2.** Played **Ka-pow!** on B (22 damage: 18 stack + 4, B to 1 HP), then
**Tinder Toss** for 1 Spark — *Twice: Set off a random enemy and deal 4 damage to
it* — which cleared both survivors at once. Fight over on turn 2 with **zero
damage taken**.

**Verdict:** the decision was made at turn 1 (which body carries the 8), and turn
2 cashed it. Tinder Toss's randomness never mattered because both remaining
bodies were lethal to any set-off. One thing I could not evaluate: with two
targets left and a card that picks randomly twice, I had no way to read whether I
was making a good play or a lucky one.

**Reward:** 16 gold; took **Coven Errand** over Mine Toss, Dodoco Cover and

## Fight 3 — Shrinker Beetle [A], 39 HP

**Turn 1.** **Coven Errand** ×2 and **Jumpy Dumpty**, all on A. Bomb 18 in three
charges.

Coven Errand prints *"Place a Bomb 5. If you played a Hexerei card this turn,
place it on ALL enemies instead."* Against one enemy that whole rider is dead
text — there is no ALL to place it on. That is a card whose second sentence
cannot do anything in a single-body fight, and I had no way to spend the
condition even if I had wanted to.

*Rejected:* two Strikes for 12. Three charges growing 12 a turn beats 6-a-card
against a body that was spending its first turn on a debuff.

**Turn 2.** The beetle applied `Shrink -1 — you deal 30% less damage with every
hit you land`. **The card faces immediately reprinted with the reduced numbers**
— Strike read `Deal 4 damage`, Ka-pow `Deal 2`, Bombs Away `Deal 2 damage to ALL`.
That is the single most legible thing the bridge did all round.

Played **Bombs Away!** (a fourth charge) and both **Defend**s for 10 block against
a 7-damage hit — deliberately overpaying so I could take a free turn of growth.

*Rejected:* setting off 30 into 39 HP. It leaves the beetle alive at 7 with my
detonator spent, and the Bomb line said the stack would be 48 next turn.

**Turn 3.** Played **Ka-pow!** on A into a 48-point stack. It killed a 37-HP body
outright.

**This was the round's cleanest confirmation of a printed rule.** Shrink says
*"every hit you land"*; if it had touched the bombs, 48 would have landed as
33.6 and the beetle would have survived at 3. It did not. The Bomb keyword's
*"Not an Attack … only Vulnerable and a cap on the enemy's HP loss move it"* is
exactly true, and it is what made the whole plan correct rather than lucky.
Screen and outcome agreed.

**Verdict:** the best-designed fight of the six. The enemy's debuff attacked the
half of my deck I was not using, the printed keyword told me so, and the decision
"eat 7 damage to gain 16 bomb" was a real trade I could price off the screen.

**Reward:** 10 gold, Potion of Binding; took **Flame Dance** over Sorry Jean…,
Bombs Away! and the Companion **Barbara — Melody Loop**.

## Fight 4 — Nibbit (1) [A] 44 HP, Nibbit (2) [B] 46 HP

**Turn 1.** **Fischl — Oz, at Your Side** → **Coven Errand** on B →
**Jumpy Dumpty+** on B.

Here the Hexerei word did real work. Fischl first meant Coven Errand's rider
fired and its Bomb 5 landed on **both** bodies instead of one. The screen
confirmed it: A `Bomb 5`, B `Bomb 5 / 11`. Playing them the other way round would
have cost me a whole Bomb 5. **This is the one turn in the run where the word on
the Companion cards changed the order of my plays.**

*Rejected:* **Bombs Away!** instead of Jumpy Dumpty+ — 3 damage to each and Bomb
2 each is 6 damage plus 4 bomb; Jumpy Dumpty+ is 11 bomb plus a Mine 4 rider on
every body when it goes off. Against 90 total HP the rider is worth more than
6 chip.

**Turn 2** (44 HP, 22 incoming). **Alice's Introduction Magic** → **Coven
Errand** on B → **Careful Now**.

I bought Alice's word here on purpose: I wanted to see whether "counts as Hexerei"
meant *both* things the keyword prints. **It meant only one of them.** Coven
Errand's ALL-enemies rider fired (both bodies took a Bomb 5, screen confirmed).
But my **Spark counter did not move — it sat at 2 across both plays**, when the
Hexerei keyword printed on that very screen says *"Playing one gives Klee 1
Spark, up to 3 a play."* Two cards that the game itself agreed were Hexerei that
turn produced zero Sparks. See section (c).

Careful Now read `Gain Block equal to your largest Bomb when played, up to 10`
and gave 10 against a 29-point stack — the cap bites hard and early.

**Turn 3** (40 HP, 22 incoming, A: 39 HP / Bomb 22 / no aura; B: 41 HP / Bomb 41 /
Electro aura). Played **Flame Dance** — *Set off each enemy whose aura is not
Pyro. Deal 5 damage to ALL enemies* — expecting both stacks to go up.

**Only B's went up.** B took 41 + Overloaded 6 + 5 and died. A took **11**:
5 from Flame Dance and 6 from the Overload splash. Its own `13 / 9` charges were
still sitting on its line afterwards, un-detonated. A had **no aura at all**, and
"no aura" is evidently not "an aura that is not Pyro". See section (c) — this one
cost me a turn.

Cleaned up with **Ka-pow!** on A into the inherited 30-point stack (`13 / 9 / 4 /
4, including 2 Mines` — B's Jumpy rider had scattered Mine 4s and then B's death
had moved its charges onto the survivor, both exactly as printed).

**Verdict:** two real decisions (the Fischl-before-Coven-Errand ordering at turn
1; block-and-grow vs. cash at turn 2) and one decision I got wrong **because I
read the printed text correctly**. The fight was won by a plan set three turns
earlier, which is the good kind of "obvious lethal".

**Reward:** 12 gold; took **Mika — Starfrost Swirl** over Countdown,
Pocket Fireworks and Vermillion Pact — partly for the Cryo, mostly because it was
the only Companion I could actually get into my hand this round.

## The kit, after 6 fights (5 won, 1 cut off)

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

1. **Detonate now vs. grow one more turn** — on the turn, every fight. This is
   the kit's spine and it is a genuinely good one, because the screen prints the
   exact number on both sides: the enemy's HP and `Set off here deals N`. Fight 1
   turn 2 (the telegraphed *Defend* forced the cash), fight 3 turn 2 (eat 7 to
   gain 16), fight 6 turn 2 (skip 21 now for 43 next turn) were all real and all
   priced.
2. **Play-order inside a turn** — on the turn. Fischl before Coven Errand
   (fight 4 t1) turned one Bomb 5 into two. Witches' Circle before any Hexerei
   card (fight 1 t1) is the same shape. Mika last into a stacking Slow (fight 7
   t1) is a third. **Ordering is where this kit's turn-level thinking lives**,
   and it is the part I enjoyed.
3. **Which body carries the big charge** — on the turn, in multi-enemy fights.
   Sharpened by two printed rules working together: Block stops bombs (so read
   the intents), and a dying body's charges move to a survivor (so a small body
   is not always a waste).
4. **What the aura should be when the stack goes off** — earlier in the fight.
   Mona's Hydro (fight 6 t2) and Mika's Cryo (fight 7 t1) are both "spend a card
   now to multiply a number two turns from now". The reaction previews make this
   legible; it is the most distinctive decision in the kit.
5. **At the draft:** taking Spark-priced cards (Tinder Toss, Fireworks Show)
   while holding a relic that mints Sparks off my own detonations. That draft
   decision is what let fights 2 and 6 end on turns where I had already spent all
   three energy. Fight 2 in particular had no on-turn decision at all worth the
   name — it was won by that draft pick.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **Jumpy Dumpty+ on turn 1, always.** Innate, biggest single charge, free rider.
  I played it as the first or second card of every single fight and never once
  considered not doing so. It is the least interesting card in the deck by virtue
  of being the most obviously correct.
- **Ka-pow!** is close behind. 0 cost and Retain means it never competes with
  anything; it just sits in hand until the number is big enough. That is a good
  *fantasy* and a slightly hollow *decision* — there is no cost to holding it, so
  there is no tension in holding it.
- **Strike and Defend.** Strike 6 is irrelevant next to a stack growing 12–20 a
  turn; I played Strikes only as leftover-energy filler or for an exact lethal.
  Defend 5 is far below a 17-and-rising Elite hit.
- **Ultimate Strike** (my Neow pick, 14 for 1) — I drew it three times and
  **never played it once**. It is the best raw attack in my deck and the kit made
  it irrelevant, which says something about how far ahead of Attack-rate the bomb
  clock runs.
- **Coven Errand's second sentence** in any single-enemy fight — dead text, with
  no way to spend the condition.
- **Sucrose — Wind Spirit Creation's Swirl** — dead in both single-enemy fights
  I played it in; the card was still worth playing for the free draw and Spark,
  which makes its printed headline effect a decoration.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its own printed text.**

1. **Alice's Introduction Magic does not grant the Sparks its own keyword
   promises.** Fight 4, turn 2. I played Alice's (*All cards in your hand count
   as Hexerei cards this turn*), then Coven Errand, then Careful Now. Coven
   Errand's `if you played a Hexerei card this turn` rider **did** fire — both
   Nibbits took a Bomb 5, so the game agreed those cards were Hexerei. My Spark
   counter **did not move**: it read `Spark 2` before Alice's and `Spark 2` after
   all three plays. The Hexerei keyword printed on that same screen says
   *"Playing one gives Klee 1 Spark, up to 3 a play."* Either the keyword text is
   wrong about who it applies to, or Alice's is only half-doing what it says.
   I could not tell which from any screen. This is the round's biggest
   text-vs-outcome gap.
2. **"Set off each enemy whose aura is not Pyro" skips an enemy with no aura at
   all.** Fight 4, turn 3. Nibbit [A] wore no aura line whatsoever and had
   `Bomb 22` on it. Flame Dance set off B (Electro aura) and not A; A lost 11 HP
   (5 card + 6 Overload splash) and its `13 / 9` charges were still on its line
   afterwards. On a plain reading, "no aura" is not "Pyro", so A should have gone
   up. It did not. Either the clause means "whose aura exists and is not Pyro", or
   the card applies its own Pyro to everything first and then checks — but the
   printed sentence orders it the other way, and the card cost me a turn I could
   not afford at 40 HP.
3. **A reaction's preview name and the debuff it leaves do not match.** Mona's
   face: `Electro-Charged … loses 4 HP at the start of its turn, 1 less each
   turn`. The body afterwards: `Poison 3 … loses 3 HP, then reduce Poison by 1`.
   Different name, different number.
4. **The "up to 3 a play" cap on Hexerei never bit and I never found the road to
   it.** Every Hexerei play I made granted exactly +1. The rider says +1 more for
   a reaction and +1 more if upgraded — so 3 needs an upgraded Companion whose
   own play triggers a reaction. I never had an upgraded Companion, so a third of
   the keyword's text was unreachable for me.
5. **Alice's Introduction Magic prints two contradictory upgrade lines** on the
   Smith screen (see the rest-site note above).
6. **Small:** the Neow row and the "Room Full of Cheese" Gorge row both carried
   *"this option's own words promise a card and the feed carried no face for it"*.
   Both delivered a normal card screen a moment later. Whatever that note is
   warning about, it did not describe what happened.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

Never wanted: **Defend**. 5 block against a 17-and-rising Elite is not a card, it
is a rounding error, and it competes for energy with a placer that is worth three
times as much. (**Ultimate Strike** is the honourable mention: my own Neow pick,
never played once.)

Happiest to draw: **Flame Dance**, despite it being the card that misled me.
"Set off *each* enemy and hit *all* of them" is the payoff the whole bomb clock is
building toward, and the turn it fired in fight 4 killed a 41-HP Nibbit outright
with an Overload splashing the other. **Careful Now** deserves a mention for a
different reason — it is the only card in the deck that reads my own bomb stack
as a *defensive* number, and it made the "grow one more turn" decision survivable.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a good one — but it was a decision about ORDER, not about which cards
to play.** With 3 energy and five cards, the set was near-forced (two Powers plus
the Innate placer). What was live was that **Witches' Circle must precede
Fischl**, or Fischl's Hexerei triggers nothing; and that **Ka-pow! should not be
played at all**, because it Retains and the bombs grow. Both of those are
readable off the printed faces on that first screen with no prior knowledge of the
kit. That is a strong opening turn by the standard of a blind seat: I made two
choices, both from text, and both turned out to be right for the reasons the text
gave.

## The word on the Companion cards

The word is **Hexerei**.

**What I understood it to do, in my own words:** Hexerei is a tag printed on the
face of *some* Companion cards — not all of them. When Klee plays a card whose
face prints it, **Klee gains 1 Spark**; +1 more if that play triggered an
Elemental Reaction, and +1 more if the card is upgraded, to a **maximum of 3
Sparks from any one play**. Nobody pays anything for it: it is a grant, not a
cost, and the cap caps *the Sparks minted by a single play*, not the Sparks you
can hold — the Spark keyword says explicitly *"with no cap"* for the pool itself.
Sparks are gone at the end of combat, and they are the price on cards like Tinder
Toss and Fireworks Show, which the faces are careful to distinguish from Energy:
*"Its 1 Spark is a price, not an Energy cost."* Separately from the Sparks, the
tag is a **condition other cards check** — Witches' Circle (*whenever you play a
Hexerei card, place a Bomb 3*) and Coven Errand (*if you played a Hexerei card
this turn, place it on ALL enemies instead*) both read it.

**Where I read it:** the battle screen's own `## Words on this screen` glossary,
which prints `**Hexerei** — A Companion card whose face prints the word. Playing
one gives Klee 1 Spark, up to 3 a play.` The +reaction / +upgraded detail comes
from a second rider printed on each Hexerei Companion's face itself:
`*Sparks from your Companion* — Playing a Hexerei card makes 1 Spark, 1 more if
it triggered an Elemental Reaction and 1 more if it is upgraded.` Both were
