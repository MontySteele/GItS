## Fight 1 — Toadpole (1) 23 HP, Toadpole (2) 21 HP

**Turn 1** (hand: Ethereal Spotlight, Freminet, Lynette — Enigmatic Feint, Aria of
Recompense, Stage Presence, Regal Bearing; Encore 2, no member).

Played **Aria of Recompense** → Encore 2→7, then **Ethereal Spotlight** (0 energy,
"Costs 2 Encore") → Encore 5, then **Freminet on Toadpole (2)**, then
**Lynette — Enigmatic Feint**.

The decision was the ordering, and it was a real one. Spotlight prints *"Costs 2
Encore"* and I had exactly 2, so playing it first would have left the pool at
zero; Aria first bought it. **Rejected:** playing Spotlight bare on turn 1 (it
would have emptied the pool that both absorbs damage and pays members), and
playing Stage Presence for 6 block instead of Lynette (Lynette was lit to 7 block
and blocked the whole 7-damage attack, so Stage Presence was strictly worse).

The Spotlight was legible: Freminet's printed line changed from `Deal 6 damage.`
to `Deal 9 damage.` in my hand the moment it resolved, and Lynette's from
`Gain 5 Block` to `Gain 7 Block`. That is the clearest single piece of feedback
the kit gave me all act.

A thing I did not expect and liked: Lynette's `Swirl an enemy's aura onto ALL
enemies` read as dead ("No aura, no effect") until Freminet applied Cryo one card
earlier — and then it copied `Cryo Aura 2` onto both Toadpoles. The screen taught
me a two-card combo without a tooltip saying so.

**Turn 2.** Played **An Invitation** (free) → *Charlotte — Framing*, then
**Salon Début** → Crabaletta deployed and performed for 6 Hydro into Toadpole
(1)'s Cryo. `Frozen 1` landed and its intent visibly fell from `3x3` to `1x3`.
Then **Charlotte on Toadpole (2)** (6 damage, draw 1, and it performed Crabaletta
a second time), then **Soloist's Solicitation** to kill Toadpole (2) exactly.

**Rejected:** hitting Toadpole (1) with Charlotte instead. Toadpole (1) had
`Thorns 2` and *a performance is not an Attack*, so I deliberately let Crabaletta
be the thing that touched the thorny one and kept my card attacks on the other.
That is a genuine decision the printed keyword handed me.

**Where the screen and the outcome disagreed:** *Charlotte* arrived in hand
printing `Deal 6 damage`, not 9 — she was **not** Spotlighted, even though
`Guest Cast 1` was up and reads *"Companion cards are Spotlighted"*. The
Spotlight evidently lights the Companion cards that exist when it is played and
does not reach one created afterwards. Nothing on the screen says so. This
repeated in the elite with *Shinobu* (printed `Gain 6 Block`, not 9).

**Turn 3.** **Freminet on Toadpole (1)** for 9 plus a Crabaletta performance
finished the fight. No alternative — but the plan that produced the lethal was
made on turn 2 (deploy + focus the low body), so I score this as a plan paying
off, not a dead turn.

I also noticed the Encore pool emptied faster than I read it would: turn 2 ended
with Encore 3 against a printed `1 damage 3 times`, and turn 3 opened at Encore 0
with 1 HP also gone. I could not reconcile the arithmetic from the screens.

## Fight 2 — Sludge Spinner 37 HP

**Turn 1.** **Ethereal Spotlight** (Encore 2→0), **Salon Début**, **Fischl — Oz,
at Your Side**, **Freminet**.

The decision here was Spotlight-versus-Encore and I worked it out on the screen:
with 2 Encore I could either light the Companions all fight or fund two
full-price performances, because *"a member with no Encore to spend performs at
three-quarters."* I did the sum — Spotlight gives Freminet +3 forever, the 2
Encore gives 2 performances +1.5 each once — and took the permanent one. The
board then confirmed the rule cleanly: 37→29 across two performances, i.e. 4+4
rather than 6+6. **Rejected:** holding the Spotlight (the relic re-adds one every
turn, so deferring is free — this is a real and well-signposted option).

**Turn 2** presented nothing. I drew Stage Presence ×2, Regal Bearing, The Gallery
Stirs and Aria; the enemy sat at 7 HP with Poison 2 and Oz's 5 Electro already
guaranteed to fire. I blocked and banked Encore. **No rejected alternative worth
the name** — the only question was which pile of block to play, which is not a
decision. That is the finding for this turn.

**Turn 3.** **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint** to perform Crabaletta into the last
point of HP. **Rejected:** Soloist's Solicitation, which was the safer, more
direct kill; I chose the Companion because I wanted to see a *full-price*
performance now that Encore was 10. It killed.

## Fight 3 — Seapunk 46 HP

**Turn 1.** **Ethereal Spotlight**, **Chevreuse** (10→**15** damage lit),
**Regal Bearing** for 3 block and 1 Weak. **Rejected:** Lynette — Magic Trick
(6 lit AoE against a single enemy is a quarter of Chevreuse's line) and Stage
Presence over Regal Bearing (Weak took the 11-damage intent down to 8, which is
worth more than 3 extra block).

**Turn 2** was the best turn of the act and every part of it was chosen.
**An Invitation** produced *Chevreuse — Vanguard's Valor*: `Your next Attack deals
3 additional damage, plus 3 if an Elemental Reaction triggered this turn.` The
enemy wore `Pyro Aura 1` from Chevreuse and my hand held both Freminet (Cryo,
previewing **Melt ×1.75**) and Salon Début (Crabaletta, Hydro, previewing
**Vaporize ×1.5**).

The whole turn was a sequencing puzzle with a right answer:

- Freminet first would spend the Pyro aura on Melt (9 × 1.75 ≈ 16) and leave
  Crabaletta hitting a bare body.
- Salon Début first spends the Pyro on Crabaletta's smaller Vaporize, but the
  reaction then satisfies Valor's second clause, and Freminet's Cryo lands on the
  Hydro Crabaletta leaves behind, which is **Frozen**.

I played **Salon Début → Vanguard's Valor → Freminet → Fischl** and the screen
printed `Passion Overload 6`, confirming both halves of Valor had counted. Seapunk
went 31 → 21 → 2 and died. **Rejected:** the Melt-first line, for a hair more raw
damage and no Frozen and no Valor bonus. This is the turn I would point to if
someone asked whether the kit's turns present decisions.

## Fight 4 — ELITE: Phantasmal Gardener ×4, 27 / 29 / 28 / 31 HP

All four carried `Skittish 6 — The first time Phantasmal Gardener is hit each
turn, it gains 6 Block.` 115 HP behind a 24-block-per-turn tax.

**Turn 1.** **Ethereal Spotlight**, then **Lynette — Magic Trick** as a probe:
I genuinely did not know whether Skittish's block would eat the hit that
triggered it. The screen answered — all four went to 21/23/22/25 *and then* showed
`Block 6`. Damage first, block after. That reversed my plan for the fight: the
first hit each turn is free and only my *later* hits are taxed, so spreading is
right and stacking is wrong. Then **Lynette — Enigmatic Feint** (7 lit block) and
**Stage Presence** (6) to eat the 15 incoming. **Rejected:** 19 block from two
Stage Presence plus Lynette, which would have taken zero damage but bought no
information about Skittish. I took 2 damage for the answer and it was worth it.

**A refusal, and a real one.** `play "Lynette — Magic Trick: Astonishing Shift"`
bare was refused: *"there is more than one enemy, so say which."* The card prints
`Deal 4 damage to ALL enemies` — but the Swirl half needs a body, so an
all-enemies card demands a target. **The very next turn the opposite happened:**
`play "Chevreuse — Ring of Bursting Grenades" on "Phantasmal Gardener (4)"` was
refused with *"does its own aiming, so it takes no `on`."* Two cards, both
printing "ALL enemies", with opposite targeting rules and nothing on either face
to tell them apart.

**That refusal cost me the turn**, and I want it in the record because it was my
error compounded by the inconsistency: I had queued Chevreuse, Aria and Shinobu
and an `end turn` together. Chevreuse was refused, the other three ran, and I
**ended turn 2 without playing my best card**. A wasted turn against 91 HP of
elite. My fault for batching; the two-rule targeting is what made the batch
unsafe.

**Turn 3** (recovering). **Curtain Rises** on Gardener (1) — 6 damage and a deploy
— then **Freminet** on Gardener (4) spreading the free first hits, then **Fischl**.
Three Companion plays performed the Usher three times for 2 block each (three
quarters of 3, Encore being 0) — six block against 19 incoming. **Rejected:**
Stage Presence for a flat 6, which would have been more block than the entire
Salon produced. That comparison is damning for the Usher.

At 0 energy I spent **Power Potion** and took **Endless Waltz** (`Add 1
Crabaletta and 1 Usher to your Salon. Salon Member numbers are 3 higher.`),
free to play that turn. It filled the stage in one card — the thing my deck could
not do for itself in four turns.

**Turn 4** paid off everything: **Chevreuse — Ring of Bursting Grenades** for 15 to
all, which killed Gardener (1) at 15, Gardener (3) at 9, and Gardener (4) at 16 —
because Freminet had left `Cryo Aura` on Gardener (4) the turn before and the AoE
**Melted** it for ×1.75. Three of four bodies in one card. Then **Shinobu** and
**Salon Début** (stage full, so it Evoked the front member) finished the last one.

That is a fight decided by two draft picks (Chevreuse, and the Power Potion
gamble) and one aura placed a turn early, not by any single turn's choice. Which
is the good version of the answer.

## Fight 5 — Fossil Stalker 52 HP

`Suck 3 — Whenever Fossil Stalker deals unblocked attack damage, it gains 3
Strength.` A block check, and the Tea Set gave me 5 energy.

**Turn 1.** **Ethereal Spotlight**, **An Invitation** (→ Vanguard's Valor),
**Lynette — Enigmatic Feint** (7, lit) + **Stage Presence** (6) = 13 block against
exactly 12, then **Valor** → **Soloist's Solicitation** for 9, then
**Lynette — Magic Trick** for 6. The decision was explicit and countable: 5 block
from unlit Lynette plus Stage Presence is 11, one short of 12, and one point
short means permanent Strength. Lighting the Spotlight was what put me over.
**Rejected:** spending the same energy on Salon Début for tempo, which would have
let the hit land and started the Strength snowball.

**Turn 2.** No block card that could reach 9. **Regal Bearing** (Weak, dropping 12
to 9) → **Curtain Rises** (deploy Usher) → **Freminet**, so that Freminet's
Companion play performed the Usher for a second slice of block. 7 block against 9;
4 got through and it took Strength 3. **Rejected:** an all-defence turn, which
still could not have reached 9, and would have surrendered 15 damage for nothing.
This was the turn where I most clearly could not afford what the kit wanted to do.

**Turn 3.** Losing HP had fired Centennial Puzzle for 3 cards, and **Chevreuse —
Ring of Bursting Grenades+** (18, ×1.75 Melt on the Cryo aura Freminet had left)
killed a 22-HP body outright. Again: the lethal was set up two turns earlier by
which element I chose to leave on the enemy.

## The kit, after 5 fights

**(a) Which decisions felt like real choices, and what they traded off.**

1. **Encore is one pool doing three jobs, and every turn asks which.** It absorbs
   damage after Block, it pays *Ethereal Spotlight*'s 2, and members that cannot
   spend 1 perform at three-quarters. Turn 1 of fight 1 was decided by playing
   *Aria* before the Spotlight; turn 1 of fight 2 was me sitting there computing
   whether +50% forever beat two full-price performances now. That is the best
   thing in the kit. It is made **on the turn**.
2. **Elemental sequencing.** Fight 3 turn 2 — deploy first so the Vaporize
   satisfies *Vanguard's Valor* and Freminet's Cryo lands on fresh Hydro for
   Frozen, versus Freminet first for a bigger Melt and no rider. Real, close, and
   readable off the screen because the cards print a `Reaction preview:` line
   naming the reaction before you commit. **On the turn**, enabled **earlier in
   the fight** by which element you left on the body.
3. **Which body wears which aura, one turn ahead.** Both of my biggest turns
   (elite turn 4, fight 5 turn 3) were Chevreuse Melting a Cryo aura that
   Freminet had applied the previous turn. **Earlier in the fight.**
4. **A performance is not an Attack.** Steering Crabaletta into the Thorns
   toadpole while keeping card attacks off it was a decision the printed keyword
   handed me for free. **On the turn.**
5. **The elite's Skittish, once I learned block lands after damage** — spread, do
   not stack. **On the turn**, and only after a probe.
6. **At the draft:** Fischl over Hearts Swelling; Curtain Rises over three
   Companions because I needed a *second deploy* more than a fourth Companion;
   and passing 336 gold at the Sunken Treasury to keep an unplayable Eternal out
   of a deck whose engine is card-driven.

**(b) What felt automatic, and what never seemed worth playing.**

- **The stage was empty at the start of 10 of my 15 combat turns.** That is the
  central problem. On those turns every Companion card printed *"No member on
  stage: performs nobody... this card is only its own line"*, and the kit
  collapses into a plain deck of 6-damage and 6-block cards. Turn 1 of a fight is
  almost always this. I opened five fights and **never once** had a member on
  stage for the first card I played.
- **Gentilhomme Usher is not worth the stage slot.** 3 block, three-quartered to
  2 when Encore is dry. In the elite, three Companion plays through the Usher
  produced 6 block; one *Stage Presence* is 6 block for one energy. The member I
  was building the whole engine to feed was worse than a basic Defend.
- **Turns where I only had block cards were automatic** (fight 2 turn 2). Not a
  kit problem so much as a hand-quality problem, but the kit gave me nothing to
  do with a defensive hand.
- **The Gallery Stirs I bought for 76 gold and never played** — its trigger is
  "the first time you spend Encore each turn", and I spent whole fights at Encore
  0 with no member to spend it on anyway. A dead purchase I would not repeat.
- **Fanfare I could not use.** It sat at 0–10 all act. The only thing I ever saw
  it do was *Aria of Recompense*'s "at least 3 Fanfare" clause. `Member numbers
  gain +1 per 10 Fanfare` is a threshold I crossed exactly once, in the fight
  where a potion had already given me `Grand Salon 3`.

**(c) What I could not understand, or that contradicted its printed text.**

1. **"Spotlight every Companion card" does not cover Companion cards made after
   it.** *Charlotte* arrived printing 6 damage and *Shinobu* printing 6 block
   while `Guest Cast 1` was up. Every Companion that was in my deck when I played
   it showed its lit number immediately. Nothing on either screen explains the
   difference, and *An Invitation* is a card whose entire job is making Companions.
2. **Two "ALL enemies" cards with opposite targeting rules.** *Lynette — Magic
   Trick* is refused without `on`; *Chevreuse — Ring of Bursting Grenades* is
   refused *with* it. Nothing on the faces distinguishes them. This cost me a full
   turn in the elite.
3. **Encore arithmetic I could not reconcile.** Fight 1 turn 2 ended at Encore 3
   against a printed `1 damage 3 times`, and turn 3 opened at Encore 0 *and* 1 HP
   down. Fight 3 turn 1: Encore 5, 6 block, a 17-damage turn, and I finished 11 HP
   down with Encore 0. I could not make the numbers close from the screens.
4. **The upgrade screen showed no upgrade at all** for *Aria of Recompense*,
   *Salon Début*, *An Invitation* and *Fischl — Oz*, with no line saying why,
   while every other card printed an `Upgraded:` preview.
5. **Shinobu, generated mid-fight by *An Invitation*, appeared in my deck list at
   the Smith** — under a heading saying it was on no list "and nothing on the feed
   says why". It also came back around in the elite's draw pile after I played it.
   I could not tell whether a generated Companion is a permanent deck card.
6. **The "member picks its own enemy at random"** never hurt me because I mostly
   fought one body, but in the elite it meant a performance would sometimes hit a
   body that already had its Skittish block up. I could not plan around it.

**(d) The card I never wanted to play, and the one I was happiest to draw.**

- **Never wanted: *Curtain Rises*** — not the 6 damage, but what it deploys. It
  puts an Usher at the *front* of the stage, and the front member is the one every
  subsequent Companion play performs, so it converts my damage engine into a
  2-block engine. I played it twice and both times regretted the stage slot more
  than I valued the deploy. (Runner-up: *The Gallery Stirs*, bought and never
  cast.)
- **Happiest to draw: *Chevreuse — Ring of Bursting Grenades*.** It killed three
  of four elite Gardeners in one card and ended fight 5 in one card. But the
  honest note is that it is happiest *because* it does not need the stage — it is
  the card that works when the kit's own machine is not running.

**(e) Did the first turn of the first fight already present a decision?**

**Yes, and a good one.** The hand held *Aria of Recompense* (gain 5 Encore) and
*Ethereal Spotlight* (0 energy, "Costs 2 Encore") with exactly 2 Encore in the
pool — so the turn asked, in its first two cards, whether to spend the pool on a
permanent +50% or keep it as a buffer, and it asked in a form where the ordering
of two cards changed whether the play was even legal. That is a real opening turn.

The qualifier: **no Companion card could perform anybody on it**, because the
stage was empty, and it was empty on turn 1 of all five of my fights. So the
opening turn's decision was about the *Encore economy*, never about the Salon.
The Salon does not exist until turn 2 at the earliest.

## Non-blindness declaration

- Commands run in the game: only `GITS_LANE=2 python -m understudy.blindplay observe`
  and `GITS_LANE=2 python -m understudy.blindplay act "<command>"`. No `harness
  state`, no `scenario`, no `staged_turn`, no `soak`, no other understudy command.
  Lane 1 was never touched.
- Other Bash use: one `mkdir -p` of the session scratchpad directory (created,
  never written to); one `mkdir -p` of this record's directory under
  `review/qa/furina-reframe-round-12-2026-09-05`; and `sed -n '...p'` piping on
  the output of `observe` calls, to re-read one block of a screen without
  reprinting the whole thing. Nothing was read from disk by any of these.
- Tools used: **Bash** (as above) and **Write**, once, for this file.
- **Repo files read: none.**

One process note that is a caveat on the round rather than a blindness breach:
in the elite I chained several `act` calls in a single shell command. The first
was refused (the Chevreuse targeting rule above) and the rest — including
`end turn` — executed anyway, costing me a whole turn of that fight. Any judgement
here about how hard the elite was should be read with that self-inflicted lost
turn in mind.
