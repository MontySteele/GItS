## Pair A — the priced draw

1. **Card involvement.** The prototype was played by both graders. Opus: “Rummage first for 1 energy, spending the 3-Spark bank to draw 3 cards.” Codex likewise played “Rummage first, spending the 3 Sparks and drawing three cards.”

2. **Change in kind of choice.** The kind changed. The shipped half preserved the automatic Attack discount and used Spark only as a draw condition. The prototype half made the bank’s destination steerable: spend it on Rummage for cards, or preserve it for the discounted Attack.

3. **Did the cost bind?** Yes. Spending the bank on Rummage made Rapid Fire cost its printed energy, constraining whether the remaining energy went to defense or a drawn card.

4. **Text problem?** One form conflicts with the replay, but not because Rummage’s printed face was misread. Opus asserted, “Then Rapid Fire… That leaves 1 energy, which I spend on one of the drawn 1-energy 7-damage Attacks.” The replay recorded only Rummage and Rapid Fire, and the actual draws were not the guaranteed set that assertion assumed. Codex’s form was consistent with the replay.

5. **Judgment: ADVANCE.** The board exposed a real draw destination, both surviving graders used the prototype, and the bank’s competing destinations produced a steerable choice worth testing in whole-fight play.

## Pair B — the Burst conversion

1. **Card involvement.** The prototype was not played. Opus seriously weighed it: “The one I actually weighed was the Slow Burn line.” Codex ignored it, considering only Attack order, and that form was refused as intent-insensitive.

2. **Change in kind of choice.** The kind changed in the surviving grader’s deliberation. The shipped half spent the bank through the automatic Attack discount while also playing Imaginary Friend. The prototype presented a mutually exclusive conversion choice between that discount and Burst-plus-defense, even though the chosen line retained the Attack destination.

3. **Did the cost bind?** Yes. Spending the bank on the prototype would forfeit the free Rapid Fire and the damage line. Conversely, spending it on Rapid Fire made Slow Burn unplayable and forfeited its Burst progress and fuller guard.

4. **Text problem?** No prototype-face misread appears in the surviving form, and its replay is consistent. In the shipped half, Opus left the meter result partly unresolved—“25 plus whatever the 3 Burst Energy is worth”—while the replay resolved the printed effects to the final meter value; that imprecision did not alter the played line.

5. **Judgment: ADVANCE.** One surviving grader seriously weighed the prototype’s distinct Spark destination and named its concrete opportunity cost. That is enough to justify asking the question again in whole-fight play, despite the second prototype form’s refusal.
