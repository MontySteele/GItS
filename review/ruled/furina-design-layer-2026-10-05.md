# Furina at the design layer: what she is for, and whether it works

**Where it comes from.** [USER]'s first run on the re-founded Stage (build
0.2.4376), 2026-10-05:
- "Honestly, not very engaging."
- "The act 1 boss was a real slog. I had plenty of block but no damage.
  There didn't seem to be any way to scale."
- "Spend doesn't feel meaningful yet since I only have the starter card to
  spend down."
- "I didn't get a guest star until act 2 (Sigewinne), and she did not feel
  impactful."
- The solo cards: "when is the stage empty?" Full House: "it's just 'act
  twice'".
- Fanfare "is basically a second resource in the flavor of stars or bombs".

Then: "Let's hold specific Furina picks for now. Can you draft a paper ...
looking at the issues from the design layer?"

Six questions follow, in [USER]'s order. Each answer can be checked
against a named file or result.

## 1. Her intended mechanics, and what each is for

| Mechanic | What it is meant to do | What it does in play |
|---|---|---|
| **The stage**: 3 seats, performers act at end of turn, front to back | The engine: value that comes without spending cards | Free Block and chip damage every turn. Both seats and [USER] called it a turn that "played itself" |
| **Salon trio**: Usher 4 Block, Chevalmarin 2 to ALL, Crabaletta 5 random | The numbers that scale | Block is plentiful; damage stays small |
| **Guest Stars**: Uncommon or Rare, with a line, an act, or both | Rule-benders that reshape a turn | They arrive late and are rare. When drafted they made the best turns (Navia, Wriothesley), and Sigewinne felt like nothing |
| **Fanfare**: one number, many sources, Spend and star payments | The thing you build and cash in | Plenty comes in and there is little to spend it on, so it piles up. [USER]: "a second resource" |
| **Spend N**: a second mode on some cards | Turns Fanfare into a bigger card | The starter card is the only common outlet seen |
| **Cue**: a performer acts now | The directing verb | Passed by every seat in act 1. A Cue is "one more small act" |
| **Bow**: a free act and +1 Fanfare as a performer leaves | Churn as payoff | Happens by accident when a summon overflows |
| **Rehearsal**: +1 per stack to every act | The scaling stat | Two card sources. It adds about +2 damage a turn per stack |

## 2. Her intended archetypes, and their status

| Archetype | Key cards | Status after the seats and [USER]'s run |
|---|---|---|
| Salon engine plus Rehearsal | trio summons, Dress Rehearsal, Premiere Season, Tutti!, Full House | Alive but slow. Full House is unconditional |
| Guest cast | ten Guest Stars, Star Billing, Star Turn, Endless Waltz | Strong when assembled (98.9% in the sim with three guests) and hard to assemble |
| Spend finisher | Bravura, Let the People Rejoice, Bring the House Down, Grand Entrance | Untested in play. Too few Spend commons to reach it |
| Flow (gain or spend this turn) | Ousia Surge, Pneuma Refrain, Navia, Clorinde, Critics' Darling | Untested by any seat |
| Bow churn | Escoffier, Thunderous Applause, Da Capo, Final Bow, A Five-Century Act | Untested |
| Solo, with no performers | Solo Verse, Between Acts, Aria for One, Soliloquy, One-Woman Show, The Last Act | **Dead**: the stage opens with Usher and nothing removes him except a Bow card |
| Hydro reactions | Chevalmarin card, Grand Deluge, Regina, Tide of Applause, Groundswell | The most reliable plan the seats found, but mostly carried by Companions, not her own cards |

That is **seven archetypes for one 78-card pool.** The base classes carry
two or three each. Seven thin threads mean any one draft rarely finds
enough of one thread to run it. That is [USER]'s run: "powers that
weren't useful in the deck I ended up in".

## 3. Do her mechanics fit StS2's three acts?

[USER]'s frame:
- **Act 1** asks for cards better than Strike and Defend, quickly.
- **Act 3** asks for scaling past "do 10 damage".
- **Act 2** is the bridge between them.

**Act 1: she fails on damage.**
- The stage gives free defence from turn 1 (Usher's 4 Block), so her
  defence is solved early.
- Her damage frontload is Curtain Rise and plain Attack commons. The
  summon commons trade a card now for 2 to 5 damage a turn later.
- The Fanfare gainers (Rising Applause, Warm Reception) do nothing on the
  turn they are played.
- A draft can easily take a summon, a gainer and a Power and still have no
  better damage than Strike. That is the act-1 boss slog.
- The old identity record already said it: "a dreadful frontload and a
  real setup tax" (`docs/current/characters/furina-identity-record.md`).
  Re-founding did not change it.

**Act 2: no ramp.**
- Multi-enemy fights want AoE. Chevalmarin's 2 to ALL is the only stage
  AoE.
- Guests are the ramp, but they are Uncommon and Rare and arrive at
  random.
- Nothing in the commons turns "I have a stage" into "my stage is getting
  bigger".

**Act 3: scaling is linear and small.**
- Rehearsal adds +1 per act per stack. With three performers that is
  about +2 damage a turn per stack, because Usher's act is Block.
- Demon Form's +2 Strength a turn reaches every attack card.
- Defect's Focus scales orbs that also *evoke* for a big number. Our Bow
  has no big number.
- The only multipliers are extra acts: Full House, Tutti!, Bis!,
  Escoffier, Endless Waltz. Full House has become unconditional, so the
  one strong multiplier is a 3-cost "always on".
- The sim's best stage number came from Dress Rehearsal. That shows the
  stat works and the pool has too little of it.

**Verdict:** she is strongest where StS2 asks least (early defence) and
weakest where it asks most (early damage, late multiplication).

## 4. Do the mechanics interact in a fun way?

A mechanic is fun when it forces a choice with a cost. Here is where each
choice actually lives:
- **Spend now or bank.** This is real; both seats named it the kit's
  steady decision. It is thin because few cards Spend.
- **Who to Cue.** This is weak. An act is a small fixed number, so the
  answer is "the biggest one", and nothing pushes back.
- **Seat order.** This is close to empty. It matters only for Charlotte in
  front of a star, Lynette's move, and who Bows next. Fable: "I would stop
  trying to make it matter."
- **Who leaves.** This is weak. A Bow is a free act plus 1 Fanfare, so
  losing a performer costs almost nothing.
- **Which guest.** This is real when it happens, but it happens late and
  rarely.

**The missing tension is loss.** In Defect, channelling a fourth orb evokes
the oldest. That is a gain and a loss in one card, and planning around it
is the class. Our performers cannot be hurt, rarely leave, and pay you when
they do. The re-founding removed the bars that once made a performer
something to protect, and replaced them with nothing.

**Too many currencies talk past each other.** Energy, Fanfare, star
payments, Rehearsal, seats and Cues are six things to track. Only Energy
and Fanfare compete for the same decision.

## 5. Is this consistent with her lore?

**What Genshin's Furina actually does:**
- Her Salon members **drain the party's HP** to hit harder.
- Her Singer **heals**.
- Her Fanfare grows on **every HP change in the party, up or down**, and
  amplifies both the damage and the healing.
- She switches between **Ousia and Pneuma** (Arkhe).
- Her story is an actress who played a god for five hundred years, a
  trial (the Oratrice), a prophecy, and an audience.

The 2026-09-07 identity record put it in one sentence: "Furina's party is
the show; she turns what happens to it into damage and back into
protection" (`review/ruled/furina-identity-concepts-2026-09-07.md` sec.1).

**What we use:**
- the three Salon names;
- the word Fanfare;
- Arkhe as a card mode;
- the Fontaine cast as guests;
- theatre vocabulary (Cue, Bow, Rehearsal, Encore).

**What we dropped:** the core of her kit, **HP volatility as fuel**. That
is now Fanfare-from-cards, which could belong to any character. The
2026-09-07 packet named this as the step "where she became an Orb
character". [USER] then picked the Tide (HP conversion) as the working
theory. Three passes later it was replaced by the Stage (R269, 2026-09-08)
because the healing law left it with nothing to do while injured.

Two facts have changed since then:
- the healing law's pet clause exists;
- Kokomi is built and owns "HP stability" (the kickoff axis: "Furina = HP
  volatility, Kokomi = HP stability").

Neither is a ruling for the Tide. They are new facts that weaken the case
against it.

**The flavour is right; the mechanics are borrowed from other classes.**
Taken one by one:
- the stage is Defect's orb slots;
- the performers are Necrobinder's pet, three times;
- Fanfare is the Regent's Stars;
- Rehearsal is Focus.

## 6. Good design, or square pegs?

**Square pegs, though each peg is sound on its own:**
1. **She is three base classes' engines bolted together** (orbs, a pet,
   Stars), each with its tension removed:
   - performers cannot die, unlike Osty;
   - leaving pays, unlike an evoke;
   - Fanfare has few sinks, unlike Stars.

   [USER]'s 2026-09-07 line, "must not be a Defect with Fanfare for Focus",
   was reopened only for a stat that is not Fanfare. We complied in name:
   Rehearsal is Focus.
2. **The stage solves the problem StS2 does not pose** (early defence)
   and does not solve the ones it does pose (early damage, late
   multiplication).
3. **Seven archetypes across 78 cards** means no draft finds enough of
   any one.
4. **The choices that exist are thin.** The one real choice, Spend or
   bank, is starved of cards.

**What is genuinely good, and worth keeping into any rebuild:**
- **Guests as rule-benders.** Navia's three hits after a Spend, and
  Wriothesley's 32 off stored Block, are the best turns any player had on
  this kit.
- **Spend as a mode on a card** ("deal 7; Spend 3: deal 17"). It is
  legible, and it is a real decision.
- **The Fanfare gauge as a visible resource.**
- **Fontaine as a cast:** ten real characters each with a recognisable
  twist.
- **HP volatility.** It is the strongest unused lore hook in the kit and
  unclaimed by any base class.

## 7. What this paper does not decide

Nothing here is ruled. These are questions for the research pass, not
answers to them:
- whether the stage stays;
- whether Fanfare goes back to coming from HP changes;
- whether guests become the main event;
- whether the starter changes.

## 8. The research pass

A research agent, run at high effort with freedom to research outside
the repo, takes this paper and returns one complete proposed build worth
trying. It is not handed a menu. Its brief:
- **Read:**
  - this paper;
  - the identity record and the 2026-09-07 concepts packet (the Tide, the
    Arkhe, the Flood);
  - the Stage brief and the re-founding paper;
  - the seat and sim records;
  - the base-game class designs in the repo's references.
- **Research:**
  - Furina's Genshin kit and story;
  - StS2's five classes and what each one's choices are;
  - card-game design writing on resource tension and scaling.
- **Produce one kit:**
  - mechanics, at most two of them new;
  - two or three archetypes;
  - a starter;
  - how it answers acts 1, 2 and 3;
  - a fight-one script with a real choice;
  - a lore map;
  - a pool sketch of about 30 key cards.
- **Say what it reused** from the current pool and what it cut.
- **Check it against standing law:** healing, starters, "not a Defect".
  It names any law it wants reopened, without assuming it is reopened.
- **Test it:**
  - give a sim slice plan with the questions that would kill the
    proposal;
  - optionally prototype that slice in the tier0 sim if it helps;
  - write no C#.

The proposal comes back to [USER] as a paper, then goes to Fable and GPT
for review. Nothing is built until it is ruled.
