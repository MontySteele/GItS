# Kokomi, the Plan-less hand: a hand of basics has no Plan decision, and the starter is not the place to fix it

Written 2026-09-08, rewritten the same evening after the starter reverts, and
rewritten again under R267 (2026-09-08) after a GPT audit found the diagnosis
overstated. The first version applied a default that was not mine to apply:
it gave Kokomi's basic Strike a Plan line (pool pass six, PR #458) and then
her basic Defend a held-Plan rider (pass seven, PR #460). The brief already
said, at line 26, that her basics are plain cards with no Plan line, and
[USER] ruled the same evening that the starter basics are never changed.
Both passes are withdrawn (#461), the starter is the base game's four Strikes
and four Defends again, and this note carries the finding and the question,
with the starter untouched.

## 1. The finding, stated at its real size

Rounds 26 to 30 name the same turn: a hand with no Plan card in it has no
**Plan** decision in it. It still has the decisions every character's thin
hand has. Which enemy to Strike, whether to Block the telegraphed hit or race
it, whether to drink a potion, and how much HP to spend on the room are all
live on a hand of basics, and the round records show the seats making them.
What such a hand lacks is the kit's own question, now-or-later.

The earlier version of this note said "no decision," which is the
overstatement GPT caught, and it matters because the two answers it led to
(a Plan on every Strike, a rider on every Defend) were answers to "no
decision," not to "no Plan decision."

How often it happens: the starter holds two Plan cards in ten (Kurage's Oath,
Slack Water); a five-card hand from ten holds neither on about a fifth of
draws, and the first two hands of every fight see the whole deck once. Round
30 lane 1 counted three of its eleven opening turns; round 27 lane 2 opened
fight 1 on three Strikes and two Defends. After the first reward the rate
falls with every Plan card drafted.

## 2. What the two withdrawn passes measured

They ran, so their reads are kept as measurement even though the rows are
gone. With a Plan line on every Strike (round 31), fifteen of fifteen opening
hands held one and the seat switched between writing and playing it against
a printed rule, but the no-decision turns did not vanish: they became
lethals, a summon with no legal play, or a hand of Defends. With the Defend
rider (round 32), both seats played "Plans first, Defend last" on every turn
that had a Plan and said the rider "stopped being a decision after the
third." A reliably satisfied clause is a tax on the intuitive order, not a
choice. So a Plan line on a basic moves the dead hand rather than removing
it, and a conditional rider on a basic is not a decision. Neither is on the
table.

## 3. Where the answer could live, with the starter fixed

- **Nowhere, for now.** Fight 1 of a run has thin hands for every character
  in the base game, and the kit's question arrives at the first Plan card.
  The records say it arrives well: every assembled lane since round 17 has
  named a turn-one decision, and the natural lanes name one from the first
  reward on. What is not yet read is whether the existing pool's Plan rows
  give enough **depth** once drafted: Feint's immediate payoff after a
  carry-out, Battle Plan's face-up Attack reward, Nereid's first-Plan
  emphasis, and the position rule Scout Ahead gets back under R267 are the
  interactions that make writing a choice rather than a habit. Reading those
  is the next round's job, and it costs no new card.
- **The pool.** Two Common slots that put a Plan line where a basic hand
  needs one, drafted or not. The earlier draft of this note named Ripple as
  the model because the seats loved it; that is the wrong model, since a
  zero-cost Plan that pays Energy and Block is the "write everything" shape
  the pool passes have been removing. If this option is taken, the slots are
  designed against the pass-three charter (both halves worth playing on some
  turn), not against Ripple.
- **The brief's rules.** A rule that gives the jellyfish something to do on
  a hand of basics. A loop change, which [USER] plays. The earlier note
  called it decoration; it still is.
