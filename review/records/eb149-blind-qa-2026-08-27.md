# EB-149 — the blind QA funnel, built (2026-08-27)

R213 accepted a four-step funnel for getting a design slice in front of [USER]
without wasting his time. Step one — proving each card does what its face says
— already had an instrument, the `EB-146` scenario harness. This is step two,
and it had none. It is built.

## What it does, in plain English

You write a **staged turn**: a small file that says which cards go in hand, how
much energy and health the player has, and what to write onto the enemy. The
tool boots the real game, walks to the first fight, sets that board, and then
**stops** — it does not play the turn, because playing the turn is the whole
point of what comes next.

It then writes a **packet**: one page showing exactly what a player would see.
The card faces as the game prints them, the health, the shield, the energy,
the meters that hold something, the enemy and what it is telegraphing. Nothing
else. No card ids, no sheet comments, no roles, no archetypes, no ruling
numbers, no hint about what the cards were designed to do.

You hand that page to a **fresh agent** that has never seen this repository. It
plays the turn and answers four questions:

1. What did you play?
2. What other line did you seriously consider?
3. What did your chosen line give up?
4. Would a different enemy intent have changed it?

Then the tool **grades the answers**, and grading here means one thing only:
deciding whether the turn is refused. It never says a turn is good.

## What it refuses, and why each one

* **No second line.** If the agent seriously considered nothing else, the board
  had one obvious play on it. R213 says that is the readiness test for a slice,
  and this is where it bites.
* **A fourth answer of "no".** If a different enemy telegraph would not have
  changed the line, the enemy is scenery.
* **An empty line.** Nothing was played, so there is no turn to read.
* **An incomplete form.** Three answers out of four is not a form.
* **A grader that designed the cards.** R213's first guard, declared in the
  form rather than assumed.
* **A form answered against a different packet.** Re-staging a turn produces a
  different board; an answer to the old one is not an answer to this one.
* **A dominating line.** See the next section.

A refused turn never reaches [USER], and the verdict file names which of those
seven rules refused it.

## The one number, and why it is 0.5

R213 F allowed exactly one measurement: **decision closeness**. The tool builds
the staged board inside the simulator, walks every line the energy can actually
buy — playing as it goes, so the order matters — scores each play with the
pilot's own existing scoring surface, and reports the gap between the best line
and the next best:

    gap = (best − runner-up) / best

**`DOMINANCE_GAP = 0.5`.** That is exactly the point where the best line is
worth *twice* the runner-up. It is derived rather than picked: the pilot
already treats its score as a value scale with a hard floor at zero, so "twice
the runner-up" is a sentence in units that surface already defines, not a
number laid on top of it. Its error direction is one-way — a threshold this
high can only ever refuse too little, letting a merely lopsided turn through to
the four-question form and then to [USER]. It cannot refuse a turn that was
genuinely close, which is the only failure that would corrupt a verdict.

Two alternatives were considered and are recorded here rather than asked:

* **An absolute gap in score points.** Rejected: the score scale is not stable
  across characters or board sizes, so a fixed number of points would mean
  different things on different boards — a falsifier whose threshold drifts
  with the board refuses different things on different turns.
* **The pilot's cost weight, 0.1 per energy.** Rejected: that is the surface's
  tie-break resolution, the smallest difference it can express, which is the
  opposite of a dominance threshold.

Under R215 B this is the one falsifier number that stays quotable, because it
reads the **turn** and not a sheet row.

## What is guarded, and how

* **The packet cannot see the design side.** The module that builds it imports
  nothing from the simulator's content loaders — a test walks its imports and
  fails if that ever changes. It copies field by field off an allowlist, then
  scrubs its own output and refuses to write a packet that contains a register
  id, a ruling number, a sheet field name, the mod's id prefix, or any
  snake_case token (which is what an internal card id looks like).
* **The grader is never the designer.** Procedurally: a fresh agent, the packet
  inline, no repo access. On the record: the form declares it, and a `true`
  refuses the turn.
* **[USER] plays the same board cold.** `stage --hold` leaves the game sitting
  at the staged board. His form is filed under the grader id `user`, and the
  ledger fills in per-question agreement. **A grader whose answer to question
  two disagrees with [USER] on three of its last five shared turns loses the
  power to mark a turn SURVIVES on its own** — the turn then needs [USER]'s own
  form beside it. That is the whole of "weighted down", and it is pinned by a
  test.

## The worked example

`understudy/turns/kokomi-first-turn-example.yaml` — three energy against five
shipped Kokomi and Inazuma cards that want different things: flat Block, a
summon that pays later, a Companion whose Block only earns its keep if an
attack follows, a meter reader, and an attack that costs a card out of hand as
well as its energy. It was staged live on the deployed build; the packet is
committed at `review/qa/kokomi-first-turn-example/`.

Two fixture forms ship beside it — one complete, one with no second line — and
the tests grade both directions through the real files.

## The funnel has now been run end to end

A **fresh Opus agent** was handed the packet inline, with no repo access and no
tools, and answered the four questions. Its form is committed verbatim at
`review/qa/kokomi-first-turn-example/form-opus-5-fresh.json` — unedited, under
the grader id `opus-5-fresh`. It named a real second line (a Pearl Barrage
exhaust line, and a Bake-Kurage setup line) and answered question four yes at
length, so `grade` returned **SURVIVES**. A no-second-line form is refused in
the same worked example, by the fixture beside it. **That is `EB-149`'s
acceptance met in full: a turn graded blind, and a no-second-line turn
refused.**

Then `execute` replayed the graded line live and the enemy came out right:
Shrinker Beetle 32 HP → 8, player Block 0 → 4, Charge 8 → 9. Those are
Guardrail-7 diagnostics on a hand-set board played once, and they are evidence
of a defect or of nothing at all.

## The seed pin, and what it cost to learn

The first `execute` FAILED, and it failed honestly. The game generated a
different encounter — a Sludge Spinner where the packet had shown a Shrinker
Beetle — and the first targeted play refused: *no enemy 'Shrinker Beetle'; the
fight has ['SLUDGE_SPINNER_0']*. The encounter is generated from the run seed,
and nothing was recording it.

Two fixes, both live-verified:

* **`stage` records the seed the game actually used** — read back off the run
  rather than taken from the request — into `packet.json` and `observed.json`,
  as envelope keys added after the packet is scrubbed and never rendered onto
  the page a grader reads. `execute` embarks with it, and refuses up front if
  there is none rather than replaying onto whatever the game felt like making.
* **`execute` checks the board before it plays anything.** `board_check`
  compares the live enemies' printed names and the hand's printed titles, as a
  multiset, against the packet, and refuses `board_mismatch` listing both
  sides. The per-play refusal is kept underneath it.

**The encounter IS seed-deterministic**, and the proof is the strongest
available: re-staging on the recorded seed `HKB8EJD5G4` reproduced the
committed packet **byte-identically** — same monster, same dealt opening hand,
same `sha256` `6a377aa6…`. The Opus grader's form was therefore still an answer
to the live board, and `execute` ran on it with `seed_honoured: true` and
`board_check.ok: true`.

## One observation from the replay, filed nowhere

The grader predicted 28 damage and the board took 24. The gap is one card: it
read *All Streams Flow to the Sea* as "base 9, plus the Charge bank of 8 read
at one point per two Charge for +4, plus the Banner's +3, for 16", and the card
landed 12. The printed face showed the number with the Charge scaling ALREADY
folded in, and then said "Scales with Charge" underneath — so a reader who
takes the face at its word adds the scaling twice. This is the same shape as
the Pearl Barrage face R215 C describes; the grader's own second answer hedges
in the same place, "on the most generous reading of 'scales with the total
cost'". Recorded here as an observation, **not triaged**: whether it is a
legibility defect or a wording preference is not this tool's call, and the
tool's job was to surface that a careful blind reader misread the face.

## What the first live run taught, and what is deferred

**The live board is not the declared board.** The game deals its own opening
hand on top of the granted one, so a turn that grants five cards stages with
ten in hand; and the encounter is generated, so the enemy and its telegraph are
whatever rolled — the example asked for an attacker and got a beetle
telegraphing a debuff. When this was written there was no dev door that
emptied a hand; `EB-165` built one, and a turn declaring `exact_hand: true`
now stages the declared hand and nothing else. Either way `stage` keeps the
raw board it saw and `closeness --observed` scores *that* — the board the grader actually read. The declared
half of the file stays as the reading available with no game running.

Deferred, deliberately, and none of it blocks the funnel:

* **Emptying the dealt hand — DONE (`EB-165`).** It needed a new dev door on
  the bridge, and `clear_hand` is it: a turn declaring `exact_hand: true` has
  its hand emptied to the bottom of the draw pile before the first grant, and
  `stage` refuses to write a packet whose live hand is not the declared
  multiset. A turn without the flag still stages the granted cards plus
  whatever was drawn, and the packet is honest about it.
* **`burst_max` is not on the wire**, so a card gated on a full Burst meter
  reads as playable on an observed board. Nothing in the funnel's way uses that
  gate today.
* **Four Furina meters have no simulator field** (the Spotlight counters and
  the two Fanfare bounds). They are reported as unmapped and read as zero
  rather than guessed at.
* **No prototype row was staged.** The quarantined surface ships empty; the
  example is entirely shipped cards.

## What this is not

It does not rate a turn. It has no verdict but REFUSED and SURVIVES, and
SURVIVES means *not yet falsified* — the sentence is written into every verdict
file so a one-word reading six months from now cannot be promoted into "the
tool liked it". Every packet, verdict, ledger and log row carries the same
disqualification: the board was set by hand, so nothing measured on it is
comparable to any run, and nothing here is a claim about whether the turn is
fun.
