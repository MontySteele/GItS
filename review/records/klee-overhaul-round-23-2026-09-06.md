## 2. What the round found

**The Spark economy, read from both edges in one round.** Lane 1, the
natural deck: Sparks ended fights at 3, 2, 3, 5, 3, 3, two spent all run,
"no turn, ever, where a Spark price stopped me playing something; income
far outran the prices and Sparks are lost after combat, so I discarded
surplus six times. Spark is not a resource yet; it reads as a second,
unlimited energy pool for a few cards." Lane 2, the deck built of Spark
cards: "the Spark loop deadlocks; Sparks need detonations, detonations
need Spark cards, nothing printed warns of it, and that ended the run
rather than damage." The elite's turn two: a Bomb 12 live, Fireworks Show
the only detonator in hand and priced out at 1 of 2, 0 Block against a
printed 23. Energy and Sparks idled on different turns, "2 to 3 Energy
unspent on four turns, 4 to 5 Sparks stranded on others." The hypothesis's
first clause is answered both ways: the price bound only when every
detonator carried one. Eight rounds of readings now lie on one line, and
the line is the starter pick (ruled R262, hold): the loop has no
Energy-priced exit in the starter and no floor in the relic. The assembled
lane's deadlock was added to that packet the same morning.

**The starter's detonator, fourth natural reading.** Lane 1: four turns
with a Bomb up and nothing to set it off, one "a 28-damage pile under an
expiring aura, rescued only because Dahlia's draw found Ka-pow!"; "three of
six picks went to detonators" because "the set-off shortage is the
starting deck's real gap (1 detonator in 10)". Nothing new is proposed
here; it is the pick's evidence.

**Hexerei, third clause: not stated, and the questions are now exact.**
Lane 1 met the word on five cards, read the tip every time, and could not
resolve three things: what "up to 3" caps, whether "and Klee herself"
makes every Klee card satisfy Coven Errand, and why Jean's shop face
prints "cost 2" with the Spark surcharge only in prose; it passed on three
cards over it. Those three questions went to the fixer rewriting the tip
(`EB-596`) with the instruction to print the engine's answers, not
guesses. "The one opaque thing in an otherwise superbly legible kit."

**The random Set off, again.** Lane 2: "random-set-off cards always found
a Bombed body first when one existed", but "the bridge prints only the
after-state, so hit order is not observable" (`EB-611`); with no bombed
body Tinder Toss doubled both hits onto one enemy. The rule holds on its
first hit on every reading; the log cannot show it.

**Block that reads the Bomb.** Lane 2: "no unconditional Block: Careful
Now and Sorry, Jean... both read the Bomb, so Block and damage are one
resource and empty boards mean no defence." Lane 1 named Dig In (1 Spark,
8 Block, no Energy) as the deny that won a fight. The assembled deck was
built without a plain Block and paid for it; recorded as the deck's
shape, not the pool's.

**The decision, both lanes.** Lane 1: "Set off here deals 28 Pyro damage.
Bomb sizes here: 16 priced my two-turn Melt plan in one number", and
"Reaction preview: Melt, this card's 12 lands 21 made a lethal visible
before I spent anything"; the natural deck's lethal arithmetic was
readable end to end. Lane 2's decisions were the same cash-or-cook until
the loop closed on it.

**Legibility.** A Spark appeared with no Bomb on the field and the Spark
row does not say where each came from (`EB-610`). Stoke the Fuse's price,
counted or not among the spent (`EB-612`). An intent number that did not
move with Strength on one enemy and did on another (`EB-607`). Rosaria's
aura rider when the hit's Melt consumes the aura (`EB-608`). The Smith
page's basics and its already-upgraded cards (`EB-609`). Flame Dance's

face does not say so). Whether Slow touches Bomb damage (`EB-495`'s table,
cited). Praised: the Bomb readout, the reaction preview, "CANNOT BE
